# Methylcobalamin injections in ALS: clinical-function evidence with a selected-population limit

JETALS supports a specific functional result: 50 mg intramuscular methylcobalamin twice weekly reduced the decline in ALSFRS-R over 16 weeks in selected Japanese patients with early ALS and a moderate observed rate of progression. Both groups deteriorated. The earlier, broader 182-week trial did not meet its primary efficacy tests; its favorable early-disease subgroup was post hoc and informed the later trial's selection. These are distinct trials, not interchangeable cohorts or proof that ordinary oral vitamin B12 treats ALS. This qualification supports a neurological-compensation comparison, with no automatic nomination change and no demonstrated BUBR1 restoration, MVA efficacy, regenerative effect, or oral/injected equivalence.

The two-trial source qualification and the 2018 protocol/UMIN follow-up were jointly accepted by an independent primary reviewer on 27 September 2026. This portable synthesis incorporates both; the historical source-stage metadata remains preserved in the companion JSON.

## JETALS: the randomized functional contrast

The 25-center Japanese study enrolled adults aged at least 20 with definite, probable or laboratory-supported ALS under updated Awaji criteria. Onset had to be within one year **at entry to the 12-week observation period**; randomized participants then had to show a 1–2 point ALSFRS-R decline during that period. They were ambulatory outpatients with Japanese ALS severity grade 1 or 2, FVC above 60%, and no prior tracheostomy or noninvasive respiratory support. This excludes both slower and faster observed progressors and advanced respiratory disease. B12 deficiency symptoms and serious liver/kidney disease were excluded. It does not establish efficacy across all ALS stages, children, or MVA. [Main Methods, protocol pp 19–20, outcome supplement pp 19–20.]

Participants received 50 mg E0302 or placebo intramuscularly twice weekly for 16 weeks. Stable riluzole was allowed; starting/increasing it was restricted, and edaravone was prohibited during the blinded treatment and within four weeks before observation entry. Rehabilitation and nutritional care were allowed. Central allocation used dynamic minimization; efficacy/ALSFRS assessors were separated from injectors and safety assessors. The red drug and urine discoloration required masking precautions; the sources describe these precautions, not a verified successful-blinding test. [Protocol §§6, 8 and 10; SAP §§3–6.]

Of 212 screened, 203 entered observation and 130 were randomized, 65 per arm. One placebo participant was excluded from efficacy and safety analyses after an alternative diagnosis of cervical spinal stenosis, leaving **129 in the full analysis set: 64 placebo and 65 active**. Three others withdrew consent. **126 completed 16 weeks, 63 per arm**. Thus the reported FAS is not an analysis of every randomized participant. The 124 entering the open-label extension do not provide another controlled efficacy cohort. [Native Figure 1.]

The primary endpoint was change in ALSFRS-R total score (0–48, higher better) from randomization to week 16. The mixed model for repeated measures included baseline score, time, treatment, minimization factors and treatment-by-time interaction, with no primary missing-value imputation. The observed/model-based contrast does not eliminate assumptions about missingness. The SAP treats measurements after treatment cessation and specified new/increased concomitant treatment as missing and plans supplemental alternatives; not every planned alternative is quantitatively reported in the paper/supplement. [Main Statistical Analysis; SAP pp 5, 13, 19–23.]

The trial's “43%” is a relative reduction in the magnitude of **score decline over 16 weeks**, not a 43% strength gain, responder rate, survival improvement or recovery. No defined death/ventilation endpoint occurred during this short controlled period, so a survival treatment effect was not estimated. The paper nevertheless describes one placebo patient's laryngotracheal separation for dysphagia at week 5 and subsequent stenosis at week 13; “no endpoint events” must not become “no airway procedures.” Main Table 2 describes noninvasive ventilation as 24-hour use, while protocol §10.2.2 operationalizes all-day use as at least 22 hours; no events occurred under the reported analysis.

The paper reports less decline in fine motor, gross motor and combined limb ALSFRS-R subscores, with no significant bulbar or respiratory subscore difference. These are supplemental component findings, not separate trials or direct restoration of muscle strength. eTable 3 gives raw week-16 changes for placebo/active of −3.46/−1.97 (limb total), −1.68/−0.89 (fine), −1.78/−1.08 (gross), −0.84/−0.84 (bulbar), and −0.51/−0.13 (respiratory); it does not print component comparison P values. Its raw total changes (−4.81/−2.94) must not replace the adjusted primary changes.

eTable 1's FAS sensitivity contrast is **1.95 (0.42 to 3.48), P = .013**, not the 1.97 primary estimate. eTable 2's linear weekly slopes are −0.3129 placebo and −0.1932 active (placebo minus active −0.1197, SE 0.0504, P = .018); the quadratic model's coefficient comparisons are P = .052 and .611, so not every sensitivity specification is positive. eFigure 3 reports homocysteine-change versus ALSFRS-R-change correlation **r = −0.0761, P = .3972**. The biomarker response does not establish mediation or neuronal renewal.

eTable 4 contains multiple nominal subgroups, including a riluzole-use contrast of **2.11 (0.46 to 3.76), P = .013**, with 58 per arm at baseline and 57 per arm at week 16. The no-riluzole subgroup is very small (baseline 6/7; endpoint 6/6) and has −0.23 (−6.04 to 5.58), P = .930. This was not a factorial riluzole trial and does not establish synergy. Favorable nominal results in men, limb-onset disease, higher baseline FVC and other strata coexist with nonpositive counterpart strata. Significance in one subgroup and not another is not a tested treatment-by-subgroup difference or a validated responder-selection rule.

The main reports a one-sided primary significance threshold of .025. SAP p 20 states a general two-sided .05 level and two-sided 95% intervals; p 21 defines primary success by the directional 95% interval excluding zero in the beneficial direction, consistent with that primary criterion. The SAP explicitly leaves secondary-timepoint P values unadjusted for multiplicity. Component/subgroup, secondary and sensitivity findings cannot be promoted to independently confirmed primary outcomes.

## Earlier phase II/III trial: preserve the overall null and the exploratory early signal

The earlier 51-center Japanese study randomized **373** patients to placebo/25 mg/50 mg (124/124/125), injected twice weekly for up to **182 weeks**; treatment ended at a primary event. Three diagnostic exclusions left FAS **370 (123/124/123)**. Eligibility allowed onset within 36 months and required a 1–3 point decline during 12-week observation, ALS severity 1/2, FVC above 60%, and no prior ventilatory support/tracheostomy. About 90% used stable riluzole. This is broader than JETALS, but still not unselected ALS.

Native Figure 1 distinguishes **260 study completions from 113 withdrawals**. Completion included a primary event: only **95** completed the full 182-week period (29/35/31); **165** completed because of an event (57/53/55). It would be wrong to call all 260 full-duration exposure completers. Three diagnostic exclusions sit outside the FAS but inside randomized disposition.

The two primary outcomes were time to death or invasive/noninvasive ventilatory support for at least 22 hours because of progression, and ALSFRS-R change to the final 182-week evaluation. Four planned contrasts used log-rank or Wilcoxon scores for placebo versus 50 mg and placebo versus both active doses combined. The SAP defines permutation-based multiplicity adjustment and a one-sided .025 criterion; its secondary/subgroup analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted. Neither primary outcome established efficacy in the overall FAS. [Main Table 2A; SAP pp 8,16–20,26.]

Table 2A prints event crude P = .33/.20 and adjusted P = .15/.13 for 50 mg/combined; functional crude P = .15/.09 and adjusted P = .18/.19. These are retained as printed, not independently recomputed or substituted with conventional pairwise tests. The supplements give the functional combined crude value as .087. Lower event rates led to a blinded increase from 300 patients/130 weeks to 360 planned/182 weeks, with two interim safety/futility reviews. The trial does not become positive because the medians numerically favor active treatment.

Missingness and event handling matter over this duration. The SAP carries forward the last post-event efficacy assessment where available, uses discontinuation assessments/last available values, censors event-free withdrawals with specified 28-day rules, and gives deaths or absent post-event data worst ranks (−999 as a ranking placeholder, not an ALSFRS-R score). Grip values above 0 but below 5 kg are handled as 0 in that SAP. Endpoint table counts therefore do not mean everyone was directly measured at 182 weeks. [SAP pp 17–20,27–29.]

The post-hoc early-diagnosis subgroup (diagnosed within 12 months after onset) has **144 in the event analysis (48/54/42)** and **143 for the functional endpoint (48/54/41)**. Event medians are 570/1087/1197 days; HR 50 mg is 0.50 (0.27–0.93), HR 25 mg 0.64 (0.38–1.09), combined 0.57 (0.35–0.92). Functional median changes are −26.5/−26.5/−22.0, with P = .003 for 50 mg and .013 combined in the supplement (.01 rounded in Table 2B). The favorable signal is retained, but it follows a null full-cohort result and extensive explorations of onset cutoffs and prognostic strata. It does not retrospectively change the original primary outcome.

The earlier secondary findings should not be flattened into either “all null” or “all improved.” Table e-1 reports these overall contrasts, ordered 50 mg/combined: FVC P = .12/.08; MMT .12/.03; right grip .28/.11; left grip .18/.03; Norris .10/.03; ALSAQ-40 .58/.63. The .03 combined-dose findings are exploratory favorable contrasts, not confirmatory findings under the SAP's one-sided .025 efficacy criterion or its unadjusted secondary framework. In the early subgroup, Table e-4 reports FVC P < .001/.004, MMT .04/.05, right grip .11/.07, left grip .08/.04, Norris .005/.008 and ALSAQ-40 .54/.35. Table e-7 also reports exploratory ALSFRS-R component and Norris subcomponent findings. Their exact contrasts, hierarchy and companion nulls are retained in the JSON and native supplement; none is an additional independent cohort.

JETALS's design was informed by a still narrower earlier subset of 58 (32 placebo and 26 receiving 50 mg), within one year at observation entry and with 1–2 point run-in decline. The prior 16-week mean changes quoted in JETALS eAppendix 3 are −5.84±4.95 versus −3.23±4.01 (difference 2.61, CI0.15–5.06, reported P = .008). Selection used a larger observed effect in 1–2 than 1–3 point decliners, with that comparison described as “data not shown.” This explains the later enriched population and sample-size assumption; it is neither the 144-person subgroup nor an independently replicated dataset.

## Safety, registration, plans and disclosure

JETALS Table 3 reports any adverse event in 40/65 active (62%) versus 42/64 placebo (66%), adverse drug reactions 5/65 (8%) versus 1/64 (2%), serious adverse events 1/65 versus 2/64, severe events 1 in each, no serious drug reactions and no adverse-event discontinuations. The table footnote includes events emerging during observation and treatment, rather than guaranteeing a treatment-only window. eTable 6 lists mild active-arm constipation, injection-site pain, fever, QT prolongation and rash, and placebo hypoesthesia. This small short trial is not a safety-equivalence trial or evidence for long-term use in MVA. The earlier trial reported any adverse events in over 97% of each arm, serious-event proportions 64.2%/62.1%/65.0%, and treatment-related events 5/123 (4.1%),9/124 (7.3%),7/123 (5.7%) for placebo/25 mg/50 mg. Progression-related manifestations were generally not counted as adverse events except death. Neither study establishes unrestricted safety. The 2018 protocol describes one cardiac-arrest death in the earlier 50 mg arm as possibly related; the 2019 trial main calls the death following myocardial infarction or arrhythmia unrelated based on history. No adjudication record resolves the conflict. It is an earlier-trial event, not a JETALS event or a new cohort.

Current [NCT03548311](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03548311) records first posting 2018-06-07 after recorded start 2017-11-01, primary completion 2020-02-14 and study completion 2025-02-18; the saved current record was last updated 2025-03-26 and contains no posted results. This NCT record was posted after study start. The separately verified current UMIN000029588 record displays registration on 17 October 2017 and disclosure on 19 October 2017, with anticipated start 7 November 2017. Registration coincides with the earliest reported observation date; disclosure follows it by two days. Historical field versions were not read, so neither public registration before every participant nor unchanged 2017 fields is established. The current record includes later open-label phases; that does not supply controlled extension results here. The paper's investigator-initiated/public-funding description and the current registry's Eisai lead-sponsor field are reported in their own contexts.

The published 21 December 2018 JETALS protocol specifies the week 16 ALSFRS-R primary endpoint, selected early population, 50 mg IM twice weekly, central minimization and baseline-adjusted MMRM with a directional 95% CI criterion/one-sided .025 level. It was published during the trial before final data collection, not before all participant observation. The later supplied protocol version 7.0 (26 June 2020) and draft-labelled SAP version 2.1 (24 March 2020; revision table calls it final) postdate current recorded primary completion; the SAP cites version 1.1 from 15 February 2018. Thus core public planning is established earlier, while exact timing of every final SAP/missingness/sensitivity choice and actual lock/unblinding remain unverified. The protocol calls for locking week 16 data before unblinding; no actual timestamp was obtained. SAP p 25 has an inconsistent interval-direction sentence and p 26 says 16 months where the trial is 16 weeks; p 21’s primary rule is clear. The 113-page protocol was read selectively, while the 2018 scientific protocol XML was read completely.

Current [NCT00444613](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00444613) records start 2007-04, first posting 2007-03-08, primary completion 2014-03 and completion 2014-07, with a 2023 update and no posted results. The paper describes a study period beginning December 2006; the SAP records first treatment registration 2007-03-26. Those differing milestones do not establish prospectivity of all outcomes. Its supplied SAP version 2.0 is dated 2014-07-29 and references protocol version 10 of 2010; no original protocol, earlier SAP version or historical registry fields were independently read. The early ≤12 month result is explicitly post hoc in the main paper and remains so.

JETALS reports AMED/public grant funding; Eisai supplied active drug and placebo, and multiple authors report Eisai nonfinancial support or other industry relationships. The authors state that funders did not control design, collection, analysis or publication. The earlier trial was funded by Eisai; several authors were employees, statistical/writing support involved Eisai, and the lead author disclosed Eisai grants and a licensed ALS-treatment patent. The shared investigator and supplier context limits claims of wholly independent replication. It does not erase the randomized result.

## Comparative interpretation and actual reading scope

Methylcobalamin deserves consideration on the same functional, exposure, durability and fidelity criteria as other compensation candidates: it has a randomized clinical-function signal at a specific injected exposure in a selected neurological population. Compared with niacin’s open-label strength observation, JETALS has stronger controlled human clinical-function design; that is not a claim of a larger effect, MVA efficacy or superiority over nominees. The broader prior null, short confirmatory duration, unconfirmed survival effect, linked direct strength/respiratory nulls, exploratory component positives, administration burden and absent MVA data limit transfer and priority. A BUBR1 mechanism is additionally necessary for a **restoration** claim, not an extra exclusion rule for all functional compensation comparisons. The present evidence does not establish renewal of muscle or neurons, chromosome-segregation fidelity, correction of BUB1B deficiency, or comparative superiority over existing nominees.
