# L-ascorbic acid: distinct treatment identity in an already reviewed oocyte source

Reviewed 23 September 2026. **A separate L-ascorbic acid appraisal is justified as reuse of existing evidence**, alongside the existing NAC entry `prior-28`. Figure 7 actually tests L-ascorbic acid alone in one arm and NAC alone in another; it does not test their combination. The distinct phosphate preparation in `new-ascorbate-2-phosphate` must not inherit this result. This integration adds one identity-specific assessment by reusing that study; it adds no unique abstract read or outer-corpus completion credit.

The source is [PMID39845481 / PMC11751902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11751902/), [DOI10.1002/rmb2.12622](https://doi.org/10.1002/rmb2.12622). The prior repository review already accurately describes its chromosome-protection evidence in `catalog-independent-review.md`, under “Ascorbic acid and NAC.” This check read all Methods; Results 3.2, 3.4 and 3.7 with their captions; targeted identity/downstream-development Discussion paragraphs; and supplementary text. Native Figures 4 and 7 were visually inspected. Other main images and the complete Introduction/Discussion were not reviewed anew; no whole-paper reread is claimed.

## Identity and experiment

Figure 7 explicitly names **L-ascorbic acid (AsA), 500 µM**. No AsA supplier, catalog, CAS, purity, stock solvent, added counterion, or final vehicle concentration is specified in the read Methods or supplement. Preserve the named acid rather than asserting authenticated sodium ascorbate, ascorbate 2-phosphate, or equivalence to a clinical/consumer vitamin-C preparation. Exact normalization of the alias `L-ascorbic acid` matches catalog entity CHEMBL196; this is a name join only, not evidence of approval, current supply, or product equivalence.

The mice were female, three to four weeks old. The original C57BL/6 Sod1 line had been backcrossed more than eight generations to CD1/ICR; WT and Sod1-knockout littermates supplied the genetic comparisons. Germinal-vesicle cumulus–oocyte complexes underwent **18-hour in-vitro maturation at 37°C in 21% oxygen**, in supplemented Waymouth's MB752/1 medium. AsA or NAC was added during maturation. This is prevention/attenuation during a culture challenge, not delayed reversal after an established chromosome error, oral supplementation, or an aged-animal experiment.

## Direct drug outcomes

Native Figure 7 compares untreated Sod1KO, Sod1KO plus AsA, and Sod1KO plus NAC. There is **no drug-treated WT arm** in that figure.

| Reported endpoint | Untreated Sod1KO | AsA | NAC | Source inference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Polar-body-I emission / maturation | 86.4% | 95.9% | 97.7% | AsA versus control P<.05; NAC P<.01 |
| Normal 20-chromosome category | 60.0% | 84.6% | 84.6% | Each treatment versus control P<.05 |
| ≤19 chromosomes | 14.0% | 12.8% | 11.5% | No significant difference reported |
| ≥21 chromosomes | 0% | 0% | 0% | No significant difference reported |
| Premature sister-chromatid separation (PSSC) | 26.0% | 2.6% | 3.8% | Each treatment versus control P<.01 |

The caption specifies Fisher's exact tests with Bonferroni correction. Chromosomes were spread and counted after Giemsa staining; this is a direct chromosome-count/PSSC endpoint, not selected-chromosome FISH or a transcript proxy. It does not exclude subchromosomal abnormalities. The benefit is concentrated in the PSSC/normal-category distribution; avoid claiming a demonstrated reduction in each numerical-aneuploidy class.

Figure 7B's caption lists **50/39/52 oocytes**, compatible with its plotted category percentages. Figure 7A repeats those counts, but its maturation percentages cannot be reconstructed as pooled integer success counts over those denominators. The source does not explain whether another input denominator or an averaging scheme produced panel A. Preserve this unresolved denominator issue, without silently repairing it or invalidating panel B. Donor-mouse numbers, independent Figure 7 culture repeats, randomization and blinded scoring are not specified in the read methods. Triplicate experiments and 60-oocyte pools are explicitly described for western blotting and cannot be reassigned to Figure 7.

## BUBR1 and downstream limits

Figure 4 compares **untreated WT versus Sod1KO**, not antioxidant-treated oocytes. Total BUBR1 protein, normalized to GAPDH, did not significantly differ between genotypes; kinetochore BUBR1 at MI was lower in Sod1KO (native rounded labels 1.0 versus 0.7; P<.01). The localization analysis used 15 oocytes per genotype and 595/600 measured kinetochores each, so kinetochores are nested within oocytes. No treatment-induced BUBR1 increase, BUBR1-dependent mediation, Bub1b-mutant rescue, or MVA benefit follows.

The source explicitly says the effect on fertilization and embryonic development remains undetermined. The Discussion's AsA-assisted two-cell to four-cell development is a citation to a different study, not this experiment; no blastocyst, embryo-transfer or live-birth benefit was measured here. Preliminary glutathione-ethyl-ester and β-mercaptoethanol comparisons are mentioned as data not shown, without more potent effects than AsA/NAC; they do not supply quantified standalone appraisals in this check.

**Disposition:** retain a chemically distinct experimental chromosome-protection comparator. Its direct meiotic outcome is stronger than an abundance-only hypothesis, but its Sod1 lesion, high-oxygen culture, absent treated-WT comparator and missing developmental outcomes prevent transfer to BUB1B-MVA or skeletal-muscle function. Niacin and apitegromab remain provisional rather than proven MVA benchmarks. Existing NAC and phosphate entries can remain unchanged while this source is explicitly shared.
