# CAPE: retain neurological function; correct the BUBR1 assay attribution

**23 September 2026.** [Konar et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2020.561925) support a neurological-function research reserve. **Figure 2C measures RNA by RT-PCR, not BUBR1 protein.** Supplementary Table S2 lists the corresponding primers; Table S1 supplies no BUBR1 antibody. The unquantified RNA reduction during neuroblastoma differentiation cannot establish protein loss, checkpoint harm or restoration.

Actual treatment improved male tau-model fly climbing and selected eclosion/thermal-tolerance outcomes. Supplementary Figure S3 retains nonsignificant phototaxis-memory and larval-crawling results. Eclosion counts are not lifespan.

In scopolamine-exposed male mice, CAPE and its γ-cyclodextrin formulation improved object-discrimination scores. The study reports nine animals/group and seven treatment days; the formulated compound had the larger plotted response. No carrier-only arm establishes formulation-specific causality.

Reporting conflicts remain: treatment before versus after scopolamine, saline/DMSO concentrations, task durations, and replicate descriptions. We do not silently repair them or multiply the stated animal count.

**Decision:** preserve measured neurological benefits, but neither BUBR1 restoration nor muscle/MVA benefit is demonstrated. CAPE is not caffeic acid or an interchangeable propolis product. Its biological effects merit consideration; they do not establish superiority over the current restoration/compensation hypotheses.
