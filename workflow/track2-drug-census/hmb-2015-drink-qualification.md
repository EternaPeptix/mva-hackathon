# HMB-containing drink in healthy older women: provisional primary-source appraisal

**Status:** root-reader source review; independent review pending. **Disposition:** retain as a low-cost, formulation-level muscle-function comparator, not a Track 2 nominee and not evidence of BUB1B/MVA efficacy.

## Identity and design

Berton et al. (PMID [26529601](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26529601/), PMCID [PMC4631374](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4631374/), DOI [10.1371/journal.pone.0141757](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0141757)) randomized 80 healthy women over 65 who already attended a mild fitness program twice weekly. The eight-week intervention was a 220 mL Ensure Plus Advance drink containing 1.5 g calcium-HMB each morning. The other arm received no drink, placebo or matched nutrition product. The preparation therefore supports a **tested drink comparison**, not an isolated HMB effect.

The prespecified primary outcome was Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB). Secondary outcomes included knee torque, six-minute walking distance, grip strength/endurance, DXA and pQCT measures. Fifteen of 80 randomized participants did not complete follow-up (8 active, 7 control); analyses report 32 active and 33 control completers. The trial was open label, although staff were asked not to learn allocation. Compliance was estimated from returned bottles and participant reports. Registration was posted after enrollment had begun; the paper says study conduct ran February–June 2014 and explains the delay as a ClinicalTrials.gov login problem. The current registry API shows first submission 16 April 2014 and first posting 21 April 2014 (estimated); its actual-start field and primary-outcome entries are blank.

## Results and limits

The primary SPPB contrast was null (between-group change p=.92); balance, chair-stand time and walking time were also null. DXA weight, fat-free mass, appendicular-mass index and abdominal fat did not differ. Maximum handgrip strength was null.

Nominal secondary contrasts favored the active drink for isokinetic knee flexion (+1.56 N·m, p=.03), isokinetic extension (+3.32 N·m, p=.03), isometric torque (+9.74 N·m, p=.02), six-minute walk (+7.67 m, p=.04), grip endurance (+21.41 s, p=.02), and pQCT muscle density at radius and tibia (both p=.03). The study tested numerous secondary endpoints without multiplicity adjustment and labels those analyses exploratory. The authors themselves caution that statistically significant secondary changes may be clinically irrelevant. Their cited repeatability estimates were 16.3 N·m for isokinetic torque, 11.9 m for the six-minute walk and 20.8 s for grip endurance; these come from separate small samples and are not clinical-importance thresholds, but they reinforce caution about the size and meaning of the observed contrasts.

Five mild symptoms were reported in the active group (two abdominal-pain, two constipation, one itching); they resolved after stopping the drink. No severe adverse events were reported. The study excluded renal failure and any history/evidence of malignancy in the preceding five years (except non-melanoma skin neoplasia). It therefore supplies little safety information for the MVA population and does not test tumor surveillance or aneuploid-cell behavior.

## BUB1B relevance and competitiveness

The complete article reports no BUB1B/BUBR1 assay, spindle-checkpoint measure, chromosome-error endpoint or MVA participant. This is generic nutritional muscle-function evidence. It provides a human, oral, low-cost *functional-adjacent* comparator, but its primary function outcome was null and its positive findings are secondary, unblinded, unadjusted comparisons from a drink-versus-no-treatment design.

Against the current leaders, HMB does not approach niacin’s proposed causal-protein connection: there is no NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 mechanism here. It is less rigorous and less pediatric/disease-specific than apitegromab’s placebo-controlled SMA motor-function evidence, although the oral supplement format is much easier to scale. Its contribution is to make an accessible muscle-support alternative visible in the rejected-routes table—not to displace either lead or to justify combining it with them. The project’s existing calcium-HMB appraisal is a separate 2021 low-muscle-mass cohort with a protocol-defined powder and a modest gait-speed main effect; this 2015 drink study is not a replication of that formulation or cohort.

**Review limits:** one root reader inspected the full PLOS article and the current ClinicalTrials.gov API record. There was no independent source review, participant-level reanalysis, product assay, HMB exposure measurement or MVA experiment. This appraisal is provisional and earns no accepted-ledger, independent-review or MVA-efficacy credit.
