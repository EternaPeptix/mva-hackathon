# Apcin: positive liver-injury evidence beside negative mechanistic comparisons

**22 September 2026.** Apcin earns a reported mouse survival and liver-injury
benefit in a pretreatment experiment. This updates the existing compound entry;
it does not establish BUBR1 restoration, MVA benefit or a clinical regimen.
The earlier [HeLa/A549 and proTAME comparisons](mitotic-qualification.md) remain
context-specific findings, not grounds for declaring universal inefficacy.

## What was administered and measured

[Shi et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9997844/) administered
Apcin at 30 mg/kg intraperitoneally in corn oil, eight hours before CCl4 injury
in male C57BL/6J mice. Controls received vehicle with or without CCl4; there
was no uninjured Apcin-only group. This was prevention of an experimentally
scheduled insult, not treatment after established liver failure.

Methods describes separate tissue and survival cohorts, each with seven mice
per group. Random allocation is stated; concealment and blinding are not.

| Endpoint | Positive result | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Survival, Figure 7B | Higher plotted survival with Apcin than injury alone. | Last displayed levels imply about 4/7 versus 1/7 survivors, inferred from the graph. Curves end around day four despite a stated seven-day endpoint. No survival P value, confidence interval or at-risk table is reported; verified seven-day fractions and statistical significance cannot be claimed. |
| ALT/AST, Figure 7G | Both decrease versus injury alone, with P<0.05 labels. | Caption n=4; Methods says four repeats, leaving biological versus technical replication unresolved. These are injury markers, not hepatic synthetic-function measurements. |
| TUNEL/H&E, Figure 7F | Representative images support less apparent injury. | Caption n=3, without quantitative apoptosis fraction, injury score or field denominator. |

Survival is an organism-level outcome and deserves positive credit. Residual
deaths in the treated injury group cannot be assigned to drug toxicity.

## What the study does not attribute to Apcin

Figure 7C–E and the supplementary expression figure compare CCl4 with vehicle;
they contain no Apcin group. Their gene changes cannot establish drug-induced
normalization of CDC20, BUB1B or hepatic function. The human hepatocyte expansion
experiment also has no Apcin arm. Its culture mixture is not a set of individually
tested liver-failure treatments.

BUB1B is a bioinformatic hub with increased transcript expression in discovery
datasets. No drug-induced BUBR1 protein change, checkpoint function, faithful
division or chromosome-error measurement is shown. CDC20 inhibition is the
drug's stated rationale; liver target engagement and a CDC20-dependent rescue
experiment are absent. No pharmacokinetics, dose response, dedicated healthy
drug control or longer-term regeneration assessment is supplied.

## Competitive position and next experiment

Retain Apcin as a **context-dependent research lead**, including this positive
organ-protection signal and the earlier negative biochemical comparisons.
Neither source demonstrates MVA muscle benefit or warrants transferring
proTAME's effects to Apcin. This does not displace the provisional niacin and
apitegromab comparison, whose MVA efficacy is also unproved.

The immediate questions are the complete survival data through the stated
endpoint, biological replication, post-injury dosing and target dependence.
A disease-relevant experiment would also need useful tissue output and direct
chromosome-fidelity measurements before a restoration claim.
