# Cesanelli 2026 hot-water broccoli and mustard powder: recovery qualification

Appraisal: `new-hot-water-broccoli-glucoraphanin`. Sole descriptive alias: **Cesanelli 2026 hot-water broccoli and mustard powder**. Source qualification date: 2026-09-27. The alias names the tested study preparation; it does not establish a current retail product or generic ingredient equivalence.

**Retain this as a preparation-specific human functional null.** Cesanelli et al. did not demonstrate improved recovery of elbow-flexor torque, movement, soreness or structural damage markers through 96 hours after severe eccentric exercise. The tested intervention was a broccoli/mustard powder reconstituted with boiling water; sulforaphane exposure and Nrf2 engagement were not measured. This is not proof of generic glucoraphanin or sulforaphane ineffectiveness, an equivalence result, or evidence about BUB1B/MVA. The study must remain separate from purified or D,L-sulforaphane animal preparations.

Source: [Cesanelli et al., Nutrients 2026;18:710](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/4/710), [PMID 41754227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41754227/), [PMCID PMC12943077](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12943077/), DOI 10.3390/nu18040710. The primary source qualification covered the complete original scientific main, all eight original figure captions and native figures, both five-page registry protocol/SAP versions, and the current [NCT07373106 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07373106) at the primary reading depth recorded below. No participant-level analysis was performed.

## Exact preparation, dose and schedule

The Methods specify **10 g freeze-dried powder containing 99.5% broccoli and 0.5% mustard seed**, supplied by The Smarter Food Company, Norwich, UK. Each serving was dissolved in 125 mL boiling water and consumed while hot or warm. The source states **320 micrograms of glucoraphanin per serving**, attributed to manufacturer confirmation. The abstract, main Methods, registry and both original protocol PDFs all print micrograms. None of the inspected sources supports silently substituting milligrams or micromoles. No batch certificate, chemical assay, residual myrosinase measurement or independently verified product concentration is supplied. Consequently, the paper's high-dose label and comparison with literature doses are not independently validated here.

The detailed schedule is six daily pre-exercise servings, a seventh serving three hours before exercise, then daily dosing for four days afterward. Figure 1 and the protocols summarize this as seven days before plus four days after, with a stated 14-day washout before crossover. The abstract instead describes two weeks of intake before exercise. The detailed Methods take precedence for this appraisal; the discrepancy remains unresolved. The Methods also say the second exercise occurs after 14 days, while the figure describes a washout between the intervention periods. Exact period-to-period calendar spacing is therefore not fully reconstructible.

The placebo contained spinach, nettle, dill, parsley, carrot, onion and basil and matched appearance and preparation. Its full ingredient quantities, nutrient profile and equivalence in potentially active constituents were not supplied. Broccoli-free is not the same as biologically inert. The result is a contrast between these preparations, not isolated glucoraphanin causality.

The 0.5% mustard component does not establish active myrosinase after boiling-water preparation. The authors suggest that heating may diminish plant enzyme activity and leave conversion dependent on residual activity or gut microbes. This is plausible interpretation, not a measured exposure result. No sulforaphane/metabolite concentration, Nrf2 target-gene response, direct oxidative-stress assay or genotype-dependent metabolism assessment was obtained. Logs and sachet counts exceeding 95% support consumption, not successful sulforaphane delivery.

## Participants, controls and crossover

Seventeen healthy men aged 18-35 were recruited; 15 were analyzed (age 26 ± 6 years, body mass 83.8 ± 13.4 kg). Two could not participate completely because of work duties. Text and caption describe exclusion before randomization of the remaining 15; native Figure 1 instead labels 17 randomized and then two dropouts. The timing and allocation of the exclusions cannot be resolved. Figure 1 depicts eight X-to-Y and seven Y-to-X completers. There are 15 people contributing paired conditions, not 30 independent participants; endpoint-specific missing counts are not separately reported.

Participants were untrained, without recent resistance training, arm injury or reported chronic metabolic, cardiovascular or musculoskeletal disease. They were instructed to maintain diet and avoid other exercise; broader diet was not comprehensively controlled. This is acute experimental damage in healthy young men, not chronic weakness, inherited myopathy or an MVA model.

Treatment order was reportedly randomized by a separate researcher using a random-number generator. Participants and assessors were masked, and sachets were identical; blinding success was not tested. Each period used six sets of ten maximal voluntary eccentric elbow-flexor contractions on a dynamometer. Both arms were measured for most outcomes; the other arm provided a non-exercised control under the same systemic supplement exposure. It is not a separate untreated person.

Section 2.2 and both protocols say the dominant arm was always exercised first, whereas section 2.3 and the protocols' exercise section describe a randomly selected first arm. The anatomical assignment is unresolved. Switching arms and allowing a nominal washout does not by itself remove exercise adaptation or a contralateral repeated-bout effect. In period two, the control arm would also be the previously exercised arm if the stated switch was followed. These are design concerns, not demonstrated explanations for the null. No period, sequence, dominance or treatment-by-period analysis is reported. Similar baseline measurements between conditions cannot exclude different responses to the second damaging bout. Reported comparable eccentric work was not numerically presented.

## Outcomes and actual comparisons

The main reports a within-person repeated-measures analysis: Time × Supplement × Arm Condition for most outcomes, and Time × Supplement for CK and soreness. Sphericity assessment and Greenhouse-Geisser correction are described; Bonferroni correction applies to post hoc time comparisons, not an established correction across the whole outcome family. A supplementary Friedman analysis of ordinal soreness reportedly agreed, but its full test output is absent. The main and protocols do not specify a single confirmatory primary outcome or outcome-family gate.

The figures report means ± SD, not confidence intervals. There are no numerical outcome tables, complete ANOVA tables, or treatment-difference confidence intervals in the retained article. Exact plotted means were not digitized or invented. Significant time and Time × exercised/control effects are retained as actual experimental responses; they are not supplement efficacy. No significant administered-preparation benefit was identified in the report.

The paper states that nonsignificant Time × Supplement effect sizes were below partial eta-squared .05; the torque Results give a broader upper bound of .105 for their listed nonsignificant interactions. These statements do not supply endpoint-specific confidence bounds or show equivalence. The feasibility sample and reported post hoc sensitivity analysis cannot justify the paper's categorical claim that insufficient power did not account for the absence of medium/large effects. Exact error degrees of freedom and individual-level model outputs are not provided, so implementation was not independently verified.

## Reporting and registration limits

- Native Figure 3 has flexion-ROM pairwise time brackets despite the text reporting no flexion omnibus effects; Figure 4 similarly has girth brackets despite its own null-omnibus caption. These may refer to different comparisons, but the stated time-triggered post hoc procedure and omitted full model output do not resolve the apparent mismatch. Do not convert selective brackets into efficacy.
- Some non-exercised-arm panels also show time brackets. The broad discussion that changes occurred only in the exercised arm is too strong; a Time × Arm interaction means differing trajectories, not necessarily zero change in controls.
- Manuscript history gives received **16 January 2026**, revised 10 February and accepted 17 February; publication was 23 February. The current registry first submitted date is **15 September 2025**, first public posting **28 January 2026**, with status not yet recruiting and an estimated start **15 February 2026**. Thus the registered future schedule cannot document the completed cohort as described when the manuscript was received. The authors' prospective-registration-as-protocol-extension claim is retained as a claim, not independently verified prospectivity. Actual recruitment dates and historical registry versions were not obtained.
- The registry narrative/protocol PDFs describe a randomized masked crossover, but structured registry fields say allocation not applicable, single group and no masking. It lists torque/ROM/girth as primary and soreness/CK as secondary, omitting ultrasound outcomes from that hierarchy. Its torque description says knee extension and Nm/s despite the actual elbow-flexor torque assay and figure labels in Nm; it lists arm girth in mm versus figure cm and CK from venous blood in IU/L versus the main's fingertip plasma in microkatals/L. Preserve these source discrepancies without substituting registry units or anatomy for the actual experiment.
- Original protocol/SAP versions 1 and 2 were uploaded 15 and 26 September 2025. Both contain the main paired-analysis structure, microgram dose and arm-assignment inconsistency. Availability of these documents does not establish that they preceded participant enrollment or data inspection. The primary reader checked all five native version-2 pages; the accepted independent review inspected native pages 1, 2 and 5 and read both complete protocol texts.

## Harms, support and publication status

The main reports no nausea, heartburn or other gastrointestinal discomfort among the analyzed participants. It supplies no comprehensive adverse-event table, systematic severity counts or longer follow-up. This is limited reported short-term tolerability, not established safety. The two participation exclusions are attributed to work duties, with timing relative to randomization unresolved.

The Smarter Food Company provided the powders and partial unrestricted funding. Authors report no related personal financial relationships, consultancies or equity interests and state the company had no role in design, collection, analysis, interpretation, writing or publication decisions. Those are source disclosures, not an independent conflict audit. Raw data are available only on reasonable request; no contact was made.

Crossref and Europe PMC metadata expose no linked formal correction/retraction in this bounded check. An indexed original publisher version-notes page reports a new PDF/XML version on **5 March 2026**, after the original 23 February version. Its change content and version-to-version differences were not inspected; an updated file label is not itself a formal correction notice. Direct publisher pages/PDF access failed, while the public Europe PMC full XML and native figures were available. The public supplementary-file endpoint returned only the eight main JPG figures and their GIF counterparts; no separate scientific supplement was linked in the retained XML.

The original source qualification deepened the reading of this one study. This portable comparison adds no new source-reading, independent-cohort or original-corpus completion credit and establishes no nomination change. It supports retaining the tested preparation's null result and its unresolved exposure and reporting limits alongside other distinct sulforaphane evidence.
