# Mitotic protein rescue and spindle-force compensation

**22 September 2026.** Two primary follow-ups strengthen specific experimental
comparators. Neither establishes a medicine that restores BUBR1-deficient muscle.
The positive findings remain in the comparison; adverse controls and endpoint
limits determine what can be transferred.

## proTAME and Apcin: an actual biochemical rescue with a combination reversal

[Douglas et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32284347/) deleted PRKDC,
which encodes DNA-PKcs, in two cancer cell lines. This is not a BUB1B knockout
or an experiment on the MVA alleles. ATM abundance also fell after DNA-PKcs
loss. An additional ATM-deleted A549 comparison separates some contributions,
without proving that every phenotype is exclusively DNA-PKcs mediated.

For Figures 7–8, cells received **12 µM proTAME**, **48 µM Apcin**, both, or
DMSO for one hour, followed by **40 ng/mL nocodazole for a further 16 hours**.
These are source-reported cell-culture conditions, not proposed human dosing.
The figures include CRISPR controls and deficient cells with and without the
challenge.

The rescue is not merely uneven between cell lines; in one panel it reverses. In
HeLa (Figure 7) proTAME significantly raised anillin, cyclin B1, securin,
Aurora-A pT288, PLK1 pT210 and TPX2 pS121. In A549 (Figure 8) only anillin,
cyclin B1, securin and TPX2 pS121 rose significantly. PLK1 pT210 was unchanged on
both normalizations, and Aurora-A pT288 relative to total Aurora-A was
significantly **lower** under proTAME than in untreated deficient cells — from a
baseline that was not significantly different from DNA-PKcs-proficient controls,
so there was no deficit there to rescue. The paper's own summary sentence cites
panels 8F, 8G, 8I and 8J as evidence that proTAME rescued Aurora-A and PLK1
phosphorylation in both lines; those panels do not carry that result, and the
source summary is not adopted here. Anillin in A549 rises above the proficient
control, which is what unconditional APC/C inhibition predicts and is not a
return to a normal value.

The figure legends specify three separate experiments, standard deviations,
loading-control normalization for abundance and total-protein normalization for
selected phosphorylation panels. The methods describe t tests or one-way ANOVA.
The central conclusions therefore have quantified support, unlike an unnormalized
representative blot alone. Their biological scope remains limited: bulk lysates
from differently arrested cell populations do not by themselves distinguish
per-cell rescue from a change in cell-cycle composition. No drug-treated
chromosome-count, completed-division, muscle-force or long-term renewal endpoint
is supplied by these experiments. The live-imaging defects described earlier in
the paper are baseline DNA-PKcs-loss observations, partly marked data not shown;
they are not a proTAME rescue assay. The clonogenic experiment tests radiation
sensitivity, not proTAME-treated productive survival.

The negative Apcin result matters, with one boundary the source's own Discussion
obscures. Apcin is inert on every HeLa panel, but in A549 it significantly
lowered total Aurora-A, Aurora-A pT288, PLK1 pT210 and TPX2 pS121 below the
untreated deficient condition. The paper's blanket "no effect" description is
therefore a HeLa statement; in A549 Apcin is suppressive rather than inert.

That distinction changes how the combination should be read. Combining the two
agents returns the panels to the deficient level or below, but no panel reports a
combination-versus-proTAME-alone comparison: every significance bracket anchors to
the deficient-plus-nocodazole bar, and the combination outcome appears only in the
Discussion. Antagonism is therefore isolated only in HeLa, and in A549 Apcin's own
suppression competes with antagonism as the explanation. The two agents act at
different APC/C interactions; this remains evidence against treating them as
interchangeable in this setting. An alternative degradation pathway is an
author-proposed explanation, not a measured mechanism in the experiment.

**The bridge and its limit.** The only thing that makes a DNA-PKcs experiment
relevant to BUB1B is the shared endpoint "APC/C active despite unattached
kinetochores". That bridge has a hard limit, and it is the most decision-relevant
fact in this source. proTAME was administered only to DNA-PKcs-deficient cells
and only under nocodazole: there is no proficient-genotype drug arm, and no arm in
which a spindle could form. Under 40 ng/mL nocodazole for 16 hours no correct
attachment is achievable, so the assay cannot in principle distinguish a restored
checkpoint from a chemically enforced arrest — and preserved securin and cyclin B1
are precisely the signature of a cell held before anaphase. BUBR1's restraint is
conditional on attachment state; proTAME blocks Cdc20/Cdh1 loading unconditionally.
A permanent brake is not a restored sensor.

**Why it stays below the nominees:** proTAME offers a mechanistically relevant
way to preserve APC/C substrates despite an upstream defect, but it has not
restored the causal protein or demonstrated faithful division. It is also not
eligible as a repurposing candidate at all: it is a research-tool prodrug,
supplied in this source as a Boston Biochem DMSO reagent stock, with no match in
the census's own 14,882-entity clinical and approval catalog and no human exposure
anywhere in the reviewed record. The same holds for Apcin. That is a resolved
finding, not an open question. Niacin's separate protein-supply chain and
apitegromab's other-disease functional evidence also leave the MVA efficacy
question open; those candidates are not exempt from it. A washout experiment
producing more absolute faithful daughters would substantially change the value of
this probe — and because of the bridge limit above, it is a falsification test
rather than merely a missing datum. This is a requirement for promotion, not a
claimed result.

## Monastrol: reciprocal structural compensation, with harm in controls

[Kaczmarczyk and Sullivan, 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0106464)
studied CENP-W-depleted HeLa cells. CENP-W loss reduced kinetochore Hec1 and
Mad1/Mad2 signals, while BubR1 accumulated at kinetochores. It is therefore a
different molecular defect from low usable BUBR1 in the proposed MVA model.

After 44 hours of siRNA treatment, the investigators added **100 µM monastrol
for four hours**, then scored fixed spindle and centrosome structure. Figures
4–5 describe at least 100 scored cells in duplicate assays, with SEM and
one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett comparisons. Those cell counts must not be
presented as 100 independent biological experiments. Monastrol reduced excess
centrosomal foci (Figure 4B) and abnormal centriole splitting (Figure 5A) in
CENP-W-depleted cells. Those two are genuine drug-on-lesion effects.

The bipolarity observation runs the other way, and the direction matters. The
Results text reports apparently bipolar spindles in more than half the observed
mitoses under the combined perturbation — but the comparator is the
monastrol-treated control, and the authors' own framing is that **CENP-W depletion
rescues monastrol's monopolar arrest**, not that monastrol rescues the lesion. The
abstract states that CENP-W depletion rescued the monopolar-spindle phenotype of
monastrol treatment, and the Figure 5B legend heading says the same. Against that,
more than 90% of monastrol-treated control cells showed the described adjacent
centriole pairs at the centre of a monopolar spindle. No bipolar rate is reported
anywhere for CENP-W depletion without monastrol, so the drug is not shown to add
bipolarity to the lesion, and this observation must not be listed beside the
fragmentation and splitting reductions as a third drug benefit. This is an
author-reported structural observation; no new percentage is reconstructed from
the plots.

A structurally beneficial interaction with one lesion does not establish a
beneficial response in intact cells or another kinetochore defect. Fixed-cell
snapshots cannot identify which individual cells changed category or establish
faithful descendants. The paper's live-cell fragmentation analysis establishes
the untreated depletion phenotype, not a longitudinal drug-rescue lineage.

Nocodazole also reduced fragmentation by depolymerizing microtubules; eliminating
the structure that transmits force is not evidence of functional spindle repair.
TPX2 overexpression supplies a separate genetic structural rescue, not another
administered medicine. Neither result is assigned to monastrol. This follow-up
extends the already appraised STLC/monastrol route rather than adding a duplicate
compound or transferring a result between inhibitors.

## Source-reading and reuse limits

For Douglas, the complete main text and all nine main-figure legends were read
from the public NCBI BioC record; Figures 7–8 were also visually checked from
the Europe PMC associated-image package. Their lane layouts support the separate
monotherapy and combination comparisons. Raw image intensity, original blots,
and biological raw data were not reanalysed. The NCBI text is CC BY 4.0.

For Kaczmarczyk, the complete main article text, methods and six main-figure
legends were read on the publisher page. Figure pixels, the supplemental file
and movies were not independently inspected. An image-link retrieval failed;
no visual audit is claimed. The publisher identifies the article as CC BY.
Original assessments and source identities are included here; downloaded article
text and images remain outside the repository. The original 256-entry abstract
ledger is unchanged by this deeper follow-up.
