# Betaine: controlled functional signals, mixed human results and unresolved preparation

Betaine warrants a **source-labelled functional reserve/comparator appraisal**, with no automatic nomination change. A small aged-mouse study reports treated-versus-old-control grip and treadmill positives; a small human crossover reports selected placebo-controlled force and power benefits. A separate CrossFit trial does not establish an added strength benefit over training plus placebo. These findings deserve comparison with niacin's open-label strength context, without requiring MVA proof that the provisional nominees also lack. They do not establish BUBR1 restoration, disease-specific human rescue or comparative MVA efficacy.

The source task began at repository commit `1aca82163b6d42430a56a90813affd416b12b053`, with 235 decisions and no betaine/trimethylglycine decision. Exactly four discovery queries were used. The returned and inspected sources did not establish an older or disease-population human functional trial; this is a bounded retrieval result, not a claim that none exists. The best age-related primary selected here is in mice. The historical poliomyelitis mention in a paper's Introduction was not followed or promoted to primary evidence.

## Administered betaine in aged mice

[Chen et al., 2024, PMID39187977](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.13558), PMC11446699, compared male C57BL/6J mice beginning at 15 months with or without **2% w/v betaine in drinking water for 12 weeks**, alongside young mice beginning at two months. Figure1 specifies six mice/group. All had standard chow; controls received distilled water. The main and supplements do not identify the betaine supplier, catalog, purity or salt/hydration form, and do not establish actual water intake, mg/kg received dose or tissue exposure. The cell experiments used **10 mM betaine**; exposure duration was not established in the inspected experimental description. Neither preparation should be relabelled as verified anhydrous betaine or betaine hydrochloride.

Actual functional positives are retained:

| Endpoint | Treated old versus old control | Denominator and interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Grip | 74.8 ± 3.3 versus 63.7 ± 8.4 N/kg body weight; P=.010 | Mean±SD, n=6/group. This is body-weight-normalized four-limb grip, not absolute force or muscle-specific force. |
| Treadmill distance | 238.2 ± 6.7 versus 200.6 ± 20.5; P=.013 | Mean±SD, n=4/group. Main text says **metres**, but native Supplementary Figure1D labels **cm**. Preserve the unresolved unit conflict. |
| Body composition | Higher lean-mass fraction, lower fat-mass fraction; native significant old-versus-treated brackets | n=5–6/group. The displayed endpoints are percentages, not a demonstrated absolute muscle-mass increase. |

The treadmill protocol lasts 15 minutes, increases speed from 10 to 25 m/min over two minutes and uses 0.5 mA electrical stimulation. S1 also reports fewer shocks; its shock-speed panel has no significant contrast marked. These are performance measures, not isolated muscle contractility. There is no pretreatment functional measurement establishing reversal of an existing deficit within the treated animals; the endpoint comparison supports benefit during aging. Not all outcomes use the cohort's full six mice, and selection/attrition reasons are not supplied. Random allocation, masking, a prespecified primary endpoint, power calculation and systematic harms or mortality ascertainment were not established. The statistical supplement specifies one-way ANOVA and Dunnett T3 post hoc comparisons, with many outcomes and no reported study-wide multiplicity correction.

Actual drug-associated molecular observations include higher selected mitochondrial ETC proteins, lower Mss51 mRNA and higher Yy1 mRNA/protein. They are not BUBR1 protein supply, direct protein folding or a half-life measurement. Mss51-overexpressing C2C12 experiments include actual betaine treatment: selected ETC-protein and superoxide responses improve, while the reduced mitochondrial number and PGC1α deficit are not rescued. Figure3C's direct gray Mss51-overexpression versus pink Mss51-overexpression plus betaine comparisons mark increased maximal respiration and spare respiratory capacity (**P<.01; caption n=5–6); the direct basal-respiration, proton-leak and ATP-production contrasts are unmarked. In the separate Figure5B experiment, direct Mss51-overexpression ± betaine comparisons show increases in basal/maximal respiration, ATP production and spare capacity, and also increased proton leak (***P<.001; n=4), with attenuation after Yy1 knockdown. Increased proton leak is a measured direction, not automatically improved ATP efficiency or a beneficial endpoint. Preserve these experiment-specific observations rather than declaring every respiratory endpoint rescued.

Promoter assays support Yy1 binding and repression of Mss51. The additional 16-to-20-month mouse experiment gives betaine to all groups except WT, then alters Yy1 in muscle. It supports pathway involvement but is not a complete betaine-by-Yy1 factorial test: a Yy1-knockdown-without-betaine arm is absent, and Yy1 itself controls muscle metabolism. No exclusive mediation is proved. Native Figure6 measures **cage locomotor distance**, even though Results calls it running distance; it is distinct from the initial treadmill test. Intended n=10/group becomes n=7–9 for body composition/weight and n=4 for locomotion/energy expenditure without a complete flow explanation. The main says comparison groups received saline injections, whereas the native scheme labels AAV vector. Yy1 overexpression does not consistently add functional improvement. GSEA FDRs around .10–.14 use an exploratory threshold (supplement says FDR<.25 or |NES|<1); these are not conventional FDR<.05 pathway confirmations.

## Controlled human positive and null

[Lee et al., 2010, PMID20642826](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-7-27), PMC2915951, studied 12 healthy recreationally active men aged 21±3 years, with at least three months of resistance training. The balanced randomized masked crossover used two 14-day periods separated by a 14-day washout. Each received **1.25 g named Danisco betaine twice daily in 300 mL sports drink**, with the same drink alone as placebo; testing days included a morning assigned dose. Two standardized conditioning sessions occurred per supplementation period. The exact chemical form/product purity is not specified. Plasma betaine rose relative to baseline/placebo, but the Results do not give a post-supplement concentration suitable for an exact exposure-response estimate.

Native Figures3/4 mark direct post-betaine versus corresponding post-placebo benefits, not merely a change in significance within one phase. Reported day-one/day-two isometric bench force was **2922±297 / 2503±28 N** with betaine versus **2175±92 / 2128±56 N** with placebo; bench-throw power was **1779±90 / 1788±34 W** versus **1374±128 / 1523±39 W**. These are reported means±SD, n=12, P<.05; exact P values, contrast CIs and numerical interaction estimates were not supplied. The Methods specifies repeated-measures ANOVA and Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc tests, but no period/sequence/carryover model or endpoint hierarchy is described. Small, short, selected-performance evidence is not a general endurance or clinical muscle benefit.

Vertical-jump and isometric-squat improvements are reported against the betaine phase's own baseline; native Figure1 lacks a placebo-comparison mark. Its caption incorrectly calls the panel squat-jump power, while the native axis and adjacent Results identify isometric squat force. Do not promote these to established placebo contrasts. Dynamic squat-jump power and squat/bench repetitions to fatigue were nonsignificant. Hydration, glucose and lactate contrasts were also nonsignificant, so proposed osmoprotection, methyl donation or creatine-mediated mechanisms were not established. All 12 are reported, but a recruitment/attrition flow, quantified adherence, systematic adverse-event reporting and long-term safety are not provided. Danisco funded the study and employed one coauthor; this is disclosed, not a reason to erase the positive results.

[2020 CrossFit trial, PMID32516959](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12061688), PMC7352895, is pertinent counterevidence. Methods reports 36 people randomized, with seven excluded (five missed the post visit, two did not finish performance testing), leaving 14 betaine and 15 placebo participants; the abstract instead describes 29 randomized. The final sample includes 14 women and 15 men. Participants received **2.5 g/day named betaine**, split twice daily in microcrystalline-cellulose powder, or a matched cellulose/flavour placebo for six weeks while continuing supervised training. Supplier, chemical form/purity and plasma exposure were not supplied. Training was not a single standardized program across the three gyms; packet returns monitored adherence without a reported numerical adherence rate.

Original Table3 reports back-squat 3-RM **95.08±35.91→98.08±36.79 kg** for betaine and **88.86±27.60→91.00±29.63 kg** for placebo. The **time×treatment P=.230** does not establish an added betaine effect. The within-betaine P=.04 and within-placebo P=.13 do not demonstrate that the changes differ. Rowing and Bergeron-test interactions are also nonsignificant (.488 and .255); body-composition/hydration interactions are nonsignificant. The abstract/Discussion's favourable strength phrasing exceeds that controlled comparison. Table3's Bergeron means also disagree with the positive percentage changes stated in prose, so no numerical Bergeron improvement is reconstructed. This selected-completer null does not prove equivalence or erase the different crossover positives. Systematic adverse-event data were not reported.

## Preparation, competitive role and reading scope

The live exact-name check finds betaine and even “betaine anhydrous” in **both CHEMBL95889 and DC347**, whose merged aliases also contain hydrochloride. Trimethylglycine has no exact alias in the checked main table. The integrated entry therefore uses **empty `match_aliases`** and preserves the source-labelled preparation. Neither catalog approval metadata nor background chemistry establishes that the trial material was a particular anhydrous medicinal product, hydrochloride, extract, chloral betaine or oxibetaine. No merge repair or product-eligibility claim is made.

The age-related controlled functional observations and direct human bench-force/power comparisons make betaine a useful muscle-function reserve, not a generic chaperone assertion. The small studies, healthy human population, mixed functional outcomes, preparation uncertainty and lack of pediatric/disease transfer prevent a clinical nomination or cross-trial efficacy ordering. Niacin's human muscle NAD engagement/open-label strength and apitegromab's disease-associated pediatric motor evidence remain different dimensions; neither is proved superior for this BUB1B genotype. No assayed BUB1B/BUBR1 response, chromosome-fidelity benefit, protein-stability rescue or MVA result was established in these selected primaries.
