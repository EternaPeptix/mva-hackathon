# D,L-3HB sodium salt: sepsis muscle-force qualification

**Retain a preparation-specific preclinical force signal with substantial safety and design qualifications.** The [2019 experiment](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2506-6) and [2021 dose-finding study](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40360-021-00517-7) report better ex vivo EDL force in selected five-day survivors. Muscle mass was not preserved, electrolyte abnormalities occurred even at the 2021 reference dose, and higher tested doses increased illness and mortality. Neither paper establishes human benefit, food/retail equivalence, or BUB1B/MVA rescue. The ledger records one sodium-salt comparison, with the two study preparations and their endpoint denominators kept separate.

## What was actually administered

Both studies used **D,L-3-hydroxybutyrate sodium salt by twice-daily subcutaneous injection**, alongside mixed parenteral nutrition, fluids, antibiotics and analgesia in 24-week-old male mice with induced CLP sepsis. These are neither oral interventions nor ketone esters.

- **2019 Study 4:** Sigma H6501, explicitly 50% D/50% L; source dose **5 mg/g/day**, divided into twice-daily 400-µL injections from day 1. Comparator: D-glucose 6.25 mg/g/day, explicitly isocaloric/isovolumetric. Injection solvent/purity/batch were not specified. Healthy controls received pair-fed chow totaling 6.55 kcal/day.
- **2021:** Sigma D,L salt, but catalogue, analytical ratio, solvent, concentration and injection volume were not specified. The **150/180/225/300 mg values are daily totals**. Glucose 187.5 mg/day was called isocaloric/isovolumetric; matching calories across all escalating salt doses was not demonstrated. Healthy controls were pair-fed 5.8 kcal/day, matching PN alone.

Neither study included a sodium-matched comparator. Do not silently substitute the ester study's Plasmalyte vehicle or its continuous infusion, or import the 2019 catalogue details into 2021. The source regimens here describe experiments, not clinical recommendations.

## Functional results and denominators

| Experiment | Direct functional finding | Survival and assay denominator |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 salt versus glucose | Absolute maximal force: **P=.002** in Results. Specific force: native Figure 5g **P≤.01** marker; exact pairwise P unstated. | Salt 17/21 survived, glucose 17/20; specific-force n=14/16, healthy 15. Absolute-force n not separately stated. |
| 2021, 150 mg/day versus glucose | Specific force higher, **P≤.05**. | Salt 16/23 survived; force n=13. Glucose 15/18 survived; force n=12. |
| 2021, 180 mg/day versus glucose | Specific force higher, **P≤.05**, alongside increased illness. | 12/24 survived; force n=12. |
| 2021, 225/300 mg/day | No significant force benefit in the pooled high-dose survivors. | 7/16 and 1/8 survived; pooled force n=8. Separate dose effects cannot be recovered. |

Specific force is **force divided by estimated cross-sectional area**, mN/mm², despite captions saying per-unit muscle mass. The 2019 absolute-force P=.002 is an actual reported comparison even though Figure 5f contains only representative traces. The 2021 paper presents no group absolute-force result. No numerical treatment difference or CI is tabulated; plots were not digitized (2019 mean±SEM; 2021 median/IQR).

The 2019 caption's ANOVA P<.0001 is omnibus; the abstract's P=.1 compares salt-treated mice with healthy controls. The 2021 abstract's P≥.3 also concerns healthy comparisons. **None proves equivalence or complete restoration.** The 2019 n=49 denotes the surviving cohort, with 56 included across its three groups and only 45 specific-force measurements. The 2021 included total is 103, with nine separately reported exclusions and varying assay counts; it is not a verified all-randomized denominator. Missing-assay reasons and a complete initial-allocation flow are unavailable. Every terminal functional result is conditioned on survival.

## Nulls, harms and reliability

TA/EDL mass remained reduced in 2019 and EDL mass in 2021. Preserve the 2019 regeneration-gene and Fbxo32 positives, but not as proof of regeneration causing functional recovery. Satellite-cell density and measured autophagy/inflammation/mTOR markers showed no drug-group benefit. Figure 5a/b ketogenesis profiles belong to the earlier obesity/lipid/genetic cohorts, not the salt intervention.

In 2021, **150 mg/day already raised sodium, pH and bicarbonate and lowered potassium/chloride versus glucose**. At 180, illness worsened although mortality versus 150 was nonsignificant. Deaths were 7/23 at 150, 12/24 at 180, 9/16 at 225 and 7/8 at 300, versus 3/18 glucose. Mortality at 225/300 was significantly higher than 150. Higher-dose kidney-injury markers and pooled-high brain/lung dehydration and neuronal-damage findings qualify the force signal. Sodium overload is plausible, not isolated causally by a matched control. Below-150 doses were not tested; no minimum effective dose or universal toxic threshold is established.

Both studies assign nonsurvivors the maximum illness score plus one, so illness scores partly incorporate death and are not wholly separate morbidity evidence. In 2021 Figure 6c, the Ppara comparison at 180 versus glucose has P≤.01, distinct from 180 versus 150 at P=.02; neither is a force endpoint.

Source discrepancies remain explicit in the structured packet: 2021 doubled-dose illness P=.004 in the abstract versus .0008 in native Figure 1; microglia prose exceeds native comparison markers; creatinine significance is versus healthy controls rather than demonstrated versus glucose. General safety cannot be inferred from selected nulls or five-day survival.

2019 states force was primary but does not explicitly document drug-arm randomization or force-assessor blinding. 2021 reports random allocation/blinding with limited implementation detail and adaptive dose finding. Both have numerous nominal tests and no reported broader multiplicity adjustment. Neither prospective hierarchy/SAP was verified.

## Lineage and transparency

The 2021 cholesterol paper explicitly reuses 2019 absolute-force measurements; it provides **no additional independent force replication**. The 2021 toxicity study is separately described, but a complete animal-level non-overlap crosswalk is absent. The other 2019 obesity, knockout and lipid interventions are not additional sodium-salt trials.

2019 disclosed a related pending patent (PCT/EP2017/081394); 2021 declared no competing interests. Both list public/ERC funding. Fresh bounded metadata checks located no linked correction/retraction. Full scientific mains were read; native drug-relevant 2019 figures/supplements and all 2021 native figures were inspected at the separately recorded source and independent depths in the [structured qualification](sodium-dl-3hb-sepsis-qualification.json). Raw data were not obtained.

The decision-critical unknowns are drug allocation/blinding detail, vehicle/purity, missing-assay flow, precise force effects/CIs, sodium-independent efficacy, lower-dose safety, and translation beyond injected research salt in male septic mice. Absence of BUB1B/BUBR1 measurement limits mechanism claims; it does not erase the observed functional evidence.

## Competitiveness

The reported absolute-force advantage is useful disease-model evidence, alongside the specific-force findings. It is not reduced to an image-only or biomarker-only observation. Its missing effect size/CI and assay denominator, survivor conditioning and uncertain 2019 drug allocation still limit what can be inferred. These limits do not prove that the force benefit is an artifact.

The injected sodium salt remains distinct from the [ketone ester preparations](ketone-sepsis-functional-qualification.md) and [oral R,R performance studies](ketone-monoester-functional-qualification.md). The absence of sodium-matched controls leaves the contribution of sodium versus the ketone anion unresolved. No tested regimen establishes a general safe exposure or net whole-cohort benefit, and no oral or retail formulation inherits the result.

Retain this as an exploratory compensation comparison under the same standards as the provisional nominees: justified measured exposure, controlled absolute useful output, renewable reserve and chromosome fidelity, with starting-unit and survival denominators. BUBR1 mediation is additionally required only if claiming restoration. Niacin and apitegromab are not proven superior by these comparisons, and the salt is not established to displace them. The [completed cholesterol/tracer qualification](sodium-dl-3hb-cholesterol-qualification.md) adds a mixed-ICU association and separate mevalonate-labeling evidence, while leaving cholesterol-mediated force rescue unresolved. Its reused force measurements receive no additional replication credit.
