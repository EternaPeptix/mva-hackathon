# Quercetin glycosides: a formulation-specific strength reserve

**Recommendation:** retain a distinct, conditional research reserve for the tested quercetin-glycoside preparation as an adjunct to resistance training. A small controlled human primary strength positive deserves explicit credit. It does not establish quercetin-aglycone efficacy, supplement-only efficacy without training, BUBR1 restoration, chromosome fidelity, pediatric benefit, or MVA treatment. The evidence does not presently displace apitegromab or the more disease-proximate creatine comparator; it also does not prove niacin superior for strength. No clinical recommendation is made.

The source qualification was bound to the clean repository at `3db5c8961499131d8feafbe87f47eb8e6bfbb0e0`, with 230 decisions. The integration adds one preparation-specific assessment. The existing `new-dasatinib-quercetin` decision is explicitly combination-only. At the 230-assessment source-review baseline, exact single-agent quercetin/glycoside aliases were absent from the decision ledger. CHEMBL50 describes quercetin aglycone; the tested preparation is not established interchangeable with it. ## Decisive primary: controlled strength benefit, with training

[Nishikawa et al., 2025, PMID 40063125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11893712/) randomized 30 healthy, physically independent adults aged 65–82 years, 15 per group. All received six weeks of bilateral isometric knee-extension training, three times weekly, three sets of ten contractions at 60% of the PRE maximal voluntary force. Both groups used the same training prescription; the load was based on PRE strength. This isolates the added supplement effect under that training regimen, not a supplement-alone effect and not a supplement–exercise interaction tested in a factorial design.

The active daily capsules contained **200 mg quercetin glycosides plus 1.8 g dextrin**, versus **2.0 g dextrin** in the placebo capsules. The glycosides were enzymatically manufactured by San-Ei Gen F.F.I. from isoquercitrin prepared from quercetin-3-O-rutinoside. The publication does not resolve the complete glycoside distribution, batch purity, measured systemic exposure, or a verified aglycone-equivalent dose. This is neither the D+Q phytosome preparation nor evidence for arbitrary quercetin products. Paper statements about absorption and GRAS status are not independent verification of clinical exposure or pediatric safety.

Four participants discontinued, two per group, for scheduling/personal reasons. **Thirteen per group completed and were analyzed**: placebo five men/eight women; glycosides six men/seven women. The native flow shows 30 randomized and 26 analyzed. This is a completer analysis, not demonstrated intention-to-treat. Native Figure 1 places POST measurement one week after the six-week ingestion/training period; long-term persistence is unknown.

The paper identifies knee-extension maximal voluntary force (MVF, reported as torque in Nm) as the primary outcome. Table 2 reports mean ± SD:

| Outcome | Placebo | Glycosides | Controlled result |

| MVF PRE → POST, Nm | 111.2 ± 47.4 → 117.1 ± 49.9 | 93.9 ± 25.4 → 105.9 ± 25.5 | Reported group × time interaction P=.036; group main effect P=.240 |

| Fast TUG, seconds | 5.7 ± .7 → 5.5 ± .9 | 5.5 ± .7 → 5.1 ± .4 | Interaction P=.420 |
| Five chair stands, seconds | 6.1 ± 1.6 → 5.7 ± 1.6 | 5.7 ± 1.2 → 5.5 ± 1.0 | Interaction P=.588 |
| Chair stands in 30 seconds | 27.4 ± 7.8 → 28.8 ± 8.9 | 28.5 ± 6.0 → 28.5 ± 6.1 | Interaction P=.488 |
| Muscle thickness, cm | 4.3 ± .6 → 4.4 ± .7 | 4.1 ± .6 → 4.2 ± .6 | Interaction P=.364 |

The strength positive is a between-treatment result, not merely significance within one arm. The 115.1% value is the mean individual POST/PRE ratio: it means approximately 15.1% improvement, not 115.1% improvement, and is not the ratio of the displayed group means. The difference between the two ratio means is 9.8 percentage points; no adjusted treatment-effect confidence interval is supplied. Baseline group-mean torque was lower in the active arm. The reported ANCOVA and ratio tests should be preserved as reported, without reconstructing participant data or claiming a precise adjusted effect size. Walking, chair-rise, mass and thickness nulls remain specific to the tested endpoints and sample; they do not erase the strength positive.

Motor-unit (MU) firing observations are supportive physiology, not direct molecular target engagement. At POST, firing rates differed between groups for all detected units, 20–40% recruitment-threshold units, and 40–60% units, with reported interactions P=.044, .015, and .028. The corresponding detected-unit counts in placebo/glycoside arms were 65/99 at PRE and 112/135 at POST for all units. These are motor units from 13 participants per group, not independent randomized people. A subject-level hierarchical treatment of the pooled MU comparisons is not described. Tracking retained 39 units per arm; significant PRE–POST changes in the active arm alone are not themselves between-arm contrasts. A glycoside-arm correlation between strength ratio and high-threshold firing-rate ratio (r=.642, P=.018; other categories nonsignificant) does not establish mediation. No BUB1B/BUBR1 assay, chromosome-count/fidelity assay, or MVA model was reported.

No systematic adverse-event table or explicit zero-event result is provided in this 2025 main report. Discontinuation reasons are reported, but missing harm reporting cannot become demonstrated safety. Plasma quercetin was unmeasured, exact exposure sufficient for proposed adenosine antagonism was unknown, and the participants were healthy adults rather than children with weakness.

## Registration and source inconsistencies

The current [UMIN000053019 record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000060482) names strength as primary and motor-unit behavior as secondary, with a target/enrolled sample of 30. It lists registration on 6 December 2023 and disclosure on 8 December 2023, after the listed anticipated start (12 June 2023) and last follow-up (2 September 2023). Thus the available record does not substantiate prospective registration of the outcome hierarchy. The registry calls the randomization unit “Cluster,” whereas the paper describes individual dynamic allocation balancing age, sex and strength; this discrepancy remains unresolved. Current registry results and harms fields are blank. No full protocol or historical registry reconstruction was reviewed.

The paper has editorial/analytical inconsistencies that warrant caution without negating the measured controlled positive: the abstract repeats 20–40% for both moderate and high MU groups, whereas Methods/native figures identify high as 40–60%; Table 2 prints a post-hoc threshold of P<.50, whereas Results report P<.001 for both MVF within-arm comparisons; one Results sentence says no time effects across fitness tests although Table 2 and Discussion show TUG time effects. Methods and Results also give differing arm age summaries. The table identifies two-way ANCOVA, while Methods describe PRE covariate adjustment explicitly for strength, thickness and mass and do not fully specify the fitness-test model. Report the endpoint-specific table results rather than silently repairing or recomputing these analyses.

## Essential counterevidence: a different glycoside/training trial

[Otsuka et al., 2022, DOI 10.3389/fnut.2022.912217](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9298516/) studied 200 or 500 mg/day glycosides from the same described manufacturing route, with 24 weeks of machine resistance training at 40% estimated 1-RM. Participants were community-dwelling adults aged 50–74. Of 185 screened, 54 were randomized; 51 began/completed and entered the safety set (17 per arm), and **48 entered per-protocol efficacy analysis (16 per arm)**. The abstract's “48 completed” wording does not replace that main-text flow distinction.

The primary thigh-CSA interaction was null (P=.915), as were other mass measures. Essential Supplementary Table 1 reports no group × time interaction for estimated 1-RM leg extension (P=.413), leg curl (.923), leg press (.448), or chest press (.837), despite within-group training gains. The supplement records six missing measurements, including four at week 24 in placebo; its nominal n=16 per group is not a complete observation count at every time point. The 1-RM test was indirect, not identical to the 2025 measured isometric torque endpoint.

Preserve the distinct secondary positive: fully flexed-knee vastus-lateralis shear-wave velocity, a passive-stiffness measure, changed by −.6 m/s in both glycoside groups versus −.3 m/s in placebo (interaction P=.023; both treatment-versus-placebo change comparisons P<.05). It is not demonstrated greater strength, walking ability, measured histological fibrosis reversal, or clinical benefit. An [erratum](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.1028810/full) removes an erroneous placebo-versus-itself significance asterisk in Table 2; the inspected XML already displays the corrected relevant row. Lower-SMI within-arm mass changes discussed by the authors do not establish a controlled subgroup benefit.

In the 51-person safety set, reported adverse-event incidence was 70.6% placebo, 52.9% low glycoside and 70.6% high glycoside; no severe events or attributed supplement side effects were reported, and incidence did not differ significantly. These are percentages of a small adult safety set, not proof of pediatric or long-term safety. The conflict statement reports funding and design/analysis/writing involvement by Suntory Wellness and four employee authors, despite the separate funding statement saying no specific grant.

## Fidelity search and fair competitive judgment

Four targeted queries were sufficient to identify the decisive trial and relevant counterevidence; the six-query cap was not exhausted. Returned BUBR1 docking papers were not administered-compound rescue. A chemical–gene database linked opposite BUB1B **mRNA** directions to PMID 21632981 and 15309432. The exact complete primary abstracts describe HepG2 genotoxicity/transcriptomics and Caco-2 cancer-cell proliferation/transcriptomics, respectively. Neither abstract establishes BUBR1 protein rescue or faithful chromosome segregation. The database's gene-specific mRNA signs were not independently verified from those full papers and remain unresolved source pointers. A separate [GEO GSE15162 reanalysis](quercetin-bubr1b-qualification.md) found small, mixed-direction BUB1B probe shifts after 3 μM quercetin in HepG2 cells; the eight-array matrix has one array per condition/timepoint, so it supplies no replicate-level significance test. This distinct dataset does not resolve the directions reported by the two database pointers. The HepG2 genotoxicity-like and Caco-2 reduced-proliferation observations are context-specific, not global clinical harm conclusions. No replicated BUB1B restoration or MVA benefit was verified in this bounded pass; absence elsewhere is not asserted.

The controlled primary strength result makes this a scientifically useful functional reserve rather than a marker-only candidate. Niacin's proposed restoration chain and open-label muscle evidence do not receive an automatic evidentiary advantage over randomized strength evidence; neither is MVA-proven. Apitegromab retains a larger disease-associated, pediatric motor-function foundation in the current appraisal. Creatine has more disease-proximate pediatric neuromuscular trials, with both strength positives and important nulls. The small healthy-older-adult completer trial, unconfirmed registry design details, preparation-specific exposure, functional-test nulls, and different longer-trial strength nulls support reserve status rather than replacing those candidates now. No head-to-head comparison or cross-trial quantitative ranking is claimed.
