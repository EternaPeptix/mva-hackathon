# Capsaicin: local incisional-pain benefit with sensory denervation

**`new-capsaicin`: retain a symptom-specific, noncancer functional reserve; do not promote as BUBR1 restoration, general regeneration, or MVA muscle rescue.** Local capsaicin pretreatment reduced postoperative pain-like behavior in rats. That is a measured benefit, not merely a transcript association. The same intervention markedly reduced local sensory innervation and prolonged heat withdrawal beyond baseline. Neither the analgesic result nor the transcriptional response demonstrates correction of a BUB1B defect.

## 2020 rat study: exposure, benefit and persistence

[PMID32909881 / PMC7493244](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7493244/), DOI[10.1177/1744806920956480](https://doi.org/10.1177/1744806920956480), *Global transcriptome analysis of rat dorsal root ganglia to identify molecular pathways involved in incisional pain*.

The study used adult male Sprague–Dawley rats, 250–300 g. Under isoflurane, the intended plantar surgical site received subcutaneous infiltration of **200 µL 1% capsaicin (10 mg/mL), 30 minutes before incision**. The nominal administered amount is 2 mg, calculated from the reported concentration and volume; this is not a human-dose translation. Vehicle was 80% saline / 10% ethanol / 10% Tween-80. Capsaicin catalogue number, batch and purity are not reported. This was a single experimental local pretreatment, not oral food exposure, a plant extract, or a test of a marketed topical formulation.

The incision involved skin/fascia and longitudinal injury of the flexor digitorum brevis. Behavioral comparators were capsaicin+incision versus vehicle+incision, **n=5 versus6 rats**, followed at3h and days1,2,3,4,5,7,9,16. Three preoperative measurement days supplied heat/mechanical baselines. Sham anesthesia controls were used for the transcript comparison. A separate capsaicin-only/no-incision arm is not reported. Randomization is not described; the behavioral assessor was nominally blinded, but authors acknowledge that the conspicuous heat-response change limited blinding. The abstract's n=6–9 wording should not replace the assay-specific denominators.

**Actual positive:** spontaneous foot-lift duration decreased, and mechanical/heat hypersensitivity was attenuated. The reported repeated-measures tests give duration P=.037 and mechanical/heat P<.0001, with Bonferroni posthoc comparisons. Figure1 shows early spontaneous-behavior differences and stronger mechanical/thermal separation. However, the frequency comparison is printed **P=.059**, despite the Results sentence calling it significant; an early posthoc star in Figure1A does not make that overall comparison significant. The printed degrees of freedom are also insufficiently explained for reconstruction. Raw animal-level values were not supplied or reanalyzed.

**Duration and sensory consequence:** the paper describes incision-induced pain-like changes lasting3–9days. In Figure1D, capsaicin-treated heat withdrawal is roughly20seconds for several days, above the roughly13-second plotted baseline, rather than simply restored to baseline. By day16, the heat curves converge near baseline. This is graph-level follow-up, not an equivalence test or proof of reinnervation. The study therefore supports acute local analgesia with reduced heat sensitivity; it does not measure improved motor force, walking performance, progenitor output, or tissue regeneration.

At 24 h, separate immunohistology cohorts, **n=3 per arm**, showed loss of advillin-labelled epidermal and dermal fibers in the sampled capsaicin-treated skin. The authors describe complete denervation; Figure1E/F provides representative local images rather than a quantified whole-paw fiber-count estimate. Merkel cells and dermal vessels appeared unchanged in those images. Later nerve regrowth, functional protective sensation, healing strength, organ effects and developmental outcomes were not measured in this paper. Those limits neither erase analgesia nor establish clinical/pediatric harm.

## BUB1B is an injury-associated transcript, not a verified drug response

Bulk L4–L6 dorsal-root-ganglion RNA was collected 24 h after sham, incision, or capsaicin+incision, with **four rats per group**. This is tissue-level RNA, not purified-neuron or BUBR1-protein measurement; the three ganglion levels must not be counted as independent animals. RNA sequencing used edgeR and reported differential selection at absolute fold change≥1.5 and FDR<.05. The table does not provide individual-gene P/q values or sample counts.

Table2 lists **Bub1b −3.42 under a column headed “log2(Fold change)” for incision versus control**. No numerical capsaicin-versus-incision Bub1b estimate, BUB1B-specific qPCR, BUBR1 protein, mitotic fidelity assay, or BUB1B-deficient model is supplied. Figure3 places Bub1b in an inferred FOXM1-related network; its predicted mitosis, tissue-size and mortality labels are not measured regeneration or survival outcomes.

Three reporting issues prevent upgrading this to capsaicin-mediated molecular restoration:

- The authors claim prevention of99/126 transcript changes, but Figure2B labels its sets “Inci/Cntl” and “(Inci+Cap)/Cntl,” with99 in the overlap and25/1 in the unique portions. As drawn, overlap is not evidence of99 reversals; the set labels/counts and prose are unreconciled. A global percentage cannot assign Bub1b's response.
- The tables label their column log2 fold change, while Results describes an approximately50-fold IFIT1B increase corresponding to a table entry50.10. The magnitude convention is inconsistent; retain the printed Bub1b value and its label without exponentiating it or calling it a3.42-fold change.
- The native tables contain35 upregulated and91 downregulated rows, versus36/90 in the prose. They still total126. Figure6's25-gene caption also differs from the27-gene text. These are source inconsistencies, not a reviewer reconstruction of expression data.

Independent qPCR did confirm incision-associated **Igf2/Igfbp6** increases and their attenuation in the capsaicin group, normalized to Actb, with technical duplicates. Methods gives biological n=3–5, while Figure5 gives n=3–6; these validators are not Bub1b. They support selected molecular responses without proving that the inferred IGF/TLR9/IL10RA networks cause analgesia. The paper itself acknowledges missing protein validation and possible non-pain contributions to transcript changes. TRPV1-related nociceptor defunctionalization is mechanistically distinct from BUBR1 restoration; this rat experiment does not include a receptor-knockout or antagonist arm establishing drug selectivity.

The article mentions “Supplemental Figure3” for RNA quality but supplies no supplementary-material link in the recovered XML. The public asset ZIP contains figures/tables only. No matching supplement or raw RNA accession was identified in the bounded publisher/NCBI checks; this is **unrecovered supplementary/raw data**, not a claim that such material never existed.

## Competitive disposition and read scope

Capsaicin deserves credit for a noncancer behavioral benefit in an appropriate acute-pain model. A symptom-specific benefit need not restore BUBR1 to be useful. Its observed target, local sensory suppression before injury, nevertheless differs from the muscle-output/renewal question motivating provisional niacin and apitegromab; **neither nominee is proved effective in MVA either**. Preserve capsaicin as a symptom-specific reserve, pending a relevant symptom/lesion and a justified sensory-function window, without calling it a general MVA lead or inferring benefit from dietary intake.
