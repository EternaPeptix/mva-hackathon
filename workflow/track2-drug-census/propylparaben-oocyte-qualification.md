# Propylparaben: consumer-exposure comparator, not a BUBR1 rescue candidate

**Source-limited appraisal, 28 September 2026.** Propylparaben is a widely used preservative. A 2024 primary abstract reports impaired mouse-oocyte maturation at a nominal **600 µM** exposure, with mitochondrial/oxidative injury, DNA damage, spindle and chromosome-alignment abnormalities, and persistent BUB3/BubR1 spindle-checkpoint activation. This is an adverse exposure result, not evidence that propylparaben improves BUBR1 or treats mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA). [Pan et al., PMID 39083874](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39083874/).

| Evidence source | Reported result | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Mouse-oocyte study, abstract only | **600 µM** reduced germinal-vesicle breakdown. The abstract also attributes meiotic resumption arrest and first-polar-body extrusion failure, mitochondrial/oxidative injury, DNA damage, MPF disruption, spindle/chromosome misalignment, unstable microtubules, impaired kinetochore attachment and persistent BUB3/BubR1 checkpoint activation to propylparaben exposure. | The main paper was not retrieved. The available abstract gives no group sizes, exposure duration, full dose-response, effect estimates, assay details, or human relevance. Checkpoint activation after faulty attachments is an injury response; it does not demonstrate increased functional BUBR1 or rescue of a BUB1B variant. |
| Human blood biomonitoring | In 139 paired Chinese adult serum/whole-blood samples, propylparaben averaged **2.1 ng/mL in serum** (reported range below detection to 21 ng/mL) and **1.3 ng/mL in whole blood**. [Mao et al., PMID 38185174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38185174/). | This cross-sectional adult sample does not measure ovarian tissue, children with MVA, or lifetime exposure. |
| Human oral pharmacokinetics | Twelve adult men received single oral doses of deuterium-labelled propylparaben at **0.6 or 2.5 mg/kg**. The abstract reports rapid absorption, a terminal half-life of **2.9 hours**, and rapid metabolism/excretion. [Shin et al., PMID 31234001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31234001/). | These controlled single doses are not everyday-use measurements and do not establish a typical tissue concentration or an oocyte exposure margin. |

Using PubChem's molecular weight of **180.20 g/mol**, 600 µM corresponds to **108.12 mg/L** (108,120 ng/mL). That is about **51,500 times** the measured mean serum concentration above, or about **5,150 times** the highest value in that small blood-study sample. This is a nominal culture-medium-to-total-serum comparison across different studies and matrices, not a risk threshold, safety margin, or proof that ordinary exposures cannot affect reproductive tissue. [PubChem CID 7175](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Propylparaben).

The readout is mechanistically relevant to chromosome segregation but limited to **mouse meiosis under a high in-vitro exposure**. The article abstract does not test somatic BUB1B-deficient cells, the Leu737Ter/Asn1002Lys alleles, muscle, chromosome transmission to offspring, or an intervention that rescues the defect. It therefore merits an adverse-exposure comparator entry in the broadened consumer-compound census; it is not a candidate to rank against niacin or apitegromab, and it supplies no inference that ordinary product use causes MVA or worsens this child's condition.
