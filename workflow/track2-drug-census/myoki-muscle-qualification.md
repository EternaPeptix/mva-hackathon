# Myoki / CG-A1: controlled human function findings with unresolved reporting conflicts

Myoki is a source-defined synthetic peptide preparation with a randomized human muscle-atrophy report, not established here as an approved or commonly available repurposing drug. The paper reports a favorable DEXA mass comparison and nominal walking/grip benefits after 12 weeks. Those functional findings deserve consideration as compensation evidence, but their printed confidence intervals and P values do not align under the stated analysis, and the flowchart reverses the arm-specific completion counts used in the outcome table. Retain the reported effects and the unresolved conflicts together; neither a confirmed functional benefit nor a zero-effect conclusion follows from this packet. [Published main](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1663850).

## Exact preparation and exposure

The final paper identifies Myoki as **EAEYEHL**, a seven-residue peptide synthesized by Fmoc solid-phase methods, with reported HPLC purity 99.8% and LC–MS/MS molecular weight 889.9 Da. Supplementary Figure 2 identifies the clinical preparation as **CG-A1 peptide + arginine + maltodextrin**, manufactured/formulated by Caregen. The paper’s explicit registry identifier links it to the official CG-A1 trial; this supports a source-specific trial-name relationship, not equivalence to any marketed product or other myostatin inhibitor.

Participants mixed one sachet in 100 mL water after breakfast and another after dinner for 12 weeks, giving 100 mg peptide per sachet and 200 mg/day in the Myoki arm. The comparator contained arginine and maltodextrin without active peptide. Quantities of those shared ingredients, total sachet mass/calories, batch identifiers and sensory/energy matching are not sufficiently specified. The registry’s comparator description also repeats a 200 mg/day peptide phrase despite labeling that arm peptide-free; preserve this apparent copying error rather than assigning active peptide to placebo. The supported contrast is the tested added-peptide preparation against the described background formulation. There is no peptide-alone clinical arm or arginine interaction test.

No human pharmacokinetic measurement, intact oral-peptide bioavailability, muscle exposure or participant myostatin target-engagement result was reported. Cell binding experiments cannot establish these. The preprint calls Myoki corn-derived and describes extraction and a different synthesis process; the final report describes the synthetic preparation above. Neither description justifies ordinary corn, dietary protein, retail supplement or batch equivalence.

## Human cohort, design and planning

Eighty adults with muscle wasting/atrophy (ICD-10-CM M62.5) were randomized 1:1, 40 per arm, in an India-based double-blind parallel trial. The mean age was 38.83 ± 9.91 years, range 23–69; the cohort was not restricted to older adults. Table 1 reports all participants as Asian, with 61 men and 19 women. Mean BMI was about 20.6–20.7 kg/m². Methods and Supplementary Table 1 require low DEXA muscle mass (men <7.0 kg/m²; women <5.4 kg/m²), plus low grip strength (men <28 kg; women <18 kg) **or** walking speed <1.0 m/s over six metres. Results instead say low grip **and** reduced performance; the supplied inclusion table supports the Methods’ “or.” The registry inclusion text does not explicitly reproduce the DEXA thresholds.

Other selection criteria include BMI <25 and nonsmoking. Exclusions include uncontrolled systemic illness, musculoskeletal or neurological disorders, several drugs affecting protein metabolism, recent anabolic steroids, recent hospitalization and institutional care. These limits matter for transfer to older sarcopenia, neuromuscular disease and MVA. Habitual physical activity was allowed; no standardized resistance-training cointervention was tested.

The [official CTRI record](https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/pmaindet2.php?EncHid=OTgzMjc=) identifies computer-generated randomization, centralized concealment and participant/investigator blinding. It was registered on 29 January 2024, with first enrolment listed as 14 February 2024, and identifies protocol BSR/CT/023/23, version 1 dated 20 September 2023. This supports a prospectively registered core design and hierarchy. Its last modification remains 29 January 2024 and recruitment status remains “Not Yet Recruiting”; these stale fields cannot describe current completion. No complete original protocol, historical version archive, SAP or full clinical study report was recovered. Supplementary Figure 2 is only a study schematic, not the full report visible in its embedded screenshot.

The printed `clinicaltrials.gov, CTRI/2024/01/061919` citation mixes registry systems. The actual matching record is CTRI; a saved ClinicalTrials.gov search returned no study. CTRI lists DEXA muscle-mass change to week 12 as primary and walking time, grip and biomarkers as secondary. The published power calculation assumes a 0.3 difference and 0.5 SD for DEXA muscle mass, with 72 required and 80 planned, but does not state the units of those assumptions. The main presents both gram lean mass and kg/m² muscle-mass index; they should not become two independent primary successes. Troponin I is in registered secondary biomarkers but has no result in the retrieved main or supplement.

The final Methods specify per-protocol efficacy, baseline-adjusted ANCOVA using SAS 9.4, least-squares means and **two-sided** P values. No clinical multiplicity adjustment is described. The preprint instead describes Student t tests using Excel while reporting LS differences. That change cannot establish when the final analysis plan was specified; core endpoint registration and final-analysis prespecification are distinct.

## Denominators and directly reported results

The main says 89 screened, seven screen failures and two withdrawals before randomization, yielding 80 randomized and 75 completers. Native Figure 7 omits the two pre-randomization withdrawals and assigns **test 37 / comparator 38** analyzed, with three versus two discontinuations. Table 2, Results and Figure 8 instead assign **Myoki 38 / placebo 37**. This is a native-source conflict, not merely an error in automatically generated alt text. Figure 7 labels test-arm discontinuations as inadequate efficacy (2) and lost follow-up (1), and comparator discontinuations as inadequate efficacy (1) and lost follow-up (1), with no adverse-event discontinuations. Do not silently remap those reasons to the table’s arm counts. Per-protocol exclusion of these participants, including inadequate efficacy, can bias the treatment estimate; no ITT sensitivity analysis was recovered.

The two grip intervals include zero, and the walking interval reaches zero after rounding, despite the reported significant two-sided P values. The table, Methods and Figure 8 caption describe these as the same adjusted between-group changes in the per-protocol set; no different population, within-arm test or one-sided convention is offered to reconcile them. Figure 8 also marks change comparisons versus placebo. The Results text introduces additional negative upper-bound signs for grip that disagree with native Table 2. Preserve the table’s exact intervals and the text discrepancy without selecting a corrected value. No P value, confidence interval, effect size or participant-level analysis was recomputed.

Thus, the authors’ secondary functional positives remain reported positives with unresolved statistical credibility. They are not upgraded from within-arm stars, and they are not erased as proven nulls. A six-metre timed walk is not a six-minute walk test. The DEXA mass signal has a favorable interval but does not by itself establish renewed contractile tissue, mobility durability or long-term clinical benefit.

## Other outcomes and harms

Table 2 reports IGF-1 +17.93 ng/mL (95% CI 1.00 to 34.87; P=.038), myoglobin −14.6 ng/mL (−20.06 to −9.14; P<.01), CK-MB −3.95 ng/mL (−6.18 to −1.72; P<.01), and AST −5.39 IU/L (−10.09 to −0.70; P=.025). TNF-α, estrogen, testosterone and IL-6 comparisons were nonsignificant. These are biomarkers, not additional functional trials, direct protein-turnover measurements or myostatin mediation. All 13 Table 2 endpoint rows and their arm-specific intervals are retained in JSON.

Safety includes all 80 randomized/dosed participants, 40 per arm. The main reports TEAEs in 10/40 Myoki participants (25%; 10 events) versus 7/40 placebo participants (17.5%; seven events). Supplementary Table 2 labels its values **event counts E (%)**, so the table alone does not independently establish unique-person counts. Event terms, placebo/Myoki, are constipation 0/1, nausea 1/3, pyrexia 2/3, nasopharyngitis 2/0, headache 2/2 and cough 0/1. All were described as mild, resolved and unrelated to product; no serious TEAEs were reported. Preserve the events despite nonattribution. No comparative safety inference or general long-term safety conclusion is warranted from this small 12-week trial.

## Separate cell and mouse evidence

C2C12 work found no significant viability reduction through 500 μM over 24 hours, increased differentiation/morphology and fusion markers, and attenuation of selected dexamethasone-associated signaling changes. Methods and native figures use 5, 50 and 100 μM for differentiation, while Results mention 1, 10 and 100 μM. DEX experiments used 10 μM DEX with 10 or 100 μM Myoki. Myogenic-marker and phosphorylation changes are not direct muscle force, protein-synthesis flux or chromosome-fidelity assays. Lower-dose panels lack uniform significance; high-dose findings should not be generalized to every dose or marker.

SPR at 3.125–200 μM and ELISA support an in-vitro interaction with myostatin. No numerical affinity constant was recovered. ELISA compares micromolar peptide coating with nanomolar activin receptor 2B; the final paper explicitly says this is not a direct affinity comparison. Figure 4D’s peptide concentrations (31.3–500 μM) also differ from the narrative coating range (125–1,000 μM). Binding does not establish receptor blockade, pathway dependence or clinical target engagement. The final Discussion expressly withdraws a definitive causal link.

Male SAMP8 mice and SAMR1 controls were assigned five per group. Myoki was given by oral gavage, 20 mg in 200 μL PBS daily for 45 weeks, versus PBS controls. The paper reports increased stained muscle area and changes in fluorescence markers, with gastrocnemius collagen reduction but a nonsignificant quadriceps collagen difference. The quadriceps MAFbx panel has no treatment-comparison significance marker, whereas the gastrocnemius panel does; a later collective sentence is broader than those panels. H&E analysis quantifies total stained area, not individually traced myofiber cross-sectional areas, despite broader wording in Results. The study did **not** measure mouse grip, endurance or other direct physiological function. No animal functional replication, muscle renewal, myostatin-dependent rescue or BUBR1 mechanism follows.

## Disclosure, versions and research use

All five authors were Caregen employees. The final paper says no financial support was received, whereas CTRI lists Caregen as monetary/material support and primary sponsor; the preprint says no external funding and declares no conflicts. These statements must remain separately attributed. The registry lists one approved ethics committee and six submitted/under-review entries at its stale January 2024 modification. This does not establish the later approval status of each site. The paper’s abstract/ethics statement uses the registry number as though it were an ethics identifier, while Methods separately provide Excel Hospital’s ECR number.

The [linked June 2025 preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202506.1181) and 2026 publication describe the same study family, supported by reciprocal metadata links, authors, peptide sequence, registry number and clinical design. They are not independent replication. The final version adds analysis detail and appropriately limits mechanistic claims; its unresolved table/flow discrepancies still require clarification. The registry classifies the study as **nutraceutical**, gives phase N/A and DCGI clearance “Not Applicable,” while its summary calls it phase III. None of this proves marketing approval, retail availability or a conventional-drug repurposing opportunity.

Myoki merits retention as a distinct, source-defined compensation research challenger with a reported mass positive and disputed nominal secondary functional positives. Apply the same function, exposure, renewal and chromosome-fidelity criteria used for other candidates. Lack of BUBR1 mediation does not disqualify compensation research; it prevents a restoration claim. No MVA benefit, genotype rescue, superiority to niacin/apitegromab or nominee change is established.
