# Nitrate-containing beetroot juice: a real acute power signal with task-specific limits

23 September 2026. **Retain the tested nitrate-containing beetroot preparation as a functional research comparator.** A randomized crossover pilot in systolic heart failure found improved high-speed knee-extension power. It did not show better isometric strength, walking distance or repeated-contraction fatigue. A later pooled analysis in older women found no fatigue or recovery benefit despite large circulating nitrate/nitrite increases. These are complementary endpoint findings, not evidence that every muscle task improves or that the positive trial is invalid.

The administered preparation in both papers was **Beet It Sport concentrated beetroot juice**, compared with manufacturer nitrate-depleted juice. Neither paper tested an isolated nitrate salt. The matched comparison supports its nitrate content within this juice matrix; it does not establish equivalence with sodium/potassium nitrate, nitrite, organic nitrate medicines, NO donors or unspecified beet powders. Existing `new-no-donors` is a different intervention. ## Acute contractile performance in heart failure

[Coggan et al., PMID26179185](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCHEARTFAILURE.115.002141) studied nine adults with nonischemic systolic HF (five men/four women; age 57±10 years; ejection fraction 28±11%, mean±SD). All nine enrolled completed; the native flow diagram distinguishes exclusions and nonparticipation among 37 screened from post-randomization loss. They were on stable medication, all on beta-blockers. This was a selected HF population without major other organ dysfunction or contraindications to exercise.

Each participant received **140 mL juice containing 11.2 mmol nitrate or nitrate-depleted matching juice**, in randomized, double-blind order, with 1–2 weeks of washout. Exercise began two hours after the single dose. Low-nitrate dietary controls and avoidance of several interfering medications/products were study conditions, not administration recommendations. Participants could not reliably identify the active visit when asked. Long-term exposure was not studied.

| 50-contraction fatigue test | No significant differences in peak/average power, total work or fatigue ratio | No demonstrated fatigue-resistance benefit at 3.14 rad/s. |

Power is normalized to body mass, not muscle mass. The maxima were predicted by fitting a parabola to the measured power–velocity curve, so the fitted power, fitted velocity and high-speed observations are correlated outcomes. No paired treatment confidence intervals were supplied. The paper used treatment×order ANOVA with repeated subjects, alpha .05, and named several primary outcomes; no study-wide multiplicity correction was established. The later registry's exact paired-test P must not be relabeled as the original ANOVA P. A nonsignificant order interaction also does not prove no carryover. Native Figure 3 has velocity above power; the Results prose reverses those panel locators.

Plasma nitrate and breath NO increased, while the plasma nitrite difference was nonsignificant. These biomarkers do not isolate a muscle NO–cGMP mechanism. Voluntary dynamometry likewise does not distinguish fiber, neural or blood-flow mediation. The authors' comparison with an external healthy cohort cannot establish that treatment restored a specified fraction of an individual patient's deficit.

Blood pressure did not change significantly, and no significant serious clinical events were reported. **One participant reported abdominal cramps and loose stools.** The study was supported by institutional/Foundation and NIH-related funding, with disclosures listed as None. Its acute size and selected eligibility do not establish chronic, pediatric or general systemic safety.

## Older-women fatigue and recovery counterexample

[Zoughaib et al., PMID37226336](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.15694) is a **secondary analysis pooled from two registered trials**, not a new independent trial of all their published endpoints. Eighteen community-dwelling women aged 65–79 (mean 70±4 years) completed. Of 26 initially enrolled after 143 screened, eight failed criteria at the initial visit; the paper does not describe these as post-treatment withdrawals. HF and important metabolic, renal and neuromuscular diseases were excluded.

The acute crossover comparison used 140 mL of the same named juice, directly measured to contain **15.6±3.6 mmol nitrate versus <0.05 mmol**, with one-week washout. Fifty maximal knee extensions at 3.14 rad/s were followed by recovery measurements for ten minutes. The statistical unit remains 18 participants, not 900 contractions. Native Figures 2/3 and all table text were examined.

Circulating nitrate and nitrite rose markedly, yet serial fatigue torque showed no treatment effect (P=.851) or interaction (P=.576). Total work was 34.6±8.0 versus 35.3±8.2 J/kg, placebo versus nitrate, **P=.260**; fatigue index was 50.8±4.8% versus 51.4±5.8%, **P=.593** (mean±SD). Recovery of body-mass-normalized torque was also null (treatment P=.326, interaction P=.281), as were fitted recovery half-lives (72.4±82.0 versus 58.1±26.7 s, P=.506). These are failures to detect benefit, not equivalence bounds. Figure asterisks denote fatigue/recovery relative to baseline, not nitrate superiority.

The paper used repeated-measures ANOVA, Holm–Šidák post hoc comparisons and paired tests for summary/fitted measures. It did not measure breath NO, muscle blood flow, pH or phosphocreatine kinetics. A larger bloodmarker response therefore cannot substitute for a functional response. Its null applies to this acute task/population and does not negate the HF high-speed result or establish a dose-response relationship across different populations.

## Registry precision, cohort overlap and safety denominators

Current registry records were read; historical versions were not. They provide useful context, without establishing the original endpoint hierarchy or another independent sample:

- [NCT01682356](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01682356) lists 17 total participants but explicitly **only nine assessed for maximal power**, with the exact paper means and SE. Its eight-person VO2peak result is a different endpoint subset; participant overlap was not established here. The current adverse-event table reports zeros among 17 per condition, whereas the paper reports the GI-symptom case. Both remain visible; no reconciliation is invented.
- [NCT03513302](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03513302) lists 47 consented, with 31 screening failures and 16 completing the broader 14-day crossover. Its current power/velocity and PROMIS means have no posted inferential test in the inspected result fields, so no extra efficacy or null claim is made from those means. Other events are reported in 5/16 placebo and 4/16 nitrate participants; screening findings are not drug harms.
- [NCT03595774](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03595774) lists 13 enrolled/nine complete, including **two adverse-event withdrawals** and two screen failures. Its zero-event table uses only nine participants, so it cannot establish zero events among all enrolled. The overall power ANOVA is P=.0131 but exact pairwise results are absent; some arm descriptions conflict, so no reconstructed dose response is claimed.

The last two registries list 11 and eight female completers. The 18-woman paper gives no person-level mapping or precise parent-trial contribution. Its acute regimen is also a subset of broader registered regimens. We do not force agreement by inventing one overlap/exclusion, and do not count the pooled analysis and parent publications as independent replication.

## Competitive use and reading boundary

This is substantive randomized human muscle-performance evidence, closer to a **compensation strategy** than a protein-supply hypothesis. It deserves comparison with other functional candidates rather than being dismissed for lacking MVA proof; existing nominees also retain disease-transfer uncertainty. Acute/task-specific benefit, small sample size, related modeled outputs, null daily-function proxies and absent durability/CI evidence limit any claim of priority. Neither nominee superiority nor a new MVA ranking is established.

No BUB1B/BubR1 abundance, localization, stabilization, checkpoint or chromosome-fidelity assay was identified in the two complete scientific mains. Long-term systemic effects, tumor/abnormal-clone outcomes and pediatric/MVA benefit remain unmeasured. NO biology does not establish BubR1 repair.
