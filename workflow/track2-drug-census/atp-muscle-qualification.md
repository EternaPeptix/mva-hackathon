# Oral ATP as a common-compound muscle-performance challenger

**Disposition: retain as a supplementary functional challenger, not a Track 2 nominee.** Oral ATP has several human performance signals, but they come from small exercise studies, postoperative recovery, or one trial program split across three papers. None establishes a BUB1B/BUBR1 mechanism, chromosome-fidelity benefit, or MVA efficacy.

This is a source-bound, single-reader qualification outside the curated decision ledger. It does not add original-queue reading credit or count companion publications as independent studies. Independent review remains pending.

## How ATP entered the appraisal

Batch 14 record 114, MED:24813646, was screened at abstract level as a randomized oral-ATP study after knee arthroplasty. Its abstract reports 244 randomized participants and postoperative quadriceps-strength, pain, and opioid-use differences favoring ATP, but supplies no numerical effect estimates. The batch-14 screen passed its integrity check; the record’s abstract SHA-256 is 1529b73fb33ee93352272f74055e347fd0d67fc8650d63cb174e22645e5f2795. The full journal article was not available through the official publisher route during this review, so the abstract signal remains provisional. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24813646/).

## Replication and reporting context

NCT01508338 lists 44 actual participants in four arms: placebo, HMB, ATP, and HMB plus ATP. It has no posted registry results. The ATP paper reports its ATP/placebo comparison; two companion publications address HMB and HMB-plus-ATP participants. A 2017 independent letter questioned cross-paper supplement protocols, shared placebo denominators, and participant accounting. The lead author’s published response explained that the papers shared placebo participants and that two later placebo participants were added to the ATP comparison, leaving 11 ATP and 10 placebo participants in the published analysis. This is a plausible clarification, not an independent reanalysis; the related papers must not be counted as independent studies. The exact all-arm participant accounting and complete trial dataset remain unavailable. [Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01508338), [independent comment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5496306/), [author response](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5704583/).

The oral exposure mechanism also remains uncertain. In a 28-day human dose study and an acute crossover study, oral ATP did not raise measured whole-blood or plasma ATP; uric acid increased after some high-dose or proximal-release exposures. This challenges a simple claim that the supplement directly replenishes muscle ATP, while leaving local or metabolite-mediated effects possible. A separate 12-person uncontrolled blood-flow pilot cannot independently establish functional benefit. [Bioavailability study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3441280/), [dose study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21129239/), [blood-flow study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4086998/).

An earlier 14-day trial in healthy men found no between-group strength or blood-ATP effects; selected within-group changes in the high-dose arm are not a randomized treatment effect. [Jordan et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15179168/).

## Relevance to BUB1B and competitive position

None of these human studies measured BUB1B RNA, BUBR1 abundance or function, spindle-checkpoint activity, aneuploidy, chromosome fidelity, or MVA. Any effect would therefore be a generic muscle-output or recovery hypothesis, not restoration of the causal protein or correction of chromosome segregation.

ATP provides controlled exercise-performance signals that niacin’s open-label muscle-strength study does not, but it lacks niacin’s NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 rationale. It is less clinically disease-relevant than apitegromab’s randomized pediatric SMA motor-function evidence and less developed across neuromuscular-disease populations than the project’s creatine comparison. Those evidence types do not support a single efficacy ranking. **The ATP route does not displace niacin or apitegromab and should not enter the pitch’s primary nominee pair.** If space permits, it can be named as an affordable functional comparator for later testing.

Promotion would require an independent, prospectively specified ATP-only replication with a justified, authenticated preparation, plus an exposure-qualified affected/corrected muscle model. Advancement would require greater absolute output without reduced renewal or worsened chromosome fidelity. A BUBR1-restoration claim would additionally require direct BUB1B/BUBR1 target and functional measurements. No treatment recommendation follows.
