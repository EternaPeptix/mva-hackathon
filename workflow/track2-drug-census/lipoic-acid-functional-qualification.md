# Alpha-lipoic acid: limited functional positives, important disease-trial nulls and harms

**Retain alpha-lipoic acid (ALA; thioctic acid) as a bounded functional/neuropathy reserve, without promoting it over current nominees.** A small randomized athlete crossover reports a direct strength-recovery interaction. A large diabetic-neuropathy trial reports a secondary examiner-rated weakness benefit, but misses its primary composite. A newer progressive-MS trial finds no walking benefit and raises renal/tolerability concerns. These results deserve endpoint-specific credit; neither antioxidant markers nor symptom questionnaires should be substituted for force or mobility.

Four targeted discovery queries were used. The source task began at committed `b1feb0d7cff05ed9632fc9fe4c0893f67f871881`, with 238 decisions and no lipoic/thioctic entry. Two complete accessible scientific mains and their supplements were qualified; the recent MS report was available only as its complete primary abstract plus current registry. Older MS pilot/companion abstracts remain explicitly bounded context. No combination study supplies single-agent efficacy. ## Direct strength recovery in young trained men

[Isenmann et al., 2020, PMID33261642](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-020-00389-y), PMC7708149, enrolled 18 healthy, strength/endurance-trained men; 17 completed and one withdrew for health reasons. Mean age was 23.5±3.1 years. In a randomized masked crossover with a four-week washout, each participant completed both ALA and placebo protocols. Each period included an acute exercise experiment, recovery days, then a six-day intensive strength/running block. These are phases of one study, not independent replications.

The preparation was an **Athenion study-specific solubilized liquid**, 3 mL containing 150 mg ALA mixed in 200 mL water, with a matched-taste/appearance placebo. During the six-day block it was administered twice daily, 300 mg/day, before and after training; the intervening rest-day dosing was once daily. Exact enantiomer composition, excipients and purity were not supplied. Native Figure1 labels “150 ml,” conflicting with the Methods' 150 mg/3 mL concentrate; the registry supports 2×150 mg. The paper labels the final assessment T2(+7d), despite also describing the preceding acute/rest phase; retain the phase description rather than reconstructing an exact whole-period calendar from that label.

| Functional comparison | Reported result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| After the acute session | Time×treatment P=.992 | No comparative strength-recovery benefit demonstrated. |
| After the six-day block | Time×treatment **P=.028**, reported d=.164 | Actual comparative recovery/preservation signal, beyond different within-condition significance. |
| Back-squat 1-RM means | ALA 128.8→130.0 kg; placebo 128.2→125.6 kg; n=17 | Preservation around baseline after a demanding block, not a demonstrated long-term training or disease-rescue gain. |

Native Figure3 and supplementary Table4 confirm the reported interaction. Within-placebo P=.032 and within-ALA P=.320 alone would be insufficient, but the interaction supplies a direct comparison. No paired treatment CI is given. The source's reported d is small; it should not be reinterpreted as a paired-change standardized effect. Individual values are provided in supplemental Table3, but its ALA SDs (27.09/26.35) differ from the main/Table4 SDs (27.9/27.2); no effect precision was silently repaired or newly re-estimated.

The repeated-measures analysis is described as a two-way ANCOVA with Bonferroni comparisons, without specification of its covariate or crossover period/order/carryover terms. Current [DRKS00018768](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00018768) lists registration on 6 September 2019 before a 9 September start, but this is a current record, not a historical prespecification audit. It lists strength and several biomarkers as primary, with no single hierarchy. Allocation-concealment/sequence details are absent; different cap colours could distinguish bottles, without a reported blinding-success check. Quantified adherence, circulating ALA exposure and systematic adverse-event reporting are absent.

CK, myoglobin, IL-6 and IL-10 **did not have significant final time×treatment contrasts** (.136, .130, .107 and .959). Oxidized LDL, HDL and LDL comparisons were also null. The Discussion's anti-inflammatory/muscle-damage language is stronger than these comparisons; cytokine trajectories do not prove macrophage mediation or BUBR1 repair. Biomarker denominators range 14–17 after exclusions. Methods specifies a >3-SD outlier rule, while Discussion describes >3.5 for CK high responders; the two separately displayed high responders do not establish a new subgroup treatment effect. The reported strength interaction should be retained independently of those mechanism limitations.

## Diabetic neuropathy: clinical weakness is not dynamometer force

[NATHAN 1, PMID21775755](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc11-0503), PMC3161301, randomized 460 adults with mild-to-moderate diabetic distal sensorimotor polyneuropathy to **Thioctacid HR film-coated ALA 600 mg once daily** or matched tablets for four years. Randomized counts were 233/227; exposed safety counts 231/225; the reported intention-to-treat set was 230/224. Native flow records 95/89 intervention discontinuations. Table2's four-year endpoint n=215/207 must not be called four-year completers: the Methods allows the last available post-randomization value for the endpoint. Myopathy and other interfering neurological diseases were excluded. Mean age was approximately 53–54 years.

The primary NIS-LL+7 composite, combining clinical lower-limb impairment and seven physiological attributes, was **nonsignificant, P=.105**. The authors report LS changes −0.45 with ALA and +0.34 with placebo. Their “ALA-placebo” label for a positive 0.78 difference conflicts with those signed means; the reported interval −0.16 to 1.73 is retained as a source-label inconsistency, not repaired into another estimate.

There are real secondary positives. Lower-limb muscular-weakness score changed **−0.21±1.57 versus +0.17±2.12 points**, ALA versus placebo, P=.045 (Table2 means±SD). Overall NIS improved (P=.028), and the response/progression distributions differed for NIS (P=.013) and NIS-LL (.025). These are examiner-rated clinical signs, including weakness/reflex/sensory examination, not quantified force, gait or exercise endurance. Weakness symptom-number/severity questionnaires also improved (P=.005/.008), a separate patient-reported domain. NIS-LL's exact Results P=.0505 is not an unequivocal P<.05 positive despite rounding in the abstract. Nerve-conduction, sensory thresholds, total symptom score and other tested physiological outcomes were null.

The primary final alpha was adjusted to .0452 for an interim analysis. That does not confer family-wide confirmation on numerous secondary comparisons. The response definitions and trial's outcome plan are described in the paper, but [NCT00977483](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00977483) was first posted in 2009 after a stated 2005 completion. Its current design field says single-group despite the described randomized two-arm trial. No posted results or historical protocol/SAP was obtained. These limitations do not erase the clinical weakness signal or turn a failed primary into a successful one.

Safety needs equal visibility. Serious events occurred in **88/231 (38.1%) versus 63/225 (28.0%)**; causal attribution is not established by this difference. Supplementary Table1 reports heart-rate/rhythm disorders in **16/231 versus 6/225, nominal P=.047**; individual cardiovascular diagnoses were nonsignificant. Conversely, deaths were 3 versus 6 and QTc prolongation >60 ms was less frequent with ALA (3 versus 10, P=.0497). Global tolerability ratings were similar, but these observations do not establish safety equivalence. MEDA sponsorship, sponsor-generated randomization and author financial/employment relationships are disclosed. No enantiomer composition or product equivalence to the athlete liquid is established by the read intervention descriptions.

## Larger progressive-MS walking trial: primary null and renal signal

[PMID41397213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41397213/) was published online in December 2025 and in *Neurology* in January 2026. Its complete abstract and [NCT03161028](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03161028) were read. Ordinary publisher access returned 403; PubMed marks the PMC manuscript unavailable until 13 January 2027. No full-main/native-figure/supplement reading or access bypass is claimed.

In primary/secondary progressive MS, 54 participants received named oral LA 1,200 mg/day and 61 placebo for 24 months. The primary timed-25-foot-walk speed did not improve: changes were −0.39 versus −0.30 ft/s, with an abstract-reported contrast −0.08 [−0.33 to 0.17]; the interval level is not labelled in the read abstract. The current registry gives mixed-model **P=.51**, and 95% intervals for each arm. Two-minute walk and fall-count registry contrasts were also null (.902/.90). The model assigns 199 seconds when a participant cannot complete a follow-up timed walk. Completion was **34/54 versus 51/61**, consistent with greater LA discontinuation (37% versus 17%); incomplete-data assumptions and full sensitivity analyses remain unqualified without the main.

The abstract's brain-volume pattern is not a functional rescue. The registry's whole-brain-volume comparison is P=.084; the primary abstract also notes greater T2 lesion volume with LA. Neither different within-arm significance nor these imaging changes establish benefit in walking or muscle force.

The abstract reports treatment-related proteinuria prompting more frequent laboratory monitoring. Current registry records **proteinuria in 11/54 versus 2/61**, serious events in 11/54 versus 7/61, and membranous glomerulonephritis in 2/54 versus 0/61. It also records fewer suicidal-ideation reports with LA (0 versus 9) and deaths (0 versus 1). Preserve these as source-reported event patterns without general causal or net-safety conclusions. The capsule's precise stereochemical/product identity was not resolved from the abstract/registry.

Earlier same-program abstracts preserve limited positives without receiving full qualification here: [PMID28680916](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28680916/) reports reduced brain atrophy but nonsignificant walking (P=.06); its [gait companion, PMID30477834](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30477834/) reports a selected 18-person lower-disability turning-time benefit (P=.048), while the 21-person TUG effect interval crosses zero. The companion and pilot share NCT01188811 and are not independent replications. Selection, interactions and full methods were not newly verified; these abstracts cannot replace the larger walking-primary null.

## Identity and competitive consequence

The exact names “alpha-lipoic acid” and “thioctic acid” join the existing **CHEMBL33864** record without another main-catalog entity. Its structure is stereochemically unspecified and aliases include DL/racemate names. That lexical match is not proof of a trial's enantiomer ratio, pure R-ALA equivalence, conversion to a dihydrolipoic-acid intervention or a choline-ester effect. Keep the Thioctacid tablets, study-specific liquid and MS capsule distinguished. No EAA/creatine/vitamin/antioxidant mixture, omega-3 combination or unspecified extract supplies ALA-alone efficacy. Catalog approval metadata is not evidence of pediatric suitability or trial-product interchangeability.

The controlled strength interaction and clinical-weakness signal justify a reserve entry with these limits. They compare fairly against niacin's uncontrolled human-strength context, without a selective demand for MVA proof. Small young-athlete evidence, clinical-score endpoints, substantial long-term missingness, disease-transfer limits, primary nulls and observed harms prevent an automatic nomination or cross-trial superiority claim. No assayed BUB1B/BUBR1 restoration, faithful daughter-cell output or MVA rescue was established in the qualified material.

Complete scientific mains were read for NATHAN 1 and the exercise study, including original XML table entries, captions, conflicts and all available supplement text. Native NATHAN flow and supplement safety tables, all six exercise figures and all three supplement pages were inspected. NATHAN supplement page1's centre list was read as text, not visually; main-table PDF layout, bibliographies and individual-data reanalysis were not performed. Three MS abstracts and selected current registry fields were read at their stated depths. No historic registry versions or additional cited primaries were qualified. Exact hashes, four-query discovery boundaries, failed access and source-specific reading scopes accompany this report.

Independent precision note: the exercise paper names Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests for biomarkers despite its crossover design, while the results use time-by-group terminology. A verified paired biomarker model cannot be inferred. This does not erase the separately reported strength interaction.
