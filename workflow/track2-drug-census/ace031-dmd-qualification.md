# ACE-031 in DMD: original abstract and posted registry results

**The complete paper and native outcomes remain inaccessible.** The accepted source qualification covers the complete original abstract of Campbell et al. (PMID **27462804**, DOI **10.1002/mus.25268**) and the relevant current official **NCT01099761/A031-03** record, including all posted outcomes and adverse-event results. It does not claim a full-main, native-table/figure, supplement or protocol review. The publisher’s full-text link returns the abstract; its PDF request returned 403 HTML. Europe PMC identifies subscription-only access, with no PMC text/PDF. Bounded public-copy searches did not recover the main.

The current official registry **explicitly links PMID 27462804** in `protocolSection.ipdSharingStatementModule.description` ([NCT01099761](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01099761); the original registry response remains in the external source archive). This establishes the registry-to-publication link. Numerical findings below remain attributed to the registry because the paper’s main, analyses and native tables/figures were not obtained or verified.

## Identity, population and exposure

The [original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27462804/) identifies ACE-031 as an **activin type IIB receptor–IgG1-Fc fusion protein** that binds myostatin and related ligands. This is a receptor fusion trap, not proof of selective myostatin-only inhibition. Exact formulation, excipients, lot/purity, achieved exposure and retail equivalence were not established from the accessible sources. The name ramatercept was included only in the bounded ledger lookup; this packet does not establish or admit a new synonym.

The [official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01099761) describes ambulant males with DMD, age at least four, clinical weakness, corticosteroid use for at least one year and a stable dose/schedule for six months. All 24 reported baseline participants were male, aged 18 or younger, enrolled in Canada and able to cover 10 m in under 12 seconds. Exact age distributions and broader baseline balance are unavailable in this record.

Two regimens were actually enrolled: **0.5 mg/kg subcutaneously every four weeks** and **1.0 mg/kg every two weeks**, each planned for 12 weeks, versus matching-volume placebo every two or four weeks. These are source exposure descriptions. The placebo composition and dose-cohort pooling/randomization details are not supplied. A planned **2.5 mg/kg every-four-week cohort enrolled zero** and contributes no safety or efficacy evidence.

## Hierarchy and denominators

Safety was the paper’s primary objective. The registry lists two primary outcomes: participants with at least possibly treatment-related adverse events, and participants with similarly attributed laboratory abnormalities. Lean mass, lumbar-spine bone density, myometry, walking and pulmonary measurements are secondary.

Registry flow reports **9 low-dose, 9 higher-dose and 6 placebo participants**, all started and completed; no individual noncompletion is listed. Termination before the third cohort is distinct from assuming that already enrolled participants failed to complete follow-up. Actual numbers of injections received, paper analysis populations, imputation, multiplicity and model details remain unread. Posted outcomes use overall denominators 9/9/6, but do not supply the denominators of the two walking-age strata.

The registry labels its results as baseline to end-of-study, **approximately 24 weeks**, and safety as 12 weeks’ treatment plus 12 weeks’ follow-up. These dates are not replaced with a presumed paper timepoint.

The abstract reports a **non-significant 6MWT maintenance trend**, alongside lean-mass/BMD/fat-mass trends. It does not establish controlled functional benefit. The registry’s lean-mass ANCOVA entries give P=.023/.012/.435 attached separately to one group each; the BMD P=.039 entry names only the higher-dose group. With no defined comparison or confidence interval, these must **not become verified active-versus-placebo positives**. Nor are they unambiguously established within-arm tests. Fat-mass numerical results and pharmacokinetic data were not recovered.

Four myometry rows, the 10-m test and three respiratory tests have descriptive values but no posted inferential analyses. The myometry title/unit says percentage change while its row labels say kg. Exact strings and all values are preserved in JSON without repairing that inconsistency. A formal strength null cannot be verified from these rows; lack of a demonstrated advantage is not proof of zero effect. No age-by-treatment interaction, pooled walking estimate or cross-dose causal comparison is reconstructed. Mass and BMD changes do not establish muscle renewal or useful function.

These categories overlap and must not be added into unique-person totals. The nonserious table uses a 5% reporting threshold; related-event and all-event categories are distinct. The abstract states no serious or severe events and identifies epistaxis/telangiectasia concerns as the reason for stopping after the second regimen. Zero serious events does not establish safety. Grade details, duration, reversibility, individual causality and exposure-adjusted risks remain unavailable. The higher-regimen pattern is important but is not a formal dose-response estimate. A specific BMP9/BMP10 or other off-target causal mechanism was not demonstrated in the accessible trial material; neither class-wide harm nor MVA harm follows.

The same registry IPD-sharing description separately states that the sponsor terminated the study early because of concerns about potential adverse drug reactions with long-term use identified in chronic animal toxicity studies. This is **sponsor-attributed stopping context**, alongside the abstract’s epistaxis/telangiectasia explanation. The available sources do not resolve their relative roles or timing. The underlying animal studies were not obtained or qualified; this statement establishes neither a specific causal mechanism nor class-wide or MVA harm.

## Planning, disclosure and comparative limits

The current record was first posted **8 April 2010**, with a month-only April 2010 start, so registration before every participant is unverified. It records completion in June 2011, results first posted in October 2016 and an October 2022 update. No original protocol/SAP, historical registry fields or document attachment was obtained. Absence of an attachment or a Europe PMC supplement flag does not prove that no supplementary material exists.

The registry is industry sponsored; the publisher author affiliations include Acceleron personnel. Full funding and conflict declarations were not obtained. Current Europe PMC metadata has no linked comment/correction entry; the accessible publisher page did not identify an article-specific notice. No exhaustive notice-history or Crossmark audit was performed.

The historical **860bfcf/276** source check found no existing exact ACE-031 assessment. At the current **b0159563/276** proposal baseline, the same bounded check finds no duplicate entry. The existing catalog maps the exact source label **ACE-031** to **CHEMBL2108674**, preferred name RAMATERCEPT. The proposed appraisal uses only ACE-031: a catalog name cross-reference does not establish formulation or batch equivalence and does not add ramatercept or generic ActRIIB aliases. The report was batch02 **order 8**, selected by the predeclared design/administration/function ordering, not its result direction.

The existing apitegromab assessment describes a randomized motor-function benefit in treated SMA, with its own source-depth, fracture, missingness and transfer limits. ACE-031’s accessible evidence does not yet establish a positive controlled functional contrast. That evidence distinction is relevant to compensation research; it is not a head-to-head result, proof of apitegromab’s safety/efficacy superiority in DMD or MVA, or a conclusion derived from target specificity alone. Function, exposure, duration, renewal and fidelity should be judged under the same criteria. This source-specific appraisal adds no MVA efficacy, original source-queue reading credit or nominee change.
