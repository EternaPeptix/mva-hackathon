# FGF4–AA cardiomyocyte maturation and hypoxia model

Choi et al., *Cells* 2021;10:2741. [Primary paper, PMID 34685725](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8534799/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells10102741), [GEO GSE171824](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE171824). Assessed 2026-09-22. Primary source qualification; no additional abstract-reading credit.

FGF4 plus AA produced genuine cardiomyocyte differentiation and optical beating improvements in one human embryonic stem-cell line. The later experiment deliberately injured those cells with hypoxia. It establishes a useful in vitro injury model, not hypoxia protection, BUBR1 restoration, skeletal-muscle restoration or treatment of MVA. The positives remain worth retaining independently of the provisional niacin/apitegromab nominations, neither of which is established as an MVA treatment.

## Positives and the endpoints actually measured

1. **Differentiation:** FGF4 alone raised cTnT, cTnI, MLC2v, MLC2a and ANP transcripts; AA alone raised MLC2v/MLC2a/ANP in Figure S2. FGF4 10–25 ng/mL produced the largest ventricular/atrial marker responses in the tested range; the response was not monotonic through 100 ng/mL. The combination markedly increased MLC2v (approximately 35-fold relative to control), with a significant comparison against FGF4 alone in Figure 1. Do not turn the authors' “synergy” wording into a demonstrated factorial interaction.
2. **Protein and subtype:** Figure 1 immunofluorescence shows cTnT/MLC2v and α-actinin/MLC2v staining in FGF4 and combination cultures. MLC2a also remains detectable. No quantified purity or fully adult ventricular phenotype is established; the Discussion explicitly acknowledges partial/incomplete maturation.
3. **Actual function:** Figure 2 records spontaneous contractions using 50-fps microscopy and light-intensity changes in selected regions over 10 s. Combination cultures show more synchronous/regular traces and shorter, less dispersed peak-to-peak intervals. The reported pooled interval comparison is P<0.001; it is not a separate formal test of variance or synchrony. This is more than marker evidence, but it is not force measurement, electrophysiology, conduction velocity or calcium-transient assessment.
4. **Hypoxia response:** In combination-pretreated cultures, Figure 3 shows higher cTnI, CK-MB and myoglobin release at days 17/19/21 under hypoxia, with significance annotations against concurrent normoxia. Figure S11 shows cleaved-caspase-3 staining and morphological membrane damage. These are injury readouts, not protection. Pretreatment can alter marker abundance or cellular composition, so more released marker does not establish a larger death fraction without cell-number/viability normalization.
5. **Recovery during continued culture:** Figure S8 shows an acute fall in beating after day 15 and recovery toward baseline by differentiation day 25. This is a surviving-culture functional observation without a drug-versus-vehicle rescue design, quantitative survival or a matched long-term normoxia series. Peak-to-peak time still carries a significance annotation at day 25. The main-text “next 17 days” description conflicts with the actual day-15–25 panel; retain the plotted schedule.

## Denominators, statistics and reporting limits

- Figure 1 and Figures S2/S4 report n=3 per group with mean±SD; independent culture/differentiation units are not fully specified for these comparisons. Figure S1 alone explicitly says three independent experiments. One hESC line is not multiple biological donors.
- Figure 2 reports **n=63 control and n=80 combination** with a two-tailed unpaired t-test. These are not demonstrated numbers of independent cultures or cell lines. The selected regions and repeated contraction intervals can be clustered; do not advertise n=143 independent biological replicates.
- Figure S8 gives **n=21, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18** at days 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25. The biological unit and repeated-measure structure are unspecified. Changing n cannot be interpreted as survival counts. Figure 3/S5 does not state the biological replicate number.
- GEO deposits only **two RNA-seq sample records: one normoxia (GSM5235256) and one hypoxia (GSM5235257)**. Whether upstream material was pooled is unspecified. Figure 5/S10 qPCR uses the same RNA samples and “triplicate data”; it is not independent biological validation. RNA-seq selection is based on fold-change thresholds, with no demonstrated replicate-based differential-expression significance/FDR in this study.
- Methods use t-tests or ANOVA followed by Student–Newman–Keuls. No comprehensive multiplicity correction or blinded/randomized design is described. Figures S2/S4 use multiple pairwise t-tests.
- **Fold-change denominator inconsistency:** reported cTnI 2.71/2.14/1.86, CK-MB 2.55/1.37/1.25 and myoglobin 5.24/6.12/7.4 match the rounded S5 hypoxia-arm values divided by that arm's day-15 baseline, not consistently concurrent normoxia as some Results wording implies. Example: day-17 CK-MB is 2.693 ng/mL hypoxia versus 0.595 normoxia and 1.055 at day 15; 2.693/1.055≈2.55. Preserve the measured direction and plotted tests, not the mislabeled control-relative ratios.
- The Results also describe an untreated-culture myoglobin increase at day 19, whereas Figure 3/S5 show 0.004 ng/mL hypoxia versus 0.085 normoxia. This does not reverse the combination-arm injury finding, but prevents uncritical use of every narrative comparison.

## BUB1B, harm and translation

Figures 4F/5B place BUB1B in a database-derived interaction network of hypoxia-responsive transcripts. Figure S10 labels **BUB1B RNA-seq 0.3-fold and qPCR 0.5-fold**, hypoxia versus normoxia after both groups received FGF4+AA. The primer appears in Table S1. Thus this is a measured transcript decrease, not merely a name mention, but also not evidence that either reagent lowers or restores BUBR1 protein. No BUBR1 protein, acetylation, half-life, checkpoint activity, chromosome segregation or variant-specific rescue was tested. Network edges are not protein-interaction experiments in these cells.

There is no drug-dose toxicity curve, quantitative survival, tumorigenicity, chromosome-fidelity/karyotype assessment or in vivo efficacy. Hypoxia caused injury, but that does not prove the maturation reagents themselves are toxic at these doses. Conversely, surviving contractions do not prove systemic safety. No skeletal-muscle force, MVA model, Leu737Ter/Asn1002Lys rescue or clinical treatment was studied. These gaps remain open; they do not erase the cardiac differentiation/function positive or imply that niacin/apitegromab have proven MVA superiority.

## Current supply and ledger proposal

Sigma currently lists [F8424 human recombinant FGF4](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sigma/f8424), an E. coli-expressed 177-aa, approximately 19-kDa cell-culture reagent in 25/100-µg packs. This verifies a research source, not the exact historical reagent or a clinical formulation; availability requires checking the supplier. Sigma [A8960](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/sigma/a8960) currently identifies magnesium ascorbyl phosphate, CAS 1713265-25-8, with anhydrous-basis molecular weight 289.54 and ≥95% assay. That catalog was not stated by this cardiomyocyte paper and is not a study-lot match.

This qualification adds the FGF4 assessment and extends the existing ascorbate-phosphate assessment while preserving its earlier reprogramming evidence. FGF4 and FGF2 remain separate. Shared differentiation reagents do not receive independent therapeutic efficacy credit. The late AA identity remains qualified.
