# Kalanchoe tubiflora KT-NB: bounded primary qualification

**KT-NB has measured antiproliferative and mitotic-disrupting activity in cultured cancer cells, with relative but incomplete WI-38 sparing. It provides no BUB1B/BubR1 response, useful normal-cell rescue, MVA compensation, or isolated-molecule efficacy evidence.** The defensible identity is the laboratory n-butanol fraction from this source, not the household plant, tea, juice, or an inferred bufadienolide.

Hsieh YJ et al. *Kalanchoe tubiflora extract inhibits cell proliferation by affecting the mitotic apparatus.* BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine 12:149 (2012). [Publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1472-6882-12-149), [original PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/1472-6882-12-149.pdf), DOI **10.1186/1472-6882-12-149**, PMID **22963191**, PMCID **PMC3557174**. Original question: Resolve cell identities, fraction composition, dose labeling and normal-cell controls; preserve measured antimitotic activity without inferring whole-plant efficacy or safety.

## Exact preparation and eligibility

The paper labels the plant **Kalanchoe tubiflora**. Authors' contributions explicitly credits **Yann-Lii Leu** with collecting and identifying it. The examined source does not specify which plant organ(s), collection locality/date, herbarium voucher, or an independently reproducible botanical authentication record. It therefore supports an author-identified species label, not an assumption that leaves or the entire plant were used.

Fresh raw tissue was chopped and extracted three times under reflux using 95% ethanol, filtered and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude extract was suspended in water and sequentially partitioned with chloroform and n-butanol. The fractions were named **KT-C** (chloroform soluble), **KT-NB** (n-butanol soluble), and **KT-W** (water soluble). Figure 1 verifies the fractionation sequence. The tested KT-NB yielded **53.79 g dry fraction from 6,638.76 g raw tissue**. It was dissolved in DMSO, passed through a 0.22 µm membrane, and diluted in cell-culture medium. Nominal doses are mass of the fraction per mL, not an active molecule's molarity. Final assay DMSO concentration and its matching across dose levels are not specified, although DMSO controls are shown.

**No isolated constituent was administered or chemically identified as the active material.** There is no composition/fingerprint, purity, active-content standardization, fraction-comparison efficacy series, or batch replication. Background citations about bufadienolides in Kalanchoe species and Discussion suggestions of CENP-E/Mps1 do not identify the active substance or target. The authors explicitly call for subsequent isolation of pure compounds. KT-C/KT-W are extraction products, not qualified efficacious agents in this experiment.

A proposed identity refinement is justified only as **“Kalanchoe tubiflora n-butanol-soluble laboratory fraction (KT-NB), prepared from an ethanol extract; PMID22963191 preparation.”** No CAS/ChEMBL/single-molecule identity or household-product equivalence is proposed. One exact-source preparation assessment is integrated, with no isolated-molecule or consumer-product identity.

## Models, doses and measured outcomes

Main experiments use **HeLa, A549, BFTC905, MCF-7, HEp-2 and source-labelled MES-SA**. Methods/caption sometimes print **MED-SA**, whereas Results and Figure 2B label MES-SA; retain that spelling discrepancy. These lines came from the Food Industry Research and Development Institute (Taiwan), but the article provides no STR or mycoplasma result.

The paper calls HEp-2 a laryngeal carcinoma line. The authoritative [Cellosaurus HEp-2 entry](https://www.cellosaurus.org/CVCL_1906) identifies it as a HeLa derivative and ICLAC-00007. Thus the panel is not independent confirmation across six distinct tumor origins. This registry correction does not identify the authors' individual vial retrospectively.

Methods describe background-subtracted MTT absorbance relative to negative control, with triplicate assays and means/SD; captions specify three independent experiments. No panel-specific inferential test/P value or validated IC50-fit uncertainty is supplied. MTT is a metabolic/growth-related signal; HeLa cell counts, mitotic index and imaging add orthogonal evidence, but do not establish exclusive killing or a specific apoptosis pathway. PI sub-G1 alone is not a mechanistically specific death assay.

## Normal-cell evidence and dose/normalization conflicts

The native [Additional file 1](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1186%2F1472-6882-12-149/MediaObjects/12906_2012_1242_MOESM1_ESM.tiff) compares **WI-38 with A549** by MTT over 0/24/48/72 h, with DMSO and three independent experiments. [Cellosaurus WI-38](https://www.cellosaurus.org/CVCL_0579) identifies it as a finite female fetal-lung fibroblast line. A549 is a carcinoma line, so this is not an isogenic normal-versus-transformed comparison and does not isolate division rate from lineage or culture differences.

- **Dose labels conflict within S1:** the caption and original PDF p.9 list **1.35, 6.75, 13.5 and 25 µg/mL**, whereas the native plotted legend lists **1.5625, 3.125, 6.25, 12.5 and 25 µg/mL**. The treatment-series assignment must remain unresolved rather than silently choosing one. The 25 µg/mL label is shared; S1B explicitly labels its images at that concentration after 72 h.
- **Normalization matters:** S1 starts every curve at 100%, but its untreated curves rise to roughly 320% (WI-38) and 370% (A549) at 72 h. This cannot be read as 100%=contemporaneous untreated control at each time point. The caption's statement that the control was set to 100% does not fully define the time reference. Figure 2B's 72-h dose curve is a distinct comparison and should not be merged numerically with S1.
- **Relative sparing is real but bounded:** WI-38 treatment curves lie above A549 at later points, yet are substantially below their own contemporaneous untreated curve. WI-38 growth/MTT is affected; the authors explicitly acknowledge an effect on dividing normal diploid cells. S1B's apparently preserved WI-38 morphology at 25 µg/mL is qualitative and cannot establish normal-cell safety, mitotic fidelity or tissue function. Do not call the normal-cell result null.
- **Other reporting limits:** the abstract compresses a 48-h/6.75 µg/mL “KT” viability claim, whereas the main text separates HeLa cell counts from the 72-h MTT series and consistently identifies the test material as KT-NB. Figure 4A lists 33% sub-G1 at 72 h while prose says 35%. Its displayed phase percentages do not sum to 100% (e.g. treated 48 h totals 71%, 72 h totals 73%); no missing category or denominator is explained. These are reported values, not a complete reconstructible distribution.
- **Figure 7D denominator remains unclear:** Results describes 37% of bipolar-metaphase cells as misaligned, but the y-axis is percent of metaphase cells and treated aligned+misaligned bars total well below 100%. Do not calculate a corrected conditional rate. The all-mitotic Figure 7E score and metaphase-only Figure 7D are separate denominators; combining them would be invalid. No raw count table resolves this.

## BUB1B, specificity and restoration boundary

The only experimental-paper-body BubR1 mention occurs in Discussion's literature account of how CENP-E regulates a checkpoint, with reference 61. **No BUB1B RNA, BubR1 protein abundance, stability, localization, kinase/checkpoint activity, loss-of-function or rescue experiment is reported.** CENP-E and Mps1 are suggested possible targets, not bound/inhibited targets demonstrated for KT-NB. No ingredient or target-specific efficacy can be assigned.

Figure 6 shows Aurora A localization and phospho-histone H3 Ser10 staining in treated mitotic cells. These observations do not amount to direct Aurora kinase binding/activity/selectivity assays. The claim of an intact interphase microtubule network/normal centrosome number is partly based on data not shown. No tubulin-polymerization or microtubule-dynamics assay establishes that effects are exclusively mitotic or excludes all tubulin actions. Such limits do not erase the visible spindle and chromosome defects.

The paper provides **anticancer-oriented culture perturbation**, not useful normal-cell compensation. There is no MVA/BUB1B-deficient model, normal-cell functional recovery, animal treatment, pharmacokinetics, clinical benefit or comparison showing superiority to a census nominee. The authors themselves call for in-vivo testing and pure-compound isolation. Laboratory fraction activity cannot be transferred to plant ownership, herbal use, tea, juice, another species, or an unmeasured constituent. No administration recommendation follows.
