# Calcium-HMB in older women: one cohort, limited gait benefit

23 September 2026. Bounded qualification of PMID 34081113 (DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqab176) and its muscle-quality secondary analysis, PMID 35288513 (DOI 10.1249/MSS.0000000000002836), associated with UMIN000028560. **Retain the reported randomized usual-gait-speed benefit as a calcium-containing formulation result. Broad exercise enhancement, durable HMB-specific function and a nominee replacement are not established.** Both result abstracts were read completely; the original result manuscripts and native result tables/figures remain inaccessible. The own-trial protocol and registry were read with the scope below. This is not full-primary qualification of either results paper.

## Intervention and control are not interchangeable with isolated HMB

Both results abstracts describe **calcium-HMB 1,500 mg daily for 12 weeks**. The own-trial protocol specifies a Kyowa powder supplying **1,200 mg HMB and 207 mg calcium**, plus 3,500 mg carbohydrate, 30 mg protein, 20 mg fat and 0.2 mg sodium. The protocol planned mixing it with 200 mL water and taking it once daily after a meal. Protocol-specified placebo had the same presentation/flavor but **no HMB or calcium**, replacing their amounts with carbohydrate. This documents the salt/formulation versus HMB-amount distinction; 1,500 mg calcium-HMB must not be rewritten as 1,500 mg free-acid HMB. Actual result-paper composition confirmation, product assay, brand and achieved exposure were not obtained. The protocol-specified contrast does not isolate HMB from its calcium-containing formulation or establish equivalence to arbitrary retail HMB products. [Result abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34081113/), [protocol intervention section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6398682/).

The 2×2 factorial study assigned 156 women aged 65–79 years with skeletal muscle index below 5.7 kg/m² to resistance training plus active powder, training plus placebo, education plus active powder, or education plus placebo. The protocol depicts 39 per arm. Training was supervised twice weekly, with body weight, bands, ankle weights and later machines; education occurred every two weeks. **The HMB main effect pools both training strata**, while the exercise main effect pools both supplement strata. Thus the gait result is neither a demonstrated supplement-by-exercise synergy nor proof of an individually significant HMB-only arm. The protocol asks participants to maintain habitual diet/activity. Supplement assignment could be masked; exercise versus education was inherently apparent despite the study's general “double-blind” description.

The protocol's native flow clarifies the registry's two-phase language: the analyzed period is the first 12-week parallel intervention and 12-week observation. A second intervention was offered later for ethical reasons and was explicitly outside the highlighted analysis period. Do not count it as an independent crossover efficacy replication. The 2022 paper calls itself a secondary analysis and has the same location, sample, four arms, duration and 156/149 baseline/follow-up counts; treat these publications as **one cohort**, not two trials. [Protocol Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6398682/), [companion abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35288513/).

## What the outcomes support

Muscle mass was the **primary outcome**; strength and physical performance were secondary. The 2021 abstract reports no exercise-by-HMB interaction on the primary mass outcomes. It does not provide numeric HMB mass contrasts or every secondary interaction, so these must not be invented. The original primary mass tables remain unreviewed. The abstract's positive supplement result is a **usual-gait-speed main effect of +0.06 m/s (95% CI 0.01–0.11)** versus placebo at the end of intervention. This is actual controlled functional evidence, not merely greater mass or a within-arm change. Its precise P value, multiplicity-adjusted status, baseline gait limitation and clinical-response distribution were unavailable. The authors describe the benefit as negligible, but the available interval is not proof that every plausible functional effect is clinically irrelevant. [2021 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34081113/).

The much broader positives belong to **exercise versus education**, not the powder: usual/maximal gait speed +0.16/+0.15 m/s, knee-extensor/hip-adductor strength +22.0/+21.8 N, timed up-and-go −0.5 s, and sit-to-stand −1.7 s. Their source confidence intervals are retained in the companion JSON. The paper's abstract/conclusion reports no broad enhancement of exercise effects by HMB. That does not prove every HMB contrast is exactly zero or allow missing strength/timed-task CIs to be reconstructed.

In the 2022 secondary analysis, functional muscle quality is knee-extension force divided by quadriceps thickness; compositional quality is rectus-femoris ultrasound echo intensity. Neither showed a significant HMB-by-training interaction. The **exercise** main effect reduced the decline in functional quality by **0.71 N/mm (95% CI 0.11–1.31)** versus education. It is not an HMB benefit, and reduced decline is not recovery above baseline. No main effect of either intervention on compositional quality was significant. The abstract does not supply the HMB functional-quality contrast, its CI, or detailed denominator handling. [2022 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35288513/).

The primary abstract reports 149 assessed at week 12 and 144 at week 24, from 156 at baseline. It states an intention-to-treat analysis. The protocol plans 20 multiply imputed datasets using a Markov-chain approach; **actual implementation, arm-specific attrition, reasons, adherence and sensitivity analyses were not verified from the unread results Methods**. The protocol specifies two-way ANOVA, multiple main/interaction/within-arm comparisons, and Scheffé adjustment following significant one-way ANOVA. That is not a verified family-wide correction of all supplement endpoints and visits. Treat the isolated secondary gait signal as exploratory pending the final statistical details.

The abstract says most improvements disappeared during the subsequent 12-week observation period. That general statement, also applied to exercise in an indexed publisher conclusion, **does not identify the HMB gait-specific week-24 estimate or establish its durability or loss**. No treatment-by-time test was available. Those details remain an explicit follow-up question rather than a reconstructed null.

## Safety, registration and biological scope

The current official registry reports eight events potentially associated with supplementation: nausea (1), stomach discomfort (2), whole-body discomfort (1), increased urinary frequency (2), pruritus (1) and high blood pressure (1). It reports no significant difference in the number of participants with these events between active and placebo groups, and no exercise-related events. It does not provide the arm allocation or denominator for these eight events; they must not all be assigned to HMB recipients. This is not proof of safety equivalence or long-term/pediatric tolerability. Participants with relevant heart, kidney or liver dysfunction or exercise restrictions were excluded. [Official registry, version 10](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000032688).

The original 7 August 2017 registry version already designated muscle mass primary and muscle function/quality among the secondary domains, with 1,200 mg HMB specified. Its earlier eligibility used the lowest muscle-mass quartile and excluded AWGS-defined sarcopenia. By version 3 (11 January 2018, before the protocol's February recruitment/March baseline), the explicit sarcopenia exclusion was absent. That version prints “SMI<0.57”; the current record, protocol and result abstracts print 5.7. Preserve this historical discrepancy without silently treating 0.57 as the actual trial cutoff or inferring an outcome-driven change. The change-history index was read, but all ten full versions were not. The published protocol appeared in February 2019 after the reported 2018 intervention/observation, so its publication alone is not prospective proof of every analysis detail. [Official history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000032688), [protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6398682/).

Kyowa supplied the powders and funded the trial; the protocol lists a Kyowa employee as an author and reports the funder was not involved in recruitment, intervention, collection, analysis or manuscript preparation. These disclosures do not erase randomized findings. No BUB1B/BubR1 endpoint or MVA experiment appears in the read abstracts/protocol; the inaccessible complete results manuscripts cannot support a comprehensive assay-absence claim. General mTOR/protein-metabolism rationales in the protocol are not measured BUBR1 restoration.

## Competitive and identity judgment

This controlled adult gait result deserves a visible functional-reserve entry; it is stronger causal evidence for that specific gait outcome than the appraised open-label niacin strength observation. It neither demonstrates niacin-like BUBR1 restoration nor establishes a broader functional advantage. The limited isolated secondary signal, unavailable detailed analysis and older low-mass population do not presently outweigh the recorded positive primary pediatric-SMA motor-function evidence for apitegromab. This comparison does not require MVA efficacy selectively: niacin and apitegromab also remain provisional, and no cross-trial MVA efficacy order has been measured.

The 225-entry baseline had no calcium-HMB assessment; this update adds one while preserving all previous objects. The NIH DSLD inventory has **group 2778, Calcium hydroxymethylbutyrate**, with a specific normalized `calcium hmb` alias. Generic HMB aliases span several groups, and the ChEMBL acid record CHEMBL4303793 does not by itself establish the calcium-salt product identity. The entry matches **calcium-HMB/calcium HMB only**, preserves the calcium/carbohydrate control distinction and avoids free-acid HMB, leucine, arginine-HMB or creatine-HMB joins. Ingredient indexing is not drug approval, clinical availability or proof that a retail preparation matches the trial.
