# Citral: cancer-cell effects with an unconfirmed BUB1B RNA decrease

**23 September 2026.** Retain the demonstrated in-vitro anticancer observations,
but deprioritize citral as a BUBR1-restoration or muscle-compensation candidate.
This result does not establish harm or benefit in normal MVA cells.

[Balusamy et al., Scientific Reports 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-41406-8)
(PMID 30890753; PMC6425008) studied citral isolated from commercial lemongrass
oil in AGS stomach-cancer cells, with an MRC-5 normal lung-cell comparator in
the viability assay. Read scope: complete main text/tables/captions, Figure 1,
8 and 9 images; complete supplement PDF text and its RNA-quality/expression
figures; workbook sheet/header inventory and BUB1B-bearing rows. Network
figures 2–7 and every unrelated workbook row were not individually appraised.
No raw sequencing reanalysis or biological replication was performed.

## What the experiment supports

- Citral reduces AGS metabolic viability and colony formation; microscopy,
  Annexin-V/PI and DNA-fragmentation assays support cell injury/apoptosis.
  The paper also reports reduced migration. These cell assays do not demonstrate
  prevention of metastasis in an organism; reduced scratch closure is also
  confounded by the compound's effects on survival and proliferation.
- Figure 1C shows a substantially higher viability IC50 in MRC-5 than AGS
  cells. This positive comparator must be retained; it is one lung-cell line,
  not a therapeutic window in patient myogenic or other BUB1B-deficient cells.
  Numeric IC50 values are not printed on the bars, so none are treated as exact.
- RNA was collected after 48 hours; the treated library is identified as
  20 micrograms/mL. Table 1 reports one control and one treated library.
  Biological RNA-sequencing replication is not established by the report.
  Other cell-assay replication does not establish sequencing replication.

## The BUB1B result needs its actual denominator and correction

Supplementary File S6 (`41598_2019_41406_MOESM7_ESM.xlsx`), Sheet1 row 514,
contains the source values below; Sheet2 row 514 repeats the expression values.
The same row identifies control and treated samples explicitly.

| Field | Source cell | Value |
|---|---|---:|
| Control expression, FPKM | H514 | 20.4812 |
| Treated expression, FPKM | I514 | 2.77232 |
| Log2 fold change | J514 | -2.88513454791642 |
| Nominal P value | M514 | 0.00535 |
| Adjusted q value/FDR | N514 | 0.542718 |
| Significant flag | O514 | no |

The direction is lower BUB1B RNA, approximately 7.39-fold by the displayed
values, but **not significant after the source's multiple-testing correction**.
The paper selected its 612-gene list using nominal P < 0.05 and a fold-change
threshold. Table 2's grouped cell-cycle value (-1.25) is not the BUB1B-specific
fold change, and Table 5's GO-category P value (0.45) is not the gene-expression
test. No BUB1B-specific PCR validation, BUBR1 protein measurement, checkpoint
rescue or segregation-fidelity assay is reported.

Thus neither a proven BUB1B-inhibitory mechanism nor a BUBR1-restorative effect
can be assigned to citral from this dataset. The broad loss of cycling-cell
transcripts during cancer-cell injury also leaves direct versus secondary
effects unresolved.

## Identity, limitations and ranking

The methods describe chromatographic isolation and spectroscopic identification
of citral from the oil, and Figure 1 depicts neral and geranial. An exact
isomer ratio/purity and an equivalent consumer preparation are not established.
DMSO is the stated negative control and cisplatin the positive comparator.
No human exposure bridge, MVA animal experiment or muscle-function result is
provided. The colony assay is described as seven days in the text and four
days in its caption; this discrepancy remains unresolved. Figure 9's narrative
also switches early/late-apoptosis quadrant descriptions, so precise fractions
are not inferred from that prose. Figure 9C1 visibly contains red fluorescence
despite the text describing its absence in control cells; that qualitative PI
panel is not treated as a clean independent confirmation of apoptosis.

**Competitiveness:** actual cancer-cell effects warrant credit, but they answer
a different question from restoring usable BUBR1 or improving muscle output.
The source provides no basis to displace the provisional niacin/restoration or
apitegromab/compensation hypotheses. Neither nominee is thereby validated, and
this appraisal does not extrapolate citral's cancer-cell effects to a child's
normal tissues. Food-substance listing does not supply efficacy, exposure or
formulation equivalence.
