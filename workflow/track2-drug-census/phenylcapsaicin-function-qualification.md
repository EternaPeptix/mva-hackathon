## Original source and administered preparation

The source is [Jiménez-Martínez et al., PMID 37601635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37601635/), [DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1215644](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1215644), an 11-page open-access original paper. The high-dose capsule contained **2.5 mg phenylcapsaicin (aXivite, Malmö, Sweden)**; the low dose was 0.625 mg. Placebo contained maltodextrin/excipients and red dye. Capsules were prepared and packed by a non-involved researcher at Life Pro Nutrition facilities and described as identical in appearance, color and taste. A capsule was taken with water under researcher supervision **45 minutes before the first weekly exercise session**; no redosing at the 24-hour session is described. The introduction's 98% composition and the discussion's enhanced-bioavailability claims cite other literature, not a trial-batch assay or pharmacokinetic measurement. Exact active-capsule excipients, batch purity/identity verification, plasma or muscle exposure and retail equivalence are unverified. No approved-medicine or pediatric-use status is established by this trial or this packet.

Twenty-five healthy resistance-trained men were enrolled (mean age 21.7 years, SD 3.7; 2–5 years of training; mean squat 1RM 125.6 kg, SD 21.0). Cardiovascular, muscular, neurological and metabolic disorders were exclusion criteria. Two withdrew during the three weeks, one due to injury and one for missing a session. The paper does not clearly identify the final analyzed population for each outcome or missing-data procedure; **23 is the enrollment-minus-withdrawal arithmetic, not a verified analysis denominator**. Weekly allocation counts still total 25 each week: HD/LD/PLA 8/8/9, 7/10/8, and 10/7/8. Their reconciliation with withdrawals is unresolved.

A third-party researcher used Research Randomizer two weeks before the experiment; conditions were described as randomized and counterbalanced. Each participant was assigned one condition weekly for three weeks, with a main session and a 24-hour assessment per condition. Packages remained coded until independent analyses were complete. Blinding success, exact sequence counts for completers, explicit pharmacological washout verification and a treatment-by-period/previous-treatment carryover model are not supplied. Weekly scheduling must not be recast as demonstrated absence of carryover.

## Exercise, hierarchy and contrasts

The abstract prints **“3% × 8 × 70% 1RM.”** Both Methods sections and native Figure 1 instead specify **three sets of eight squat repetitions at 70% 1RM**, with two minutes between sets. That source-supported Methods protocol is retained; the abstract wording is recorded as a discrepancy. Load was set daily from warm-up performance and individual load–velocity relationships. The fixed repetitions/relative-intensity design is distinct from a repetitions-to-failure test; total absolute volume was not assumed identical.

Testing occurred before the squat protocol, immediately afterward and at 24 hours. Immediate tests were CMJ at one minute, two squat repetitions at 60% 1RM at two minutes, and maximal isometric squat at 90° at three minutes. Pre-exercise testing followed the capsule/warm-up schedule; it is not an untreated predose baseline. Infrared jump height, barbell mean propulsive velocity and force-platform isometric force/RFD are actual performance measures. The initial 1RM test set the exercise loads; an acute improvement in maximum 1RM was not demonstrated.

No explicit primary/secondary outcome hierarchy or prospectively locked protocol/SAP was recovered. Power was based on a prior total-volume outcome, with effect size .60, alpha .05, power .80 and at least 21 subjects. The main uses repeated-measures ANOVA, condition × time, Greenhouse–Geisser correction where needed, and Bonferroni pairwise comparisons; total volume uses one-way repeated-measures ANOVA. Significance was P≤.05. A correction across all outcomes, 24 individual repetitions and families of analyses is not established. No treatment-effect confidence intervals are given; reliability ICC intervals are not efficacy intervals.

## Supplement, reporting issues and registry scope

Tables S1–S3 compare measurements grouped by study week. They do not demonstrate an absence of order/carryover effects as printed:

- S1's squat-protocol MDF week/“condition” test prints P=.05, at the main's stated significance threshold; it must not be described as all order effects nonsignificant.
- S2 prints RFDmax interaction **P=1.83** and RFD0–150 condition **P=2.89**; S3 prints CMJ time **P=1.57**. These are invalid P values, not evidence of null results. No decimal repair is made.
- Table 2 and S2 label several isometric RFD time rows “Set 1/2/3” despite the pre/post/24-hour testing design. MIF is labelled percent but printed as .88–.95 in the main; its scale is not silently multiplied or redefined. Several numeric strings have inconsistent separators.
- Main and week-grouped supplement descriptive trajectories differ materially (for example, CMJ is near 40 cm before exercise in the main, whereas week-specific baseline means are 37.82, 36.71 and 35.19 cm). The source supplies neither matching denominators nor participant data to reconcile these presentations. No new pooled estimate or corrected table is constructed.
- CMJ interaction wording differs between abstract, Results and Table 3; the abstract's very small pairwise P values are not substituted for the native interaction P=.01. The main also gives ranges that do not exactly match all printed table entries.
