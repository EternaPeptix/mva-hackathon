# Alpha-tocopherol: partial assay protection without demonstrated BUBR1 restoration

**Retain a limited prevention/co-exposure comparator.** Alpha-tocopherol improved
the MTT readout in amyloid-challenged cells at both 100 and 200 µM, but neither
returned it to the uninjured-control level. The study does not establish recovery
after injury, independently confirmed cell survival, neuronal function or MVA
benefit. [Gugliandolo et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci9080196).

## What was tested

SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells were differentiated with 10 µM retinoic acid for five
days. They remain an established cell-line model, not primary normal neurons.
Alpha-tocopherol was given for 24 hours before a further 24 hours with 10 µM
amyloid-beta 1–42. Untreated, alpha-tocopherol-only, injury-only and combined
conditions were included. There was no delayed-treatment arm after established
injury.

The source does not specify alpha-tocopherol stereochemistry, supplier/catalog,
purity, stock solvent or dispersion formulation. Aβ preparation used DMSO/PBS;
that does not identify the alpha-tocopherol vehicle or supply a matched vehicle
control. Tocopheryl esters, tocotrienols, vitamin E mixtures and retail products
do not inherit this result. The separately reviewed DL-alpha-tocopherol aged-oocyte
experiment is a different source, preparation description and endpoint.

The saved catalog's exact-name layer returns both **TOCOFERSOLAN** and **VITAMIN E** for the alpha-tocopherol alias. This remains an ambiguous name relation, not two verified matches to the tested preparation. It confers no formulation equivalence, approval eligibility or efficacy transfer.

## Actual results

| Comparison | Finding | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| 100 µM plus Aβ versus Aβ alone | Improved MTT signal, P<0.0001 | Still below uninjured control, P<0.001 |
| 200 µM plus Aβ versus Aβ alone | Improved MTT signal, P<0.001 | Still below uninjured control, P<0.0001; no additional benefit over 100 µM established |
| 10 or 50 µM plus Aβ | No significant improvement reported versus Aβ alone | This does not prove equivalence or general inactivity |
| Alpha-tocopherol alone, 10–200 µM | No reduction in the MTT readout reported | Assay-specific observation, not comprehensive safety |
| iNOS/Nrf2 immunocytochemistry | Representative staining shifts toward the control pattern | Qualitative markers, not direct ROS, neuronal function or quantified survival |

MTT was the only quantified viability assay. A higher signal can reflect cell
survival and/or altered metabolism. No orthogonal count, membrane-integrity or
death assay, cell-free interference control, or clearly documented washout before
MTT was supplied. **Missing controls do not prove assay interference.** They limit
the interpretation of a real measured assay improvement.

MTT was repeated in triplicate, with ANOVA/Bonferroni and mean ± SD reported;
technical versus independent-culture replication is not explicit. RNA preparation
was also described as triplicate, without a per-library sample map or raw-data
accession in the retrieved report. Immunocytochemistry used three experiments
with duplicate coverslips, not six independent biological experiments.

## BUB1B direction and mechanistic boundaries

RNA sequencing compares Aβ alone with Aβ plus **100 µM alpha-tocopherol**.
Table 3 lists BUB1B expression **192.20 → 162.39**, with log₂ change **−0.24**.
The paper includes this among genes selected at q<0.05, but supplies neither an
individual BUB1B q/P value nor a clearly specified expression unit or gene-level
uncertainty. The values establish the reported downward RNA direction without
assuming a unit. No uninjured RNA arm demonstrates return to a normal baseline.

There is no BUBR1 protein, localization, checkpoint-function or chromosome-fidelity
measurement. Lower cell-cycle RNA does not establish either protein restoration
or harm to BUB1B-deficient tissue. Likewise, pathway transcripts do not establish
APP processing, amyloid clearance, respiratory function, autophagic flux or actual
cell-cycle inhibition. The source's interpretation diagrams are not additional
functional experiments.

The result is weaker evidence for our muscle-focused proposal than a demonstrated
functional or protein-restoration outcome. It remains useful as a protection
hypothesis requiring orthogonal survival and tissue-function assays. Neither
availability nor nutritional familiarity establishes MVA efficacy or a suitable
human regimen; the provisional niacin/apitegromab comparison remains unproven in
MVA as well.
