# EZH2 and BUBR1: stabilization is not reproduced by enzyme inhibitors

**23 September 2026.** [Qu et al., Nucleic Acids Research 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkw463)
(PMID 27226494, PMC5027481) supplies a direct BUBR1-regulation mechanism,
but no pharmacological restoration candidate. GSK343 and DZNep are admitted as
distinct mechanism probes, not promoted over niacin or apitegromab.

The complete main text, seven main captions, supplementary S1–S7 captions and
MIQE text were read. Main figures 4 and 5 and supplementary PDF pages 3–4
(S5–S7) were visually inspected. Other images, MIQE embedded plots and raw data
were not independently analyzed. This is source qualification, not replication
or a new independent review.

## What each intervention establishes

| Intervention | Observed result | Interpretation for the compound search |
|---|---|---|
| **Genetic EZH2 depletion** | Lower BubR1 protein, chromosome misalignment, impaired kinetochore–microtubule attachments and more aneuploid mouse oocytes. | Supports a biological link; not a drug experiment and not this child's BUB1B genotype. |
| **Genetic EZH2 overexpression** | Higher BubR1 protein, but also impaired maturation, chromosome misalignment and more aneuploidy. | More protein did not automatically produce better division in this context. This does not prove that restoring deficient BUBR1 in MVA would be harmful. |
| **EZH2 domain and interaction experiments** | The EZH2 SET-containing fragment interacts with BubR1 and raises its abundance; catalytic-inactive EZH2 overexpression still disrupts meiosis. EZH2, BubR1 and PCAF associate in immunoprecipitation experiments. | A noncatalytic interaction/scaffold mechanism is supported. Inhibiting methyltransferase activity is not equivalent to supplying that interaction. |
| **GSK343** | 1 µM for 14 h, compared with DMSO: reduced H3K27me3; no obvious reduction of EZH2 protein, and no significant change in the displayed polar-body extrusion assay. | Target-pathway perturbation without demonstrated drug-mediated BUBR1 restoration. The experiment starts with ordinary mouse oocytes, not a deficient model rescued by treatment. |
| **DZNep** | 5 µM for 14 h, compared with DMSO: reduced H3K27me3 and also H3K9me2; no obvious EZH2 loss or significant polar-body extrusion change under these conditions. | A distinct methylation perturbation, not interchangeable with GSK343 or proof of selective EZH2 inhibition. No drug-specific BubR1 or mutant-rescue result is supplied. |

The drug Figure 4 panels show three independent experiments, but do not supply
the number of oocytes in each drug group or an equivalence margin. A
nonsignificant difference is not proof of normal meiosis under all exposures.
The source names Millipore for DZNep and Selleck for GSK343; exact product IDs,
batch identity and current stock were not verified.

## Two caveats absent from the abstract-level interpretation

**Rare abnormal polar bodies after GSK343.** Supplement S7 shows giant, small
and double polar bodies after the stated 14-hour treatment. The main text
describes about one or two abnormal oocytes among 50 and says their chromosome
counts were incorrect, but those chromosome-spread data are not shown. It also
reports no significant overall treatment-associated aneuploidy change without
a displayed drug karyotype comparison. Retain this low-frequency observation
and its reporting limit; do not replace it with either “safe” or “causes MVA.”

**The claimed functional rescue is less secure than the protein result.**
Figure 5C's re-expression experiment shows restored BubR1 protein. However, its
polar-body timing curves compare control, depletion and rescue and report no
significant differences among them. That panel does not itself demonstrate a
significant reversal of a depletion defect. The earlier Figure 2B timing
comparison and the authors' rescue interpretation do not remove this limitation.

## Competitive decision

The useful finding is the separation of **EZH2 abundance/scaffolding** from
**methyltransferase inhibition**. Neither tested inhibitor reproduces the
genetic restoration. This supplies no current basis for replacing the
restoration or muscle-compensation leads, and no numerical estimate of efficacy.

A future small molecule that stabilizes the productive EZH2–BUBR1 interaction
would be a new hypothesis, not an efficacy property of GSK343 or DZNep. It would
need to improve faithful divisions in a deficient model without excessive
checkpoint arrest or abnormal chromosome segregation. This complements the
same abundance-plus-function requirement already imposed on the NAD/SIRT2
route; it does not establish which route will succeed in MVA.
