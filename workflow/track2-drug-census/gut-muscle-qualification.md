# COPD gut–muscle interventions: butyrate and Vivomix

Both exact trials report changes in measured strength or physical performance, so they merit retention as preparation-specific compensation research signals. The available analyses do **not establish a treatment-versus-placebo functional benefit**: the probiotic scientific main and the accessible butyrate primary sections use paired before/after comparisons within each arm. A significant change in one arm and no significant change in the other is not a test of the difference between arms. This is an unresolved causal contrast, not proof that the true treatment effect is zero.

Neither trial establishes MVA efficacy, BUBR1 restoration, or superiority to the provisional niacin/apitegromab research nominations. Lack of BUBR1 mediation does not disqualify a compensation strategy; it limits restoration claims. Source depth differs substantially below.

## Butyrate: PMID 38135194

[Qaisar et al., Respiratory Medicine 222, 107510](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2023.107510), online December 2023 / issue February 2024. The original abstract was read in the frozen discovery record. Additional evidence comes from **incomplete, search-indexed primary journal sections**, not a complete downloaded main or native figures.

| Dimension | Source-supported finding and limit |
|---|---|
| Population and denominator | Men aged 61–73 with COPD, recruited at Gomal Medical College, Pakistan. The indexed methods describe stable, ambulatory COPD without major comorbidity. Of 168 screened, 37 were excluded before randomization; 67 placebo and 64 butyrate were randomized. Eight placebo and 12 butyrate participants were subsequently excluded, leaving 59 and 52. Reasons for these post-randomization exclusions and endpoint-specific completeness were not recovered. |
| Preparation and comparator | The primary methods identify sodium butyrate, commercially purchased as BODYBIO 300 mg capsules through Whizz, Abu Dhabi; one capsule daily for 12 weeks. Placebo comparison and double blinding are reported. Release technology, excipients, batch composition, placebo contents/calories and masking details remain unverified. The study name does not establish equivalence to current retail products. |
| Measured function | HGS, gait speed, and SPPB balance/walking components improved within the butyrate arm. Chair-rise score did not improve; it declined in placebo and remained unchanged with butyrate. That pattern does not establish prevention of decline without a between-arm contrast. |
| Nulls | The retrieved results report no change in skeletal mass index, appendicular mass, fat mass, BMI, daily steps, phase angle or sarcopenia proportions. Plasma 8-isoprostane was unchanged. Total/HDL cholesterol and reported medication use were unchanged. |
| Other measured changes | Zonulin, LBP, CAF22, CRP and CK decreased within the butyrate arm; placebo CAF22 increased. FEV1 and HbA1c changes are reported separately from muscle function. Biomarker associations with function are not mediation evidence. |
| Statistical interpretation | Section 2.5 specifies paired within-arm t-tests and Pearson associations at nominal P<.05; the recovered Figure 2/3 and Table 1 captions also identify comparisons with baseline. No treatment-by-time, change-score, ANCOVA, confidence interval or numerical between-arm functional effect was recovered. No new estimates were calculated from graphs or separate stars. |
| Hierarchy, registration and harms | A prospectively specified endpoint hierarchy, analysis plan, registration and multiplicity correction were not verified. A discussion statement says participants had no malabsorption or gastrointestinal upset; this does not substitute for a systematic adverse-event table or explain attrition. Plasma butyrate was not measured; diet and medication effects were not investigated. |
| Disclosure/access | Europe PMC and Crossref identify University of Sharjah award 22010901121. The complete primary funding/COI text was not obtained. Live publisher/OpenAlex metadata now marks an open archive/bronze copy, unlike the older discovery record’s subscription flag; actual main/PDF access still failed. No complete native figure, full table, supplement or registry reading credit is claimed. |

The title’s NMJ-repair language exceeds the measurements recovered: circulating CAF22 and associations do not directly demonstrate repaired NMJ anatomy, transmission, or causal mediation. Likewise, LBP is a measured marker, not a direct bacterial count or an established diagnosis of sepsis. Unmeasured plasma butyrate leaves the systemic exposure bridge open. These limits preserve the functional observations without converting them into a validated intervention effect.

## Vivomix: PMID 35567889

[Karim et al., Archives of Gerontology and Geriatrics 102, 104721](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.archger.2022.104721), 2022. The complete scientific main was read from a publicly linked seven-page publisher-layout PDF; native Figures 1–3 and Tables 1–2 were inspected. The PDF was hosted by a commercial research page: that access provenance supplies no evidence of equivalence to the host’s products.

| Dimension | Source-supported finding and limit |
|---|---|
| Population and denominator | Stable COPD male outpatients, 63–73 years, at two Pakistani tertiary-care centers. Figure 1: 137 assessed, 33 excluded, 104 randomized (53 placebo/51 probiotic). Four probiotic participants were excluded for poor compliance; 53/47 were analyzed. This is not an analysis of all randomized participants. Myopathy, arthritis, digestive disorders, malignancy, liver disease, recent hospitalization and recent probiotics/antibiotics were exclusions. |
| Preparation | Vivomix 112 billion live bacteria per capsule, once daily for 16 weeks; home storage at 4°C. The paper lists DSM 24731, 24736, 24732, 24737, 24735, 24730, 24733 and 24734; named taxa and incomplete species assignments are preserved verbatim as data in the JSON. Maltose and silicon dioxide are stated. No per-strain proportions, batch identity, independently assayed viability or release profile are provided. |
| Comparator and cointerventions | Similar capsules containing unspecified inactive agents; neither participant nor investigator knew capsule contents. Block randomization used computer-generated numbers, with allocator and outcome investigators separated. Placebo calorie/excipient matching cannot be verified. No structured training cointervention is described; diet was not measured. Daily steps were tabulated without a significant-change mark. |
| Endpoint hierarchy | The main names HGS and SPPB as primary outcomes; biomarker associations with HGS/SPPB are secondary. A target sample size of 46 and 5% dropout allowance are stated without a full reproducible power calculation. Registration is reported as GMC clinical trial unit, GMC-CREC-00263, but no dated public protocol/registry record was recovered. Article labeling is not verified prospective specification. |
| Functional positives and test | Figure 3 shows within-probiotic changes in HGS, gait speed and all three SPPB components: balance, 4-m walk and five chair stands. Figure 3’s caption defines nominal P<.05 versus the same arm’s baseline using paired t-tests. No between-arm functional effect estimate, CI, change comparison or interaction is reported in the inspected main. Graphs were inspected but not digitized. |
| Nulls and other outcomes | ASMI and both EWGSOP2/SPPB-defined sarcopenia proportions were unchanged. Creatine kinase was nonsignificant (P=.061). Table 1 shows no significant-change marks for BMI, appendicular mass, fat mass or steps. FEV1 increased while phase angle decreased within the probiotic arm; these are not muscle-function substitutes. |
| Biomarkers/mechanism | Zonulin, claudin-3, CAF22, CRP and 8-isoprostane changes and biomarker/function associations are reported. The authors explicitly acknowledge no direct evidence of NMJ disruption or gut permeability in this study, and did not measure microbiome composition or verify colonization. Neither causal mediation nor NMJ repair follows from the correlations. |
| Multiplicity, harms and disclosure | Nominal .05 testing spans several functional components, biomarkers and many associations; no familywise/FDR adjustment or ordered testing plan is described. Seven probiotic patients reported flatulence/bloating resolving in 1–6 days; systematic serious-event ascertainment is not documented. University of Sharjah awards 20010190177 and 1901090168; authors report no financial/personal conflicts. |

Several source inconsistencies matter for reproducibility. Methods place claudin-3 in **urine**, while abstract/results/figure describe plasma. The printed diagnostic gait threshold is **≤8 m/s**, not silently repaired here to 0.8. The Figure 2A placebo zonulin distribution shifts downward visually, while the results text says it rose; no direction is adjudicated from a numerical reanalysis. Figures use box-like distributions but captions say mean±SD without defining box/whisker components. Table 2 labels association coefficients without clearly distinguishing r from r². These are reporting uncertainties, not grounds to invent corrected data.

PMID [36894846](https://doi.org/10.1007/s15006-023-2478-y), Joachim Bargon’s German comment “Helfen Probiotika gegen Muskelschwund?”, is linked commentary metadata with no abstract recovered. Its full text was not read. It contributes neither an independent trial nor confirmation/refutation of efficacy; no criticism is attributed to it.

## Comparative research interpretation and scope

Under the same function, exposure, durability/renewal and fidelity standards used for the provisional comparators, these records offer measured human functional signals in a different disease and population, with material statistical and preparation limits. Neither establishes lasting benefit after withdrawal, muscle renewal, chromosome-segregation fidelity or a BUB1B-variant effect. The butyrate paper did not measure circulating exposure; the probiotic paper did not establish colonization. Those missing measurements are unknowns, not demonstrated failures. The two studies share investigators and overlapping institutional context; they are different interventions, not independent replication or evidence of their combination.

A separate dietary rabbit study of Aladdin sodium butyrate reports muscle-fiber morphology and a cecal cell-cycle transcript cluster that includes BUB1B; it does not validate the BODYBIO capsule or establish a BUB1B effect. See the [separate source qualification](sodium-butyrate-rabbit-qualification.md).

Retain both as bounded research leads requiring resolution of the actual randomized between-arm contrast and preparation/exposure details before competitive upgrading. Do not infer equivalence among sodium butyrate, phenylbutyrate, hydroxybutyrate, HMB, generic probiotics or current retail formulations.
