# Nicotinamide riboside: a human walking signal omitted from the earlier comparison

**Research decision:** upgrade NR from a mechanism-only reserve to a substantive, preliminary human-function comparator. The NICE randomized trial supplies a treatment-versus-placebo walking result. It does not establish BUBR1 restoration, muscle NAD target engagement in that population, or benefit in MVA. It warrants a fair comparison with niacin rather than dismissal based only on an earlier negative metabolic trial. No clinical treatment or nomination change follows from this appraisal alone.

Primary source: [McDermott et al., NICE, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49092-5), PMID 38871717 / PMC11176364. Its [publisher correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51289-7), PMID 39134546, restores the trial-registration identifier to the abstract; it does not change the reported efficacy results. The [current trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03743636) and published supplement were also inspected at the scopes below.

## What was actually tested

NICE randomized 90 older adults with lower-extremity peripheral artery disease in Chicago: 28 to NR, 33 to NR plus resveratrol, and 29 to placebo. Mean age was 71.2 years; 46.7% were female. Walking limitations primarily attributable to other conditions, critical limb ischemia, end-stage kidney disease and significant liver impairment were among exclusions. This is an ischemic vascular population, not a BUB1B, childhood or primary myopathy cohort.

The six-month, masked intervention used ChromaDex 250-mg NR capsules, two twice daily (1,000 mg/day). The combination added a separate ReserveAge capsule containing 125 mg/day of 98%-pure trans-resveratrol. Matching placebos gave each participant five daily pills. The current registry also names Niagen. The paper's administered NR preparation must not silently become niacin, NMN, nicotinamide, NR plus pterostilbene, or every marketed NR formulation. No independent chemical-composition or salt-equivalence verification was performed here.

An adherence-enriching run-in required at least 70% of placebo pills over 14 days. Eight of 104 starting run-in failed it. At least 75% intervention adherence was later achieved by 21/28 NR, 17/33 combination and 22/29 placebo participants. Higher combination gastrointestinal-event rates may have contributed to adherence, but a causal mediation analysis was not established.

## The primary result is positive under the stated pilot rule

The publication and supplied protocol define **one-sided P < 0.10**, one-sided 90% confidence intervals, two primary comparisons and no multiplicity correction. Either positive primary comparison qualifies the trial as positive under the supplied protocol. This is a permissive signal-seeking design; it is not a conventional two-sided 5% confirmatory result. Equally, calling its declared primary result simply negative would misstate the study's rule.

The inspected protocol is version 15, dated 6 March 2023, before the publication's final follow-up date but long after enrollment began. It contains the analysis rule and missing-at-random MMRM specification. Original registry versions and earlier protocol/SAP versions were not audited. Accordingly, prespecification is supported by the supplied document and author report, not independently established from a pre-enrollment archive. The current registry labels the trial Phase 3, whereas the article describes Phase II; this metadata difference is preserved rather than resolved by assumption.

The 6-month effect compares adjusted changes of approximately +7.0 m for NR and −10.6 m for placebo. It is not a 17.6-m within-NR gain. The source regards roughly 8 m as a small and 20 m as a large clinically important difference **in PAD**. Those thresholds must not be transferred to a child with MVA.

The model used baseline, 3-month and 6-month observations and adjusted for baseline walking distance, age, sex and race. It included participants with missing visits under a missing-at-random assumption. Eighty-nine participants have the primary six-month measurement (28/33/28 by NR/combination/placebo). Native Figure 1 labels all 29 placebo participants as completing six-month follow-up testing but only 28 as completing the primary outcome; registry overall completion similarly lists all 90. Use endpoint-specific denominators rather than treating these labels as identical.

One placebo participant with unusually low baseline walking distance lacked the final primary measurement. The peer-review response explains why the displayed raw baseline/follow-up means do not subtract to the adjusted change: among the 28 placebo participants with final measurements, the unadjusted change was −8.6 m, closer to the adjusted −10.6 m. This is a documented denominator/model issue, not by itself proof that the treatment estimate is erroneous. No independent participant-level reanalysis or missing-not-at-random sensitivity analysis was completed here.

## Secondary outcomes constrain the mechanism claim

NR's peak treadmill-time contrast was +2.1 minutes, P=.08 under the same one-sided rule. Only 10 NR, eight combination and eight placebo participants had paired treadmill outcomes because of pandemic restrictions. This small subset cannot bear the same weight as the main walking endpoint.

NR did not improve the reported Walking Impairment Questionnaire distance score, free-living activity measures, speed or stair scores, or SF-36 physical functioning. These nulls make a broad assertion of improved daily function unjustified. Failure to meet the test criterion is not proof of no possible effect.

Muscle-biopsy measurements were sparse: NAD data in seven NR, five combination and five placebo participants; satellite-cell/fiber data in seven, four and five respectively. No muscle NAD or type-I-fiber advantage was demonstrated. The NR satellite-cell contrast was +11.14 cells/100 fibers, P=.060 under the pilot rule. It is exploratory, based on seven versus five participants, and is not a direct assay of renewal, force production or chromosome fidelity. Absolute satellite-cell counts need not increase to produce a favorable relative contrast when placebo declines.

Tables 4/5 repeat several contrast estimates appearing in the NR-alone comparisons despite describing pooled NR groups, with an additional adjustment for resveratrol. These are overlapping analyses, not independent confirmation. No attempt was made to resolve that model parameterization from the participant data. The combined-group six-month walking comparison itself was nonsignificant under the trial rule (+14.05 m, P=.12).

The greater walking estimates among participants taking at least 75% of pills (+31.0 m NR and +26.9 m combination vs placebo) are post-hoc adherence-selected comparisons. They cannot replace the assigned-group primary result or establish that adherence caused the larger response.

No BUB1B/BUBR1 abundance, mutant stability, SIRT2-mediated rescue, spindle-checkpoint function, chromosome missegregation or MVA phenotype was measured in this trial. Its NAD rationale is not a measured BUBR1 mechanism. The favorable walking contrast remains real study evidence even though that mechanism bridge is open.

## Harms and availability

Supplementary Table 5 reports at least one adverse event in 18/28 NR, 24/33 combination and 21/29 placebo participants. Diarrhea was reported by 11/28, 18/33 and 8/29; nausea/vomiting by 4/28, 12/33 and 7/29. These are descriptive counts, not a demonstrated between-arm safety equivalence. A placebo participant developed chest pain during a walk test, was hospitalized and later underwent coronary bypass; the paper reports no other serious event related to participation.

Six months in selected older adults does not establish long-term tumor-surveillance or pediatric safety. The publication describes NR as an over-the-counter supplement, which makes it relevant to the expanded common-compound search. A trial registry's intervention type of “drug,” an IND, or supplement availability does not establish an approved MVA medication or product-level submission eligibility.

## Competitive decision

| Route | Evidence retained | Consequence for our comparison |
|---|---|---|
| NR alone | Randomized, masked, assigned-group walking signal in PAD, with permissive inference and no demonstrated muscle NAD/BUBR1 response | Upgrade to a substantive human-function comparator. It cannot fairly remain characterized solely by the earlier obese-men metabolic null. |
| NR plus trans-resveratrol | Six-month primary null, three-month signal and no demonstrated additive benefit over NR | A distinct tested combination with no basis here for preference over NR alone. Do not assign its effects to resveratrol alone. |
| Niacin | Existing separate deficiency-context muscle NAD engagement and open-label functional observations | Retains a distinct target-engagement rationale. NICE offers stronger randomized functional design for NR, but does not establish NR's MVA superiority; niacin likewise has no demonstrated comparative MVA superiority. |
| Apitegromab | Existing pediatric randomized motor evidence in a different neuromuscular disease | Still addresses a different compensation axis. The NICE study neither reproduces that population/outcome nor compares the drugs head to head. |

The earlier tissue-engagement observation also remains: [Elhassan 2019, PMID 31412242](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6702140/) tested 1 g/day NR chloride in 12 older men for 21 days in a masked crossover trial. The abstract and selected Results report higher muscle NAAD and clearance metabolites, but no rise in measured muscle NAD itself (210 versus 197 pmol/mg, P=.22) or mitochondrial-bioenergetic improvement. Selected Results also report no peak or body-weight-adjusted hand-grip advantage after those 21 days (P=.96 for each); this short, different-population functional null does not negate NICE. This is evidence of altered muscle NAD metabolism, not demonstrated BUBR1 rescue or restored force. Only the abstract and selected Results were read anew here; no full requalification is claimed.

The existing NR appraisal's obese, insulin-resistant male trial ([PMID 31710095](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31710095/)) remains relevant counterevidence to universal muscle-metabolic benefit. Its full abstract was re-read in this pass; detailed main methods, figures and supplements were not. Its different population and 2,000-mg/day regimen cannot nullify the PAD trial's measured 1,000-mg/day walking contrast. The appropriate outcome is a better-balanced reserve assessment, not a claim that any NAD precursor is established therapy.

To challenge the submission nominees, NR would still need exposure-qualified useful function in affected/corrected models, an explanation of any endogenous BUBR1/checkpoint effect, and preserved productive lineages and surveillance. These requirements also apply to the nominees. A favorable relative cell fraction without absolute functional output would not satisfy them.
