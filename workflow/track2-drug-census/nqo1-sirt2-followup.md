# NAD support can restore mitotic completion without proving BUBR1 rescue

**21 September 2026, America/Phoenix.** This follow-up resolves the intervention
behind the NQO1/SIRT2 question in the [previous abstract pass](next576-findings.md).
It strengthens NMN as a pathway reference and adds dicoumarol as an adverse
mechanistic comparator. Neither result demonstrates treatment of MVA.

## The administered agents and the measured outcomes

[Kang 2018, PMID30114477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30114477/)
reports **0.5 mM NMN** improving completion of mitosis in NQO1-depleted MCF-7
breast-cancer cells, assessed by live imaging (Figure 4F). This is a functional
cell-division observation, beyond a change in protein abundance. Separately,
**0.5 mM NAD+** reversed a tubulin-acetylation readout (Figure 3H).
**Dicoumarol** impaired mitotic progression and sensitized cancer cells to
paclitaxel; its tubulin-acetylation experiment used 100 micromolar and a separate
concentration series. The rescue and inhibition experiments must not be merged.

NQO1 knockdown reduced the NAD+/NADH ratio and SIRT2 activity. Genetic experiments
support SIRT2-dependent deacetylation, while APC/C-complex experiments connect
this pathway to mitotic exit. The NMN rescue experiment itself does not establish
BUBR1 mediation or the chromosome complement of the resulting daughters.

## Competitive decision and experimental consequence

**NMN remains a substantive mechanistic reference.** It now has an additional
cell-division result alongside the previously reviewed BUBR1-abundance evidence.
The actual administered precursor was NMN, not nicotinic acid, and the depleted
protein was NQO1, not BUBR1. This experiment cannot supply niacin efficacy,
actual-allele rescue, muscle force, a clinically reachable exposure or a
product-specific approved-drug status. It does not displace the niacin restoration
nomination or apitegromab compensation comparator on current evidence.

**Dicoumarol is not elected for restoration.** The observed direction opposes
the NAD/SIRT2 support hypothesis in this model. Cancer-cell killing is a different
objective from preserving useful muscle. This is context-specific counterevidence,
not a claim that all exposures or every clinical use of dicoumarol are harmful.

The useful design lesson is that SIRT2 can affect APC/C and mitotic exit as well
as BUBR1 stability. Therefore, an NMN or niacin response must be checked for
BUBR1-dependent function, correct segregation and daughter-cell output.
Quicker cytokinesis or SIRT2 dependence alone is insufficient. The paper's
anti-mitotic response experiments motivate checking abnormal-clone survival;
they do not show that niacin or NMN causes cancer in MVA.

## Reading depth and remaining uncertainty

The complete main article text and all six main figure legends were read through
the official [NCBI BioC service](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/bionlp/RESTful/pmcoa.cgi/BioC_json/PMC6170003/unicode).
The main text reports at least three repetitions generally, but an exact
Figure 4F cell denominator, NMN exposure duration, comparison-specific test and
effect estimate remain unverified. No numerical rescue percentage is claimed.
Figure pixels, supplementary files and movies were not reviewed. Main figures
and supplement qualification remain open; the live-imaging result is the authors'
reported result, not our remeasurement. No randomization or blinding information
was established for this experiment.

PMC and publisher HTML access was unavailable in this session; the BioC service
provided lawful full text. Raw responses and retrieval records remain outside
Git. BioC lists an author manuscript and text-mining/fair-use terms, not a blanket
licence for republishing its contents. This file contains original assessment.

This primary follow-up does not add an abstract record to the reading count.
