# Cocoa study preparations and physical function

Source qualification date: 2026-09-27. This comparison preserves two separate appraisals: **new-cocoa-pad-beverage**, with the sole descriptive alias **COCOA-PAD flavanol-rich cocoa beverage**, and **new-cosmos-cocoa-extract**, with the sole descriptive alias **COSMOS Mars Edge cocoa-extract capsules**.

COCOA-PAD provides a randomized walking benefit at 2.5 hours after the last study drink, alongside timing-dependent findings, a post-recruitment pilot threshold, missingness and harms limits. COSMOS provides overall functional nulls in a different preparation and population, while retaining a small tandem-stand benefit and opposing subgroup signals. These results are not pooled and do not identify interchangeable products. Neither isolated epicatechin, generic cocoa, chocolate nor flavanols inherit either study's efficacy. Manufacturer/supplier attribution does not establish complete composition or a currently marketed product.

The two source qualifications received an independent **ACCEPT** at their stated reading depths. The complete qualified study narratives follow, with administrative and heading edits logged outside the portable files. Primary readers and the independent reviewer had different native-source scopes, detailed below; this synthesis adds no fresh original-source or native-pixel reading credit. No BUBR1 restoration, actual-allele rescue, MVA benefit or superiority over provisional nominees is established.

## COCOA-PAD: preparation-specific walking benefit with pilot and timing limits

**Retain the tested beverage as a substantive controlled mobility-compensation signal.** In 44 older adults with peripheral artery disease (PAD), six months of the Hershey-manufactured study beverage improved the adjusted six-minute-walk contrast measured 2.5 hours after the last drink. The 24-hour primary contrast was not significant under the study's own pilot rule in the observed-outcome analysis, and both 48-hour treadmill outcomes were null. This is walking evidence, not isolated muscle-force, BUBR1-restoration or MVA-efficacy evidence. The result belongs to this cocoa preparation, not purified epicatechin, every cocoa food, a retail product, or the separately assessed ECGU mixture.

The source is [McDermott et al., PMID 32078436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32078436/), DOI [10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.119.315600](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.119.315600), Circulation Research 126:589–599 (2020). The primary source qualification covered the complete accessible [PMC author-manuscript main](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7141749/), all five HTML tables and both main figure captions, native Figures 1 and 2, relevant sections of the [41-page protocol/SAP v15](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/87/NCT02876887/Prot_SAP_000.pdf), and the substantive design, results and harms fields of the [current trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02876887). Supplement and native table pixels remain unverified. Exact reading and failed-access scope are retained separately.

### What was administered and to whom

The Hershey Company manufactured and supplied the flavanol-rich cocoa and matching placebo packets. The paper specifies **15 g cocoa and 75 mg epicatechin daily**, delivered in three beverages for six months; participants mixed packet contents with water or milk. Placebo lacked cocoa/flavanols and was described as similar in appearance and taste. Protocol v15 describes approximately 180 calories per day in each arm and nearly identical nutritional content. The inaccessible Online Table I prevents verification of the complete ingredient/macronutrient, caffeine/theobromine and total-flavanol composition. Manufacturer attribution does not establish equivalence to a currently sold Hershey product. A suitable descriptive source label is “COCOA-PAD flavanol-rich cocoa beverage”; generic cocoa, chocolate and epicatechin aliases would overstate identity.

The single-center double-blind trial randomized **23 to cocoa and 21 to placebo**. Mean age was 72.3 ± 7.1 years and ABI 0.66 ± 0.15; 29/44 were men. Eligibility required age at least 60 and PAD, with important exclusions including critical limb ischemia, wheelchair or walking-aid dependence, non-PAD walking limitation, recent/planned major vascular procedures and very low/high baseline walking distance. This was not an inherited-muscle-disease or healthy population. Randomization used permuted blocks stratified by baseline walk distance; protocol v15 specifies block sizes four and six. Assessors and participants were masked. Baseline BMI, smoking and race were imbalanced, and the reported estimates adjust for these factors. Current smoking was 11/23 versus 3/21.

Forty participants completed at least one follow-up measure, but that is not the denominator for either primary endpoint. Native Figure 1 shows 19 cocoa and 21 placebo completing follow-up; the four cocoa participants without primary/secondary outcome testing comprised one death, one refusal and two questionnaire-only participants. Excluding two cocoa participants who underwent revascularization yielded +42.3 m (P=.006) at 2.5 hours and +25.2 m (P=.058) at 24 hours. Adjusting for adherence yielded +46.5 m (P=.002) and +18.9 m (P=.114). Thus the 24-hour conclusion is analysis-dependent. Seven-day free-living activity was also nonsignificant (15/19; P=.30). The current registry reports pooled endpoint-family denominators of 19/21 for walking and 18/19 for treadmill; these do not replace the paper's timepoint-specific 17/20, 19/20 and maximal-treadmill 18/20 denominators.

### Protocol and statistical limits

The paper used one-sided P<.10 as a pilot threshold and numerous nominal outcome tests without demonstrated multiplicity control. Protocol v15, dated 24 May 2018, contains an explicit section 11.3 amendment: investigators selected the one-sided .10 rule **after recruitment completed**, while stating that this was before reviewing results. Earlier sections still describe two-sided 95% intervals. The amendment was verified on native protocol page 22. Do not call the relaxed threshold fixed before enrollment. The document was uploaded in April 2020; original protocol versions, registry history and evidence independently verifying when results were first examined were not obtained. The current record shows registration posted August 2016 before the January 2017 start, but it cannot establish the original wording of every endpoint.

The main reports a 70% power calculation for roughly a 30 m difference at the one-sided .10 level. The earlier protocol calculation instead emphasizes interval precision. Protocol missing-data text anticipates low attrition and an adherence sensitivity; it does not document the main's detailed multiple-imputation procedure. Protocol v15 places perfusion and several regeneration measures among secondary aims, whereas the main calls these exploratory; planned TFAM is not reported in the reviewed main. These differences limit confirmation without erasing the randomized 2.5-hour signal. Baseline adjustment does not ensure that all imbalances or small-sample uncertainty are removed.

The 2.5-hour endpoint combines six months of exposure with a recent drink. It does not isolate a chronic effect or prove an acute-by-chronic interaction. The 24-hour estimate, differences in placebo performance between consecutive tests, possible learning/fatigue and the 48-hour treadmill nulls prevent a clean claim of durable post-withdrawal benefit. Timing could explain differences, but that is an interpretation rather than a demonstrated reason for the null results.

### Muscle biology and harms

The biopsy subset was optional and small: 21 yielded baseline tissue, 17 repeat biopsies, generally 10/6 for biochemical assays and 11/6 for histology. Relative to placebo, COX activity had a positive adjusted contrast (+85.5 nmol/min/mg protein, P=.013), largely reflecting a larger placebo decline: adjusted changes were −4.6 versus −90.0. Capillary density and central nuclei also had relative contrasts, rather than broad within-cocoa restoration. MRI perfusion was nominally positive at the pilot threshold (13/13, P=.098; analysis based on log-transformed data). Myostatin, follistatin, PGC1α, citrate synthase, Pax7+ cells, embryonic fibers and oxidative-stress measures were nonsignificant. The author-manuscript abstract gives central-nuclei P=.024, whereas Table 5 and the final PubMed abstract give .033; the table result is retained.

Native Figure 2 shows the same sample before/after lamin A/C staining, verifying that DAPI-positive central structures have nuclear envelopes. It is not a before/after treatment comparison. These assays do not establish mediation of walking, increased isolated force, satellite-cell self-renewal, chromosome fidelity, or BUBR1 restoration.

The main lists **seven serious events in cocoa versus two in placebo**; the registry identifies affected participants as 7/23 versus 2/21. Cocoa events included one fatal myocardial infarction, two lower-extremity vascular-procedure hospitalizations, ischemic stroke, foot infection, urinary difficulty and depression. Placebo events were two hospitalizations for atrial-fibrillation/dyspnea and cough/chest pain. The paper states that none was considered likely related to study participation. The protocol planned blinded DSMB review, but case-level adjudication records were not obtained. That attribution neither establishes causation nor proves safety. Smoking imbalance is a possible explanation raised by the authors, not a verified causal account. Native Figure 1 also labels four cocoa discontinuations as side effects, and the current registry explicitly says information about other adverse events was not collected. Its zero/zero nonserious-event fields are not evidence of zero nonserious events.

Packet-count adherence was 68%/80%, or 80%/87% after excluding seven beverage discontinuers who returned. Plasma epicatechin metabolites and theobromine support exposure, not ingredient-specific efficacy. NIH funded the trial; Hershey supplied drinks and Mars performed metabolite assays. The paper discloses an acknowledged assay contributor employed by Mars and a coauthor's Cardero Therapeutics co-founder/stockholder interest.

This is one cohort already encountered in bounded discovery and qualified more deeply in the original source packet. The source-labelled epicatechin sarcopenia, ECGU, methyl-relative and CINRG-vehicle evidence remains distinct. Deeper reading supplies no new biological replication, isolated-ingredient efficacy, clinical regimen or nomination replacement.

## COSMOS cocoa extract and physical function — Chou 2026

**Preparation-specific overall nulls with retained exploratory positives.** In 603 generally healthy, ambulatory older COSMOS participants, two years of the tested cocoa-extract capsules did not demonstrate an overall benefit on grip strength, walking speed or SPPB. A small tandem-stand benefit and several subgroup contrasts favored cocoa; other grip subgroups favored placebo. These findings should remain visible without converting them into confirmatory overall efficacy, equivalence, or a cocoa-wide failure. [Original report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13089503/) (Chou et al.; PMID 42004609; DOI 10.1093/jbmrpl/ziag041).

### Preparation, design and denominators

The intervention was **two Mars Edge cocoa-extract capsules daily containing 500 mg total flavanols, including 80 mg (−)-epicatechin, approximately 50 mg theobromine and 15 mg caffeine**, versus cocoa-extract placebo. The separately randomized factor was Centrum Silver multivitamin/multimineral (MVM) versus its placebo. The accessible reports do not give a complete capsule/excipient composition or establish whether the cocoa placebo matched methylxanthines. This is whole-extract evidence, not isolated epicatechin evidence or evidence for arbitrary chocolate.

COSMOS was a double-blind, computer-randomized 2 × 2 factorial trial of 21,442 adults. The functional report concerns a clinic subset within 50 miles of Boston, not a separately randomized new trial: **299 cocoa / 304 cocoa placebo**. The four baseline cells were cocoa + MVM 154, cocoa + MVM placebo 145, cocoa placebo + MVM 135, and double placebo 169. Eligibility included women aged at least 65 and men at least 60, no lifetime myocardial infarction or stroke, and no recent cancer apart from nonmelanoma skin cancer. A two-month placebo run-in selected participants reporting at least 75% pill adherence. Personal cocoa and MVM supplements were prohibited during the trial.

The clinic sample's mean age was 69.7 years; 49.3% were women and 96.5% non-Hispanic White. Few had low grip strength or low SPPB, so ceiling effects limit interpretation of performance preservation. **535 returned at two years (88.7%); 68 did not complete the visit.** The native flowchart does not provide arm-specific losses or reasons. Endpoint counts below are pooled observations at each visit, not per-arm or necessarily complete-pair analysis counts. Functional follow-up concluded in November 2020; the parent intervention's median 3.6-year duration is not the duration of these functional comparisons.

### Direct overall functional contrasts

The reported effects are adjusted cocoa-minus-placebo differences in two-year change, averaged across MVM assignment, from repeated-measures models adjusting for age, sex and MVM. **Parentheses contain SE, not confidence intervals. No functional CIs are reported in the inspected tables, and none were calculated for this appraisal.**

### Positive and opposing exploratory findings

- **Tandem stand:** cocoa improved by 0.52 (SE 0.12) seconds and placebo by 0.17 (0.11); direct effect **+0.35 (0.16) seconds**, P=.03 in Supplementary Table 3 (P=.028 in prose), with 579 baseline / 511 year-two observations. This was a sensitivity analysis of a ten-second balance task with ceiling effects. Semi-tandem standing remained nonpositive (−0.04 [0.03] seconds, P=.19).
- **Prior-year fall history and grip:** among those with at least one fall, changes were −7.66 (0.57) kg with cocoa (baseline subgroup n=90) versus −9.49 (0.57) with placebo (n=87), treatment P=.02. In those without a prior fall, changes were −10.34 (0.40) versus −8.71 (0.39), treatment P=.004, favoring placebo. The **fall-history × treatment × time interaction was P<.001**. These are distinct subgroup treatment tests and an interaction test.
- With at least two prior falls, cocoa/placebo changes were −8.68 (1.06) / −11.13 (0.90) kg (baseline n=31/41): treatment P=.08, **interaction P=.01**. The interaction does not make the within-subgroup contrast significant. In those without at least two falls, treatment P=.04 favored placebo. The additional sex analysis within prior fallers reported treatment P=.016 in men, P=.85 in women and interaction P=.016; sex-specific endpoint counts were not supplied.
- **General health and normal gait:** Table 3 reports interaction P=.01 and, in the excellent-health subgroup, cocoa/placebo changes of −0.01 (0.01) / −0.07 (0.01) m/s, treatment P=.003 (baseline n=98/113). This conflicts with the prose claim of no modification by general health; retain the table finding as exploratory rather than erase it. The cocoa contrast in MVM-assigned participants favored SPPB (P=.02), but the cocoa-by-MVM interaction was P=.10 and does not establish differential response to the combination.

All P values were explicitly unadjusted for multiple hypotheses. These are genuine reported signals requiring cautious interpretation, not findings proven spurious merely because exploratory. The source does not establish their reproducibility. Parallel MVM results arise from the same participants and provide no additional independent cohort.

### Hierarchy, missingness and measurement limitations

Physical performance was the **secondary aim of the falls/physical-performance ancillary study**. Within that aim the authors identify grip, walking speed and SPPB as prespecified primary outcomes; the duplicate placement of normal walking speed in the secondary list is unresolved. The [2023 design report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10732156/) supports these functional aims and planned repeated-measures comparisons, but was published after functional follow-up. The current [NCT05232669 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05232669) first posted on February 10, 2022 and presently lists injurious and recurrent falls, not the functional endpoint hierarchy. Author-reported prespecification is retained; prospective public registration of that hierarchy was not independently established. No original protocol, historical registry versions or pre-unblinding analysis plan was read. The design report planned adjustment for recruitment source and key baseline imbalances; the final paper names age, sex and MVM adjustment. Diabetes and alcohol differed at baseline; the effect of the adjustment difference is unknown.

Models used all available observations under a missing-at-random assumption. Missingness reasons and arm-specific endpoint counts remain unreported in the inspected functional report. The claimed statistical power is not an equivalence margin. Grip declined approximately 12 kg in men and 6–7 kg in women in both arms. The authors acknowledge a **possible dynamometer calibration issue** between visits while stating that equipment and protocols were comparable across randomized groups. This raises uncertainty about the absolute grip trajectory and measurement validity; it does not by itself demonstrate that the randomized contrast is wholly invalid. Calibration records and participant-level data were not available for review. The urinary flavanol-metabolite treatment difference was P<.001, supporting exposure rather than functional efficacy.

Table 3 also prints +10.35 kg for the cocoa high-baseline-gVLM grip-change cell against −9.38 kg placebo, despite treatment P=.22 and generally negative grip trajectories. The sign is unresolved and was not silently repaired or used as positive efficacy evidence.

### Safety, interests, cohort relationship and access

The functional subset supplies no adverse-event counts. A targeted read of the linked [parent COSMOS primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9170467/) found more self-reported nausea with cocoa (HR 1.06, 95% CI 1.02–1.11), alongside lower flu-like symptoms and other headaches (both HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.91–0.99) and migraine (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78–0.93). These are parent-cohort safety observations, not clinic-subset counts; the parent's safety supplement was not inspected. The functional report's adverse-effect citation numbered 30 resolves to a dynamometer paper, so the relevant parent report was checked directly rather than relying on that citation.

NIA/NIH supported the ancillary and parent trials. Mars Edge supported parent-trial infrastructure and provided study pills/packaging; Pfizer Consumer Healthcare, now Haleon, provided pills/packaging. Sesso and Manson disclose related support; Sesso also discloses other industry grants/honoraria/travel, and LeBoff unrelated Amgen stock. Data and code are available only on approved request; no individual data were read or reanalysed.

**COCOA-PAD is a different preparation and population:** the original retained primary source describes a Hershey beverage delivering 15 g cocoa and 75 mg epicatechin daily for six months in 44 people with PAD, testing six-minute walking, versus these Mars Edge capsules over two years in a generally healthy clinic subset. The COSMOS source packet reused selected frozen COCOA-PAD context; the separate completed COCOA-PAD qualification is preserved above. Its positive walking result and this study's overall nonpositive findings should not be pooled or treated as contradictory tests of one interchangeable product. COSMOS MVM, fracture and cardiovascular reports reuse the COSMOS parent cohort; they are not independent replications of functional benefit.

The supported inference is preparation- and population-specific: **no demonstrated overall functional benefit, with small or subgroup-specific positive signals and material measurement/analysis limits**. No MVA/BUB1B rescue, clinical superiority, broad cocoa failure, food equivalence, or nomination replacement follows.
