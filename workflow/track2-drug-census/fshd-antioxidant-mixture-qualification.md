# Passerieux 2015: controlled FSHD antioxidant-combination function

**Retain as a credible chronic inherited-muscle-disease compensation challenger.** The randomized comparison reports actual quadriceps strength and endurance gains for the tested combination. The walking endpoint used to power the trial was nonsignificant. These are mixture-level functional results, with no demonstrated MVA efficacy, BUBR1 restoration, or superiority to niacin or apitegromab.

The source is [Passerieux et al., PMID25246239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25246239/), DOI [10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2014.09.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2014.09.014). Complete scientific-main text, all five extracted tables and both figure captions were read from a public author-uploaded ResearchGate copy. Its typeset pages have provisional pagination, so this is an author-hosted proof-text reading, not a verified final publisher PDF or native-pixel review. The official abstract and current trial-registry record were also read.

Times are minutes:seconds. Table 4 reports SD, not confidence intervals. Arithmetic differences of its rounded change means are +1.13/+1.60 kg, +3:10/+3:08, and +4.09 m, respectively; these are descriptive calculations, not independently recovered mixed-model estimates or CIs. No functional contrast CIs are supplied in the accessible main. MVC is strain-gauge output reported in kg. Endurance uses repeated knee extensions against 30% MVC at ten movements/minute; whether follow-up load was fixed to baseline or recalibrated is not explicit. It should not be described as improved endurance at a verified common absolute workload.

The study randomized 53 adults with FSHD, 26 active and 27 placebo, after 54 eligibility assessments. Participants had 4–9 D4Z4 repeats, positive family history, and were 18–60 years old; important comorbidities, smoking, wheelchair confinement and medication/supplement use were excluded. Computer-generated blocks of four and baseline-MVC stratification were reported, with masked participants/personnel and pharmacist-held sealed allocation envelopes. BMI and steppage gait were imbalanced. The intervention lasts 17 weeks; the procedural timeline places functional testing at V3 after 16 weeks, followed by end-of-treatment biomarker sampling at V4.

Methods specify CRID PHARMA capsules containing vitamin C, **dl-alpha tocopheryl-acetate**, zinc gluconate and selenomethionine, compared with matching microcrystalline-cellulose capsules. The main explicitly says **25 mg zinc gluconate provides 3.5 mg elemental zinc**. Its 200 µg *selenomethionine* wording differs from the registry’s 200 µg *selenium in the form of selenomethionine*; elemental selenium delivery remains unresolved. The registry’s zinc wording also differs. Exact reported exposures and capsule timing are retained in JSON solely as study descriptions. Individual ingredients, ordinary foods, and purchasable supplements inherit no result. The sole descriptive appraisal alias for this preparation is “vitamin C, dl-alpha tocopheryl-acetate, zinc gluconate and selenomethionine combination”; this descriptive label does not establish a commercial brand or current retail equivalence.

The abstract/Methods call the five functional scores primary, while power calculation and Discussion emphasize the two-minute walk. Linear mixed-model interaction tests support the direct strength/endurance contrasts, not just within-arm changes. No multiplicity correction is described across those outcomes and numerous secondary/correlation analyses. Although called intention-to-treat, the tables start with 25 active/27 placebo and smaller assay-specific sets; the missing active participant’s reason is unresolved without the native flowchart. Inability to perform the tests accounts for further functional missingness. No imputation is described. The walking comparison remained nonsignificant in the subgroup without steppage gait.

[Current NCT01596803](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01596803) first submission was 5 April 2012 and posting 11 May 2012, after the May 2010 start: prospective registration is unsupported. The current record has broad outcome wording, 54 actual enrollment and no posted results. Its February 2012 primary-completion date also predates the article’s April 2012 recruitment end. Original registry history and protocol/SAP were not obtained.

The main reports no observed/reported side effects and 96%/100% completer adherence, without a detailed adverse-event table or long-term safety evidence. Funding was from Stichting FSHD, Amis FSH Europe and Montpellier University Hospital; authors declared no conflict. CRID PHARMA supplied active/placebo and handled product quality control. Correlations and selected antioxidant biomarker changes do not establish mediation or component-specific causality.

No correction/retraction notice was detected in the retained PubMed/Crossref metadata and bounded exact-DOI notice search as of 27 September 2026. Native figures/table pixels, full publisher PDF, supplements, historical protocol and raw data remain unverified; no known HAL/MDA failures were retried. The exposed conference report earns no extra cohort credit. These limits constrain confirmation while preserving the real controlled functional gains.
