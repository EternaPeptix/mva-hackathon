## L-tyrosine: acknowledge the positives and the failed transfer

The original five-patient report includes four infants and one adolescent, with dietary L-tyrosine at 250–3,000 mg/day. Its abstract describes early salivation/energy improvements in infants and strength/exercise-tolerance improvement in the adolescent. A 2024 NEB compound-heterozygous infant report also describes improved strength after treatment. Original mains were unavailable; quantified controlled benefit, individual preparations and concurrent-treatment attribution are not established. These reported responses earn consideration without becoming randomized evidence. [Ryan 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/0883073807309794), [primary institutional abstract](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/4dca3f8f-f043-4e6a-84b4-b1a141ec0620), [Dalal 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/bcr-2023-259303).

Nguyen's ACTA1(H40Y) study reports higher open-field ambulations after ten days and forearm grip after four weeks of 25 mg/day oral L-tyrosine. Early grip was nonpositive. Native Figure 5 confirms only three treated versus six untreated knock-in females for the later grip result; the ambulation comparison used six treated versus fifteen untreated knock-ins. Methods, Results and the figure caption give inconsistent treatment ages, which prevents treating the schedule as settled. Later Messineo experiments used an additional 2% dietary preparation without demonstrating running/rotarod or maximal-force/fatigue benefit in ACTA1 models. The measured serum (+57%) and quadriceps (+45%) exposure increases belonged to the young Tg ACTA1(D286G) arm, rather than every negative arm. Separate neb−/− nonsense-mutant zebrafish received 10 µM L-tyrosine disodium salt hydrate and showed no swimming or muscle-pathology rescue. These are different preparations/models; the negatives neither erase the earlier signal nor establish universal human ineffectiveness. They materially weaken transfer to another inherited myopathy. [Nguyen 2011](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awr274), [author-hosted original](https://medicalsciences.med.unsw.edu.au/sites/default/files/Nyugen_Kee_Brain.pdf), [Messineo 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6068151/), [Sztal 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5977763/).

## Riboflavin: a metabolic lesion is the explanation, not a generic vitamin effect

In Hellebrekers' SLC25A32 case, oral riboflavin 10 mg three times daily accompanied a reported maximum walking-distance increase from 100 m to at least 500 m after 1.5 months; strength remained unchanged. No standardized walking protocol or controlled comparison was described. A separate 14-year-old with phased SLC25A32 stop/missense variants had reported clinical/biochemical improvement, but the main letter supplies no dose, duration or numerical function estimate. Its yeast genetic rescue is a separate intervention. [Hellebrekers 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5520074/), [Schiff 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4867164/).

Response is not guaranteed even among flavin-related disorders. An infant with homozygous ACAD9(A220V) received riboflavin without clinical improvement; treated patient fibroblasts showed no ACAD9 stabilization or complex-I recovery. This is actual-treatment counterevidence, not an untreated natural-history comparison. None of these sources establishes deficient flavin delivery, BUBR1 restoration or a relevant riboflavin response in BUB1B MVA. [Nouws 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3897792/).

A larger retrospective ACAD9 report describes 70 patients. Its main text supplies physician-rated response for 31: 20 benefited and 11 had no effect; treated fibroblasts improved complex-I-related activity in 9/15 lines. Supplement S3 instead has 70 rows, with 23 labelled beneficial, 11 no effect, 21 untreated and 15 unavailable, which does not reconcile with the main treatment paragraph's 67-patient classification. The supplement includes an A220V infant whose distinctive history matches Nouws; treat that case as overlapping, with an inconsistent reference pointer. Do not calculate a reconciled response rate or add the case to the cohort denominator. The early-onset survival association is observational, and the authors explicitly flag treatment-selection/survival bias. This strengthens a metabolic reserve while retaining variable response, sparse standardized functional measures and uncertain exposure details. [Repp 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6053715/).

## Rutin: functional reproduction, with important assay boundaries

The 2026 primary study used supplier-identified rutin after cyclophosphamide injury in mice, with oral 75/150 mg/kg interventions for four weeks. Litter size and later embryo-development measures favored treatment. The mating experiment used three dams per group; offspring-count Fisher tests did not account for litter clustering. Estradiol and primordial-follicle counts were nonpositive, so this does not establish complete endocrine or primordial-reserve restoration. [Zhao 2026, Methods 2.1–2.3 and Figure 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cell-and-developmental-biology/articles/10.3389/fcell.2026.1872070/full).

Native Figures 8–9 support a 50 µM maturation effect and 25/50 µM spindle-formation effects after chemically induced oocyte injury. The text claims a significant 25 µM maturation effect, while Figure 8D marks only 50 µM. Figure 8A depicts rutin pretreatment and six-hour injury exposure; Methods 2.3 says four hours before washing. Preserve those inconsistencies and the preventive context. No chromosome count or completed faithful-mitosis assay was performed. BUB1B/BUBR1 was not assayed, and marker changes do not establish causal mitophagy flux. Purity, lot and human exposure were not supplied; catalog-looking product codes are labeled as “CAS” in the source. This preparation cannot inherit the separate quercetin-glycoside human results.

## Resolved menaquinone forms and the unresolved neonatal study

The [neonatal K2 qualification](vitamin-k2-neonatal-qualification.md) concerns nucleus accumbens, not cardiac tissue. Its supplement measures Bub1b RNA and normalized BUB1B-labeled immunofluorescence; cycling/cell-composition effects remain unresolved. Fresh lawful source checks did not recover the scientific main/Methods, so subtype, exact regimen and supplier remain unknown. The studies below cannot fill those blanks by family-name similarity.

MK-4's post-ovulatory-aging experiment uses MCE HY-B2156 at 10 mg/kg intraperitoneally, administered with hCG according to Methods; oocytes were collected at 24 hours, versus the fresh 14-hour reference. Figure 5 shows fewer abnormal chromosome alignments, spindles and MTOC patterns and less fragmentation. A caption conflicts with the early-treatment timing. These are fixed MII morphology measures. The separate 5 µM, ten-hour in-vitro arm has oxidative/protein outcomes and does not inherit the parenteral spindle result. Neither arm establishes chromosome number, live faithful segregation or BUBR1 rescue. [Zheng 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrd.70141).

The MK-7 worm study specifies Sungen Bioscience water-soluble powder, purity 99.63%, with lot/catalog unreported. At 5 µM it reports greater body bending, head beating, pharyngeal pumping and lifespan. At 10 µM, head beating, pumping, fecundity and lifespan worsened; native Figure 1C marks the body-bending contrast as nonsignificant. Egg counts at 1/5 µM were nonpositive. Methods describe exposure from egg stage, Results describe ten-day treatment, and native Figure 1A depicts L3 through day 14 followed by experiments; that schedule is not settled. Native supplement S1 marks the 10 µM ROS and ATP contrasts nonsignificant despite main-text directional claims; the adverse motor/lifespan results remain. Loss of benefit in selected mutants is conditional, and 5 µM lowered mitochondrial membrane potential in daf-16 mutants. The worm source identifies sir-2.1 as a SIRT1 homolog; it cannot inherit mammalian SIRT2–BUBR1 findings. No BUB1B, mitosis or aneuploidy endpoint was measured. [Worm primary study](https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.70530).

The separate MediQ7 genotoxicity study tests fermented/purified MK-7 subsequently formulated in MCT oil. Its negative chromosome-aberration/micronucleus conclusions are author-reported assay findings without a damaged-cell rescue arm. The MCT-oil stock is approximately 5% MK-7; preparation mg/kg is not pure-active mg/kg. Native Table 13 prints treatment/control doses as zero, and individual micronucleus counts in both the MCT and first MK-7 groups conflict with the printed means/SDs; do not silently repair them. Additional native structural-aberration tables have contradictory cell totals or comparator labels, so the published null cannot be independently reproduced from these tables. Reported preparation doses are not automatically pure-active doses. One low-dose male group's grip-strength increase lacked a dose response and a verified vehicle-controlled efficacy contrast. Methods also reports temporary diarrhea at 5,000 mg/kg despite the broader no-toxic-effects wording. This is not established muscle rescue or unrestricted safety. Neither MK-7 source replicates the neonatal BUB1B result or makes MK-4 and MK-7 interchangeable. [Genotoxicity primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-56151-w).
