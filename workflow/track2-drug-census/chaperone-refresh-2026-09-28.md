## What the HCC combination study establishes

The full primary report describes 72-hour exposure of HuH7 cells to loratadine, raloxifene and sorafenib at their previously determined IC20 concentrations. It used four biological replicates per condition for the transcript microarray, three for proteomics and six for metabolomics. Table 8 includes **BUB1B** among the cell-cycle genes treated as differentially expressed. The authors describe the broader cell-cycle program as downregulated, but their main text does not give BUB1B's own direction, fold change, confidence or adjusted P value. The paper's data statement offers analyzed and raw data on request rather than a public accession. Its referenced supplementary ZIP was not retrieved in this update.

This is a real drug-combination/BUB1B-transcript association in a cancer cell line, so it belongs in the discovery record. It does not show higher usable BUBR1, restored checkpoint function, more accurate chromosome segregation, muscle benefit, or a response in BUB1B-deficient cells. The combination cannot assign any observed change to loratadine, raloxifene or sorafenib individually. A separate 2023 HCC-cell study reports metabolic-activity, apoptosis and colony-formation endpoints for individual drugs and combinations, not a BUB1B/MVA rescue; the 2026 transcript signal must not inherit a component-specific effect from it ([2023 primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37103074/)).

The conservative alias policy identifies three specific catalog co-mentions in the new record: loratadine (`CHEMBL998`), raloxifene (`CHEMBL81`) and sorafenib (`CHEMBL1336`). They are three ingredients in one intervention and one source, not three independent efficacy findings. This refresh adds no candidate, curated route appraisal or efficacy claim; the curated appraisal count remains **282** and the provisional niacin/apitegromab comparison is unchanged.
