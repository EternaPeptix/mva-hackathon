# Mono-n-butyl phthalate: BUB1B-down exposure comparator, not a treatment

**Source qualification, 28 September 2026.** A primary study reports that mono-n-butyl phthalate (MnBP), a measured urinary phthalate metabolite, reduced BUB1B RNA in a human granulosa-cell culture. This is an adverse-direction exposure observation; it does not support MnBP as a therapeutic candidate.

## Evidence and limits

The study recruited 123 women with surgically confirmed endometriosis and 78 controls. Urinary MnBP was higher in the endometriosis group after the authors' adjustment for number of deliveries, BMI and medication use. MnBP also correlated with the other measured phthalate metabolites, so the human association cannot isolate MnBP or establish causation.

For the cell experiment, the authors pooled granulosa cells collected at oocyte retrieval and exposed them to MnBP at 67.5, 250 or 500 µg/mL for 24 hours for RT-qPCR, or 72 hours for the mitochondrial-membrane-potential assay. The full text describes lower BUB1B, BIRC3, CDC20 and cyclin-B1 expression after treatment; it does not provide a numerical BUB1B effect estimate in the prose. Cells were pooled because there were too few cells to seed the clinical groups separately on the same day, limiting donor-level replication. The same source reports lower AMH and inhibin-B expression and reduced mitochondrial membrane-potential ratio; these are not BUBR1-protein or checkpoint-function measurements.

The study did not assess BUBR1 protein, faithful chromosome segregation, muscle output, MVA, or either nominated variant. It does not establish an effect at ordinary human exposure. Its high nominal culture concentrations and pooled-cell design restrict transfer. No treatment benefit is shown.

The CPDat v4.1 “Phthalate” article record is not a verified identity match to MnBP. It is excluded from the exact-name review class because “phthalate” is a broad class/name fragment; this qualification does not claim that CPDat records MnBP in any particular commonly used product.

## Decision

Retain MnBP as a source-specific BUB1B-down exposure comparator and decline it as a restoration or muscle-benefit lead. Do not infer risk from ordinary exposure or transfer the result to all phthalates, dibutyl phthalate (DBP), other metabolites, consumer products, or MVA. No exact current-catalog identity join is asserted.
