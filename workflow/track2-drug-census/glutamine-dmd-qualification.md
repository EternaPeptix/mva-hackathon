# Oral L-glutamine in ambulant boys with Duchenne muscular dystrophy

Mok and colleagues’ 2009 randomized crossover trial found **no demonstrated overall benefit of four months of oral L-glutamine over maltodextrin on walking speed or the other measured functional outcomes**. It also reported a **nominal positive treatment effect in five corticosteroid-treated boys**, explicitly from an unplanned subgroup analysis. That signal merits retention as exploratory evidence; it does not establish a steroid interaction, synergy or a confirmed responder group. Neither the overall null nor this small subgroup establishes an effect in MVA.

This is the [original PLOS One report](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0005448), PMID **19421321**, PMC **2673684**, registered as [NCT00296621](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00296621). The source producer read the complete scientific main, all eight native PDF pages, both tables and the flow figure. Independent primary review accepted the qualification after reading the complete main, four native main pages, the extracted protocol/supporting prose, both native ethics pages and the current registry. Portable synthesis adds no fresh primary reading.

## Population, preparation and comparison

Thirty ambulant, prepubescent boys aged 2–10 years, median 6.1 years, were randomized at three French centers. DMD was established clinically and by dystrophin testing/molecular biology. Eligibility required ability to walk at least 170 m; wheelchair dependence, weight above 60 kg, hepatic/renal insufficiency and planned surgery were exclusions. Five boys used corticosteroids; none used another potentially anabolic co-intervention such as creatine. This selected ambulant pediatric DMD population is distinct from MVA and later nonambulant disease.

The actual report specifies **L-glutamine prepared by the central AGEPS pharmacy**, at **0.5 g/kg/day orally for four months**, versus **equal-weight maltodextrin** for four months, with a one-month washout and randomized treatment order. Each sequence contained 15 boys. Both powders had matching taste, odor and appearance, banana/caramel flavoring and aspartame, and were supplied in identical **5 g sachets**. Families administered the powder once each morning mixed with water or yogurt. The dated protocol planned a single pharmaceutical-quality lot and dosing based on inclusion weight; the parent information form says that the absolute quantity remained fixed throughout the study. The report does not provide a lot identifier, purity assay or full quantitative excipient formula. The carbohydrate placebo was not an amino-acid nitrogen control; equal powder weight and similar reported diets do not establish calorie equivalence.

Computer-generated blocks of six and allocation concealment were managed centrally by staff outside the outcome evaluation. Participants, clinicians, investigators, assessors and analysts were blinded; the code remained concealed through statistical analysis. No adverse event required unblinding. Sachet returns were used for adherence; the paper says adherence was similar but gives no numerical percentage. No trial exercise-training co-intervention is described.

## Denominators, hierarchy and analysis

Of 34 screened, three families declined and one child exceeded the weight limit. **All 30 randomized boys completed follow-up, but only 29 were analyzed for the primary outcome**: one boy could not walk 170 m at the four-month assessment of the placebo phase because of gastroenteritis. The native flow figure places the exclusion in the glutamine-first sequence (14 analyzed versus 15 in the placebo-first sequence). Completion must not be equated with complete outcome data.

Table 2’s descriptive treatment-period counts are **30 glutamine and 29 placebo** for walking speed, 2-minute walk, work and power. Other counts differ: BIA body composition 29/27, urinary-creatinine muscle-mass estimate 26/27, CPK 30/30 and 3-methylhistidine/creatinine 24/25, respectively. These are repeated treatment-period observations from the same children, not independent arms; the exact paired sample for every secondary model is not reported. No imputation is described.

The primary endpoint was change in comfortable walking speed over a standardized 170 m course after each four-month period. The main used repeated-measures analysis of variance, assessing treatment, order and interaction terms, with log or square-root transformations when needed. It gives no numerical primary treatment contrast or confidence interval, and no exact overall primary P value. Reported medians are descriptive and cannot be subtracted to reconstruct the paired treatment effect.

| Outcome; four-month median change (Q1–Q3) | L-glutamine | Placebo | Reported comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Primary walking speed, m/s** | **0.0 (−0.1 to 0.1)** | **0.0 (−0.1 to 0.1)** | No significant treatment improvement reported; exact P and CI absent |
| Secondary 2-minute walk, m | −2.8 (−10.0 to 2.3) | +2.0 (−16.2 to 13.3) | No significant treatment improvement reported |
| Secondary work, reported kcal | +0.7 (−5.1 to 2.0) | −1.1 (−7.1 to 6.3) | No significant treatment improvement reported |
| Secondary power, Table 2 watts | +10 (−17 to 20) | +11 (−13 to 23) | No significant treatment improvement reported |

Work and power were calculated from the walking task, body mass and time; they are **derived performance measures**, not direct muscle-force, calorimetry or energetic-mechanism assays. The main Methods and registry label power in kcal/s, while native Table 2 and the protocol’s calculation section use watts. The protocol’s energy-conversion notation also warrants caution. These source labels are preserved without recalculating or repairing the results.

The planned sample size sought a 10% walking-speed improvement with 80% power and α = .05. The supplied protocol makes the within-person correlation assumption explicit as uncertain, showing different required samples for different correlations and choosing 30 within recruitment constraints. The trial does not establish equivalence or exclude every smaller effect. Multiple secondary endpoints and subgroup analyses are presented without a reported multiplicity correction.

## Positive subgroup result and other outcomes

In the five boys taking corticosteroids, the main explicitly reports a glutamine treatment effect on function, **P < .05 by variance analysis after log transformation**. Their median walking speed during placebo was 1.0 m/s (Q1–Q3 0.7–1.0) before and 0.6 (0.6–0.7) after four months; during glutamine it was 0.9 (0.9–1.0) before and 1.0 (0.8–1.0) after. The authors call this analysis **unplanned and small**. This is an actual nominal treatment finding, not merely a comparison of separate within-arm significance labels. However, no confidence interval, exact P value or formal steroid-by-treatment interaction is reported. It cannot confirm that steroid users respond differently from other boys or establish combination efficacy.

The cohort’s lack of deterioration over nine months is a separate within-cohort observation, not a treatment effect. Likewise, families’ impressions—15 favoring improvement during glutamine, 12 during placebo, three rating the phases equivalently—are descriptive. The authors’ placebo-response explanation is not proved without an untreated comparison. Discussion of a younger subgroup in the earlier CINRG study is a citation to that separate trial, not fresh primary qualification of its subgroup results.

Body composition and biochemical endpoints were not uniformly null:

- **BIA fat-free-mass gain was smaller with glutamine:** +0.4 kg (−0.2 to 0.7) versus +0.7 kg (0.2 to 1.2), nominal P < .05. An order effect was present, P = .001.
- **BIA percentage fat mass increased more with glutamine:** +1.9 percentage points (−0.8 to 4.9) versus +1.2 (−1.6 to 2.3), nominal P < .05. DXA endpoint fat percentages were 20.2% (14.1–27.4) after glutamine versus 18.5% (14.1–28.8) after placebo, P = .03; the specific paired DXA denominator is not supplied.
- Urinary-creatinine muscle-mass estimates, CPK and urinary 3-methylhistidine/creatinine showed no treatment differences; the separate 24-hour 3-methylhistidine excretion comparison was P = .25. The lack of a meat-free diet limits the latter biomarker’s interpretation.

These body-composition differences deserve retention as a potentially unfavorable secondary pattern, with order, multiplicity and measurement limitations. BIA fat-free mass in DMD includes water/connective tissue and is not a muscle-mass or renewal assay. The paper itself considers chance and disease-course explanations. Neither the body-composition pattern nor biomarker nulls prove a specific mechanism of harm or benefit.

## Harms and disclosure

Reported adverse-event counts during glutamine/placebo periods were gastroenteritis **1/2**, urticaria **1/0**, allergic reaction **0/1**, nervousness **1/0**, rhinitis **0/1** and pharyngitis **0/1**. These category counts do not establish unique affected-patient totals. The paper reports no significant difference in side-effect profiles or biochemical parameters, no withdrawals and no unblinding. It provides no detailed severity table or numerical renal laboratory contrasts and explicitly states that this was not powered as a safety study. “Well tolerated” in this small selected four-month exposure does not establish general, longer-term or MVA safety.

Assistance Publique–Hôpitaux de Paris sponsored the trial. Support included the Programme Hospitalier de Recherche Clinique National 2003, a Fonds de la Recherche en Santé Québec doctoral award, a nutrition prize from the named French home-care/nutrition organizations, and Fédération Hospitalière de France/Les Entreprises du Médicament support. AGEPS prepared glutamine and placebo. The authors declared no competing interests and stated that funders had no role in design, collection/analysis, publication decisions or manuscript preparation.

## Protocol, registry and source limits

**Protocol S1 is labeled version 3, 7 December 2005**, before the reported February 2006 start. Its walking-speed primary endpoint, four-month crossover periods, washout, dose and planned sample are consistent with the core report. It specifies primary analysis among children completing both periods and sequence/treatment/order comparisons using parametric or nonparametric tests. The publication uses repeated-measures ANOVA and transformations; no separate final SAP or amendment history was retrieved. The two-minute walk outcome is listed in the current registry and paper but was not located in the supplied protocol text. Thus the dated protocol supports core planning, without proving every final endpoint/model was prospectively fixed.

The registry was first submitted **23 February 2006** and first posted **27 February 2006**, while recruitment is reported only as beginning in February 2006. Registration before the first participant cannot be resolved to the day. The record was last submitted for update in December 2007, gives actual enrollment 30 and completion November 2007, retains an estimated primary-completion date of February 2008, and posts **no results**. Current fields and the existence of a dated supplied protocol do not substitute for historical registry-field verification.

The native ethics statement is dated 20 September 2004, describes a 15 September meeting and removal of an earlier 7 July reservation. The December 2005 parent form instead calls 7 July the favorable-opinion date. The same form describes a 20–30 g sachet generically, whereas the main reports 5 g sachets and weight-based administration; the protocol planned one dispensing per period while the main describes two-month dispensing. These document differences are retained rather than silently harmonized. The main is the source for the reported administered packaging.

## Scope for the existing glutamine comparison

The existing `new-glutamine-source-dmd` comparison retains its separate [six-month CINRG evidence](creatine-muscle-qualification.md) at abstract/current-registry depth. That prior object and alias are preserved in the companion JSON, with historical and current proposal checkpoints distinguished. The present follow-up adds its own L-glutamine preparation, four-month exposure, overall null, exploratory steroid-subgroup signal and body-composition/safety limits. It does not prove preparation or batch equivalence to CINRG or retail glutamine or create a duplicate appraisal.

Compensation evidence should be compared on function, exposure, duration, tissue renewal and fidelity using the same standard as other candidates. BUBR1 mediation is additionally relevant to restoration claims, not a prerequisite for recognizing this trial’s functional evidence. This report establishes neither MVA benefit nor superiority to another candidate. Its discovery came from the predeclared broad batch 02 order, not selection by a favorable result.
