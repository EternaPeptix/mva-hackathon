# Gemini-Cur: BUB1B suppression in a cytotoxic cancer-cell experiment

**PMID 35684411 supports a formulation-specific cancer-cell effect, not BUBR1 restoration or useful normal-cell function.** The primary reports BUB1B RNA downregulation and shows a weaker BUB1B protein band. Its qPCR graph conflicts with the stated direction, so the validation is not uniformly concordant. No checkpoint rescue, chromosome-segregation improvement, MVA model or normal-tissue benefit was measured. This does not displace the provisional niacin or apitegromab nominations, neither of which is MVA-proven.

The study is Azeez et al., *Molecules* 2022, 27:3470, [DOI 10.3390/molecules27113470](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27113470); [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9182402/) and [publisher PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/molecules/molecules-27-03470/article_deploy/molecules-27-03470.pdf). At repo baseline `b090335`, the exact PMID/formulation search found existing abstract and NIH query-role annotations, but no distinct primary qualification or curated Gemini-Cur appraisal. Those prior readings are reused, not new abstract credit.

## Intervention and controls

The actual intervention was **curcumin (reported CAS 458-37-7) in mPEG urethane gemini surfactant nanoparticles**, called Gemini-Cur, tested in HCT-116 colorectal cancer cells. Methods describe nanoprecipitation with nominal 6 mg curcumin per 100 mg surfactant, methanol/PBS and solvent evaporation. The paper does not give this batch's size distribution, loading/encapsulation efficiency, residual solvent measurement or complete carrier structural specification. Earlier formulation references were identified but not independently read. Free curcumin, turmeric and unspecified polyphenols are not interchangeable identities.

Figure 1A includes **carrier alone (Gemini) and free curcumin (Cur)**, not merely untreated controls. Gemini-Cur visibly reduces viability more than either comparator across the plotted range up to about 100 µM. Results 3.1 reports a 24 h IC50 of 51.50; the text omits units, while the figure axis specifies µM. The carrier's nominal molar-dose basis, per-point replication, errors and fit uncertainty are not supplied. The graph is not a verified numerical IC50 reconstruction.

AO/EB images at 24, 48 and 72 h provide qualitative apoptotic morphology after an “IC50 dose”; no counted apoptotic fraction is supplied. Methods mention 5-FU positive-control images, but Figure 1B shows only untreated and Gemini-Cur time points. For RNA-seq, three treated and three untreated RNA samples follow 24 h exposure. Methods describe library preparation from each sample, and Figure 2 visibly labels C1/C2/C3 and T1/T2/T3. Thus this is not a reported one-library-per-arm design, but independent-culture/passages provenance is unclear. The exact drug concentration is not explicitly restated for that arm. The expression experiments do not include reported carrier-only or free-curcumin arms. No matched solvent-control description or healthy-cell comparator is given. “Normal” controls in one analysis paragraph refer to untreated HCT-116, not normal tissue.

Further concrete reporting issues bound confidence: Table 1 prints the **same ATF3 and beta-actin primer sequences**, despite different claimed product lengths. This is a reporting/reproducibility issue, not proof of which primers were actually used. The paper's GADD45G RNA-seq increase was not significantly confirmed by PCR and was not detected by Western in its discussion; Figure 6 instead labels GADD45A. Table 5's “upregulated” caption includes BUB1B, which the text classifies as downregulated. These discrepancies must remain visible rather than treating every assay as an independent confirmation.

The recovered supplementary table exposes a separate count discrepancy: **3,891 data rows** (3,892 including its header), only **820 with adjusted P<0.01**, and **243 up/197 down** with adjusted P<0.01 and absolute log2FC>2. The paper reports 3,892/244/198. This is an arithmetic check of the supplied result table, not a raw-RNA reanalysis. It does not invalidate the directly observed negative, significant BUB1B row, but the global counts should not be repeated as independently verified.

The drug's functional positive is cancer-cell viability inhibition/apoptotic morphology. Its checkpoint, spindle and segregation claims are **gene-list enrichment terms**, not actual assays of reliable division. No BUB1B-binding/engagement, rescue/epistasis, kinetochore localization, checkpoint challenge, mitotic completion, aneuploidy or faithful daughter-cell yield is shown. Treatment-associated expression changes could accompany cytotoxic stress or altered cell-cycle composition. Cited earlier subG1/Hoechst findings are not newly qualified experiments in this review.

## Competitive and integration decision

A distinct **`new-gemini-cur` formulation appraisal** is justified if the ledger records evaluated but non-nominated interventions: it has actual drug exposure and primary BUB1B direction, unlike a mere computational candidate. Suggested aliases are Gemini-Cur/gemini curcumin/gemini nanoparticle curcumin; do not make all curcumin or polyphenols equivalent, or create a second appraisal from the generic polyphenol hit. Update the existing NIH role annotation from “direction absent in abstract” to “primary RNA/protein support suppression; qPCR graph contradictory.”

Preserve the anticancer positive fairly, but do not nominate it for MVA restoration or compensation. Relative to niacin's provisional restoration hypothesis and apitegromab's separate muscle-function evidence, this paper supplies no useful normal-function benefit. That is a source-specific assessment, not proof that all curcumin exposures harm MVA or that either provisional nominee benefits the patient's genotype. No head-to-head comparison was performed.
