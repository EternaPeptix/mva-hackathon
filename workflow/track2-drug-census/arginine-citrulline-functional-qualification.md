# Isolated L-citrulline and L-arginine: bounded functional qualification

Source review completed 23 September 2026. This integration adds two separate comparisons without changing nominee order or original-queue reading counts. The two ingredients, their salts/formulations and the existing deliberately assigned L-citrulline–metformin DMD combination remain separate.

**Decision:** retain L-citrulline as a preliminary human functional comparator: a small controlled crossover study shows a modest calf 10-repetition-load benefit, with handgrip null. Retain chronic oral L-arginine as a discriminating adverse-direction functional comparator: in NO-PAIN the walking gain was smaller than placebo. Neither result establishes BUBR1 restoration, chromosome fidelity, pediatric or MVA benefit. The limitations below constrain comparison without imposing an MVA-proof requirement that the provisional nominees themselves do not meet.

## L-citrulline: an actual controlled calf-load signal

The 2025 T2D study is PMID40944179, DOI10.3390/nu17172790, NCT06016478. I read its complete scientific main, all three tables and five captions, and viewed all five native figures. The current registry explicitly calls the intervention **L-citrulline**. The article gives citrulline 6 g/day, eight 750-mg capsules split morning/evening for four weeks, versus matching microcrystalline-cellulose capsules, with eight weeks of washout before the opposite condition. NOW Foods supplied both products. This is not citrulline malate, an AKG salt, watermelon or an exercise mixture. These are study exposures, not administration recommendations. [Primary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12430577/), [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06016478).

Native Figure 1 and Results identify **22 randomized, 16 analyzed after completing both periods**, rather than the abstract's simplified 16 randomized. Each initial sequence had 11, with one discontinuation per sequence. After crossover, the citrulline period had one further discontinuation; the placebo period had two lost to follow-up and one discontinuation. Reasons and arm-specific harms are not supplied. There is no ITT analysis or imputation reported. Analyzed participants were seven men/nine women, 53–72 years, mean 62 ± 6; BMI 33.4 ± 4.9, T2D duration 8 ± 5 years. **None met the study's low-grip or low-appendicular-mass criteria.** This is not a demonstrated sarcopenic cohort. Table 1 includes metformin in 13/16, insulin in three and other usual medicines. The randomized difference is addition of this supplement against placebo on usual care; background metformin does not turn the trial into the assigned DMD combination or establish a citrulline–metformin interaction.

Calf strength means the greatest external load moved through ten plantarflexion repetitions on a leg-press machine, not isolated muscle force, specific force, a maximal isometric test or walking performance. Grip was the best of three dominant-hand maximal contractions. Relative measures divide by body mass. Table 2 distinguishes within-condition tests from the time-by-condition interaction:

| Outcome | Citrulline pre → post | Placebo pre → post | Controlled interaction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Calf 10RM load, kg | 99 ± 26 → 100 ± 26 | 102 ± 27 → 101 ± 27 | P=.03 |
| Calf 10RM load/body mass, kg/kg | 1.07 ± .22 → 1.09 ± .21 | 1.11 ± .23 → 1.11 ± .23 | P=.006 |
| Handgrip, kg | 32 ± 8 → 33 ± 8 | 32 ± 8 → 32 ± 8 | P=.16 |
| Handgrip/body mass, kg/kg | .35 ± .09 → .36 ± .09 | .36 ± .10 → .35 ± .09 | P=.09 |

All dispersion is source-labelled SD. Reported calf-load changes are +1.5 ± 2.8 versus −.3 ± 1.2 kg, and relative changes +.02 ± .03 versus −.01 ± .02 kg/kg. Rounded table means must not replace these reported changes. No treatment-contrast confidence interval or meaningful-change threshold is supplied. The controlled positives are not inferred from within-arm significance. Native Figure 2C marks the relative calf comparison; Figure 2B leaves relative grip unmarked. Walking, endurance, everyday function and persistence after stopping were not measured in this report. DXA was obtained at baseline only; there is no measured gain in muscle mass.

Plasma arginine, finger reactive hyperemia and several forearm oxygenation responses also improved. Table 3 gives interaction P=.01 for RHI and log-RHI, .008 for TOI range, .03 for hyperemic response and .007 for reperfusion AUC; baseline TOI and occlusion magnitude are null (.20 and .06). The article prints arginine in “µM/L” and AUC in “%/s”; retain those source labels, not silently repaired dimensions. NO itself was not measured. Figure 4 pools citrulline and placebo change markers in the reported Pearson association between log-RHI and relative calf load (r=.37, P<.05); the main does not describe a repeated-person correlation model. This association does not establish vascular mediation of the strength effect.

The main's sample-size calculation targets RHI, not strength. Its analysis uses two-way repeated-measures ANOVA followed by Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc tests, without a described period/sequence/carryover model or study-wide multiplicity adjustment across all outcomes. The **current**, not historical, registry calls muscle strength secondary, including handgrip, leg extension and plantarflexion; the paper reports grip and plantarflexion. The current registry lists actual enrollment 36 versus the paper's 22 randomized, and first submission/posting in August 2023 after an actual May 2023 start. It also contains inconsistent age limits (structured 40–75, an exclusion line >65). These discrepancies are unresolved; they do not license inventing additional analyzed people, prospective endpoint history or an explanation for omitted outcomes. No results are posted in the retrieved registry. No separate protocol/history was read.

Adherence by returned capsules was 92 ± 6% versus 91 ± 7%. One person reported mild stomach discomfort; its assignment and relation to withdrawals are unstated. The paper reports no other adverse effects, without a detailed safety table. This small selected sample cannot establish safety equivalence. The authors declare no conflicts and no external funding, and acknowledge donated NOW Foods products. The complete scientific main supplies no BUB1B/BUBR1 expression, protein, stability, checkpoint or chromosome-fidelity assay. No supplementary-material element was present in the retrieved XML; the repository asset archive supplied the native main figures, not an additional efficacy study.

## L-arginine: chronic oral PAD counterevidence, with a strict access limit

NO-PAIN is PMID17592080, DOI10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.683656, NCT00284076. The complete official abstract and selected indexed publisher Methods/opening Results were read. Publisher PDF and an ordinary public PDF copy returned 403; no full-main, native-table/figure, supplement or complete harms reading is claimed. This limits the depth of qualification, not the validity of the source. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17592080/), [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.106.683656), [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00284076).

The single-center randomized double-blind study assigned **133 adults with PAD/intermittent claudication**, 66 to oral L-arginine and 67 to placebo, for six months. The abstract confirms 3 g/day. The accessible Methods excerpt specifies 1 g three times daily with meals, manufactured by Ajinomoto and encapsulated by CosmoPharm, with sampled capsule-content checks. The chemical counterion and full excipient specification remain unverified; no arginine-HCl, AKG, malate or mixture equivalence is asserted. Eligibility included age ≥45, ABI<.9 and stable reproducible claudication. The excerpt describes a modified-ITT population (dose plus valid follow-up treadmill), LOCF for missing six-month values and baseline-adjusted analysis. An opening Results excerpt gives 61 placebo/58 active remaining, but the final primary-analysis denominator, complete missingness/safety tables and precision were not verified from the inaccessible remainder.

The primary outcome was six-month change in absolute claudication distance on the Skinner–Gardner treadmill. **Both groups improved, but the arginine gain was smaller: 11.5% versus placebo 28.3%, P=.024.** The abstract's sentence lists “28.3% versus 11.5%” after saying arginine improved less; the ordering here follows that explicit direction and the indexed main Results, rather than assigning the first number to active treatment. There is no verified confidence interval in this reading. This is attenuation of walking improvement, not evidence that every participant's walking deteriorated. Increased circulating arginine did not translate into benefit: the abstract reports NO-availability measures reduced or not improved against placebo. This supports declining this chronic oral PAD regimen as a positive functional lead, not a global claim that every route or population is harmed.

The exact 1998 JACC source found during discovery, DOI10.1016/S0735-1097(98)00375-1, reports a short **intravenous** L-arginine claudication signal against a different control context. Only the returned primary abstract was encountered; main/protocol, allocation denominators and the meaning of its significance tests were not qualified. It remains an explicitly unqualified positive pointer and is not transferred to six-month oral treatment or counted as independent confirmation of oral efficacy. HeartBar and citrulline-malate/exercise studies encountered in discovery are mixtures/different interventions and were not qualified here.

The current NO-PAIN registry describes both an earlier 80-person dose-ranging study and a planned prolonged study with 12-month language, versus the six-month publication. It has no posted results or current enrollment count. Do not pool the phases, replace publication duration or invent additional outcome data. Initial registration was in 2006 after the stated 2000 start; history was not read. Its exclusion of active/prior malignancy is an applicability limit, not a newly established universal contraindication. The registry names Stanford and NHLBI; the indexed main acknowledges NHLBI funding. PubMed/Europe PMC identifies a 2008 comment/author reply (PMID18268155), not an erratum; its text was not read. No BUBR1 assay was reported in the source portions read; absence from the inaccessible remainder was not established.

## Competitive and identity decision

Citrulline contributes an actual randomized functional contrast, which should not be dismissed because MVA efficacy is unproved while accepting a similarly unproved transfer for niacin or apitegromab. Its small, selected, secondary 10RM result with grip null does not establish generalized muscle restoration or cross-trial superiority. Niacin's proposed BUBR1 bridge and human muscle-NAD evidence remain distinct; apitegromab's controlled SMA motor-function evidence remains a different pediatric disease context. No new primary-reading credit for those nominees is claimed here. Neither a shared NO rationale nor a rise in arginine transfers the citrulline signal to L-arginine, nitrate or the DMD combination.
