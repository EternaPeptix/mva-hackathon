# Cholesterol mechanism: useful pathway evidence, causality unresolved

[Goossens et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-021-03688-1) supports a plausible link between ketone-derived carbon, muscle sterol metabolism and force during mouse sepsis. **It does not establish cholesterol as the necessary mediator of the force benefit.** Its human component is observational, its force measurements reuse the 2019 sodium-salt cohort, and its distinct tracer experiment measures mevalonate labeling rather than labeled cholesterol. This mechanism appraisal refines the existing [sodium-salt assessment](sodium-dl-3hb-sepsis-qualification.md), adding no drug or independent force-study count.

| Evidence layer | Actual finding | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Human EPaNIC subset | Lower serum cholesterol associated with subsequent MRC-defined weakness; adjusted odds ratio 0.94, P=.05, or P=.03 after adding statin use | Association in a selected cohort; neither cholesterol nor ketone treatment efficacy |
| Reused mouse intervention cohort | D,L-3HB sodium salt increased circulating cholesterol, muscle total cholesterol and selected sterol-pathway transcripts | Preparation-specific biochemical changes accompanying an already-counted force result |
| Separate tracer cohort | Carbon from injected 13C4-D,L-3HB detected in mevalonate, with greater normalized muscle labeling during sepsis | Entry into the mevalonate pathway; not direct final-cholesterol tracing or a measured absolute flux preference |
| Correlations and nulls | Cholesterol–force R²=.1; mitochondrial enzyme activities were not restored | A mechanistic hypothesis, without necessity, sufficiency or mediation established |

## Human association and selection

The 600 patients were the EPaNIC weakness substudy, not a new ketone trial: **232 weak and 368 nonweak**, with MRC sum score <48 defining weakness at post-randomization day 8±1. Only 264/600 (44%) had admission sepsis. The cited parent study assessed awake/cooperative patients; the complete EPaNIC population was larger. This paper does not report cholesterol-sample missingness, regression complete-case counts or an analysis that accounts for selection into assessable survivors.

Stored serum was sampled on admission and day 3, or the earlier last ICU day. Day-3/last-day cholesterol was 67.6±2.1 versus 77.8±1.8 mg/dL in weak/nonweak patients (mean±SE; P=.0002). Models adjusted for nutrition randomization, age, sex, BMI category, nutritional risk, APACHE II, diabetes, malignancy, pre-admission dialysis, sepsis and admission category; a second model added statin treatment. The cholesterol OR was **0.94 (95% CI .89–1.00), P=.05**, becoming **0.94 (.89–.99), P=.03**. Illness-course confounding and observational statin prescribing remain possible.

Native tables preserve material ambiguities: the OR is labeled **per mg/L added**, whereas descriptive concentrations use mg/dL; its effect scale must not be silently rescaled. The sepsis OR changes from 2.26 to 0.44 across models without explanation. Admission cholesterol P is printed “0004”; Table S2 leaves its coefficient scale/transformation unclear and reports an asymmetric dialysis estimate/CI. The separate cholesterol model's R²=.07 describes explained cholesterol variation, not weakness prediction. These issues constrain quantitative interpretation; they do not erase the observed association.

Statin use was associated with lower cholesterol but was not significantly associated with weakness (adjusted OR .66, CI .40–1.08, P=.1). Nutrition randomization did not significantly change cholesterol. Neither result supports prescribing cholesterol, changing statins, or assigning cholesterol as the sole nutritional mechanism.

## Mouse intervention, reuse and measurements

The first mouse study reports **150 mg/day D,L-3HB sodium salt in twice-daily subcutaneous injections**, with isocaloric/isovolumetric glucose and mixed parenteral nutrition. Healthy controls were pair-fed. Solvent, batch/purity and catalogue details were not restated. It is distinct from oral foods, retail products and ketone esters.

Figures 1/4 explicitly reuse **2019 absolute EDL force**. Cohort labels are healthy 15/placebo 17/salt 17; survival is 17/20 versus 17/21 (P=.8), not proof of equal safety or freedom from survivor selection. Ubiquinone assays use n=13/15/14. Correlation complete-pair counts and reasons for assay losses are unspecified; the earlier specific-force n=15/16/14 must not be substituted silently for absolute-force pairs.

Native figures support higher total/esterified plasma cholesterol (P≤.05), free cholesterol (P≤.01), mevalonate (P≤.05), muscle Srebf2/Hmgcs1/Hmgcr, Ldlr and Nceh1, and muscle total cholesterol (P≤.05). Free cholesterol also exceeds healthy controls, so not every fraction “normalizes.” Total cholesterol was extracted from **whole EDL homogenate per mg protein**, not isolated myofiber membranes. Plasma esterified cholesterol correlated with force at R²=.1/P=.02; muscle cholesterol at R²=.1/P=.04. These pooled, unadjusted correlations do not separate treatment/illness group effects.

Plasma ubiquinone-9 was not improved, and tibialis citrate-synthase/complex-I activities remained depressed. This narrows support for those measured pathways, without excluding all mitochondrial mechanisms. Many hepatic and other muscle transcripts were null; RNA expression is not enzyme activity or flux.

## What the isotope experiment adds—and leaves open

A separate cohort comprised **7 healthy, 7 day-2 septic and 7 day-5 septic mice**, each given a **single 75-mg subcutaneous D,L-3HB-13C4 sodium-salt bolus** (Sigma 606030), then sampled two hours later. These are endpoint counts; starting denominators/mortality were not supplied. Healthy controls were full-fed, unlike the first cohort. No force outcome was measured in this tracer experiment.

LC–MS measured labeled 3HB, mevalonate, citrate and fumarate in plasma, liver and gastrocnemius. Each metabolite's labeled fraction was normalized to plasma 3HB labeling. The authors report muscle mevalonate labeling approximately sixfold/day 2 and sixteenfold/day 5 above healthy controls. This is meaningful carbon-incorporation evidence. Single-time fractional enrichments across different metabolite pools, however, do not directly quantify uptake rate, total synthesis flux, ATP production or relative pathway throughput. Final cholesterol was not isotope-measured. No sterol-pathway blockade, cholesterol-specific rescue, or formal mediation test linked labeling to force.

Two additional reporting boundaries remain: Figure 2c places the hepatic healthy-comparison marker over Hmgcs1 whereas prose names Hmgcr; neither has a treatment-comparison marker. Also, the reported **Acat1 assay Mm00507463_m1** maps to acetyl-CoA acetyltransferase 1, distinct from cholesterol-esterifying Soat1, despite the prose's esterification interpretation. The [manufacturer assay record](https://www.thermofisher.com/taqman-gene-expression/product/Mm00507463_m1) and [NCBI gene identities](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/20652) support retaining this unresolved marker annotation, without assuming which reagent was actually run.

Funding included public/ERC support and Baxter Healthcare; authors declared no conflicts. No linked correction/retraction was located in bounded current metadata checks. Full main, native Figures 1–4, human tables and supplemental scientific material were read at the recorded depths. No BUB1B/BUBR1 assay, MVA model, dietary cholesterol intervention or human ketone efficacy experiment is present.

## Published correspondence and research priority

The [published letter and embedded authors response](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-021-03722-2) address measurement timing, endogenous ketones, pharmacokinetics and alternative metabolic routes. The authors explain that ICU blood sampling ended at discharge: an early timepoint retained short-stayers who would be excluded by later sampling. This is a rationale for the design, not proof that confounding is removed. They acknowledge that pharmacokinetics and indirect conversion through other tissues require further work. The exchange adds no intervention outcome or independent replication.

The injected D,L sodium salt remains an exploratory muscle-compensation comparison. The isotope result strengthens a pathway hypothesis while leaving the causal link to force unresolved. Earlier force and toxicity evidence remains credited at its own scope; neither the human association nor this tracer study upgrades it to demonstrated MVA efficacy or BUBR1 restoration. Niacin and apitegromab remain provisional, and no preparation is established superior by these comparisons.

A useful next experiment would combine justified measured exposure and sodium-matched controls with absolute useful output, renewable reserve, chromosome fidelity and survival/starting-unit denominators. Sterol-specific perturbation and rescue, together with final-cholesterol and membrane measurements, would test the proposed mediator. Oral products, other ketone preparations and dietary cholesterol do not inherit the injected-salt result.
