# L-citrulline plus metformin: pediatric DMD functional evidence

23 September 2026. Source qualification of Hafner et al., PMID 31664444, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.14171; one randomized trial, NCT01995032. This is a combination-specific functional reserve, with an early strength signal and a favorable planned subgroup, but an inconclusive overall primary comparison. It does not establish either component's monotherapy efficacy, BUBR1 restoration, or MVA benefit. The original source review changed no ledger or reading counts. This integration adds one combination-specific assessment, with no new original-queue reading credit.

## What was actually administered

Forty-seven ambulant boys with genetically confirmed Duchenne muscular dystrophy, aged 6.5–10 years, were randomized at one Basel center: 23 to active treatment and 24 to matching placebo for 26 weeks. Eligibility required at least 150 m in the six-minute walk and MFM-D1 at least 40% of its maximum. Active treatment was oral **L-citrulline 2.5 g plus metformin hydrochloride 250 mg three times daily**—7.5 g and 750 mg daily. Citrulline powder was dissolved in water; the hospital pharmacy prepared treatment and matching mannitol placebo. The main paper calls metformin tablets, while the supplied and published protocols call them capsules; that dosage-form discrepancy remains unresolved. There were no citrulline-only or metformin-only arms. The earlier five-patient pilot used L-arginine, not this citrulline regimen, and is not credited as replication here. [Primary Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824222/), [own published protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4973063/).

Allocation used an independent pharmacist and a mixed block scheme; investigators, assessors, families and participants were masked. Stable corticosteroid treatment for more than six months or no corticosteroid treatment was allowed. Baseline steroid use was markedly imbalanced: **12/23 active versus 21/24 placebo**. Returned medication was counted, and the protocol planned a final metformin concentration, but the reviewed results do not supply a numerical adherence rate or achieved concentration. They do not establish a measured exposure–response relationship.

## Preserve the early positives and the final nulls

All contrasts below are active minus placebo, adjusted as reported, with 95% confidence intervals. MFM units are percentage points of the relevant maximum score, not relative percentages. The unit of randomization is the child. The trial's primary endpoint was change in standing/transfers MFM-D1 at week 26. [Table 2 and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824222/), [official scientific supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/6824222/bin/jamanetwopen-2-e1914171-s002.pdf).

Week-13 MFM-D1 was nonsignificant: +2.1 points (−2.5 to 6.6), P=.37. Six-minute walking and both timed tasks were also nonsignificant at week 13. No multiplicity adjustment across these secondary endpoints, visits and subgroup tests is described in the reviewed sources. The published protocol explicitly calls secondary-endpoint analyses hypothesis-generating. The early positives should be retained with that status, not omitted because the primary endpoint was null or promoted to confirmed efficacy. No treatment-by-time interaction was reported: an early positive and later nonsignificant comparison do not themselves prove waning efficacy.

The stable-walker subgroup (baseline 6MWD ≥350 m, n=29: 13 active/16 placebo) had **+6.7 MFM-D1 points (0.9 to 12.6), P=.03** at week 26. The <350-m ITT subgroup had n=18 and an imprecise +3.9 (−13.2 to 20.9), P=.63. Crucially, **treatment-by-stability interaction P=.67** does not establish greater treatment efficacy in stable walkers. Steroid subgroups were post hoc; their interaction was P=.20, and neither steroid stratum was individually significant. These comparisons do not resolve the baseline steroid imbalance.

The paper says the stability analysis was specified before trial end/unblinding. The 2016 published protocol explicitly plans a 350-m subgroup and ANCOVA, supporting planned status; it writes >350 rather than the paper's ≥350. However, it was published after the trial ended, and no separately dated SAP or historical registry version was obtained. The supplied September 2015 V8 protocol instead specifies Wilcoxon and last observation carried forward (LOCF), with no threshold in its statistical section. The published protocol also uses LOCF; the final paper uses 9,999 chained predictive-mean-matching imputations. These are documented analysis-plan/version differences, not evidence that the subgroup was secretly post hoc. [Published protocol statistical analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4973063/), [supplied V8 protocol, pp. 20–22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/6824222/bin/jamanetwopen-2-e1914171-s001.pdf).

## Missingness, biomarkers and harms

**45 completed the study, but only 43 had complete primary-outcome data.** Native CONSORT shows 23 active study completers, with two fractures preventing week-26 MFM, leaving 21 complete cases; placebo had 22 completers/complete cases, one never started and one withdrew at week 13. ITT retains all 47. eTable 1 confirms 21/22 complete cases and primary +5.5 points (−1.0 to 12.1), **P=.10**. The main prose claiming 45 complete cases/no missing data is inconsistent with its flow diagram and supplement. Complete-case stable/unstable numbers are 28/15, explaining why the abstract's unstable n=15 must not be assigned to the n=18 ITT subgroup. eTable 1 also has inconsistent week-13 column headings under its week-26 title and steroid subgroup counts summing to 44 rather than 43; these remain reporting uncertainties. [Native CONSORT and supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824222/).

The Discussion's claim of a significant placebo-controlled reduction of urine nonessential amino acids is not supported by its numerical supplement: week-26 **−235.6 mmol/mol (−508.7 to 37.5), P=.09**, and week-13 **−212.0 (−454.2 to 30.3), P=.08**. Within-active decreases are significant, but do not establish a treatment contrast. No listed biomarker was significant at week 26. Week-13 GGT was nominally lower (−1.6 U/L [−3.0 to −0.3], P=.02), so “all molecular measures were null” would also be too broad. Neither finding demonstrates the proposed NO/mitochondrial pathway or a BUB1B effect. No BUB1B/BubR1 RNA, protein, turnover, checkpoint, chromosome-fidelity or genetic-rescue assay was reported in the scientific main/supplement reviewed.

Table 3 reports at least one adverse event in **17/23 versus 13/24**, and gastrointestinal symptoms in **4/23 versus 2/24**. It labels three active-arm events “severe,” versus none on placebo: a deep leg cut and two fall-associated fractures (tibia and femur). One fracture occurred with steroids and one without. This small trial cannot assign their cause, establish equivalence of harms, or establish long-term safety. The AE table uses randomized denominators despite one placebo participant not receiving treatment. No fatal event or laboratory-related discontinuation was reported. The conclusion's claim of better safety than corticosteroids is unsupported by a randomized head-to-head comparison: this was placebo-controlled, with background steroid use. Known/suspected cancer and clinically important renal, liver and heart disease were excluded; that limits transfer and is not itself a contraindication determination for another child.

The University Hospital Basel sponsored the trial; the paper reports Swiss National Science Foundation funding and no funder role. Disclosures include outside-work industry fees/grants and study-related foundation/public grants. Supplement 3 states individual data are not available. This review did not reanalyze participant-level data.

## Competitive and identity decision

This is more direct pediatric, disease-associated controlled muscle-function evidence than niacin's small open-label strength observation, even though its strongest overall early outcomes are nominal and the final primary is inconclusive. Niacin retains a different NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 research hypothesis, whose drug-specific restoration remains unproved. The combination therefore deserves a visible functional-reserve entry rather than blanket rejection inherited from metformin's older-adult MASTERS result. It does not yet outweigh apitegromab's recorded positive primary motor-function comparison in pediatric SMA. That judgment rests on endpoint confirmation, duration and design—not a selective requirement for MVA efficacy that neither nominee satisfies. Neither cross-trial superiority nor a patient treatment recommendation follows. Comparison uses the existing repository appraisals; it adds no primary-reading credit for those nominees.

Preserve existing **prior-27** and all its metformin/AICAR distinctions. Any added appraisal must name **L-citrulline plus metformin hydrochloride**, preserve its two-component design, and avoid assigning its benefits or nulls to either component alone, AICAR, foods, or arbitrary citrulline products. The new source supports a combination-specific reserve/refinement; it does not establish a new standalone citrulline efficacy claim.
