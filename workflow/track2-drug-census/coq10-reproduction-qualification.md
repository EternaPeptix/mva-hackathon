# CoQ10: reproductive function, chromosome surrogates and a Bub1b transcript null

CoQ10 merits a **normal-tissue reproductive-function reserve**, based on actual aged-mouse ovarian, spindle-alignment and live-pup litter-size benefits. The evidence does not establish BUBR1 restoration or MVA efficacy. The mouse study actually measured **Bub1b mRNA**, with no reported significant positive effect in Supplemental Figure S2A; it must not be described as unmeasured. A small human oral randomized trial was null. A separate human culture study reports maturation and polar-body chromosome benefits, but its full text and linked commentary were not accessible in this check, so their unresolved methods remain explicit.

## Aged mice: retain the functional benefit and its unit

The 2015 study treated nine-month-old ICR retired breeders; young comparators were seven- to eight-week-old virgin females. CoQ10 came from Sigma-Aldrich or Advanced Orthomolecular Research and was dissolved in sesame oil. This **injection** experiment is not an oral-supplement experiment. Oocyte analyses followed 12–13 weeks; the Figure 1 follicle caption specifies 15 weeks. Breeding began after 12 weeks of pretreatment, with treatment continuing for up to another month and stopping when pregnancy was detected (about 10.5 days after mating). Different assay schedules should not be flattened into one exposure.

Native Figure 1 supports primordial and secondary follicle-count benefits (P=.01); the primary-follicle comparison is P=.059. The aged treated ovulation comparison is P=.05 and remains below young controls. Supplemental S1A separately shows a positive CoQ10 ovulation comparison, P<.02.

Litter size is a substantive organism-level positive: treated old dams exceeded old controls (P<.01), and the treated-versus-young comparison was nonsignificant. This is not an equivalence test. The plotted old groups are **15 and 11 successful litters**, while Methods enrolled 21 control and 12 treated dams, of whom 15 and 11 delivered. Do not represent the plot as an all-enrolled pregnancy rate or infer a tested pregnancy-rate difference. Each aged dam produced only one litter in the trial.

Figure 3A shows fewer abnormal spindles and misaligned chromosomes in treated aged oocytes (60 young,73 old,51 treated oocytes scored). It measures tubulin/DAPI morphology and chromosome positioning, **not chromosome number, direct embryo euploidy or faithful postfertilization divisions**. Donor counts for these cell-level comparisons and a donor-clustered analysis are not clearly supplied. The intervention unit is the mouse; multiple oocytes and three-oocyte RNA pools are not independent treated animals. Random allocation and blinded scoring are not specified in the inspected main.

ATP and oxygen-probe results improve alongside other mitochondrial measures. Importantly, MitoSOX signal **decreased with age and increased with treatment** here; generic antioxidant language must not turn this into an observed ROS-lowering result. Table S1 confirms greater CoQ10 in whole ovaries:67.2±1.0 ng/mg protein with vehicle,227.2±34.3 after injections and 766.9±144.6 with LiQsorb (n=3–5 mice/group, both treated P<.01). These are whole-ovary exposure measures, not isolated-oocyte concentrations. The authors could not directly measure oocyte CoQ and did not detect a significant age reduction in total ovarian CoQ9/10.

## The direct Bub1b result and the distinct genetic model

Native S2A includes a **Bub1b qRT-PCR panel** for young, old and old-plus-CoQ10 oocytes, without a significance marker. Its caption describes the plotted transcripts as not significantly changed with age and/or treatment. Table S2 provides Bub1b primers. This is a nonpositive transcript result, not protein equivalence, proven absence of all effects, or a rescue of BubR1 supply. No BubR1 protein or Bub1b-mediated functional-rescue experiment is reported in the inspected material. The axis says “Reference/Target,” while Methods describes delta-Ct normalization to β-actin; raw Ct data are unavailable, so bar height must not be converted by eye into a validated RNA fold change.

The genetic experiments instead disrupt **Pdss2**, a CoQ-biosynthesis component, with oocyte-restricted ZP3-Cre. Untreated mutants lose follicles and develop mitochondrial/alignment defects and infertility. Rescue exposure is **LiQsorb (Tishcon)0.4 mg/mL in drinking water**, given to mothers during pregnancy and to offspring after weaning, with lactational exposure described. Figure 5 refers to birth through seven weeks; Results also describes evaluation near two months. Exact fetal/milk exposure and ingested mg/kg are unknown. This is early preventive exposure, not demonstrated delayed reversal of established adult infertility.

Figure 5 supports partial restoration of follicle reserve, ovulation, ATP and chromosome alignment. The respiring mitochondrial pool remains unchanged with rescue (Figure 5C, P=NS). The study does **not** establish live-birth rescue in treated Pdss2-mutant females; aged wild-type litter benefit cannot be transferred to that arm. Young wild-type LiQsorb controls show no significant improvement in the displayed breeding, ovulation, reserve and mitochondrial measures.

The paper also compares untreated human GV oocytes from eight younger and eight older women, one oocyte per patient. Its COQ6 age association is neither a human intervention result nor a BUB1B assay.

## Human oral trial: controlled null, incomplete cohort accounting

The 2014 double-blind trial randomized 39 women aged 35–43 (17 CoQ10,22 placebo). Only 27 started supplements (12/15);24 were described as completing IVF, polar-body biopsy and transfer (10/14), versus a planned 27 per arm. The primary outcome was euploid eggs per retrieval. Clinical pregnancy rates were 33.3% versus 26.7%, and live-birth rates 25% versus 26.7%; no outcome difference reached significance. Higher embryo-quality point estimates also remain nonsignificant.

The preparation was micronized AOR CoQ10 in 100 mg softgels,600 mg once daily with breakfast; matched placebo is described as softgels/gelatin capsules containing rice oil/starch. The paper does not independently establish the exact redox state, batch chemistry or ovarian concentration. Stimulation protocols and ICSI were shared clinical care, not additional CoQ10 efficacy arms. Treatment began two months before stimulation, continued through the IVF cycle, and stopped if pregnant; up to three cycles were planned.

One or two polar bodies were sampled after fertilization and analyzed by array CGH, described as covering all 24 chromosome targets. This is a **maternal meiotic/oocyte inference**, not an embryo karyotype and not a test of paternal chromosome errors or postzygotic mosaicism. The study was stopped because of concerns about **polar-body biopsy** impairing embryo quality/implantation, not demonstrated CoQ10 toxicity.

Several internal denominators remain unresolved:114 zygotes are reported but arm totals 41+74 equal 115;24 participants are described as completing transfer, but three are later said to have no transfer; pregnancy/live-birth percentages lack explicit denominators; and stimulation-protocol percentages do not transparently match the reported analysis groups. Table 2 gives percentage means with error terms; Methods lists Welch t tests without fully explaining patient weighting or clustering of multiple polar bodies. Do not reconstruct a clean intention-to-treat benefit or treat embryos as independently randomized patients. There is no detailed adverse-event table establishing long-term safety.

## Human culture study and commentary: access remains incomplete

The 2020 abstract describes 45 older donors (38–46 years) and 18 donors aged≤30. In addition to maturation and oocyte-aneuploidy findings above, it reports chromosome-aneuploidy frequency 4.1% versus 7.0%, P=.012. These are real **source-reported positives at abstract depth** and must be retained. The young group showed no demonstrated benefit; this contrast alone is not a formal age-by-treatment interaction.

Neither exact redox form/vehicle nor sibling-oocyte allocation, donor-level clustering, polar-body sampling scheme, NGS chromosome coverage, assay failures or downstream developmental outcomes can be verified from the abstract. No human oral-dose equivalence follows from 50 µmol/L culture exposure. Lack of a reported live-birth or BUB1B assay in the abstract is not proof that the inaccessible full text contains none.

The linked [PMID 32622659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32622659/), Robert Casper's *A battery boost for old eggs?*, is an editorial/comment, not a correction or retraction. It has no supplied abstract. Its body and scientific position remain unread. Casper also participated in the other two papers; the comment is not independent efficacy replication.

Official publisher metadata marks both articles as open archive, but publisher body/PDF requests returned 403 and the official FULL API returned 401. Default API responses contained only bibliographic core data. Exact-source public searching produced no verified full body. Failed PDF responses are retained as HTML and receive no PDF/full-text reading credit. No access controls were bypassed and no authors were contacted.

## Other actual compounds, reuse and disposition

The mouse pilot actually tested **alpha-lipoic acid 33 mg/kg** and **resveratrol 10 mg/kg**, subcutaneously three times weekly in sesame oil for at least 12 weeks. Neither significantly improved ovulation in S1A. These are narrow negative comparators, not general inefficacy findings. ALA stereochemistry is not specified; the earlier oral-paper background's “R-ALA” wording must not establish the identity of the 2015 reagent. Resveratrol already has a ledger appraisal. **Antimycin 8 µM for 30 min** is an ATP/oxygen-assay inhibition control, not a restorative lead; a specific antimycin subtype is not stated.

The 2014 oral paper cites a 2009 conference abstract for preliminary mouse results. That source was not read; it is not independently counted replication of the 2015 work. The later Nanjing IVM study, Toronto oral trial and mouse study have distinct designs, but no participant/animal-ID reuse audit was possible. Their multiple doses, panels and genetic arms are not additional independent studies.

At the initial source review, the 216-assessment ledger contained no CoQ10/ubiquinol/ubiquinone/ubidecarenone match. This integration adds one distinct CoQ10 assessment without changing the nominees. CoQ10 is a credible source-grounded reproductive-function reserve; **oral human efficacy, BubR1 restoration and BUB1B/MVA compensation remain unestablished**. The culture study's stronger chromosome-surrogate claim still needs its actual Methods and full results qualified.
