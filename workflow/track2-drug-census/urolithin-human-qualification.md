# Urolithin A: two human muscle trials, bounded qualification

**Actual oral urolithin A has randomized, placebo-controlled human muscle-function signals worth retaining.** The strongest are increased hand-muscle fatigue resistance at two months in older adults and increased hamstring/knee-flexion torque at four months in middle-aged adults. Neither study established its primary endpoint. The older trial's endurance advantage was not significant at four months; the middle-aged trial's walking and aerobic-endurance claims rely on within-group gains or between-group trends. This supports an **exploratory downstream muscle-function comparator**, not demonstrated BUB1B restoration, MVA compensation, or superiority to existing nominees.

This report qualifies [Liu et al., JAMA Network Open 2022, PMID35050355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8777576/), DOI10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.44279, and [Singh et al., Cell Reports Medicine 2022, PMID35584623](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9133463/), DOI10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100633. Full scientific main text and the scientific supplements were read, with native figures inspected. Registry and protocol scope is stated below. Raw assets remain external. This integration adds a distinct urolithin A assessment; the current nominations remain provisional.

ENERGIZE fatigue testing counts repeated voluntary contractions at70% of measured maximum voluntary contraction, starting60 contractions/minute and increasing10/minute each minute. This is fatigue resistance, not evidence that maximal force increased. ATLAS measured reciprocal concentric knee extension/flexion at60°/second, using the mean of five peak torque scores and the highest score. Both are more direct muscle-function evidence than a biomarker alone, but neither establishes improved daily independence, disease progression or MVA muscle performance.

**Source transcription discrepancy:** the JAMA structured abstract explicitly gives the UA leg ATPmax change as −0.03 (0.10)mM/s, supporting the0.10 SD used above; the running Results XML instead prints −0.03 [10]mM/s. Both official abstract representations agree on0.10, and native eFigure1 shows the small-scale distribution. The Results discrepancy remains recorded rather than silently repaired.

**Confidence intervals:** numerical adjusted95% CIs for these clinical UA–placebo contrasts are **not supplied in the main text, numerical tables or inspected scientific supplements**. ENERGIZE Methods and captions say95% CIs were calculated, while Fig2 displays group means with SEM; those are not the contrast CI. ATLAS provides nominal P values and plotted groups without numerical adjusted contrast CIs. No individual participant data were publicly retrieved; the data statements route additional data to author requests, which were not made. Accordingly, the compact outcome JSON leaves those CIs unavailable. It does not reconstruct ANCOVA/mixed-model CIs from raw means, SEM, scattered dots, or assumed denominators. The18.3m/83.7/35.7 differences above are transparent arithmetic differences of reported means, not adjusted estimates.

## Prespecification, multiplicity and missing observations

ENERGIZE explicitly reports that its original primary endpoints—hand ATPmax and hand endurance—were changed **after the study started** to6-minute walk and hand ATPmax. The article says a statistical analysis plan was prepared before unblinding. Supplied Supplement1 is protocolV6 dated05June2020, with statistical sections on pages59–62; page60 says a detailed SAP would be prepared before unblinding. A separately dated final SAP and the original protocol versions were not located. This limits verification of the exact final secondary analyses; it does not establish that the study was unblinded during amendment.

The amendment was informed by the other trial. Protocol pages8/59 explicitly identify **NCT03464500**, cite its completed walking results (UA33.43±51.07m,n25;placebo−0.52±59.08m,n27), and explain revising the primary endpoint/sample-size expectations. They describe stopping at66 enrolled amid COVID-19 recruitment constraints. Therefore, the studies are distinct cohorts, but not independent endpoint-selection histories. The raw ATLAS summary quoted there is not a new replicate or a significant adjusted treatment contrast.

ENERGIZE's revised primary hierarchy controls the two primary tests by testing ATPmax confirmatorily only after a successful walk result. Secondary and biomarker findings should remain exploratory. Its synopsis/current registry foreground percentage endurance change; detailed protocol page61 also lists **absolute leg contraction change**, so the absolute leg result is not assumed unplanned. The differing percentage/absolute and ANCOVA/Wilcoxon results are retained. No family-wide multiplicity correction for the secondary muscle findings is established; plasma analyses explicitly used none.

ENERGIZE randomized66 but native Fig1 labels only30/arm in the ITT analysis and25/arm in PP. It shows3/arm not receiving the intervention and withdrawals of2 consent+1 AE per arm; the prose describes6 lost to follow-up. Ten participants could not complete some secondary tests due to COVID restrictions. Protocol page60 defines the full analysis set as dosed participants with a post-dose primary measurement; page62 specifies **valid-case analysis without LOCF/imputation**. Thus the published “ITT” wording should not be equated with complete outcome ascertainment for all66 randomized participants. The source does not provide a clear endpoint-by-visit denominator table for the fatigue analyses. Reported compliance was97% UA and96% placebo.

ATLAS randomized88;79 completed, with2 placebo,2 low-dose and5 high-dose discontinuations. Five additional low-compliance participants were excluded from PP, yielding74 (native flow:25/26/23). The article says all88 enter main clinical ITT analysis, but Methods defines ITT as treated participants with post-randomization efficacy information. Linear or repeated mixed-effects models adjust for baseline; a detailed clinical missing-data/imputation sensitivity analysis is not supplied. Do not substitute PP counts for clinical ITT estimates or assume missing outcomes were imputed. All endpoints are called exploratory in its statistical section even though peak power is the designated primary. Tukey adjustment is described for baseline pairwise comparisons after a significant baseline test, not demonstrated family-wide control of the numerous clinical endpoints/doses/time points. Biomarker multiplicity is explicitly uncorrected; separate omics analyses use their stated thresholds.

## Biological measurements and harm observations

The trials demonstrate exposure to UA and measure mitochondrial/inflammatory markers. They do **not** establish that mitophagy causally mediated the muscle-function changes. ENERGIZE's ATPmax was null. ATLAS has RNA/proteomic enrichment and selected pre/post immunoblots, including phospho-Parkin and OXPHOS proteins. Much of the omics analysis compares pre/post **within each arm**, then removes pathways significant in placebo. “Significant in treatment but not placebo” is not by itself a significant treatment-by-time difference. RNA enrichment uses adjustedP<.1; proteomic pathway inputs can include protein FDR<.25; the westerns use six paired subjects per group. NativeS7 shows UBE2N P=.06 and phospho-Parkin/total-Parkin P=.09, whereas phospho-Parkin/tubulin in main Fig5 is significant at500mg. These are selected, normalization-dependent mechanistic signals, not direct mitophagy-flux or mediation proof.

No BUB1B/BubR1-specific treatment result is reported in either main paper or the examined supplements. The targeted protein panels are not BubR1 panels. TableS2 is a selected protein table, not a complete detected-protein inventory. A symbol/name/accession search (including BUB1B, BubR1 andO60566) adds no hit, but is not a validated protein null. The deposited transcriptome was not mined for a new post hoc BUB1B claim. No chromosome-fidelity, BUB1B-variant, MVA, cancer-surveillance or pediatric outcome was tested.

ENERGIZE reports33 AEs total (18 UA,15 placebo),31 treatment-emergent AEs (16 UA,15 placebo), no serious AEs and one AE-related withdrawal per arm. Categories include laboratory abnormalities and two cardiac events in UA (angina and sinus bradycardia); these counts do not establish causation. The table supplies event totals, not a clean count of distinct affected people. The paper reports no significant safety-test difference. ATLAS reports102 events among45 people:42 events/17 participants with placebo,24/13 at500mg,36/15 at1000mg. SupplementS1 reports overall P=.113 and includes headaches, GI symptoms and biopsy-related musculoskeletal complaints. Its percentages use **event counts** as denominator (for example8 headaches/36 high-dose events=22.2%), not30 randomized people. Neither four-month study establishes long-term, rare-event, MVA or pediatric safety.

Both trials were funded by Amazentis. ENERGIZE explicitly gives the sponsor roles in design, conduct, data management/analysis/interpretation, manuscript preparation and submission; relevant employee, board/shareholder and patent interests are disclosed. ATLAS likewise involves sponsor employees, board members and scientific advisers in design/analysis/authorship. Distinct Seattle and Ontario recruitment cohorts do not mean independent sponsorship or analysis teams.

## Registry boundaries and source discrepancies

The live official API records were read for identification, dates, design, arms, outcomes, sponsor and sites. Both currently contain **no posted results module or attached protocol/SAP documents**. The public history view was inaccessible and the attempted history API returned403; historical versions were not verified.

- ENERGIZE was first posted14September2017; current actual start15February2018, primary completion25September2020 and study completion28October2020. The article reports March2018–July2020. Its current registry lists additional outcomes absent from this publication, including SPPB, cycle performance, grip/leg strength, quality of life and optional biopsy endpoints. Their absence here is unresolved, not proof of failure or selective suppression. Current hand-ATP timing2/4months also differs from article baseline/4months.
- ATLAS was first submitted5February2018, submitted meeting QC7March and publicly posted14March2018; current actual start6March2018. Current registry says90 actual enrollment and September2019 completion; the article says88 randomized and last completionAugust2019. Retain the difference. A current matching primary-power/secondary-strength entry supports current concordance, not proof the original record was unchanged. Other registered outcomes not quantitatively reported here include chair stands, quality of life and activity questionnaire results.
- ATLAS Table2 provides dose-order peak-power changes4.3% at500mg and3.7% at1000mg; its Discussion reverses/changes nearby rounded values. Use Table2/native panel for the outcome table. EstimatedVO₂max P differs between main(.058) and nativeS2B(.068); both remain nonsignificant. Caption shorthand must not upgrade the quadriceps null.
- Current Europe PMC metadata lists both as randomized research articles and supplies no comment/correction list. This is a bounded metadata check, not an exhaustive notices search.
