# SuperbaBoost krill oil: parent muscle trial and exploratory subgroup report

**Disposition: retain a substantive, preparation-specific controlled human-strength comparator with completed independent review; no established BUB1B-MVA efficacy or superiority to provisional niacin restoration/apitegromab compensation.** This integrated qualification adds the exact studied preparation to the comparison. The frozen source packet preceded repository admission; its source-stage history is preserved below.

The parent trial reports controlled six-month improvements in knee-extensor torque and grip strength, plus an ultrasound thickness change. Those are meaningful measured outcomes and should not be demoted to biomarkers. Its walking, chair-rise, whole-body muscle-mass, rate-of-torque-development and quality-of-life findings are nonpositive. Reporting and missing-data limits constrain confidence. The later subgroup paper analyzes the same cohort and adds no independent replication.

## Identity and administered exposure

The live census at the start of this work was HEAD `8251103f7fd7f26e7d591fb325c60967a4f3d487`, with 279 decisions. Exact inspection found free-EPA (`new-epa`) and Lovaza ethyl-esters (`new-lovaza-muscle`) decisions, but no krill/SuperbaBoost decision. A fresh identity check before freeze confirmed HEAD `ccf7ff8ed64485bd7867c46a2d7c9f5e0426b57f`, still 279 decisions and no krill/SuperbaBoost decision; the earlier check is preserved as source-stage history. These existing records are context, not newly read evidence. The discovery pointer is batch 08 order 41, the secondary report.

The studied product was **SuperbaBoost krill oil from Aker Biomarine Antarctic AS**, 4 g/day for six months: four 1-g capsules, two with lunch and two with dinner. Each gram supplied 322 mg long-chain n-3 PUFA, including 193 mg EPA and 96 mg DHA, plus 79 mg choline. Simple dose multiplication yields **772 mg EPA, 384 mg DHA, 316 mg choline and 1,288 mg total long-chain n-3 PUFA daily**; 4 g is the oil dose, not the EPA/DHA dose.

The introduction describes krill oil generally as containing more than 40% of its long-chain n-3 PUFA in phospholipid form and discusses astaxanthin. It does not establish the exact tested batch’s phospholipid fraction, molecular composition, astaxanthin dose, capsule excipients, oxidation status or independent batch assay. Do not import a modern retail specification. This mixture cannot inherit free-EPA, Lovaza, generic fish-oil, arbitrary krill-product or approved-medicine equivalence.

The comparator was 4 g/day mixed vegetable oil: extra-virgin cold-pressed olive, refined maize, refined palm-kernel and medium-chain triglyceride oils in a 4:4:3:2 ratio. Its stated long-chain n-3 PUFA concentration was 4 mg/g, with less than 1 mg/g EPA and DHA. Both capsule types were supplied by Aker and described as identical in appearance and taste. This is the tested oil-versus-oil comparison, not an isolated EPA, DHA, choline or astaxanthin effect.

Blood fatty-acid changes support biological exposure: the parent reports relative-to-control six-month increases in EPA of 214% (95% CI 166–262), DHA 36% (24–48), and omega-3 index 61% (49–73). Krill-group omega-3 index was 6.5% at baseline, 9.7% at six weeks and 10.0% at six months (Table 4). No pill-count adherence estimate, muscle-tissue concentration or ingredient-specific mediation was established in the read materials.

## Population, design and denominators

This was a parallel, randomized, blinded trial in community-dwelling Glasgow adults above 65 years, BMI below 35 kg/m², reporting less than one hour of structured exercise weekly. The authors say none met probable, confirmed or severe sarcopenia criteria. Significant disease, ambulatory limitations, muscle-affecting medication, anticoagulants, seafood allergy, supplement use and frequent oily-fish intake were excluded under the paper’s criteria. The result is not a trial in children, MVA, frailty or established muscle disease.

An independent colleague generated 1:1 allocation using online software after baseline assessment. The lead outcome assessor and statistical analysis were blinded; blinding success was not assessed. Participants maintained usual diet/activity and attended fasting visits at baseline, six weeks and six months, avoiding exercise for 48 hours before visits. There was no resistance-training intervention.

The native flow diagram establishes:

|---|---:|---:|
| Randomized and received intervention | 53 | 49 |
| Lost to follow-up | 2 | 2 |
| Excluded from analysis because final visits were cancelled during COVID-19 closure | 2 | 2 |

Overall, 120 were assessed, 18 excluded before randomization, and 102 randomized. The krill loss reasons were difficulty swallowing capsules and health issues; control reasons were loss of interest and health issues. Individual counts by reason or causal attribution were not supplied. The trial stopped short of its recruitment target because of lockdown and time constraints. The power calculation sought 50 per group, with 120 planned to allow attrition.

**The paper labels its analysis intention-to-treat, but the flow explicitly shows only 94 analyzed.** No imputation, missing-data model or full-102 sensitivity analysis is described. Preserve both facts; do not claim demonstrated full randomized-population ITT. Individual outcome/timepoint denominators are not separately supplied in the main tables. The later report explicitly selects 94 participants with complete data.

## Parent outcomes: positive and nonpositive findings

The parent uses two-way repeated-measures ANOVA for time, group and their interaction, followed by Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise t-tests when an interaction is significant. No overall correction across the many secondary/exploratory endpoints is described. The percentages below are the authors’ reported treatment effects from percentage changes, not newly calculated effects or digitized bars.

The main and Fig. 2 also report higher final six-month torque versus control after post-hoc correction. | Primary outcome. Actual force/torque evidence; not a muscle-mass proxy. |

The authors call the findings clinically significant, but acknowledge no established knee-strength or grip MCID; the knee threshold was estimated from another context. These results justify research comparison, not a verified clinical benefit threshold in MVA. There is no follow-up after supplementation ends, so durability beyond ongoing six-month exposure is unknown.

## Registration and hierarchy

The current registry agrees on a primary baseline-to-six-month knee-extensor strength outcome. It lists grip, thickness, SPPB, body fat, lean mass, glucose, lipids, CRP and erythrocyte fatty acids as secondary. Main Methods 2.4 names a narrower secondary list and calls all other outcomes exploratory; the abstract calls neuromuscular measures secondary. These differences are preserved.

The study began **7 March 2018**; first submission was **26 July 2019** and first posting **7 August 2019**, before completion on **10 March 2020** but after recruitment began. A fully prospective pre-recruitment hierarchy is therefore not established. Only the current record was recovered; no historical-version comparison or separate protocol/SAP was read. Current registry masking is “triple” (participant, investigator, outcomes assessor), while the main describes double blinding with blinded assessment/statistical analysis. The registry has no posted results and states no planned IPD sharing. These are reporting facts, not grounds to discard the measured outcomes.

## Secondary report: same cohort, exploratory interactions

PMID 41344001 reuses the same 94 complete cases. It uses mixed-design ANOVA with time within participants and group plus sex/age/BMI between participants, estimated marginal means and Bonferroni pairwise comparisons. It explicitly lacks power for subgroup interactions. Null interactions do **not** demonstrate equivalence or universality of response, and this paper is not replication.

For sex, the actual group×sex×time interactions are P=.206 for torque, .188 for grip and .893 for thickness. The report gives controlled changes of 11.70 Nm (3.05–20.35) in women and 20.24 Nm (10.34–30.15) in men; grip 2.93 kg (1.81–4.04) and 4.07 kg (2.80–5.35). These sex-specific estimates do not prove a sex difference. Walking and chair-rise group×time findings remain nonpositive (P=.802 and .419).

Unlike an inference from different within-group P values, **a formal M-wave group×sex×time interaction is reported (P=.009)**. Reported treatment effects are 1.94 mV (−0.80–4.69) in women and 7.61 mV (4.46–10.76) in men. This is an exploratory signal among many tests. BMI×group×time for RMS is also nominally significant (P=.030), but adjusted post-hoc response comparisons are all nonsignificant. Neither establishes a sex/BMI-specific treatment recommendation or a causal strength mechanism.

The native subgroup paper contains unresolved inconsistencies:

- Table 1 lists krill BMI subgroups n=24 and n=26, totaling 50 despite krill n=49 overall; no denominator is silently changed.
- The BMI Results section repeatedly labels several interactions “Age” instead of BMI. The printed BMI cutoffs (≤24.9 and >25 kg/m²) also leave the exact boundary unclear.
- The male control M-wave change is printed as −3.71 [1.58–6.05] mV with P=.059. Its sign, interval and P value are not mutually interpretable as printed; they are retained without repair.
- The age-specific grip effects/CIs repeat the sex-specific figures, while Table 4 and Fig. 3 present different age-group descriptive patterns. Exact age-effect reporting requires clarification; no replacement estimate is reconstructed.
- Tables 2 and 4 omit explicit labels above the two sex/age column blocks. Fig. 2’s BMI torque pattern conflicts with Table 3’s subgroup ordering. These source labels and visual patterns are preserved, not reassigned.
- Figure captions say controls show no change while appending “all P<.05,” and the abstract refers broadly to physical-function increases despite the nonpositive walking/chair-rise Results. The formal results control interpretation.

Full printed table cells, figures and source text are retained in the packet. The discrepancies reduce confidence in detailed subgroup interpretation without erasing the parent trial’s controlled strength findings.

## Safety, disclosures and equal comparison criteria

No systematic adverse-event table, explicit zero-event finding or safety analysis denominator is reported in the read papers. The CONSORT checklist marks harms and protocol access “NA.” Swallowing difficulty and health-related withdrawals are visible in the flow, but their attribution and severity remain unknown. This is not evidence of proven safety, pediatric tolerability or chronic harm.

Both papers report a Saudi government studentship for SA and no conflicts of interest. The parent states that Aker supplied both oil types; both papers state that the manufacturer had no role in design, conduct or analysis. Ingredient attribution, independent manufacturing verification and all possible sponsor roles are not established beyond those statements.
