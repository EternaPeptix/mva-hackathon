# HMB after liver transplantation: source-specific qualification

**Independent source review: ACCEPT, no required corrections.** Retain this bounded study appraisal as a separate evidence row; it demonstrates neither comparative functional efficacy nor a calcium-HMB product identity.

PMID **31546969**, DOI **10.3390/nu11092259**, NCT **03234920**. This is an open-label, small randomized adult transplant study with actual grip, walking and timed-up-and-go measurements. It provides arm trajectories and within-arm tests, but the recovered sources do **not report a postbaseline between-treatment effect estimate, confidence interval, or treatment-by-time test** for those outcomes. The positive within-HMB findings must not be converted into a controlled functional benefit. Equally, identical final grip means do not settle a baseline-adjusted treatment contrast. No new analysis is performed here.

The administered material is described only as HMB. Neither the original main nor its own current registry identifies calcium salt versus free acid, supplier, brand, batch, purity or excipients. It therefore remains a source-specific preparation with unresolved chemistry, separate from the existing Kyowa and TSI calcium-HMB evidence. No automatic alias or preparation-equivalence link is created. Its accepted appraisal does not promote it as a Track 2 nominee.

## Design, population and preparation

The single-center Sapienza study screened 41 transplant recipients and enrolled 22 men: 12 assigned HMB and 10 control. Thirteen women, one pediatric patient, three people moving out of region and two declining participation were excluded. Participants were clinically stable, out of hospital, and enrolled 30 days after liver transplantation. The paper gives mean age 59.9 ± 6.2 years, mean MELD 16.2 ± 7.5, and approximately 72% hepatocellular carcinoma within Milan criteria. It was not exclusively a sarcopenic cohort. Low compliance was an exclusion; generalizability to unstable recipients, women, children and MVA is unestablished.

HMB was prescribed at **3 g/day**, divided into **1.5 g in 200 mL fruit juice twice daily for 12 weeks**. Controls received 200 mL fruit juice twice daily. Juice composition, energy matching beyond common volume, supplement calcium and other excipients were not specified. No sensory-matched placebo or assessor blinding is described; the current registry explicitly states no masking. The paper describes computer-generated allocation; the current registry describes blocks of four, 1:1 allocation, an independent statistician and code knowledge limited to the physician. Actual concealment implementation is not independently verified.

Both groups received nutrition advice and encouragement of an active lifestyle, without prescribed structured exercise. The main methods target 25–30 kcal/kg/day and 1.2 g protein/kg/day; its discussion gives 25–30 kcal/kg/day ±10% and 1–1.2 g/kg/day. The registry narrative instead gives 20–25 kcal/kg/day ±10% and 1.2 g/kg/day. Improved intake is reported qualitatively, without arm-specific follow-up intake or activity data. Immunosuppressive drug levels were monitored; exact agents, doses, steroid taper and arm-specific changes are not provided. Introductory discussion of immunosuppressants must not be treated as documented individual exposure.

## Denominators, hierarchy and analysis

One control recipient died from sepsis during the second month after transplantation and was removed from further analysis. Table 1 presents enrolled baseline groups of 10 control/12 HMB; Table 2 and reported follow-up results use **9 control/12 HMB survivors completing T12**. The paper does not report an intention-to-treat analysis, death-inclusive estimand, imputation or sensitivity analysis. No endpoint-specific missingness beyond these completer labels is supplied.

The article names muscle mass at 12 weeks as primary and function, safety and longer follow-up as secondary. The current registry specifically names **DEXA FFMI at 3–4 months after transplantation as primary**, with later FFMI, 6MWT, TUG, grip, hospital days, metabolic syndrome and tolerability as secondary. The paper reports only minor nonsignificant FFMI changes while emphasizing ASMI and grip. ASMI should not silently replace the registered FFMI primary outcome.

The paper calls the sample size of 22 arbitrary for a pilot. The registry narrative instead describes 12 per arm, 80% power and an expected 1.2 kg/m² FFMI difference, with SD 1. Its statistical narrative specifies Mann–Whitney and ANOVA followed by t-tests; the paper methods mention Mann–Whitney and paired-data analysis, whereas Table 2 says Student's t-test. The exact fitted longitudinal model, covariates and multiplicity control are not reported. There is no reported baseline-adjusted grip contrast, interaction or inferential comparison of the arm changes.

The registry's actual start is 25 September 2015; first submission was 17 July 2017 and first posting 1 August 2017. Thus registration followed study start. Last sponsor update was posted 11 March 2019, after the listed September 2018 completion. The paper describes the transplant screening interval as March 2015–September 2017 and ethics approval on 23 July 2015. These are different chronology statements; the screening interval is not silently relabeled actual randomized enrollment. Historical versions were unavailable, so prospective stability of the current outcome and analysis descriptions is unverified.

These are genuine observations from concurrent randomized groups, including directly measured final scores. The HMB group began with a lower mean grip, improved within arm and reached a similar mean to controls; this remains compatible with multiple baseline-adjusted explanations. Neither different within-arm significance nor equal final means proves benefit, null effect or equivalence. The paper's causal conclusion exceeds its presented comparative analysis. Walking and TUG are actual observed, nonpositive within-arm outcomes, not absent measurements and not proven between-treatment nulls.

Figures 3–4 show individual trajectories, not treatment contrasts or confidence intervals. They were inspected visually but not digitized. Figure 1 is a literature-derived mechanistic diagram; it supplies no trial measurement of satellite-cell renewal, mTOR/IGF-1 engagement or fidelity.

## Reporting conflicts and missing outcomes

- Table 2 marks HMB T12 ASMI with an asterisk defined as P<.001, but the text gives P=.006. T12 grip text P=.001 also does not literally satisfy that strict footnote threshold. Neither value is silently corrected.
- The FMI paragraph attributes a significant control increase to 7.1 ± 2 to 8.0 ± 3.2, values resembling the HMB row; Table 2 instead marks control 7.3 ± 4.4 to 8.7 ± 4.7. Attribution and exact inference remain unresolved.
- Table 1 has four control subjects below the ASMI fifth percentile, while the results describe five controls as sarcopenic. Whether the larger count reflects the combined ASMI-or-FFMI definition is not specified; do not silently equate these counts. HMB ASMI baseline is 6.9 in Table 1 and 6.8 in Table 2 despite twelve HMB participants in both labels.
- The native control ASMI plot's mostly stable/rising traces appear difficult to reconcile with the lower Table 2 T12 mean. This is a visual reporting concern only; no individual values, corrected mean or statistics were reconstructed.
- Six-month assessments are planned in main and registry but not reported in Table 2 or the outcome figures. Hospitalization, metabolic syndrome and tolerability scales lack complete endpoint-specific results. The narrative's diabetes percentages (57% control, 66% HMB) have no clear analyzed denominators and should not be converted into counts.

## Durability and safety

The prescribed intervention ends at twelve weeks, with T12 observation later. This offers long follow-up after a nominally short course, but actual cessation, subsequent supplement use, nutrition/activity changes and drug exposure were not quantitatively documented. Paper methods define T12 from enrollment, while the registry and discussion often frame it from transplantation. Approximately nine months after the scheduled course is an interpretation of the methods, not a verified exposure-free interval. Persisting within-arm grip/ASMI change does not establish durable treatment benefit in the absence of a comparative analysis.

The authors report no HMB-attributed side effects or discontinuations and stable monitored immunosuppressant levels. Compliance was assessed only from patient reports, without a numeric adherence estimate or objective assay. Transaminases, urea nitrogen, creatinine, lipids and glucose are said not to change significantly; numerical data are not shown. Readmission complications are described as equally distributed without counts. The control sepsis death remains a serious clinical event and the reason for exclusion; no treatment-related interpretation is established. This small selected study does not demonstrate safety equivalence, pediatric safety, or absence of uncommon harms.

Funding was Bando di ricerca Sapienza 2017/201c; authors declared no conflicts. The registry states no drug-company sponsorship. No named supplement supplier or analytical product certificate was found.

## Equal criteria and identity disposition

- **Function:** real grip and task measurements in randomized groups, but no reported comparative efficacy analysis; grip improvement is within arm, and walking/TUG are nonpositive within arm.
- **Exposure:** nominal oral HMB dose and duration are specified; chemical form, manufacturer, adherence and objective exposure remain uncertain.
- **Durability:** post-course observations exist, but sustained causal benefit and verified off-treatment exposure are unestablished.
- **Renewal:** no measured productive cell/tissue renewal; DXA and circumference do not demonstrate it.
- **Fidelity/restoration:** no BUBR1, chromosome-segregation, aneuploidy, genomic-fidelity or BUB1B-MVA outcomes.

Against provisional niacin restoration and apitegromab compensation, this source deserves the same preservation of controlled design, actual function, uncertainty, harms and preparation limits. It establishes no comparative MVA efficacy or superiority and supplies no basis to replace a nominee. It also does not negate the distinct Kyowa gait finding or erase TSI bed-rest functional results.
