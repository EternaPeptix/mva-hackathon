## What the primary antecedents actually support

The directly cited [2005 BUBR1 primary, PMID 16040755](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1366764/), was read in full prose, including Methods, Results, Discussion, Table 1 and all figure captions; Figures 2, 4, 7 and 8 were visually inspected. It studies isolated human BUBR1(1–204), a 36–204 deletion, and PP5(16–181). Air–water adsorption, surface pressure, rheology and microscopy establish physical surface activity. No fatty-acid binding or treatment assay is reported. Arachidonic acid appears as PP5 literature background.

The main interface comparison used 30 µg/mL protein: 1.2 µM BUBR1 and 1.5 µM PP5, pH 7. Methods specify 1–80 µg/mL in 0.02 M phosphate; low-concentration runs used 1 µg/mL (40/50 nM respectively). Pure-buffer baselines were recorded for at least 30 minutes and subtracted. PP5, deletion-construct and pH 2/7 comparisons are biophysical comparators, not validated controls for a fatty-acid-binding test. Surface activity cannot be substituted for ligand binding or MVA rescue. These experimental details must not be assigned to the unread comment.

The actual CommentOn record, [Galat 2006, PMID 16996313](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16996313/), was read at complete-abstract depth only. It describes sequence-attribute comparisons and a proposed FKBP52/FK506 membrane mechanism. Its abstract provides no BUBR1 fatty-acid binding experiment; its precise body claim remains unverified. Calling it an experimentally demonstrated positive BUBR1-binding paper would overstate this reading.

The directly cited [Kang 2001, PMID 11523989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11523989/) was also read at complete-abstract depth only. It reports **PP5** mutagenesis and lipid activation, explicitly including arachidonic acid. This positive result belongs to another protein; it does not establish BUBR1 binding. Assay concentrations and controls were not qualified from its abstract.
