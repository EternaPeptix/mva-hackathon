# Resveratrol: a substantive functional comparator, with an unresolved BUBR1 signal

**Elevate resveratrol to a serious experimental comparator for muscle compensation, and retain a separate, access-limited protein-restoration question.** The strongest admitted evidence is an actual human resveratrol-plus-exercise comparison with significant group-by-time interactions for two muscle outcomes. The rat BUBR1-staining claim is promising enough to pursue, but its full methods and figures have not been verified. Neither result establishes BUB1B-MVA rescue.

This assessment changes the earlier treatment of resveratrol as mainly a toxin-exposed oocyte observation or a computational co-mention. It does not infer efficacy from supplement availability, nor assign resveratrol the NMN/SIRT2 mechanism without an experiment.

## Human muscle function: preserve the between-group positives

In [Alway et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glx089), older adults completed 12 weeks of exercise with resveratrol or placebo. The main paper reports randomized, double-blind assignment and **15 completers per arm**. One placebo and four resveratrol participants withdrew for time commitments, and replacements were recruited. Thirty is therefore the completer count, not the entire randomized population or an intention-to-treat denominator.

The reported **condition-by-group interactions** support additional improvement in average knee-extension power (**P=.004**) and average peak torque over seven repetitions (**P=.038**) at 60 degrees/second. These are stronger evidence than noting significance within one arm only. The resveratrol arm's reported 14.0% power and 14.1% average-torque gains are within-arm changes, not placebo-adjusted effect sizes. The main text reports other muscle-function indices unchanged relative to placebo.

The same paper does **not** establish added maximal oxygen uptake or mitochondrial-volume benefit: the corresponding interactions were nonsignificant. Oxidative DNA-damage reduction also did not differ between arms. The study measured neither BUBR1 nor chromosome fidelity. Tissue-assay participant counts and detailed safety remain unresolved without the inaccessible supplements; hundreds of fibers are subsamples, not hundreds of people. A 60% versus 80% female-composition inconsistency remains in the main text.

## A separate pilot: strength signal, inconclusive registered primary outcome

[Harper et al., 2021, published online 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33068691/) randomized 60 adults aged at least 65 with functional limitations to exercise plus placebo or one of two resveratrol amounts. All arms exercised. The main analysis used mixed models under an intention-to-treat approach; 55 participants had final observed outcomes. Muscle samples came from 22 participants, not all 60. This was a feasibility pilot, not a trial powered for definitive efficacy comparisons.

The [registered primary outcome](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02523274) was walking speed. Its three within-arm change intervals all included zero. Secondary quadriceps-strength changes were **-3.24 Nm [-9.70, 3.23]**, **+10.50 Nm [4.28, 16.70]**, and **+6.15 Nm [-0.31, 12.60]**, respectively. These are within-arm estimates; the paper does not provide a definitive between-arm efficacy test. Positive six-minute-walk changes likewise cannot all be attributed to the supplement. Adverse-event reports were 8, 12 and 7, mostly gastrointestinal or musculoskeletal; this small, short study cannot establish pediatric or MVA safety.

The registry and paper differ slightly on the placebo walk-distance change (9.34 versus 9.45 m), and registry arm-description schedules conflict with the paper. The paper's mitochondrial-lesion prose assigns a negative value to EX500, whereas Figure A.4C depicts that negative value in EX0. No DNA-protection claim is drawn from that inconsistency. The native supplement could not be obtained; its referenced results receive no independent reading credit.

## Relevant counterevidence remains part of the comparison

[Gliemann et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23878368/) reports that, among 27 older men undergoing higher-intensity training, placebo produced a larger increase in maximal oxygen uptake than resveratrol. [Olesen et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24514907/) reports no additive resveratrol benefit for knee-extensor endurance and attenuation of some exercise-associated oxidative-stress and inflammatory changes. These publications arise from an overlapping Copenhagen research cohort and are not independent replications. Here their complete primary abstracts and selected source descriptions were read; their full methods, supplements and linked commentaries were not fully reviewed. The abstracts justify retaining context-dependent null/adverse findings, not declaring universal harm or resolving the disagreement between regimens and populations.

## BUBR1-specific evidence: two different observations

| Source | Actual evidence and current interpretation |
|---|---|
| [Aged-rat marrow study, online 2021 / issue 2023](https://doi.org/10.21608/ejh.2021.94120.1559) | The publisher abstract reports 40 aged rats in four duration groups, improved cell-growth measures, DNA index and anti-BUBR1 immunocytochemical staining. The public PDF repeatedly failed retrieval. Direction, assay specificity, quantification, biological unit, intervention details and alleged genetic stability have not been audited. **Retain as an access-limited restoration lead; do not present it as verified increased functional BUBR1.** |
| [Methylglyoxal-exposed mouse oocytes, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0077960) | Actual resveratrol co-treatment partly improved polar-body extrusion and spindle/chromosome alignment. At the highest tested concentration, extrusion rose from 21.8% to 53.3%, below the 80.5% unexposed control. The BubR1 observation is loss of insult-associated kinetochore staining as arrest is relieved, **not increased total protein**. No chromosome-count, live-lineage, offspring or MVA-muscle endpoint establishes faithful rescue. |

The oocyte paper reports three independent experiments with many oocytes, rather than hundreds of independent animals. Figure 3C's lowest-dose bar conflicts with a repeated numerical value in the Results text; the higher-dose partial benefit is retained without inventing corrected low-dose data. Its Figure 7 is localization imaging, not a total-protein or kinase-activity measurement.

## Competitive decision and falsifiable next step

Compared with niacin, resveratrol has more direct randomized evidence for selected human muscle-function outcomes, albeit small, exercise-dependent and inconsistent across studies. Niacin retains a different advantage: human muscle NAD engagement plus a cross-intervention connection to BUBR1 stability. The unavailable rat paper might strengthen resveratrol's protein-supply rationale, but cannot presently settle that comparison. Compared with apitegromab's disease-specific SMA functional trial, resveratrol's support is less conclusive and comes from aging rather than congenital disease. Neither comparator has demonstrated MVA efficacy.

**Place resveratrol among the serious functional reserves, above a generic antioxidant rationale.** Do not claim it already replaces either nominee. First resolve the rat assay and test exposure-relevant resveratrol in affected/corrected myogenic cells. Measure usable BUBR1 separately from cell-cycle composition, absolute contractile output, new segregation errors and progenitor renewal. Increased staining, cell survival or fiber size alone would not establish the proposed rescue. No food, wine, retail formulation or human regimen inherits the experimental outcomes automatically.
