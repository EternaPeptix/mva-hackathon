# NMN: bounded randomized human functional evidence

**The NMN appraisal includes qualified human functional evidence alongside its unchanged North/Kang mechanistic evidence.** One selected randomized trial reports a favorable walking contrast; a newer, longer trial reports no physical-function benefit despite increased blood NAD. This strengthens NMN as a substantive comparator to niacin's uncontrolled human functional observations, without establishing superior efficacy, an optimal dose, MVA benefit or approved-drug submission eligibility. This existing-entry refinement changes neither the nomination nor the assessment count.

Reviewed 26 September 2026, America/Phoenix; retrievals continued into 27 September UTC. This is a purposive **two-trial** qualification, not an exhaustive NMN review. Yi was selected for actual randomized walking comparisons across three oral doses. The newly published Igarashi trial was selected for its longer duration, older population, strength and walking outcomes, and current counterevidence. Other trials surfaced during selection, including Igarashi 2022, but were not fully qualified or treated as additional evidence in this pass. NMN, NR and niacin remain different administered interventions.

## Yi 2023: reported improvement in treadmill walking

[Yi et al., GeroScience, PMID 36482258](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-022-00705-1) randomized 80 healthy adults aged 40–65 at two Indian clinics to **AbinoNutra NMN** 300, 600 or 900 mg/day, or placebo, for 60 days. The food-grade crystalline powder was manufactured by Aba Chemicals with Abinopharm and packed by Polifarma into 150-mg capsules. The rice-flour placebo group pooled 7, 7 and 6 participants receiving the corresponding capsule counts; the analyzed groups therefore contained **20 participants each**, not 20 placebo participants per dose. Oral administration was before breakfast in the paper. All 80 reportedly completed without protocol violation and entered the per-protocol walking analysis; only HOMA-IR lost two nonfasting measurements.

The six-minute test used a **manual treadmill and its digital odometer**, with measurements at baseline, day 30 and day 60. It should not be relabeled as a standard corridor walk or assigned corridor-based clinical-importance thresholds.

Figure 2 explicitly defines the comparisons as **changes from baseline**, with mixed models for repeated measures. At day 30, all three comparisons against placebo are marked P<.001. The 600-versus-300-mg comparisons are P<.01 at both visits; 900 versus 600 mg is nonsignificant. Table 2 instead reports within-arm tests: the 300-mg arm's P=.23/.079 at days 30/60 does not negate its reported randomized contrast. These rounded group means are not published adjusted treatment-effect estimates. No contrast confidence intervals were supplied, and none is reconstructed. Nonsignificance between the higher doses does not establish equivalence or an optimal dose.

The paper calls serum total NAD (**NAD+ plus NADH**) primary and walking/SF-36 secondary. The [current NCT04823260 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04823260) instead labels NAD, walking and SF-36 as Part I primary outcomes. It describes 80 randomized participants plus 10 separate open-label participants, who must not be added to the randomized denominator. The record was first posted before recruitment, but its original historical version was not read; the hierarchy discrepancy remains unresolved. No full protocol/SAP was recovered. The official CTRI trial-specific record could not be obtained through its security-code search, and no bypass was attempted.

The model formula, covariance structure, covariates, full numerical contrasts and broad multiplicity adjustment are not specified. Numerous outcomes, visits, doses and comparisons therefore warrant **reported, nominal** statistical language. Native Figure 2B/Results support the 300-mg day-30 positive, whereas the Discussion calls it an exception. The supplemental walking plot labels all four clusters “Placebo” and repeats a time label. These defects are preserved, without reassignment of individual points. Table 1 also reports a placebo-versus-300-mg age difference (P=.04) despite the prose's broad balance assertion. The trial is evidence for a qualified functional signal, not an independently reproduced confirmatory analysis.

Nine adverse events occurred in seven participants: six events in five placebo recipients and three in two 300-mg recipients; no events were reported in the higher-dose arms. Authors classified all as mild/moderate and unrelated to treatment, with no serious event or adverse-event withdrawal. This is limited 60-day tolerability evidence. The trial was fully funded by Aba Chemicals and Abinopharm; three authors were company employees, with sponsor-affiliated contributions to design and writing. Serum NAD and laboratory-derived “biological age” do not demonstrate muscle NAD engagement, lifespan benefit, BUBR1 restoration or MVA rescue. The full main reports no BUBR1 or chromosome-fidelity assay.

## Igarashi 2026: increased blood NAD without physical improvement

[Igarashi et al., npj Aging](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00482-7), published **25 September 2026**, is an **Article in Press** that may receive further editing. The complete 15-page manuscript and six-page supplement were obtained from the publisher. The trial randomized **62 adults aged ≥65 years, 31 per arm**, to MIRAILAB BIOSCIENCE NMN **1,250 mg/day** or matched starch/starch-hydrolysate placebo for 24 weeks. The registry specifies five 250-mg NMN tablets or five placebo tablets once daily. The main does not independently certify product content or retail-product equivalence.

Two placebo participants were excluded at week 0, leaving a reported efficacy/safety cohort of 31 NMN and 29 placebo recipients. One NMN recipient discontinued for elevated liver enzymes and one placebo recipient missed week 24; the endpoint tables use **30 versus 28** observed participants. These are not complete observations on all 62 randomized participants, and no full-randomized missing-data analysis is demonstrated.

Hearing was the primary endpoint, confirmed in both the [original 11 October 2022 registration](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/pages/en-detail/25443/jRCT/1) and [current jRCT record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-latest-detail/jRCTs031220373). Physical function and six-minute walking were registered secondary outcomes. The sample-size statement concerns hearing, not independently verified adequate power for physical function. A full protocol/SAP was not acquired.

|---|---:|---|
| Right-hand grip | 0 / −1 kg | .179; .454 |
| Left-hand grip | +0.5 / +1 kg | 1; 1 |
| Thirty-second chair stands | +1 / +1 count | .937; .999 |
| Six-minute hallway walk | −7.5 / +5 m | .423; .645 |

These are **medians of individual changes**, not differences calculated from the baseline and follow-up medians, and not published adjusted treatment-effect estimates. The walking test used a 15-m hallway course. Table 3 also reports no gait difference (P=.797, q=.999), but labels values such as 7.03 as “m/s” while Methods describes recording the time for the central 10 m. That unit/scale inconsistency remains unresolved; no speed conversion or directional effect is invented. Native tables provide no treatment-effect confidence intervals. Null significance does not establish exact zero benefit in every population or regimen.

The analysis used between-group comparisons of changes, generally Mann–Whitney U tests, with baseline-adjusted ANCOVA where indicated. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction was applied to the reported overall comparison set; subgroup/exploratory analyses were not adjusted. Nominal lean-tissue and dietary findings did not survive correction and do not establish muscle-function benefit. Whole-blood LC-MS/MS showed increased **NAD+**, distinct from Yi's serum total NAD assay. Neither muscle NAD nor functional BUBR1 was measured in these qualified human sources. Explanations involving SIRT1, nicotinamide accumulation or an optimal dose window are hypotheses, not mechanisms established by the randomized comparison.

Adverse events affected 7/31 NMN and 6/29 placebo recipients. One NMN recipient with baseline liver-enzyme abnormalities developed further increases and discontinued at week 6; a causal contribution could not be excluded. **The native supplementary plot shows AST rising after discontinuation while ALT falls slightly**, whereas the prose describes no further increases or slight improvement. Both enzymes must not be described as improving, and the graph does not exclude drug contribution. No exact values were digitized. One serious pancytopenia event occurred in placebo. Small changes in platelets, total protein and potassium were reported within reference ranges. This does not prove long-term safety or causal harm. MIRAILAB funded and supplied the intervention; three authors were employees, including a senior company executive.

The native flow supports 84 screened, 22 excluded and 62 randomized; the prose additionally mentions a pre-enrollment consent withdrawal that does not reconcile arithmetically. The randomized and observed denominators above follow the flow and tables without silently repairing that sentence. Bounded publisher/Crossref checks identified no correction relation; Article-in-Press status remains explicit.

## Fair comparison and proposed scope of refinement

NMN now has an actual controlled human functional signal that the prior mechanistic-only description omitted. That is a stronger design for the tested walking comparison than niacin's inherited uncontrolled strength observations. It is **not** evidence that NMN has a larger effect than niacin, because the populations, preparations, tasks and designs differ and no head-to-head trial was read. The longer NMN trial provides meaningful counterevidence to generalized functional benefit; it neither erases the earlier assigned-arm positive nor proves that every NMN dose fails.

Keep the existing North/Kang claims unchanged: NMN-associated wild-type BUBR1 abundance and mitotic-completion observations remain separate from these human trials. No human BUBR1 mechanism can be constructed by joining blood-NAD and walking findings across studies. Apply the same function, renewal and fidelity criteria to NMN and the nominees; do not demand MVA rescue only from the challenger. Product-specific approved-drug submission eligibility is a separate unresolved question, not an efficacy judgment. No current regulatory audit, pediatric safety conclusion or patient dosing advice is supplied.

The integrated `prior-04` refinement appends these two trials while preserving its identity, aliases and North/Kang rationale. The ledger remains at 247 assessments; the other 246 decision objects and the nominations are unchanged. Source-specific retrieval records, exact hashes and reading manifests remain in the external package. Full main/supporting reading was performed by the named source reviewers; registry records and notice checks are not extra independent trials or original-queue reading credit.
