# Lithium chloride in adult optic-nerve astrocytes

**PMID31477687 supports a distinct lithium-chloride effect on astrocyte morphology and reporter-positive cell counts, accompanied by lower deposited Bub1b microarray signals. It does not demonstrate BUBR1 protein restoration or functional rescue.** Chemical identity is explicit, so this source can refine the existing `new-lithium-chloride` object without adding another compound or transferring the prior oocyte findings. [Primary paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-019-0542-2).

The intervention was Sigma-Aldrich **LiCl, nominal 20 mM for 3 days**, added to organotypic cultures of adult mouse optic nerves with retina attached. Controls were culture medium in the paper and saline-labelled in GEO. No explicit matched chloride/osmolarity arm is described. The authors say restricted pia permeability gives 1–2 mM inside the nerve, citing earlier work; this study provides no measured tissue lithium concentration or human exposure bridge. This qualification did not read that earlier exposure paper.

Native Fig1A–C shows a pronounced change from stellate cells to polarised, relatively unbranched processes/palisades and approximately doubled GFAP-EGFP-positive counts (visually about 21→38 cells per fixed field, not digitized; author-reported p<0.001; mean±SEM). Those effects are retained. The authors call this proliferation, but the reviewed main and supplements contain no EdU/BrdU/Ki67, division tracking or independent survival/death assay. A reporter-positive count alone does not separate cell birth, survival and reporter detectability. No neuronal support, conduction, synaptic function, behavior, muscle output or BUB1B-deficient rescue was tested; “neuron-supportive” is an interpretation of the phenotype.

Bub1b is a **network node**, distinct from Bub1, in native Fig1Hi. Node/enrichment colours and pathway P values are not gene-specific RNA direction or protein measurements. The nine-gene native Supplementary Table1 has no Bub1b row, which is not evidence of a null experiment.

The linked [GSE132397 deposit](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE132397) resolves the RNA direction descriptively. Official GPL1261 annotation maps **three** probe sets to mouse **Bub1b, Entrez 12236**. All eight samples were retained: controls GSM3861604–607 and lithium GSM3861608–611. Important source qualifications:

- The paper specifies C57/BL10 for wild-type transcriptomics; GEO specifies **C57BL/6**. Imaging uses GFAP-EGFP reporter mice. The strain discrepancy remains unresolved.
- Imaging uses 6 nerves from 3 mice/group and treats each nerve as `n`; within-mouse clustering is not described. Transcriptomics is described as 12 nerves/6 mice/group with “triplicate” experiments, whereas the deposit has 4 arrays/group and each sample description says 6 optic nerves. Exact pooling and donor independence remain unresolved; no animal count was reconstructed. Allocation was not blinded; measurements were reportedly blinded, with no excluded samples.
- Results says **Fstl1 increased**, but native Supplementary Table1 lists signed FC −2.73/P 7.47×10⁻⁵. Do not use the narrative direction as a verified mechanistic link.
- Fig4 independently tests BAPN, pioglitazone and a methyl-epicatechin preparation for 3 days. All increase reporter counts/change morphology. BAPN concentration is unreported and its one-star bracket is undefined in the caption; pioglitazone is 1 mM with a two-star control bracket (p<0.01). The flavonoid is 3 µM in Methods, called `3-O-Epicatechin` there but **3′-O-methylepicatechin** in Results/Fig4/caption, with a three-star bracket (p<0.001). Do not transfer this to unmodified dietary epicatechin. There is no formal equivalence comparison against lithium.
- Main Table1 is a 40-row computational drug-signature list, not 40 administered arms. Its top BD candidate is **Betulin**, whereas the narrative says betulinic acid. Neither was tested here; quercetin was also only a prediction. Supplementary Fig1 is a proposed mechanism schematic, not an additional functional experiment.
