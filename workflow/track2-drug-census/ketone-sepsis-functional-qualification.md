# Ketone ester preparations: sepsis force signals and toxicity

**Retain a preclinical muscle-force signal with major preparation, survival and toxicity qualifications.** This [2022 primary study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-14961-w) supports selected nominal gains in ex vivo, cross-sectional-area-normalized EDL force among five-day survivors. It does not establish whole-animal rescue, human sepsis benefit, or BUB1B/MVA efficacy. The existing R,R appraisal is refined; S,S and the 50:50 R,R/S,S mixture are separate experimental-preparation appraisals.

The full scientific main, native Figures 1–6 and all ten pages of the [official supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-022-14961-w/MediaObjects/41598_2022_14961_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) were read. The [structured qualification](ketone-sepsis-functional-qualification.json) records exact endpoint counts, source bindings, limitations and the separate source, independent and root reading depths.

## Actual preparations and contrasts

D-3HHB is **(R)-3-hydroxybutyl-(R)-3-hydroxybutanoate** (Innov’Orga); L-3HHB is the **S,S ester** (Synthenova). D,L-3HHB is their **50:50 mixture**, not sodium D,L-3-hydroxybutyrate. All were dissolved in Plasmalyte. Only the mixture underwent continuous intravenous infusion; no D-only infusion was tested.

Twenty-four-week-old male C57BL/6J mice received five-day CLP sepsis with fluids, antibiotics, analgesia and parenteral nutrition. Healthy mice were full-fed. Random allocation and blinded caretakers/data collectors are reported; sequence-generation/concealment details are absent. Dose escalation continued until 14 surviving mice with force measurements when toxicity did not intervene. Controls from successive steps were pooled. The force sample-size basis is stated, but no prospective endpoint hierarchy/SAP was verified; **multiple comparisons were explicitly uncorrected**.

The following doses are source descriptions in mmol/kg/day, not clinical recommendations. Boluses were subcutaneous twice daily from day 1; infusion was continuous from day 1.

| Preparation/route | Force versus septic glucose placebo | Survival and analysis scope |
|---|---|---|
| L ester, bolus 20 | Specific force higher, P=.007; reported 93% of healthy level | 19/24 survived; force n=18 versus placebo 67 |
| D ester, bolus 40 | Higher, P=.05; 93% of healthy level | 16/21 survived; force n=16 versus 67 |
| Mixture, bolus 40 | Higher, P=.04; 90% of healthy level | 17/23 survived; force n=17 versus 67 |
| Mixture, infusion 40 | Higher, P=.03 | 17/21 survived; force n=17 versus placebo 15 |
| Mixture, infusion 80 | Numerical improvement, P=.08; not established benefit | 14/19 survived; force n=14 versus 15 |

Bolus placebo had 80/103 survivors but force n=67; healthy force n=62 of 80. Infusion placebo had 15/17 survivors; healthy n=15. Therefore paperwide totals **376, 27 and 72** are not functional endpoint denominators. Detailed missing-assay reasons are unavailable. Force/tissue/blood conclusions are conditional on survival, not intention-to-treat functional effects.

Native force axes are **mN/mm²**; methods divide force by estimated muscle cross-sectional area. The captions’ “per unit muscle mass” wording is imprecise. Figure 2b provides only a representative absolute-force trace, not a group absolute-force contrast. Group mean differences/CIs are not tabulated; “93% of healthy” is not a treatment-effect percentage or proof of normalization.

## Positives, nulls and toxicity together

D10/L10 and D20/mixture20 did not significantly improve specific force; L40 has no significant placebo marker. Most muscle atrophy persisted and Trim63/Fbxo32 remained elevated. Retain the exceptions: mixture40 attenuated tibialis loss and L40 attenuated EDL loss (each P=.01), although L40 was toxic. D10 had a lower illness-score marker (P≤.05, exact P unstated). D20/L20 had lower creatinine than placebo.

**Every 80 bolus animal died:** D 8/8, L 8/8, mixture 7/7. L40 increased illness (P=.01) and mortality (10/24 versus 23/103, P=.05). D20/mixture20 raised sodium; L40 lowered bicarbonate. Native Figure 4c shows worse liver-damage distributions at D10/L10 (text P≤.02). These explicit findings qualify the discussion’s broad statements of no electrolyte/liver harm. TNFα and liver triglyceride nulls, and GGT reported as “data not shown,” do not establish safety.

Infusion deaths were placebo 2/17, mixture40 4/21, mixture80 5/19, without significant mortality/illness differences. No mass benefit or detected sodium/bicarbonate/glucose/creatinine difference accompanied infusion. Nonsignificance is not equivalence. The striking bolus/infusion contrast comes from separate experiments, not a randomized direct route comparison, and does not establish D-only infusion safety.

The separate kinetic experiment measured **D-3HB only** (total n=27; four group counts unavailable). Apparent clearance was slower in sepsis at both tested daily-dose labels (P=.04/.02); neither group cleared the higher exposure by 180 minutes. L-3HB, intact ester and 1,3-butanediol were not quantified, so a specific lethal mechanism remains unproved.

Placebo was described as isovolumetric glucose, not demonstrated calorie-matched across doses. A material unresolved unit inconsistency remains: supplement bolus glucose **6.25 mg/g/day**, main bolus **187.5 mg/day**, but supplement infusion **6.25 mg/kg/day**. This appraisal does not silently repair it.

## Lineage, transparency and decision boundary

The [structured lineage assessment](ketone-sepsis-functional-qualification.json) confirms that [2021 cholesterol](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-021-03688-1) reuses absolute-force measurements from [2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-019-2506-6). Those preparations were D,L-3HB **sodium salt**. Matching overall cohorts do not resolve differing endpoint counts. The [2021 salt toxicity](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40360-021-00517-7) and 2022 ester experiments are separately described; full animal-level independence is unverified. Four papers are not four independent force replications.

Funding included public/ERC grants and Baxter Healthcare, maker of the parenteral nutrition; authors declared no competing interests. No correction/retraction link was located in current PubMed, Europe PMC or Crossref metadata; raw data are available on request but were not obtained. No BUB1B/BUBR1 assay or MVA model was present. The useful contribution is preparation-specific preclinical force evidence, with severe dose/route constraints—not proof of a general ketone therapy.

## Competitiveness and what would advance it

This adds a disease-model muscle endpoint to the [human monoester comparison](ketone-monoester-functional-qualification.md). The nominal force gains deserve functional credit; survivor conditioning does not establish that they are merely selection artifacts. It leaves the net benefit across the original cohort unresolved. Normalized force, representative absolute-force traces and whole-animal function must remain distinct.

R,R retains its oral human-performance evidence and gains a separate bolus mouse result. S,S and the 50:50 mixture earn their own preparation-specific records. The lower nominally positive S,S dose does not establish clinical potency superiority, and the mixture infusion result cannot be transferred to R,R alone. The severe harms, uncorrected comparisons and unresolved vehicle units preclude a general safety or therapeutic-window claim.

These are exploratory compensation comparisons. The same advancement standard applies to them and to the provisional nominees: justified measured exposure, controlled absolute useful output, preservation of renewable reserve and chromosome fidelity, with survival and starting-unit denominators accounted for. BUBR1 mediation is additionally required only for a restoration claim. No nominee is established superior by this comparison. Earlier salt reports remain at lineage-review depth, and broader source follow-up remains incomplete.
