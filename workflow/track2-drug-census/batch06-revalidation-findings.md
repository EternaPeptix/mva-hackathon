## Explicit rescue and functional findings

**MED20538976 reports rescue by okadaic acid and by combined Greatwall/PP2A knockdown.** Human Greatwall/MASTL depletion produced dose-dependent phenotypes: complete loss caused G2 arrest, while partial depletion altered checkpoint maintenance and could lead to premature exit with severe cytokinesis defects. The source states that PP2A suppression rescued these phenotypes. That chemical/genetic rescue earns positive credit. It does not supply okadaic-acid concentration, the fraction rescued for each endpoint, phosphatase selectivity or sustained viable euploid daughter output. Greatwall depletion is also distinct from BUB1B deficiency. Those questions determine whether this is accurate rescue, partial phenotype correction or bypass in this specific model.

Curcumin results are context-specific. At 5–10 µM for 12 hours, colorectal lines mainly arrested at the G2/M boundary with some mitotic arrest, spindle/congression defects and mitotic DNA-damage markers (MED23125222). Damage-signaling inhibitors reduced mitotic arrest without much effect on boundary arrest; this is not demonstrated productive rescue. HL-60 and K562 showed different arrest phases and caspase activity despite greater overall K562 sensitivity (MED29616136). Dose and normal-cell comparisons remain important.

TC-A2317 suppressed lung-cancer growth while worsening centrosome/spindle defects and disabling a paclitaxel-triggered checkpoint (MED27713168). Hesperadin caused anaphase entry with numerous monooriented chromosomes and rapidly overrode taxol/monastrol arrest (MED12707311). These are informative adverse-direction drug effects, not chromosome-repair evidence.

## Outcomes that should not be merged

Polyoma small T-associated PP2A effects caused mitotic catastrophe; unnamed G1/S inhibitors prevented death by preventing entry (MED24998850). That protective arrest is different from the Greatwall-depletion rescue and supplies no blanket beneficial direction for PP2A inhibition. Hydroxyurea, aphidicolin and thymidine increased DNA-break-positive micronuclei, whereas paclitaxel induced another subclass (MED21525980); its 20% to nearly 50% figure concerns a micronucleus subclass, not all cells.

Bleomycin-damaged oocytes could still extrude polar bodies but developed multiple pronuclei/micronuclei (MED23518501). Ska3-depleted cells aligned chromosomes yet failed checkpoint silencing and later separated chromatids abnormally (MED19646878). High SPDL1 expression associated with better survival and CIN markers (MED35163739), without a survival-improving intervention. These sources show why morphological completion, alignment, survival association and actual fidelity are separate observations.

The BUB1 patient-derived iPSC report (MED39490209) concerns **BUB1, not BUB1B**: one clone had a normal karyotype and another trisomy 8; both had trilineage differentiation capacity. The normal clone was not drug-corrected, and that patient's reported variants establish nothing about the current patient's phase or missense effect.

The six molecules in MED37893423—suramin, rifaximin, telmisartan, tucatinib, olaparib and TG.02—were **docking candidates only**. No administration, target inhibition or tumor response was measured. Network hubs, enriched folate/progesterone pathways and predictive accuracy are likewise not new therapeutic outcomes.
