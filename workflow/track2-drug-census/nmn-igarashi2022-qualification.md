# Igarashi 2022 NMN: exploratory motor signals after a dispensing error

**Retain the reported randomized gait and left-grip signals, with substantial limits from the dispensing error, endpoint hierarchy and uncorrected multiplicity.** The registered primary skeletal muscle index was nonpositive. A full-cohort six-week chair-stand change comparison was positive, whereas several other change comparisons were not. This is additional, qualified human functional evidence for actual NMN; it does not establish general muscle restoration, an optimal dose, MVA efficacy or superiority over niacin.

This separate source review was completed on 26 September 2026, America/Phoenix, with retrievals on 27 September UTC. It is integrated as an additional source for the existing NMN appraisal. The previously accepted Yi 2023/Igarashi 2026 qualification files, the other 246 decision objects, the 247-assessment count and the nominations remain unchanged. Source reviewers supplied no optional entry; the integrating reviewer recorded the appended `prior-04` evidence separately.

## Exact source, preparation and participants

[Igarashi et al., npj Aging 8, 5 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-022-00084-z), published 1 May 2022; [PMID 35927255](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35927255/), [PMC9158788](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158788/). Title, authors and identifiers match the official publisher PDF, full-text XML and bibliographic records. The complete 11-page scientific main, all native main tables/figures, complete supplementary-table text and relevant native supplementary tables were read. Custom free-text fields in the publisher's embedded Reporting Summary were also read; its static PDF extraction otherwise displays an Adobe placeholder.

The trial was conducted in Japan during July–November 2019. It randomized **42 healthy men aged at least 65 years, 21 per arm**, to oral **NMN 250 mg once daily** or placebo for an intended 12 weeks. The supplier and funder was Mitsubishi Corporation Life Sciences Limited. Eligibility included BMI 22–28, nonsmoking, no active disease, and exclusion of potentially confounding medications/supplements and habitual exercise of at least one hour daily for six months. Participants were instructed to maintain their lifestyle and avoid vitamin-B3-related supplements. This was not an MVA, sarcopenic-patient or pediatric cohort.

The main identifies NMN and supplier but does not establish a specific retail product, batch/lot, purity certificate, tablet/capsule composition or placebo excipients. Those details must not be imported from AbinoNutra, MIRAILAB, NR or niacin. The error described below concerns supplement distribution; it is not evidence of chemical impurity.

Third-party C&C QUALITATIVE RESEARCH INSTITUTE managed randomization/allocation; participants and data collectors were blinded until the code was released after completion. A detailed sequence-generation algorithm or blinding-success assessment is not provided.

## The dispensing error changes the analysis population

Supplements were dispensed at baseline and week six. After study completion, the investigators learned that **11 participants in each arm received the other supplement at the six-week visit** because of a supplier error. Following the ethics committee's decision, all 22 affected participants' week-12 data were excluded. Their baseline and week-six data remained in the longitudinal analyses.

Consequently, Tables 2–3 use **21 per arm at baseline/week six and 10 per arm at week 12**. They also display baseline/week-six values for the retained ten-person subsets. The native flow labels zero dropouts and 11 protocol deviations per arm; elsewhere the text calls the 22 “dropped out.” Describe this as excluded week-12 data after wrong supplementation, rather than 22 adverse-event withdrawals or an intended crossover experiment. No usable complete-randomized week-12 contrast, crossover analysis or missing-data sensitivity analysis is presented.

Balance was weakened in the retained 20: baseline gait speed was 1.50±0.20 m/s with NMN versus 1.31±0.19 with placebo (P=.041). Native Figure 1 accounts for 65 screened, 23 excluded and 42 randomized, including ten excluded because enrollment was complete; the prose lists only the other 13 exclusions. The flow resolves that screening-count omission.

## What the functional comparisons actually show

Gait was assessed over the central 10 m of a 16-m course, averaging two measurements; Table 3 reports speed in m/s. Grip was tested twice per hand with a Smedley dynamometer and the highest value used. Chair stands counted rises from an armless chair over 30 seconds.

Changes are reported mean±SD from Table 3; they are not published adjusted treatment-effect estimates. Confidence intervals are not supplied or reconstructed. The longitudinal tests use the available 42/42/20 observations at the three visits; their nominal positives are not merely active-arm before/after claims. They also must not be recast as significant individual week-12 change contrasts.

The frequently highlighted **P=.002 for gait at week 12** is an unadjusted comparison of endpoint means, **1.60±0.13 versus 1.30±0.22 m/s**. It is not the P value for improvement from baseline. The retained subset was already different at baseline, so the 0.30-m/s endpoint separation is not an adjusted treatment effect. The full 42-person week-six endpoint comparison is P=.023, while the corresponding baseline-adjusted change comparison is P=.111. In the retained 20 only, the six-week gait-change ANCOVA is P=.030. Those populations and tests remain distinct.

## Primary endpoint, multiplicity and other results

The [official UMIN000036321 record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000040733) and all three accessible historical versions specify **skeletal muscle index (SMI)** as primary and glucose-metabolism parameters/FMD as key secondary outcomes. Gait, grip, chair stands and NAD are not listed there. Registration on 27 March 2019 and disclosure on 1 June 2019 precede the paper's conduct interval. This establishes prospective registration of the listed outcomes, not of the unlisted functional analyses. Omission is not proof that they were invented after data collection: the complete original protocol/SAP was not recovered.

The main Methods describes broader primary objectives—blood NAD and body composition—and secondary aging-related functions. The Reporting Summary, dated 24 January 2022, calls SMI primary and lists broad secondary measurement categories. It states that the full protocol is available from the author on request; no request was sent. The public registry does not supply that protocol or an analysis plan resolving the hierarchy differences.

SMI was nonpositive: mixed-model P=.979, MMRM P=.874, with week-12 change ANCOVA P=.805. The paper's post hoc power calculation concerns an assumed large SMI change, not proof of adequate functional-endpoint power or exact absence of smaller benefits.

The preferred repeated-measures analysis was a mixed model, with MMRM when the mixed model failed; interaction tests, endpoint tests and baseline-adjusted change tests were also reported. The authors explicitly describe **all analyses as exploratory and apply no multiplicity correction**. These are nominal findings across numerous endpoints and methods. Statistical modeling does not restore the randomized balance lost after excluding over half the week-12 data.

Table 2 also contains nominal findings against NMN in the retained 20 at week six: right-arm lean change −0.07 versus +0.04 kg (ANCOVA P=.012) and trunk lean change −0.3 versus +0.2 kg (P=.039). Their overall longitudinal tests are nonpositive, and the subset comparisons are uncorrected. Preserve them as exploratory counterevidence, not general NMN-induced muscle wasting. The main's statement that no muscle-mass comparison was significant is too broad for those displayed cells.

Whole-blood LC-MS/MS, assessed at baseline and week 12 in the retained ten per arm, supports greater NAD+ change with NMN (native Figure 2, P<.001), alongside selected related metabolites. It does **not** measure skeletal-muscle NAD, SIRT2 activity, BUBR1 abundance/function, chromosome segregation or mechanistic mediation of gait/strength. Supplementary Table 4's endpoint comparisons and Figure 2's change comparisons should not be interchanged; for example, NA and NAM have significant endpoint differences but nonsignificant change comparisons. No muscle biopsy or MVA genotype was studied.

## Harms, funding and publication notices

Authors report pill adherence above 90%, no serious adverse event and no clinically important laboratory abnormality in the analyzed NMN group. High pill adherence does not correct the wrong-supplement exposure. Twelve-week laboratory comparisons are based on ten per arm. A complete treatment-emergent adverse-event table with arm-specific counts is not supplied, so “no serious event reported” must not become “no adverse events” or proven long-term safety.

Mitsubishi funded the study and supplied NMN. Three authors were employees and participated in trial conceptualization; the remaining authors declared no conflicts. The sample is small, restricted to healthy older men and followed briefly, limiting safety and population transfer.

The publisher-linked [Crossmark record](https://crossmark.crossref.org/dialog/?doi=10.1038/s41514-022-00084-z) reports the document current without an update. Exact Crossref, PubMed and Europe PMC notice fields and bounded exact-source searches identified **no trial-specific correction or retraction requiring a change to these results**. This is an as-of, accessible-record check, not a guarantee against future or unindexed notices. The dispensing error is disclosed in the original published main and Reporting Summary; it is not a newly discovered publisher correction.

## Relationship to the two already qualified NMN trials

This third, separate source adds exploratory gait/left-grip interaction signals and a six-week full-cohort chair-change positive. It supports retaining NMN as a mechanistic and mixed controlled human-function comparator. Its major exposure error and analysis limits materially weaken a simple “NMN improves muscle function” conclusion.

Yi 2023's manual-treadmill walking comparison and Igarashi 2026's nonpositive strength/hallway-walking results remain separate qualified sources. These trials used different preparations, populations, durations and tasks; they are not a randomized low-versus-high-dose experiment, and cannot establish a preferred dose or formulation. This 2019 cohort is distinct from the later trial but does not represent replication by an independent research team.

Compared fairly with niacin, the randomized motor signals deserve credit beyond an uncontrolled human strength observation. Neither relative effect magnitude nor MVA superiority is established without a comparable head-to-head setting. Preserve the existing North/Kang evidence and apply the same functional, renewal and fidelity criteria to NMN and the nominees; product eligibility remains separate. No patient dosing advice, nomination change or automatic source-count update follows.
