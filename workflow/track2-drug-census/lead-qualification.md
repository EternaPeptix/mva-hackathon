# Which new leads survive primary-source review?

**21 September 2026.** Aurora-B balancing merits a higher experimental priority;
geraniin retains a chromosome-fidelity signal but has an unresolved exposure
bridge. TG101209 remains a thermal-response probe. None establishes superior
clinical efficacy to niacin or apitegromab. The present nominees also lack rescue
of the actual BUB1B alleles: that shared gap cannot by itself exclude challengers.

| Route | Strongest relevant observation | Competitive position and next discriminating result |
|---|---|---|
| Niacin | Human muscle NAD target engagement; a separate NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 rationale | Retain restoration nomination. Demonstrate drug-mediated usable BUBR1 and faithful, productive divisions; human NAD correction alone is insufficient. |
| Apitegromab | Drug-specific human motor-function evidence in treated SMA | Retain separate muscle-compensation nomination. Establish engagement and useful output in MVA models without impaired renewal or chromosome fidelity. |
| Aurora-B balancing | Administered inhibitors restore chromosome attachments after actual BUBR1 depletion | **Elevate functional-compensation comparator.** Complete accurate divisions after release from arrest would be more decisive than another abundance measurement. Exposure and harm constraints remain. |
| Geraniin | Compound treatment improves chromosome-related endpoints in noncancerous colon cells | Retain experimental fidelity comparator. Recover the full dose-response, distinguish parent from metabolites, and measure absolute productive output. |
| TG101209 | Reported BUB1B thermal stabilization in renal-cancer cells | Retain protein-stability probe. Test unheated abundance, turnover and function with competing pharmacology controlled. |
| KP372-1 | Drug-associated prevention of arsenic-induced aneuploidy in epithelial cultures | Retain contextual finding; decline MVA lead. The lesion, incompletely specified exposure and complex redox/Akt pharmacology prevent a clean restoration inference. |

The first two rows refer to the already appraised [nomination evidence](../../reports/appendices/track2-evidence-pipeline.md).
This is a comparison of evidence and test priority, not a ranking of measured MVA
efficacy. Experimental reagents do not inherit approved-product status.

## Aurora-B: stronger functional evidence, narrower meaning than “rescue”

In BUBR1-depleted HeLa cells, **hesperadin 20 nM for one hour** improved
recognizable metaphase plates from **30% to 85%**, and cold-stable attached
kinetochores from **5 ± 2% to 84 ± 4%**. A second inhibitor, AKI-1 at 1 µM,
supported the result. Treatment occupied the last hour of a two-hour 10 µM
MG132 arrest. Some attachments remained syntelic. These are attachment/alignment
endpoints under arrest, not accurate daughter-cell production.
[Lampson and Kapoor, primary paper and supplement](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/6/210/files/2016/09/Lampson-and-Kapoor-2005-1vyd2lh.pdf).

A later BUBR1-depletion study also found more stable attachments but residual
alignment defects. Its Table 1 and Figure 8 combine different drug/codepletion
conditions; the reported recognizable-plate improvement uses the subset already
having more than four misaligned chromosomes. It must not be presented as
complete alignment or an independent successful-division experiment.
[Logarinho et al., primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2291436/).

In the KARD study, **ZM447439 2 µM for the final hour of a two-hour 5 µM MG132
arrest** restored cold-stable attachments in BUBR1-defective HeLa conditions.
The related 2013 study used 2 µM for one hour within a three-hour 20 µM MG132
arrest to improve congression in engineered BUBR1-B56-binding-defective cells.
Its patient-fibroblast rescue was **genetic PP2A recruitment**, not that drug.
[Suijkerbuijk et al., primary article](https://www.hubrecht.eu/app/uploads/2015/08/Kops_Research_2012_Suijkerbuijk_Integration-of-kinase-and-phosphatase-activities-by-BUBR1-ensures-formation-of-stable-kinetochore-microtubule-attachments.pdf),
[Xu et al., primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3654266/).

The stronger completed-segregation experiment concerns **BUB1 excess**, a
different lesion. At 2.5 nM ZM447439, lagging chromosomes fell from 22%/20% to
1% in two transgenic MEF lines. Treatment from passages P0–P5 reduced T264
aneuploidy to 9%, but raised wild-type aneuploidy to 17%. The authors also report
15% failed mitoses irrespective of genotype, without depicting those data.
Benefit and harm coexist at this
concentration. The three independent primary lines and live-division endpoint
strengthen the finding without transferring it to BUB1B deficiency.
[Ricke et al., Figure 5 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3115799/).

**Decision:** prioritize a partial, timed compensation experiment with drug
withdrawal, complete division tracking, daughter-cell chromosome counts,
checkpoint surveillance and absolute progenitor output. Stable incorrect
attachments or survival during MG132 arrest fail this test. The separate
[AZD1152 mouse conference result](literature-review.md#azd1152--barasertib-direct-same-gene-pharmacology-limited-muscle-relevance)
remains distinct; neither its survival result nor its sarcopenia null belongs to
the ZM447439 experiments.

## Geraniin: a defined cell exposure, no established systemic bridge

The primary abstract reports opposite checkpoint responses in HCT116 cancer and
CCD841 noncancerous colon cells. Selected original captions specify **40 µg/mL
for 24 hours**, transcript qPCR and chromosome-damage assays; the nocodazole
challenge includes 100 ng/mL for 24 hours. The full manuscript and original
figure images were not obtained. Replicate denominators, numerical effect sizes,
the lowest active concentration and BUBR1 causal mediation remain unverified.
This does not establish useful BUBR1 protein or exclude slower division/selection
as contributors to the chromosome endpoint.
[Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30403302/),
[publisher supplemental caption](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/em.22265),
[original Figure 5 caption](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Effects-of-geraniin-treatment-on-the-relative-expression-of-SAC-genes-in-colon-cancer-and_fig5_328784761).

A preliminary oral rat experiment found no detectable parent geraniin after
100 mg/kg in two rats, with an assay LOD of 0.07 µg/mL and LLOQ of 0.2 µg/mL.
That small, assay-bounded observation does not establish zero human absorption.
[Primary pharmacokinetic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5543565/).
A 2024 rabbit study likewise did not detect parent after oral free or formulated
geraniin at 100 mg/kg (n=3 per listed group). Improved plasma exposure concerned
**ellagic acid**, not intact geraniin; numerical detection limits were in an
unretrieved supplement. Relevant primary manuscript text was read, without
independent figure-image inspection.
[Primary formulation study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpharm.2024.124333).

**Decision:** retain the cellular comparator, but do not call oral parent
exposure qualified or transfer its BUBR1 hypothesis to metabolites. Unknown
minimum activity and human tissue exposure prevent a defensible exposure-margin
calculation. Test measured parent/metabolite concentrations separately before
advancing a systemic muscle proposal.

## TG101209: competing pharmacology matters

The [source assay review](target-assay-review.md) preserves the primary abstract's
thermal direction and the independently unverified ChEMBL quantitative
annotations: 2.19-fold CETSA signal at 20 µM, not measured useful protein supply.
The original development study reports JAK2, FLT3 and RET IC50s of 6, 25 and
17 nM respectively. Competing pharmacology is credible at the much higher
thermal-assay concentration; cross-assay values do not establish a toxicity
threshold or clinical window. No human-exposure or eligible-product bridge was
established. TG101209 remains distinct from TG101348/fedratinib.
[Primary development study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17541402/).

## KP372-1 and okadaic acid: identify the actual intervention

The arsenic paper used **KP372-1**, not only genetic Akt suppression. BEAS-2B
cells and keratinocytes received 0.5 µM arsenite with drug or PLK1-siRNA
comparators for two months. Figure 7 reports chromosome counts in 100 cells per
treatment, with five experiments, and prevention of the arsenic-associated
aneuploidy increase. The KP372-1 concentration was not located in the inspected
full text, captions or Figures 6–7. Figure 6C's DNA-content endpoint is G2/M,
which does not distinguish those two phases. A terminal aneuploid fraction does not distinguish fewer
new errors from selection or altered proliferation, and this is an imposed
toxin lesion rather than BUB1B loss.
[Primary article](https://www.genesandcancer.com/article/185/text/).

Calling KP372-1 a selective Akt rescue is also unsupported. A separate primary
study found NQO1-dependent redox cycling and cytotoxicity, with transient Akt
activation; other Akt inhibitors did not reproduce its redox effects. These
contextual findings do not negate the arsenic experiment, but prevent assigning
that result to an Akt-inhibitor class or assuming harmless genome protection.
[Primary mechanism study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4427571/).

In a different experiment, **okadaic acid 0.5–1 µM** restored kinetochore
Bub1/BubR1 localization during 10 µM 5-iodotubercidin treatment in HeLa cells.
This is a phosphatase-inhibitor assay result, not an unnamed restorative medicine.
Restored localization alone does not establish checkpoint or chromosome rescue.
[Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3471222/).

## Remaining evidence work

The author/institution-hosted Aurora PDFs and accessible primary XML/text were
read; exact depth is stated where only selected sections or captions were
available. Primary geraniin and TG101209 figure-level questions remain open.
Neither failed access nor missing annotations count as negative drug experiments.
These new lines mean the requested “no further leads” condition remains unmet;
repository publication and Track 2 submission remain pending.
