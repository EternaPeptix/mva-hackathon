# Sodium bicarbonate: context-dependent acid-base route

**27 September 2026 UTC.** Sodium bicarbonate is a common compound with a plausible compensation rationale: correcting an acid-base disturbance could reduce a burden on muscle without repairing BUB1B. The reviewed trials do not support promoting it as a general MVA muscle candidate. Retain the positive pooled-salt finding below, rather than interpreting the CKD nulls as universal failure. Niacin/restoration and apitegromab/compensation remain provisional nominees.

**Denominators and analysis matter.** BiCARB retained allocation despite 18 placebo participants starting prescribed bicarbonate. Pill-count adherence was 73% in both arms; mean all-time bicarbonate separation was only +1.1 mmol/L. Missing outcomes and limited separation restrict generalization, while its imputation analysis still showed no primary benefit. Melamed lost 28/74 active and 17/75 placebo participants; its mixed-model analysis of randomized participants does not mean all supplied 24-month data. Dawson used a completer/exclusion analysis and reported a sex interaction without its statistic; prospective endpoint hierarchy and multiplicity control were not verified. None provides a controlled MVA comparison.

**Safety findings are not interchangeable.** BiCARB recorded 457 versus 400 **events**, but similar proportions with any event: 131/152 versus 132/148, P=.38. Melamed recorded any events in 33/74 versus 24/75 (risk difference .13, CI −.03 to .28) and fewer hyperkalemia cases, 14 versus 30, P=.006. The latter is a clinical positive even though muscle outcomes were nonpositive. Dawson included gastrointestinal discontinuations and sodium-arm blood-pressure/headache withdrawals; causality was not established. These trial findings neither prove general safety nor justify avoiding clinically indicated correction of acidosis.

**BUB1B link and competitive decision.** A bounded direct-gene search found no restoration intervention in the checked sources. The [FBXW7/BubR1 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11072012/) uses sodium bicarbonate as routine culture medium; that occurrence earns no intervention credit. No reviewed trial measured BUBR1, chromosome fidelity or MVA rescue. The route remains conditional on a documented, relevant acid-base abnormality and a useful functional response under the same evidence standards as other compensation candidates. The positive pooled-salt result warrants follow-up but does not displace the nominees or support a sodium-specific efficacy claim.

**Scope:** study amounts identify research exposures, not recommended regimens. BiCARB's NIHR monograph and Dawson's bone companion are the same respective cohorts. BiCARB was publicly NIHR-funded, Melamed NIH/NIDDK-funded, and Dawson NIAMS/USDA-funded; the source packets retain disclosure details and access/reporting limitations. Original protocols/SAPs and participant data were not comprehensively read; current registry fields are not historical verification. Raw primary material stays external. The companion JSON records source and independent/integrating reading depth; these appraisals add no reading credit to the original abstract queue and do not close broader discovery.
