# KSM-66 root extract: a functional reserve with mixed confirmation

**Retain the tested KSM-66 ashwagandha root preparation as an exploratory muscle-compensation comparison.** A small randomized trial reports greater strength gains during prescribed resistance training. A later trial reports an endurance estimate and has some positive public-data sensitivity results, but its strength narrative, tables, protocol and deposited data cannot be reconciled. These findings supply controlled functional evidence without establishing BUBR1 restoration, MVA benefit or superiority over niacin or apitegromab.

This is one preparation appraisal, covering two reported trials. Publication versions, supplements and our recalculations are not additional cohorts. The intervention is a multicomponent root extract; results do not identify an active molecule or transfer to isolated withanolides, withaferin A, leaf extracts or arbitrary retail products.

## The 2015 strength result deserves credit

[Wankhede et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-015-0104-9) randomized 57 healthy men inexperienced in resistance training: 29 received extract and 28 starch placebo. Of 79 screened, 50 completed and were analyzed, 25 per arm. Four extract and three placebo participants stopped training. No intention-to-treat estimate or missing-data sensitivity was supplied.

Both arms received the same prescribed training program. KSM-66 was described as a water-extracted root product standardized to 5% withanolides by HPLC, supplied by Ixoreal BioMed. The source exposure was 300 mg twice daily for eight weeks; that describes the study, not a recommended regimen. A lot-level independent composition assay was not provided.

| Reported strength change | Extract, n=25 | Placebo, n=25 | Direct between-arm result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bench-press 1RM | +46.05 kg | +26.42 kg | P=.001; arithmetic difference +19.63 kg |
| Leg-extension 1RM | +14.50 kg | +9.77 kg | P=.04; arithmetic difference +4.73 kg |

These are genuine reported treatment comparisons, not an inference from improvement in one arm. The source's confidence intervals describe each arm's change, not the between-arm difference. The outcome is task-specific machine load assessed through a described multiple-RM variant of a 1RM protocol, rather than isolated-muscle dynamometer force or daily function.

Strength was the publication-stated primary domain. Prospective registration and endpoint hierarchy remain unverified after bounded searches. Randomization and double blinding are reported, but allocation generation/concealment and assessor-specific implementation are not detailed. Numerous secondary comparisons and two strength tests lack a reported multiplicity adjustment; the leg result is nominal. Methods prescribe three weekly sessions while Table 1 lists four day labels, so achieved schedule and equal workload are not established.

Estimated arm size and chest girth favored extract; thigh-size change did not (P=.36). Girth/skinfold estimates and bioimpedance do not establish directly measured muscle mass. Figure 2e's chest cm² label conflicts with methods/Table 3 girth in cm; retain cm. Testosterone change favored extract, while final levels did not differ significantly. The CK endpoint compares the 24-to-48-hour post-exercise increment before and after training; it is a biomarker, not measured recovery of force. Table 6 signs govern over inconsistent narrative wording. No serious adverse effects were reported, but detailed event counts and a separate safety denominator were not furnished.

## The later study is not uncomplicated strength replication

[Verma et al., version 2](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.130932.2), published in 2024 after a 2023 first version, reports a different trial in healthy trained adults of both sexes. Of 92 screened, 80 were randomized, 40 per arm with 20 men and 20 women. All completed follow-up, but seven poor supplement compliers were excluded from efficacy analysis: 37 extract and 36 placebo remained. The manuscript calls this modified ITT; it is not all-randomized analysis.

The named root product is again KSM-66, batch KSM/VG/18/1020, reported as >5% total withanolides by HPLC. The eight-week exposure and starch comparator were similar, with prescribed resistance training in both arms. Exact equivalence to the 2015 lot is unverified. Diet was not quantified; three active and four placebo participants took capsules with milk. The lower-body task is inconsistently named: equipment/abstract indicate leg extension, while tables call it leg press.

| Version 2 published outcome | Adjusted active-minus-placebo difference (95% CI) | P |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bench 1RM absolute change | +2.5 kg (−0.96 to 6.04) | .152 |
| Lower-body 1RM absolute change | +4.4 kg (−0.02 to 8.85) | .051 |
| Estimated VO₂max change | +1.6 mL/kg/min (1.08 to 2.10) | <.0001 |
| Mid-arm circumference | +0.4 cm (0.16 to 0.70) | .002 |
| Chest circumference | +1.3 cm (0.59 to 2.08) | .001 |

Table 4 adjusts for sex, baseline BMI and baseline chest circumference, not baseline strength. Its thigh and body-fat contrasts are not statistically significant. VO₂max was estimated from Bruce treadmill duration, not measured respiratory gases. The reported endurance and girth positives deserve credit at that scope; girth is not muscle mass. These are nominal comparisons without a stated multiplicity correction.

The abstract and Results instead claim much stronger between-arm strength significance. That discrepancy was present in version 1 and remained after a reviewer query and author response. The [public protocol](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.22081895.v4) specifies **percentage strength change** on page 44. Therefore, the nonsignificant absolute-change tables alone do not establish failure of the intended primary endpoint. Nor can the narrative success claim be accepted without a reconciled calculation.

The matched [official registry](https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/pmaindet2.php?EncHid=MjIzMTA%3D) confirms registration on 19 July 2018, a prospective label and strength as the primary domain. Its first-enrollment date differs from the article; original frozen registration history was not obtained. The protocol's analysis sets, margin language and sample-size rationale differ from the report. Planned CK recovery, testosterone and SF-36 outcomes are unreported here; their direction is unknown. The publicly deposited protocol carries a 2018 date but first appeared in the repository in 2023. These are reporting limits, not evidence that the study was never registered.

## What the posted data can and cannot reproduce

The official CSV/XLSX contains 80 complete records, 20 per sex per arm, with no identifiers for the seven efficacy exclusions. The source measurements agree across formats apart from BMI display rounding. Our independent calculation retains every record, with no exclusions or imputation. Two-sided Student tests, Welch sensitivities and exploratory ANCOVA yield nominal estimates without multiplicity correction.

| All-80 posted-data calculation | Active-minus-placebo difference (nominal 95% CI) | P |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bench absolute change | +6.445 kg (1.303 to 11.587) | .0147 |
| Lower-body absolute change | +6.741 kg (0.281 to 13.200) | .0411 |
| Bench percentage change | +11.485 percentage points (−0.117 to 23.086) | .0523 |
| Lower-body percentage change | +7.942 percentage points (−6.787 to 22.671) | .2863 |
| Estimated VO₂max absolute change | +2.450 mL/kg/min (0.249 to 4.651) | .0296 |

An exploratory all-80 ANCOVA using the article's covariates gives a positive bench percentage contrast, +13.266 points (1.308 to 25.224), P=.0302; the leg percentage contrast is not statistically significant, P=.4065. Thus not every percentage analysis is nonsignificant. These positives must remain alongside the nonsignificant results.

**These calculations do not reproduce or replace the published 73-person analysis.** All 20 active women were reportedly retained, yet their public-data bench baseline/change means are 56.069/16.7575 kg versus the paper's 49.0/11.2. Their baseline estimated VO₂ is 47.35 versus 28.8. The seven stated exclusions alone cannot explain that mismatch. Its cause remains unresolved; no fabrication or absence-of-effect inference follows. We do not select an undocumented subset to obtain a desired result.

The [replay script](replay_ashwagandha_public_data.py) checks the reviewed source hash and emits all 42 exploratory contrasts and six ANCOVA fits. Download the [official CSV](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/39372206) outside the repository. With Python, NumPy and SciPy installed, run from the repository root:

```sh
python3 workflow/track2-drug-census/replay_ashwagandha_public_data.py /path/to/raw-data.csv /path/to/new-result.json
```

The script refuses changed input or an existing output path. It was exercised on macOS with Python 3.14, NumPy 2.5.1 and SciPy 1.18.0; every output field matched the independent computation. This verifies our calculation's reproducibility, not the clinical validity of the deposit or the paper's primary analysis. Raw participant rows remain outside Git.

## Safety, commercial context and competitiveness

The later trial reports no adverse events in its stated 80-person safety population. Its safety appendix nevertheless contains significant paired laboratory changes in both arms, contradicting the blanket no-significant-change sentence. Displayed arm comparisons are nonsignificant; missing units, reference intervals and assay denominators limit interpretation. This does not establish treatment-specific clinical toxicity or broad safety.

The 2015 authors acknowledged supplied extract and declared no conflicts; cash support and sponsor role were unspecified. The later article declares no grants/conflicts, while its protocol and registry name Shri Kartikeya Pharma as sponsor/supporter and Abiogenesis as CRO. Preserve that commercial context without assuming it explains the results. [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha) describes preparation variability and safety concerns, including liver-injury reports and interactions; its guidance adds no trial efficacy or retail-equivalence credit. These short adult studies cannot establish pediatric, cancer-context or long-term safety. Approved-drug submission eligibility is not established here.

The 2015 controlled strength contrast is a substantive functional signal, stronger in causal design than an uncontrolled strength trajectory. The later study adds reported endurance evidence and positive exploratory calculations but does not provide cleanly reconciled strength confirmation. Neither comparison establishes greater clinical benefit than apitegromab's different pediatric disease trial, or BUBR1 restoration comparable to the hypothesis motivating niacin. The nominees also lack demonstrated MVA rescue, so that missing evidence cannot be used only against challengers.

Keep the exact preparation as a research reserve. Advancement would require justified measured exposure and controlled useful muscle output, renewable reserve and chromosome fidelity in relevant affected/corrected models. A BUBR1-restoration claim additionally needs functional BUBR1 mediation; compensation credit does not. No nomination change, new biological experiment or worldwide-completeness claim follows. The [structured qualification](ashwagandha-functional-qualification.json) preserves both source packets, separate independent and root reading scopes, exact source bindings and all exploratory numerical results.
