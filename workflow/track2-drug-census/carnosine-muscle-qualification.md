# L-carnosine physical performance: overall nulls with two exploratory subgroup signals

**Source qualification, 23 September 2026.** Retain L-carnosine as a bounded human functional comparator, not a demonstrated broad strength/endurance treatment or a replacement nominee. The actual randomized trial measured function, but its published analysis is restricted by asymmetric exclusions after randomization. Overall comparisons were null. Preserve two nominal subgroup positives with their confidence intervals; they do not establish validated age/sex responder groups. No BUB1B/BubR1 or chromosome-fidelity outcome was measured.

## Selection and exact intervention

Six targeted discovery queries prioritized ergothioneine and carnosine after a duplicate check at committed **3db5c8961499131d8feafbe87f47eb8e6bfbb0e0, 230 decisions**. Neither had an appraisal at that source-review baseline. Carnosine was selected for full qualification because [O’Toole et al., PMID42308284](https://doi.org/10.1080/15502783.2026.2679716) is an accessible 299-person randomized study with actual muscle/physical-function measures. Its electronic publication was 17 June 2026; the December issue date is not the date of first availability. The full paper is [PMC13276806](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13276806/).

This is a secondary physical-performance analysis of **NEAT, NCT03314987**, a trial primarily designed around air-pollution-associated vascular outcomes. Louisville-area adults were eligible at ages 22–65, preselected for low urinary carnosine; they were not a pediatric, congenital-myopathy or low-strength-defined cohort. Baseline means were about 45 years. Current registry eligibility uses below-median carnosine; the published protocol describes the lower third. The exact historical threshold/version was not reconstructed. No exercise-training intervention was assigned; activity status was not the basis for recruitment.

Actual treatment was **2 g/day carnosine orally**, four 0.5-g capsules, two twice daily, against identical cornstarch capsules, for about 12 weeks. The current registry explicitly identifies **L-carnosine**. The supplied 14 May 2020 protocol plans bulk L-carnosine from BulkSupplements, compounded into gelatin capsules by the University of Colorado pharmacy, with purity/stability testing. This documents the planned formulation; final batch certificates and retail-product equivalence were not verified. It is not beta-alanine, zinc-carnosine, N-acetylcarnosine, anserine or a mixture.

## The randomized population and the analyzed population differ

Native Supplement Figure S1 confirms **153 active and 146 placebo randomized**. Baseline function was available for 147/136, visit 3 for **97/101**, and visit 4 for **91/101** (active/placebo). Visits averaged 40.3±4.6 and 77.4±5.7 days of supplementation. These are evaluable functional sets, not 299 analyzed participants.

The Methods require at least 80% capsule-count adherence and exclude active participants whose urinary carnosine increased by less than 10%. The Discussion and native flow clarify that placebo nonadherence was not used for exclusion and a placebo response filter could not be applied. The authors explicitly state that **they did not conduct an intention-to-treat analysis**. Removal of active nonresponders can disturb the randomized comparison; no equivalent all-randomized estimate or sensitivity analysis is provided. The source assertion that missing measures were random does not establish that assumption. “38% attrition” describes loss of active baseline functional observations, not simply study dropout: withdrawals, quality-control failures, adherence and response filters are pooled.

There is a further denominator ambiguity: Table 1 has active men increasing from 39 at visit 3 to 45 at visit 4 while the flow displays 97→91 after six further exclusions. The source does not reconcile these counts or establish nested analytic samples. Subgroup-specific sample sizes for the two positive comparisons are not supplied in Tables S2/S3 or native Figures 2/3. Do not invent them from baseline age/sex counts.

## Actual function results and their units

All seven overall between-arm percentage-change comparisons were nonsignificant at both visits in native Figure 1. Age/sex-adjusted mixed-model treatment coefficients in Table S4 were also nonsignificant. Handgrip—including initial and post-fatigue maximums—was null; so were gait and overall step/calf-raise outcomes. This does not prove every possible effect is exactly zero.

|---|---|---:|---|
| Calf raise, all participants **≤40**, visit 3 | **0.102 (0.016–0.189)**; reported as a 10.2% relative improvement (1.6–18.9%) | .018 | A nominal comparison of change from baseline. Supplement S1 quantifies the calf-raise measure in **repetitions/second**; do not relabel it as 10.2% more maximal force, total repetitions or time to exhaustion. |
| Two-minute step count, **men >40**, visit 4 | **0.074 (0.016–0.132)**; reported as 7.4% (1.6–13.2%) | .010 | A nominal repeated-step performance signal; not measured peak VO2 or a seven-point clinical motor-scale benefit. |

Tables S2/S3 print fractional change coefficients alongside columns called percent change, while figure axes use percentages. Exact source values and labels are retained in the numerical JSON; no new test or inferred subgroup denominator is calculated. The calf-raise positive applies to all participants ≤40: neither the male-only ≤40 comparison (P=.078) nor female-only ≤40 comparison (P=.339) is independently significant. All-men step P=.06 is also nonsignificant. Do not turn these results into established male-only efficacy.

Eight age/sex subgroup comparisons, seven measures, two visits and absolute/percentage-change analyses create substantial multiplicity. No correction is described in the results paper. No treatment-by-age or treatment-by-sex interaction result is supplied. An overall adjusted null does **not** prove the effect is confined to subgroups, despite the source interpretation. The main title/Discussion's broad improved-endurance wording therefore needs qualification.

The [2020 published protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7772308/) prospectively describes physical tests, but power and formal primary/secondary hierarchies focus on vascular/cellular outcomes. Its analysis plan calls for both ITT and per-protocol analyses, with ITT primary. The uploaded 14 May 2020 protocol/SAP (posted in 2023) mentions age/sex subgroup evaluations but stratifies randomization at ages **22–44/45–65**, not the publication's ≤40/>40 cutoff. That is not proof that the later cutoff was post hoc; its specific prespecification was not established. Protocol versions also differ in vascular endpoint counts and multiplicity language. Current registry history was not read, so no definitive amendment chronology is claimed.

## Molecular evidence and safety remain separate

No BUB1B/BubR1-specific RNA, protein, localization, checkpoint or chromosome-content assay was found in the complete scientific main and supplement. Urinary/erythrocyte carnosine accumulation is exposure evidence cited from another NEAT report, not a muscle biopsy measurement in this analysis and not independent replication. Intramuscular carnosine, pH buffering and the proposed sex/age redox mechanism were not directly established here. The paper's transfers from beta-alanine studies do not qualify carnosine monotherapy by themselves.

The current [registry results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03314987) report zero deaths and **3/153 versus 3/146 participants with serious adverse events**, and **24/153 versus 16/146 with other adverse events**, during the reported five-month window. Reported events include tingling (11 versus 3), nausea (13 versus 13), hypotension (1 versus 2), and isolated other events. No between-arm safety-equivalence test or causal attribution is established by these counts; the functional paper does not supply a detailed adverse-event table. Short adult exposure does not settle pediatric or long-term safety. Current registry study-completion counts (140/133) are distinct from the selected functional sample (91/101); they should not be merged. NIH funding and no potential conflicts are declared in the paper.

## Essential counterevidence, with narrower reading scopes

The complete original abstract of [PMID39770949, DOI10.3390/nu16244328](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu16244328), describes a separate randomized double-blind trial of **49 adults with prediabetes/T2D, 43 completing**, at 2 g/day for 14 weeks. It reports no advantage over placebo in handgrip, relative upper-limb strength, body composition or bone measures. This reinforces the lack of broad strength confirmation while leaving NEAT's distinct subgroup endpoints unresolved. Only the abstract/metadata were read here: detailed effect sizes, missingness and formulation are not inherited from NEAT.

The complete original abstract of [PMID12459876](https://doi.org/10.1023/a:1020625225685) reports **64% and 85% more aberrant spermatogonia/round spermatids**, measured by micronucleus testing after carnosine in aging-resistant SAMR1 mice, and no change in aging-prone SAMP1 mice. This is relevant counterevidence to a blanket chromosome-protection assumption. Dose, route, animal/cell denominators, significance and centromere content are unavailable at abstract depth; it is not proof of human genotoxicity or a measured BUBR1 mechanism. Neither abstract is a second full-primary qualification in this pass.

## Competitive conclusion and discovery boundary

Carnosine supplies actual controlled human functional measurements and two exploratory subgroup signals; those deserve credit beyond a pathway hypothesis. Its overall nulls, active-only response/adherence selection, multiplicity and absent subgroup interaction evidence prevent calling it a robust functional challenger. This judgment does not demand MVA evidence selectively: niacin's open-label strength observation also has causal limits, and apitegromab's pediatric SMA motor result remains disease-specific. No comparative MVA superiority is established, and no nominee change is justified by this source alone.

Ergothioneine remains an unqualified pointer: the bounded search found a [healthy-mouse treadmill study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.834597) with an actual endurance positive, but only primary search excerpts were examined. No null or global absence conclusion follows from this search. Alpha-lipoic acid was not pursued once a decisive first-pair source was found. The existing CoQ10 report's narrow ALA ovulation null remains distinct.
