# Cholecalciferol (vitamin D3): bounded human functional qualification

Source review completed 23 September 2026. Four targeted discovery queries selected three randomized trials. This integration adds one cholecalciferol appraisal without changing nominee order or original-queue reading counts. Cholecalciferol remains distinct from ergocalciferol (D2), calcifediol, calcitriol, and calcium/exercise mixtures.

**Decision:** retain a separate D3 appraisal containing the reported low-vitamin-D chair-rise signal and the stronger qualified functional null/counterevidence. This pass does not verify a positive randomized muscle-function contrast sufficient to advance D3 as a general functional lead. That judgment rests on the contrasts and access limits below, not a selective requirement for MVA evidence. Treatment of severe documented deficiency is not disproved by these trials, and neither BUBR1 restoration nor clinical MVA benefit follows.

## Daily D3 in younger postmenopausal women: reported positive, controlled effect unresolved

PMID25956283, DOI10.1007/s00198-015-3151-9. The complete official abstract, publisher abstract/disclosures and institutional access statement were read. The publisher offers subscription access; the UNESP repository explicitly marks the item restricted and supplies no public article file. No complete main, native tables/figures, supplement, own protocol or registry was read. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25956283/), [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00198-015-3151-9).

The double-blind trial assigned 160 Brazilian women aged 50–65, postmenopausal for more than 12 months and with a fall in the prior year, to oral D3 1,000 IU/day or placebo, 80 per group, for nine months. The source calls this vitamin D alone; exact product/excipients, exercise context, adherence and attrition cannot be qualified from the abstract. Low mean baseline 25-OHD does not establish that deficiency was an enrollment requirement or that every participant was deficient.

Reported mean±SD 25-OHD changed from 15.0±7.5 to 27.5±10.4 ng/mL with D3 and 16.9±6.7 to 13.8±6.0 with placebo. The source prints a +45.4% active change; its percentage denominator is not specified and is not silently repaired. The abstract reports **a 25.3% chair-rise-derived lower-limb strength increase within the D3 group, P=.036**, and a 6.8% lean-mass decrease within placebo, P=.030. Neither sentence supplies the treatment-by-time contrast, between-group CI or the other arm's chair-rise change. These cannot be promoted to verified randomized strength restoration, mass preservation or prevention of sarcopenia. Chair-rise is a functional task, not an isolated-force measurement; the exact time/repetition definition and grip result remain unverified.

The authors state ITT analysis using ANOVA, Student's t and Tukey tests, but complete-case counts, missing-data handling, endpoint hierarchy, multiplicity and harms are not available at this reading depth. FAPESP supported the work; the publisher declares no conflicts. No BUB1B/BUBR1 assay appears in the read portions; no absence claim is made about inaccessible material. Preserve the reported positive as a bounded follow-up lead, not a confirmed placebo-superior effect or a measured null.

## Daily D3 in low-functioning older adults: serum response without functional benefit

PMID37084814/PMC10447505, DOI10.1016/j.ajcnut.2023.04.021, EVIDENCE/NCT02015611. Complete scientific main, all three main tables, both native main figures, the complete linked corrigendum, selected current registry protocol/results modules, and posted protocol/SAP pages 1,2,8–11 were read. The supplement DOCX request returned HTML rather than a document; its tables/figures were not read. The 2020-dated protocol is a later posted version, not proof of the original 2013 specification. [Primary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10447505/), [corrigendum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10493470/), [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02015611).

The trial randomized 136 community-dwelling adults aged 65–89 with SPPB≤10 and screening 25-OHD 18 to <30 ng/mL: 66 D3 and 70 placebo. Mean age was 73.4±6.3 and SPPB 7.8±1.8. **The intervention began at 2,000 IU/day, but ten active participants increased to 4,000 IU/day after month four because their clinical-laboratory result remained below 30 ng/mL.** Matched placebo participants increased capsule count to preserve masking. Tishcon supplied the purchased capsules; the study did not independently verify supplement content. Background vitamin-D supplements were allowed within the enrollment limit and some participants changed them during follow-up. Calcium or exercise training was not a randomized cointervention.

Screening and research assays were materially different: screening mean 24.5±3.2 ng/mL versus later baseline LC-MS/MS mean 19.6±4.6; the latter ranged 7.2–32.9, with 36% below 18. These are not interchangeable baseline strata. The corrigendum corrects only the abstract conversion: 30 ng/mL corresponds to 75 nmol/L, not the originally printed 50. Severe deficiency was excluded by the screening assay, so these findings do not settle every deficiency-repletion context.

At 12 months 64 active/65 placebo remained; the five placebo intervention discontinuations did not all represent withdrawal. Native Figure1 and the registry distinguish this from seven participants not completing follow-up. Outcome-specific denominators were smaller. The primary body-mass-normalized leg-power contrast was **−.03±.06 W/kg, P=.63** (D3 minus placebo, least-squares mean±SE; 61 per arm at month12); absolute power was −4.00±5.26 W, P=.45. Other direct contrasts were knee extensor torque −4.25±2.37 Nm,P=.08; grip −.15±.69 kg,P=.82; SPPB −.18±.29 points,P=.53; usual gait speed .00±.02 m/s,P=.83; chair time −.08±.59 seconds,P=.89; and TUG .03±.27 seconds,P=.92. These are null treatment contrasts, not evidence from an active arm's own change. No treatment-contrast CIs were supplied, and none is reconstructed.

Meanwhile 25-OHD increased by a treatment contrast of 9.1±1.1 ng/mL (mean±SE),P<.0001. A biomarker increase did not establish functional recovery. No beneficial muscle-fiber composition, cross-sectional area, single-fiber force/velocity/power or VDR-protein response was demonstrated in the small biopsy substudy. Thirty-eight were selected,37 had a baseline biopsy, and outcome-specific paired samples were smaller; many type-II contractility measurements were unavailable. Main type-II-fiber comparison P=.91 and current registry P=.64 use differently described adjusted analyses; both are null and remain distinct.

The main uses constrained longitudinal models adjusted for age, sex, race and season. The current registry exposes exclusions from some secondary analyses for altered walking aids/pain and changes >2 IQR; the posted SAP describes potential outliers ≥3 IQR beyond quartiles. This discrepancy is unresolved and is not rewritten into a fully observed 136-person analysis. The primary leg-power registry instead lists seven missing month12 measures among completers. No broad multiplicity adjustment is described. Current registration dates to December2013, with results posted in2022; history was not read.

The main reports 10 versus15 serious events and72 versus93 nonserious events, none of the serious events attributed to intervention/procedures; two biopsy-related events occurred, one per arm. No serum calcium exceeded10.6 mg/dL. Six versus10 falls recorded as AEs are **not all falls**: registry calendars show mean1.40±2.85(n=60) versus1.47±2.76(n=64) falls, without a posted treatment test. Do not infer fall prevention or safety superiority from AE counts. Registry serious-event person counts also differ from event counts and cannot be summed into interchangeable incidence measures. NIH/NIA funded the study; authors declared no conflicts. BUBR1 and chromosome-fidelity assays are absent from the complete scientific main; VDR is a different protein.

## Monthly D3: active-comparator trial, no functional advantage and a falls signal

PMID26747333, DOI10.1001/jamainternmed.2015.7148, NCT01017354. Complete scientific main/disclosures were read through the official publisher's web representation; both native figures and all three native tables/footnotes were inspected. Full PDF, original protocol and supplement were not acquired; supplementary outcomes are therefore qualified only where the main reports them. The publisher's correction concerns the corresponding-author address. [Primary](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2478897), [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01017354).

Two hundred community-dwelling adults ≥70 with a prior fall were assigned monthly 24,000 IU D3(n=67),60,000 IU D3(n=67), or24,000 IU D3 plus300µg calcifediol(n=66), for12 months. There was **no untreated/placebo-only group**. DSM supplied masked oral solutions/capsules whose contents were assayed. The combination arm cannot be assigned to isolated D3. Monthly administration is not biologically established as equivalent to its arithmetic daily average. At baseline116/200 had 25-OHD<20 ng/mL, including26<10; the remainder were replete by the study definition.

The functional primary SPPB treatment-by-time test was null(P=.26), despite improved serum target attainment. Twelve-month adjusted SPPB changes(95%CI) were .38(.07,.68),.10(−.21,.41), and.11(−.19,.43), respectively. A positive within-arm CI for the lower dose does not establish benefit over placebo. Secondary adjusted faller percentages were47.9%(35.8–60.3),66.9%(54.4–77.5), and66.1%(53.5–76.8), with a **three-arm P=.048**; native Table2 marks both higher arms as pairwise different from24,000 IU but supplies no exact pairwise faller P values. Mean falls had overall P=.09; the main separately gives60,000 versus24,000 IU P=.02. Multiple secondary/exploratory tests were not adjusted, so these are regimen-specific safety signals rather than universal D3 harm. Fall counts were capped at five per participant for analysis.

Native Table3 explicitly reports **no significant treatment-by-baseline-deficiency interaction** for any primary/secondary endpoint. Achieved-serum quartiles are post-randomization observational analyses, not assigned treatment groups or established clinical target levels. The functional strategy changed during trial implementation: painful knee strength testing was abandoned and SPPB alone became the primary functional measure. The current registry still lists the four-test binary composite as primary and SPPB as secondary; exact amendment timing was not established from a history/protocol read.

All200 are included in the analyses. However, native Figure1 lists two deaths in the60,000-IU arm among four discontinuations; other arms include stroke, hip surgery, consent withdrawal and frailty. Do not repeat the prose's follow-up wording as proof that all200 had observed12-month measurements, or attribute the two deaths causally to D3. Adherence exceeded98% initially and94% later. Biochemical safety was similar except transient increased calcium excretion in higher arms at two weeks; this does not erase falls. Funding included Swiss National Science Foundation/VELUX and investigator-initiated industry support; relevant author financial relationships were disclosed. No BUBR1/fidelity assay is reported in the complete main.

## Competitive and identity limits

These sources retain an actual reported functional positive, measured randomized nulls, and a higher-versus-lower-dose falls signal without treating them as one homogeneous population or exposure. They do not justify replacing provisional nominees: the accessible positive lacks a verified between-group functional contrast, whereas the fully read daily trial supplies a primary muscle-power null. Niacin's proposed restoration bridge and apitegromab's SMA motor-function evidence are distinct hypotheses/clinical contexts, not proven cross-trial MVA superiority. No new original-source reading credit for those nominees is claimed.

The read-only check found no standalone D3 decision at clean committed243 `f07c1742d5ae6939aed222e68eb5ab366a0bba00`. Exact cholecalciferol/colecalciferol aliases map to CHEMBL1042/DrugCentral2840; broad NIH DSLD vitamin-D3 aliases span multiple groups. Catalogue approval metadata is not product/indication verification. The optional appraisal uses the exact molecule identity, preserves regimen-specific limitations, and leaves existing `new-calcitriol` unchanged. No D2, calcifediol, calcitriol or combination efficacy is inherited.
