# BODYBALANCE trial preparations: bounded primary qualification

**Decision: retain the tested BODYBALANCE collagen-peptide preparation as a qualified, limited functional-compensation challenger.** The 2015 study reports a positive controlled secondary isokinetic-strength comparison during supervised resistance training. The 2021 study reports a non-significant secondary isometric-force comparison, despite positive fat-free-mass results. Both results remain visible. These are different populations and assays, with overlapping investigators and manufacturer support; they establish neither independent-laboratory replication nor generic-collagen efficacy. No BUBR1 restoration, BUB1B-variant/MVA benefit, or superiority to provisional niacin/apitegromab is established.

## Trial 1: older sarcopenic men, 2015

Source: Zdzieblik et al., PMID **26353786**, PMCID **PMC4594048**, DOI **10.1017/S0007114515002810**. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4594048/). Evidence locators: Methods, Results, Tables 1–2 and Figures 1–5.

**Population and accounting.** Community-dwelling men over 65, reporting loss of strength/performance, capable of training, with low grip strength (<32 kg) and DXA-confirmed class I/II sarcopenia. The authors describe them as independently living and non-frail/non-cachectic, without regular exercise (>1 hour/week). Important exclusions included relevant chronic disease, exercise contraindications and recent cancer. They are not an MVA or pediatric population. Figure 1: 148 assessed, 88 excluded (80 eligibility, 6 declined, 2 other), 60 randomized and received allocation, 30 per arm. Four active and three placebo participants were lost/noncompliant; **26 versus 27 completers** were analyzed. The prose describes the 88 exclusions differently as sequential 42 plus 46 screening exclusions; preserve the total and the figure's reason breakdown. Completers averaged 72.2 years; 21 were class I and 32 class II (active 11/15; placebo 10/17). No intention-to-treat analysis was done.

**Exact exposure and comparator.** The intervention was 15 g/day of GELITA BODYBALANCE collagen peptides, derived by multistep degradation of type-I collagen, mean molecular weight approximately 3 kDa. Animal species, batch certificate and peptide sequences are not supplied. Table 1 reports, by weight, glycine 22.1%, proline 12.3%, hydroxyproline 11.3%, arginine 7.8%, leucine 2.7%, lysine 3.8%, isoleucine 1.3% and valine 2.4%, with the remaining amino acids preserved in the source table. The authors explicitly acknowledge relatively low biological value/low BCAA content. The comparator was silicon dioxide, **Sipernat 350 (Evonik)**; its gram amount is not specified in the inspected Methods. It supplied no extra calories/protein. The 2015 paper does not quantify the active product's calories. Thus the contrast is not isocaloric or isonitrogenous and cannot isolate a collagen-peptide-specific effect from providing protein/energy.

Both powders were dissolved in 250 mL water, taken as soon as possible and within one hour after training, with no other food in that hour, and at a comparable time on non-training days. Unused supplements and diaries checked adherence, but achieved numeric supplement adherence is unreported. Published exposure is not a proposed treatment regimen. No plasma-peptide pharmacokinetics or muscle exposure/engagement was measured.

**Training and diet.** Both arms received 12 weeks of guided, individually adapted whole-body resistance training, three 60-minute afternoon sessions weekly. Repetitions progressed from 15 in weeks 1–4, to 10 in weeks 5–9, to 8 in weeks 10–12, at four seconds per repetition. Missing >10% of training sessions triggered exclusion. Four-day dietary records suggested protein intake 0.91 g/kg/day and energy 1854 kcal/day, no significant arm/time dietary differences, with acknowledged underreporting possible. There was no supplement-only/no-training factorial comparison: the trial does not establish pharmacological synergy or benefit without training.

**Hierarchy and inference.** Change in DXA fat-free mass was the explicitly powered primary endpoint (assumed 2 kg change, SD 2.5, alpha .05, power .80; 25/arm plus dropout allowance). Strength was a secondary/non-primary outcome, not the endpoint powering the study. Random-number generation and investigator/participant blinding until secured data/statistical analysis are described; detailed allocation concealment and a prospective protocol/SAP are not available. No registration number appears in the main; bounded searching did not identify one. Prospective registration is therefore **unverified**, not asserted absent. Analyses used two-way repeated-measures ANOVA (treatment × time) for arm contrasts and paired tests within arms. No multiplicity control across secondary/body-composition outcomes is reported.

**Unresolved reporting defects.** The abstract gives strength +16.5 (SD 12.9) versus +7.3 (SD 13.2), whereas Results gives +16.12 versus +7.38 and labels the same dispersions SEM. The native Figure 5 caption also says SEM. Body-composition dispersion labels likewise differ between abstract and Results/figure caption. The rounded Table 2 means do not settle the exact change values. Table 2 calls an Nm measurement “Power”; the Methods identifies isokinetic strength, and it must not be reclassified as watts. The prose's blanket P<.001 within-group strength statement conflicts with the placebo Table 2 single star (P<.05). Figure 4's placebo caption says n=26 although the reported completer placebo group is 27. Figure 5's motor-control axis is labelled cm whereas Table 2 uses path length in mm; the plot and table do not support a clean quantitative conversion here. None of these issues is silently repaired; they limit numerical certainty without deleting the author-reported secondary strength interaction.

**Harms.** Seven dropouts were attributed to noncompliance, not supplement/placebo side effects; no serious adverse events or pathological routine-blood findings were reported. Diaries and before/after laboratory checks were planned, but there is no comprehensive armwise event table. Selected older men followed for 12 weeks do not establish long-term, pediatric, MVA or high-comorbidity safety.

## Trial 2: middle-aged overweight sedentary men, 2021

Source: Zdzieblik et al., PMID **33946565**, PMCID **PMC8125453**, DOI **10.3390/ijerph18094837**. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8125453/), [publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/9/4837), [registry DRKS00008925](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00008925). Evidence locators: complete scientific main, Tables 1–4, native Figures 1–2 and the one-page Table S1 supplement.

**Population and accounting.** Men 30–60 years, BMI 27.5–35, fat mass >25%, stable weight/eating habits, and no regular physical activity >60 minutes/week over the past year. Cardiovascular/metabolic/renal exercise contraindications and contraindications to products excluded participation. Figure 1: 126 assessed, three ineligible and three declined, **120 randomized and received allocation, 40/arm**. Noncompliance/nonappearance caused losses of 10 collagen, 9 placebo and 4 whey participants. The per-protocol main comparison has **30 collagen versus 31 placebo**; the exploratory three-arm analysis has **97 total**, including 36 whey. Mean analyzed ages were 51.8, 47.4 and 49.6 years respectively. The paper states an ITT analysis confirmed the PP findings, but provides no corresponding denominators, missing-data method, endpoint estimates or results table; that sentence is not a separately auditable confirmatory analysis.

**Preparation and nutrition.** BODYBALANCE (Gelita) was given as a 15 g daily powder sachet, compared with a 15 g silicon-dioxide sachet; the exploratory group received 15 g of **Volactive 90% whey-isolate product**. Each was dissolved in 250 mL water and taken within one hour after training or at the same time on non-training days. The paper describes the protein products as a 60 kcal/day caloric addition; silica was not protein/energy matched. The whey brand's “90%” and a 15 g powder dose should not be silently converted into an assayed 15 g of pure protein.

Table S1 reports the collagen amino-acid profile by % protein, including leucine 2.7%, glycine 22.1%, arginine 7.8%, and **tryptophan/cystine 0%**; whey has leucine 10.6%, glycine 1.4%, arginine 2.1%, tryptophan 1.4% and cystine 2.2%. These composition data and the authors' description of approximately 0.4 g leucine and 2.5 g essential amino acids per 15 g collagen characterize a protein source of different quality from whey. The overlapping collagen percentages match the 2015 table, and the paper calls the preparation identical. This supports a shared named preparation and reported amino-acid profile, **not verified batch identity, peptide sequence/distribution identity or current retail equivalence**. The 2021 main does not independently provide a batch certificate or molecular-weight measurement.

Three-day food records excluded the test products. Baseline dietary protein averaged 1.05, 1.09 and 1.11 g/kg/day, and the authors judged participants not deficient. The placebo group reported declining energy intake (2573 to 2419 kcal/day; within-arm P=.043); collagen reported 2739 to 2863. Final energy and carbohydrate intake differed between collagen and placebo (P=.016/.026), and reporting error is possible. Authors argue the 60 kcal addition is insufficient to explain their findings; that argument does not create a nutrient-matched control. Table 4 labels the whey dietary sample n=31, versus n=36 in the main outcome tables, with no clear accounting explanation.

**Co-intervention and hierarchy.** All groups performed 12 weeks of supervised resistance training, three 60-minute sessions/week, with warm-up and a three-set program including bilateral leg press, reverse crunch, lat pull, sit-up and chest press. Repetitions/load progressed from 15 at 70% 1RM in weeks 1–2 to 12/75%, 10/80% and 8/85% in subsequent blocks; loads rose by 5–10% when technique/sets were completed. PP compliance required at least 30 of 36 sessions and at least 80% supplement use. Achieved numeric adherence beyond these thresholds is not given. No untrained supplement arm tests synergy.

FFM in kg, collagen versus placebo, was primary. Fat mass, weight, waist and maximum voluntary isometric leg-press contraction were secondary; whey contrasts were explicitly exploratory. The main uses linear mixed models with treatment/time factors and Tables 2–3 identify treatment × time tests; separate within-arm tests and Hedges-corrected Cohen effect sizes are also reported. Multiple secondary outcomes, relative/absolute body measures and exploratory comparisons were tested at two-sided .05, without a stated multiplicity correction. The paper says its sample-size calculation used the earlier trial but does not supply the full assumed effect/variance/power calculation.

**Registration/reporting limits.** The current DRKS record labels registration **retrospective**, dated 2015-09-01, with actual start 2014-11-01 and completion 2015-09-30. It lists DXA muscle mass as primary and arm/leg strength plus metabolic risk outcomes as secondary. Arm strength and several registered metabolic outcomes have no results in the inspected main/supplement. The current registry also mentions reduced muscle mass/handgrip function at inclusion, whereas the article principally describes overweight sedentary men. Its ethics approval date (2014-10-10, 205/14) differs from the article's 2014-11-05 (205-14). These are unresolved reporting/eligibility discrepancies, not proof of outcome switching or misconduct. No public protocol/SAP is linked in the current record. The history index lists three releases (2015-09-01, 2018-09-10, 2025-02-19); the index was read, but prior-version field contents were not recovered or compared. Current registry content alone does not establish prospective prespecification. The Methods' verbal subtraction direction for FFM is opposite to the positive changes labelled as gains in Results; this qualification follows the explicit Results directions without altering source values.

**Harms.** No dropout was attributed to product-related adverse effects. The authors report no adverse events in the **PP population** and no abnormal routine creatine-kinase/urea findings. Comprehensive all-randomized harms and longer-term safety are not reported.

## Correction, critical letter, and independence

The [2025 corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711452510425X), PMID **40859798**, explicitly corrects the 2015 acknowledgements/COI. It states Gelita supplied study samples and paid participant compensation and sample-analysis costs, while reporting no specific agency grant. It discloses Steffen Oesser as co-inventor on a related patent application filed **13 May 2013**, with no royalty or other exploitation remuneration. Investigator independence is still asserted. The correction does **not** revise strength/body-composition numbers, dispersion labels, analyses or endpoint hierarchy. It is not a retraction.

The 2021 report already states Gelita product provision and partial funding, Oesser's partly Gelita-supported lectures and collagen-patent co-inventorship, and Zdzieblik's move to the Collagen Research Institute after study completion. It says funders had no role in design, collection, analysis, interpretation or publication and that assessors/analysts lacked financial/competing interests. These assertions coexist with the disclosed relationships. The overlapping team and institution mean the second trial is not independent-laboratory replication.

The full [Phillips et al. 2016 critical letter](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000711451600221X), PMID **27267663**, was read as text and both native PDF pages. It challenges the unusually large FFM gain/fat loss, asks whether DXA FFM represents skeletal muscle or other tissue, and disputes proposed leucine, arginine/blood-flow and creatine-substrate explanations at the stated intake. It compares the magnitude with other literature and asks for a better explanation. The cited comparator studies were not newly qualified here. Its quoted dispersion/arginine figures are the letter's claims, not corrected trial data. The letter provides **no torque reanalysis, replacement strength estimate, or proof that the observed strength contrast is false**. Its own COIs include dairy-industry grants/speaking/travel and Pepsico for Phillips; beef/dairy research/advisory and egg advisory interests for Paddon-Jones; and Nutricia consulting for van Loon. Those interests do not by themselves settle its scientific objections.

Original/comment metadata links the letter to the trial and the later correction; bounded exact-title/author reply searches identified no linked author reply. **No reply was recovered**, not “no reply exists.” The 2021 discussion is a later study discussion, not a verified formal response to the letter. Raw retrieval/search records retain this boundary.

## Fair comparison and next evidentiary threshold

The 2015 randomized, blinded training-plus-supplement contrast supplies more direct controlled strength-design evidence than niacin's small open-label strength observation. It does not demonstrate a larger effect, a disease-specific benefit or superiority to niacin. Apitegromab's different pediatric SMA primary motor endpoint cannot be quantitatively ranked against these secondary dynamometer outcomes. No incumbent or challenger receives automatic preference from its status or incomplete source access.

The same evidence bar applies to the tested collagen preparation and the provisional nominees: justified preparation/exposure, useful absolute function, sustainable tissue renewal and chromosome-segregation fidelity in relevant affected versus corrected models. Collagen has oral trial exposure and a bounded secondary function positive, but **no direct measured muscle exposure, tissue-renewal evidence, fidelity result or MVA efficacy** in these two trials. Mechanistic discussion of mTOR, connective tissue, creatine or DXA mass does not fill those gaps. Functional BUBR1 mediation is additionally required only if claiming restoration; it is not an extra gate uniquely imposed on a compensation challenger.

A preparation-specific compensation comparison is justified at research-triage level. Confidence would increase with prospectively specified, independently conducted functional replication using an authenticated preparation, appropriate protein/energy comparator, complete randomized accounting and harmonized reporting. This is a decision criterion, not an authorized experiment or clinical recommendation. Keep the 2021 functional null attached to any future positive summary. Do not pool Nm with N, infer functional benefit from mass alone, or transfer either result to generic collagen, retail mixtures, MVA, or a child.
