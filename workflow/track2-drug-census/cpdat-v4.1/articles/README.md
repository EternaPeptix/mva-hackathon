# CPDat v4.1 article-kind discovery screen

This is a separate screen of CPDat v4.1 composition rows where Product Use Category kind is **Article**, alongside the [Formulation screen](../README.md). Article categories include construction materials, batteries, furnishings, vehicles, food-contact items, toys, and industrial components. They are not a proxy for products commonly purchased or for measured personal exposure.

After requiring a DTXSID, the v4.1 export contains **76,213 Article-kind rows**, **2,530 DTXSIDs**, **9,929 normalized names** and **14 categories**. The frozen 7,362-record BUB1B/BUBR1 title-and-abstract screen retained **195 review-class field rows**, across **122 source/ID records** and **79 DTXSIDs**. It separately counts **14,986 low-specificity** and **41 multi-DTXSID ambiguous** matches. A match is a chemical name in a title or abstract of a paper returned by the gene/MVA full-text query; it need not be a direct chemical–BUB1B result.

The v4.0 Article-kind snapshot has 63,457 rows with DTXSIDs, 2,468 DTXSIDs and 190 review-class rows across 125 source/ID records and 79 DTXSIDs. Across the current v4.1 Article and Formulation subsets, 1,634 DTXSIDs occur in both; **896 occur in Article but not Formulation**. Only **six** of the Article-only exact-hit DTXSIDs remain review-class after the collision downgrades. Their initial source-role triage is in [new-article-only-hit-triage.tsv](new-article-only-hit-triage.tsv); none establishes an MVA restoration or muscle-benefit route.

Two potentially misleading name matches were explicitly separated. The article on MnBP exposure in human granulosa cells reports reduced BUB1B RNA, but CPDat's generic “Phthalate” DTXSID is not MnBP; the source-specific [MnBP appraisal](../../monobutyl-phthalate-bubr1b-qualification.md) is not joined to that identity. The ovarian-toxicity study uses **4-vinylcyclohexene diepoxide**, not CPDat's parent **4-vinylcyclohexene**. That parent-name fragment is downgraded to low-specificity and receives no effect inheritance. These are adverse-direction exposure/model comparators, not treatment leads or evidence of ordinary-use harm.

The new article-only exact-hit triage resolves several other apparent leads. The **pyrene** match is a lexical fragment of *benzo[a]pyrene* in a computational endocrine-disruptor abstract, not a pyrene administration result. The interferon-gamma study suppressed NF1-associated MPNST models through an IFITM1/Ras-ERK mechanism, but did not measure BUB1B or MVA. Ethidium bromide was a cell-staining reagent. The ozone exposure study reports **2.16-fold lower whole-lung Bub1b RNA at 24 h** after 1,000 ppb exposure for 3 h in neonatal mice (published q=0.043); independent GEO reanalysis reproduced the Bub1b row to rounding. BUBR1 protein/function, MVA and muscle outcomes were not tested. This pollutant exposure is not a BUB1B-restoration candidate, and the result does not transfer to ambient exposure or a distinct medical-ozone route. See the [source qualification and reanalysis](../../ozone-bubr1b-qualification.md).

Reproduce from the repository root with `build_cpdat_bub1b_screen.py --puc-kind Article`. The [source manifest](source-manifest.json) binds the downloaded bytes and outputs. The v4.1 bulk export uses Data Document ID + Product Name as a descriptive key because it has no stable product-ID field. Categories and product keys differ by version; these counts do not estimate product prevalence or exposure.
