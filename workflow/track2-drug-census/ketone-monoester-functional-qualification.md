# Ketone monoester: a real training signal with context-dependent counterevidence

**Retain an exploratory muscle-performance comparison, without changing the provisional nominees.** The exact assessed molecule is (R)-3-hydroxybutyl (R)-3-hydroxybutyrate. It has controlled functional positives, but the nutritional comparator, exercise setting and treatment timing matter. These studies do not establish BUBR1 restoration, MVA benefit or comparative superiority over niacin or apitegromab.

## Training benefit: credit the measured contrast

Robberechts used pure monoester bought from KetoneAid against an equal-energy MCT drink with a bitter additive. Both arms received common carbohydrate and protein/carbohydrate recovery support. Intake was described immediately after training and before sleep; the off-day schedule was not fully explicit. This is an active nutritional comparison, not an inert-placebo test or evidence for interchangeable retail ketones.

One participant per arm withdrew with illness, leaving 14 completers per arm. The entered count and post-allocation withdrawals support 30 randomized, but that allocation count is reconstructed rather than explicitly displayed. The [current official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05932420) was first submitted after primary completion. Time-trial power is one of four registered primaries; prospective endpoint selection and family-wide correction across the four are not established. The paper's repeated-measures analysis and Šidák comparisons do not remove that limitation.

Peak ramp-power improvement also favored monoester by 27 W (CI 13–41), while the absolute final comparison was not significant (P = .091). Relative VO2peak improvement had a baseline imbalance. Added lean-mass, stress-recovery and cardiac adaptation effects were not detected. Citrate-synthase and complex-II observations do not prove mediation, and ketone-related metabolomics findings did not survive multiplicity correction. Illness/training interruptions were reported and no arrhythmias were detected; no comprehensive adverse-event table establishes broad safety. The appendix and peer-review files remain unread. Academic funding and no declared competing interests are recorded in the structured qualification.

## Acute results do not answer the same question

Poffé used the same molecule from TdeltaS during an acute exercise protocol, with common carbohydrate and a bicarbonate factor. The collagen-based comparator matched taste, volume and viscosity; equal energy was not established. Native Figure 4B supports the pooled ketone decrement. Figure 4C additionally shows the ketone-only versus control interval crossing zero graphically; no exact pairwise endpoints or P value were reconstructed. The **5-second sprint benefit belongs to bicarbonate**, not ketone supplementation. Numeric performance interaction results remain unreported; the abstract/main denominator disagreement is not resolved into invented attrition.

Ketone conditions had slightly higher GI-symptom scores. Bicarbonate changed circulating ketones as well as acid–base measures, so this design does not isolate acid–base causality. An acute decrement does not directly refute a post-exercise training-adaptation strategy. The newly obtained Europe PMC ZIP contains original figure versions, not an additional experiment or supplementary outcome dataset; the full PDF remains unavailable.

Cox study 5 retains a genuine reported distance positive under a different carbohydrate contrast. Its eight-person performance group cannot inherit biopsies from other studies in the paper. Native Figure 7 and the supplement disagree on carbohydrate proportions and some panel descriptions; the exact 411 m result is explicit in the results text, but an unambiguous mixture ratio is not asserted. Patent/royalty and TdeltaS relationships were declared. This remains a targeted study-5 check, not a complete five-study appraisal.

## Clinical and older-muscle findings remain incomplete

The McArdle abstract reports an acute monoester exposure in a disease that prevents muscle-glycogen use. Carbohydrate outperformed ketone by 21.5% (P = .001), while its 12.6% estimate versus placebo had P = .057. The latter is not an established conventional superiority result. Ketosis was achieved, illustrating that biochemical engagement need not improve useful function. Full methods, paired precision, harms and overlap with earlier McArdle cohorts are unknown. This does not establish MVA harm or failure of the ketone class.

Andersen's abstract retains older-group local power/capacity positives, economy and mitochondrial nulls, and fatigue/pH tradeoffs. A time-torque integral is not relabeled as mechanical work in joules. A higher relative power decline might partly reflect a higher initial peak; final absolute power is unverified. Group-specific significance does not establish an age interaction. The [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05732909) describes KE4 monoester, a lipid comparator matched for calories/volume/taste, shared glucose infusion and at least four weeks between visits. Those details are registry information, not verified final administered methods. Its first submission followed the recorded actual study start; historical versions were not audited. The full main, native supplements, endpoint denominators and harms remain unavailable.

## Competitiveness and the next decision

The controlled performance positives earn compensation-route credit under the same standards as the nominees. No BUBR1 assay is required merely to recognize useful function. However, these populations, endpoints and exposures do not establish a general muscle-rescue effect or an MVA ranking. Niacin and apitegromab remain provisional, not proven superior.

Priority would rise with a relevant metabolic phenotype, measured achievable exposure and controlled improvement in absolute useful output while preserving renewal and chromosome fidelity in affected/corrected models. BUBR1 mediation is additionally required if claiming protein restoration. Bounded source searches found no qualifying BUB1B/BUBR1 intervention; this is not a universal-negative claim. Salts, acetoacetate ester mixtures, free racemic butanediol and ketogenic diets remain distinct. No pediatric treatment regimen is proposed.
