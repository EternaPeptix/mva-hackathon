## What the primary abstract actually supports

The abstract describes **62 men with sarcopenia**, age **68.63 ± 2.86 years**, in a supervised **eight-week randomized trial**. The four arms are resistance training (RT), epicatechin (EP), RT+EP, and placebo (PL). It describes a double-blind method, pre/post measurements and one-way ANOVA for between-group differences. Allocation details, the scope of blinding and whether 62 is the complete analyzed population are unavailable at this depth; the age dispersion is not explicitly labelled SD or SEM in the abstract.

| Outcome | Reported comparison | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Timed up-and-go (TUG) | All experimental arms, including **EP alone**, reported better results than PL, at the stated P≤.05 threshold | Absolute time/change, CI, baseline adjustment, attrition and whether RT+EP exceeds RT specifically on TUG |
| Appendicular muscle mass index | All experimental arms reported improvement versus PL | Numerical change, assay method, meaningful threshold and durability |
| Leg-press and chest-press strength | RT+EP reported the greatest significant increases compared with RT, EP and PL | Exact test protocol, numerical treatment effect and whether EP alone improves strength versus PL |
| Follistatin and follistatin/myostatin ratio | RT+EP reported the greatest significant increases compared with the other arms | Exact concentrations, effect estimates, assay characteristics and mediation |
| Myostatin | Significant decreases reported only for RT+EP and RT | The reference contrast for that statement and an independently verified EP-versus-PL null |

The combination-versus-RT strength statement is relevant to an added-supplement effect under training. It does not show pharmacological synergy, because the abstract supplies no factorial interaction test. Conversely, the combination strength advantage must not be assigned to epicatechin alone. The TUG statement gives a genuine reported single-agent functional positive; it should not be erased because single-agent strength details are absent. The abstract's broad “greatest improvement” conclusion is not a licence to assert a significant combination-versus-exercise difference for every endpoint.

Plasma myostatin/follistatin responses are biomarkers, not proof of a causal muscle-growth mechanism, selective target inhibition, or equivalence to an anti-myostatin drug. No BUBR1/checkpoint or MVA outcome is reported in the abstract. Full-paper assay absence is not claimed from abstract depth.

## Information that must remain unknown

- Administered epicatechin stereochemistry, source, purity, dose, vehicle, formulation and achieved exposure.
- Randomization sequence, allocation concealment, matched-placebo details, assessor masking and what could be blinded in the exercise arms.
- Per-arm randomized/analyzed denominators, withdrawals, exclusions, adherence and the analysis population.
- Numerical baseline/follow-up values, change scores, treatment effects, uncertainty intervals, baseline imbalance and covariate handling.
- A prospectively defined primary endpoint, registry/protocol, exact statistical model and multiplicity correction across outcomes and comparisons.
- Systematic harms, tolerability, durability and pediatric or neuromuscular-disease transfer.

The abstract's P≤.05 language is not a substitute for exact tests and uncertainty. Missing harm reporting is not a zero-event or safety result. The endpoint results are reported primary findings; their reproducibility and clinical magnitude cannot be independently assessed from the available abstract alone.
