# jeweled — Track 2: testing two ways to preserve muscle

**Research proposal, revised 21 September 2026.**

**Provisional after the broad drug search:** the [new competitive assessment](../workflow/track2-drug-census/decision-report.md) recommends niacin plus apitegromab, tested separately, for a muscle-benefit research objective, with everolimus retained as a conditional signaling probe. The preceding niacin/everolimus proposal below and its video await incorporation of that recommendation. Track 2 is unsubmitted; no medication has demonstrated MVA benefit.

The preceding proposal nominated **niacin (nicotinic acid) as the research lead and everolimus as a downstream mechanism-probe arm**, tested separately. The aim is to preserve useful muscle and its ability to renew in a child with recorded muscle atrophy and failure to thrive. The lead question is whether increasing usable BUBR1—the protein that helps cells distribute chromosomes correctly—can improve that function.

**Protein insufficiency is an experimentally supported MVA1 mechanism.** [Suijkerbuijk and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2887387/) linked reduced transcript supply and unstable missense proteins to low BUBR1 in selected patient cells. Our proposed pair matches that reported architecture, although N1002K's actual effect is unmeasured. Niacin leads because the NAD/SIRT2 hypothesis addresses this proximal defect and has human muscle NAD-engagement evidence. Everolimus tests a downstream signaling hypothesis whose elevation is not established here. Neither drug has demonstrated benefit for this genotype; the ordering prioritizes mechanism match, not proven efficacy or pediatric safety. The [route decisions](appendices/track2-route-decisions.md) retain counterevidence and promotion conditions.

## What the genetic result means


**BUB1B is the gene; BUBR1 is its protein product.** Our [Track 1 hypothesis](jeweled_track1_report_submission.md) involves two changes: Leu737Ter, which predicts an early stop and a 736-amino-acid protein, and Asn1002Lys, which changes one protein building block. The second change remains a variant of uncertain significance: its functional effect is unmeasured.

Phase remains unresolved. **Confirmed cis would give `[L737Ter;N1002K]/WT` and retire this pair as the compound-heterozygous explanation of MVA1.** Ordinary translation from the shared allele stops before Asn1002Lys; the two changes do not establish biallelic disease. Continuing a BUB1B-based case explanation would require an independently demonstrated additional pathogenic allele in trans or dominant mechanism. Neither uncertain protein dosage nor a carrier-mouse observation supplies that evidence. The present drug rationale is conditional on the causal model; a confirmed cis result would suspend advancement on its biallelic premise. RNA decay, protein abundance and actual function still require measurement.

## Why niacin leads

In the 2010 study's HeLa titration, reducing BUBR1 to about 6% caused missegregation in most cells, whereas about 13% had little effect. This supports an abundance-dependent rescue hypothesis; these assay-specific levels are not a patient treatment threshold.

**NAD is a molecule cells use for energy chemistry and several regulatory enzymes.** Niacin can supply material for making it. In [North and colleagues' experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088/), the NAD-dependent enzyme SIRT2 stabilized BUBR1. A different NAD precursor, NMN, increased BUBR1 abundance; genetically increasing SIRT2 improved selected cardiac outcomes and male survival in BubR1-deficient mice. Those results were not niacin treatment or demonstrated skeletal-muscle rescue.

Niacin nevertheless has a human muscle connection. [Pirinen's small open-label mitochondrial-myopathy study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32386566/) corrected patient muscle NAD, but strength also improved in niacin-treated healthy controls. Patient knee-extension strength rose about **1.1-fold after ten months**; other muscle groups improved more. With no placebo group, this does not establish a disease-specific strength benefit. We use it as evidence of human muscle NAD engagement, not demonstrated disease rescue. A [pediatric mitochondrial-myopathy case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39173541/) had biochemical correction with little clinical improvement and continued progression. Correcting NAD therefore cannot serve as the success endpoint.

The missing chain is specific: can niacin reach and change the relevant muscle chemistry, increase functional BUBR1, and preserve productive muscle? More defective protein might be unhelpful. A benefit through another mechanism could still be valuable, but would not prove BUBR1 restoration. Niacin, nicotinamide, NMN and NR are different substances; their results are not interchangeable. Prescription-niacin pediatric safety and effectiveness are unestablished.

## Why everolimus remains a downstream probe

**mTORC1 is a cellular growth-and-nutrient signaling system.** Too much activity could impair muscle maintenance, but too little could also compromise growth and repair. [Sieben's Figure 5C](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/126863/figure/5) shows visually stronger phospho-S6K and phospho-4EBP1 bands in muscle from monoallelic BubR1+/X753 carriers, with **two mice per genotype**. Ponceau S provides a loading control, but total S6K/4EBP1 denominators, band quantification and inferential statistics are absent. This is a qualitative blot observation, not demonstrated increased pathway activity, an established association in biallelic MVA1, or drug rescue.

[Everolimus](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=2150f73a-179b-4afc-b8ce-67c85cc72f04) inhibits this system and has pediatric formulations and monitoring experience in other diseases. The proposed test is partial normalization toward the corrected-cell range. A genetic reduction of pathway activity would first help distinguish a harmful signal from an accompanying marker.

Counterevidence matters. [Baker 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3785294/) mentions similar age-related phenotypes with and without rapamycin as a parenthetical unpublished observation, not a fully reported treatment study. [Goutas 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10149375/) found that everolimus lowered BUBR1 after 24 hours in late-passage, nonmutant umbilical-cord cells, with no corresponding early-passage reduction. This is a cell-state-specific concern to test, not established harm in MVA. These observations make preserved muscle growth, renewal and BUBR1 function essential checks. An intermittent schedule is a testable option, not an established solution.

A [2026 fly study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13121444/) adds a relevant intervention precedent: rapamycin increased neural-stem-cell counts after depletion of the checkpoint gene **bub3**. This was not BUB1B-mutant muscle or everolimus. Genetic interventions that boosted autophagy preserved more stem cells without restoring their progeny counts or brain size. This supports testing a downstream proteostasis route while reinforcing the need to measure productive tissue, not cell survival alone.

## What the completed computation established

The [sequence and population analysis](../workflow/track2-model/README.md) reconstructed possible protein products and explored declared assumptions. The standalone **[Cell selection and tissue output explorer](../workflow/track2-model/explorer/README.md)** makes 192 retained comparisons available offline, with assumption controls, absolute output and downloadable scenario data. In one synthetic selective-killing example, the fraction of cells with the expected chromosome count rose **16.8 percentage points while modeled differentiated-cell time fell 11.0%**. This exposes a selection-induced false improvement: a healthier-looking percentage can coexist with less tissue. The reusable contribution is an assay decision rule—track the starting lineages and absolute tissue output alongside the fraction of surviving chromosome-normal cells. The implemented model tracks aggregate cell compartments; following individual starting lineages is the proposed experimental extension. The result is not a drug effect or a patient forecast.

**A completed second worked example tests transfer across genes.** The [public replay](../workflow/track2-transfer/README.md) reconstructs this BUB1B pair and a synthetic homozygous TRIP13 Arg354Ter genotype from public sequence. It computes the latter's stop product and positional RNA-decay prediction, then applies the same source-curated mechanism gates. The BUBR1-restoration rationale does not transfer to TRIP13, and no qualified lead emerges from the three reviewed routes. This demonstrates two workflow executions, including one synthetic genotype—not two-patient validation. Variant selection and mechanistic evidence curation remain expert inputs; this is not raw-genome-to-drug automation.

We also [reanalyzed public muscle RNA counts](../workflow/track2-rna/README.md): nine mice, 12,989 retained genes and 150 pathway comparisons. None passed the global statistical threshold. The mTORC1 comparison had P = 0.219 and adjusted false-discovery rate = 0.983. RNA measurements did not corroborate pathway activation; they neither validate nor refute the qualitative phosphoprotein-band observation. This reused study data and supplies no independent biological replication. Neither analysis ranks drug efficacy.

## A staged experiment a partner team could undertake

This staged design describes the preceding niacin/everolimus comparison. It awaits incorporation of apitegromab-specific myostatin target-engagement, muscle-function and bone/tumor risk assessments from the new review. It is not ready for portal submission in this form.

No laboratory partnership or patient-derived model access is established. The proposed partners are a genetics center, a laboratory that measures muscle formation and contraction, a clinical pharmacologist and the treating oncology team. The [detailed experimental plan](appendices/track2-evidence-pipeline.md) defines the proposed comparisons and decisions. The first partner milestone is a reproducible muscle defect in an interpretable affected-versus-corrected model; drug confirmation follows successful model and exposure qualification.

**First experiment: qualify muscle function before adding either drug.** Compare affected and genetically corrected myogenic cultures, with procedural controls, for absolute stimulated muscle output, viable tissue and progenitor outcomes. Verify allele products and retain independent editing/preparation identities. An engineered model must be labeled and retain unresolved phase alternatives. The deliverable is a genetically interpretable functional deficit, or an explicit qualification failure—not a drug-negative result. The [execution milestones and required capabilities](appendices/track2-evidence-pipeline.md#first-execution-milestone-and-dependencies) identify what a partner must establish before committing resources.

1. **Qualify the model.** Resolve phase early and apply the cis stop above; measure each allele's RNA, protein and function. Compare affected cells with genetically corrected and procedural controls. Engineered models must retain the unresolved alternatives. Initial fibroblast work can screen allele mechanisms economically, but muscle benefit needs a muscle model.
2. **Test mechanisms and exposures.** Establish whether niacin improves working BUBR1 through NAD, and whether excessive mTORC1 activity independently contributes to the defect. Test the drugs separately at measured, pharmacologically justified concentrations. In parallel, qualify whether senescent cells—cells persistently withdrawn from division—harm muscle renewal; same-gene genetic clearance evidence warrants this reserve investigation.
3. **Measure useful muscle, then confirm.** Follow both an initial population of renewable muscle progenitors and an already formed muscle population. Measure absolute contractile output and repeated-stimulation performance, alongside productive cell replacement and chromosome accuracy. Freeze one regimen per drug after a pilot; confirm with independent preparations, masked measurements and the specified correction for multiple comparisons.

Treatment must improve affected-cell output against matched vehicle controls. Responses in genetically corrected cells help distinguish a general functional benefit from a genotype-selective effect; improvement in both can still be useful, but does not by itself prove BUBR1 restoration.

**Improvement in either muscle population can qualify, provided the other population, renewal capacity and chromosome accuracy are preserved.** Both need not improve. Fewer division errors because cells stopped dividing or died do not count as repair. Reduced cellular-aging markers alone do not count as muscle benefit. A precise negative applies to the tested genotype, exposure and assay; inadequate exposure is an uninterpretable nonresponse. The experimental results can reverse the current ordering; both implementations must meet the same functional standard.

## Scope and contribution

This is a research nomination and future experimental plan, with no patient drug-response data or dosing recommendation. Recorded rhabdomyosarcoma makes [tumor and treatment compatibility](appendices/track2-evidence-pipeline.md#rhabdomyosarcoma-evidence-and-treatment-compatibility) consequential: current tumor status and regimen are unknown, and neither candidate is assumed to protect against cancer. Renal function, interactions, growth, immune and metabolic effects require individual assessment before translation.

If independently validated, this comparison could distinguish a rescuable BUBR1 deficiency from harmful downstream growth signaling and identify which muscle-cell state benefits.

The innovation claim centers on the lineage-and-output assessment: distinguish restoration from apparent improvement caused by selective cell loss, then require greater useful muscle output without sacrificing renewal or chromosome accuracy. The synthetic counterexample above makes that decision problem explicit; it does not establish how often real assays are misled or claim invention of lineage tracking. The scalable component is the sequence-to-mechanism-to-function decision process, supported by reproducible public-data analyses. Drug choices and exposures must be requalified for each genotype and tissue. The code and decision rules are reusable; new tissue models remain the principal experimental bottleneck. This addresses the challenge's [rigor, impact, innovation and scalability criteria](https://huggingface.co/spaces/SageBio/rare-disease-real-kid-mva-hackathon-2026/blob/main/tabs/about.py) without claiming a new drug discovery.
