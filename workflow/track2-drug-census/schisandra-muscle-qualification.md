# Schisandra chinensis extract: a common-compound muscle-force reserve

**Disposition: retain as a supplementary functional challenger; no nominee change.** Two small randomized adult studies report gains in instrumented knee-extension strength after 12 weeks of a specific *Schisandra chinensis* extract. Neither study measured daily mobility benefit, BUB1B/BUBR1, chromosome fidelity, aneuploidy or MVA. This is a muscle-compensation hypothesis, not a BUBR1-restoration route or an MVA treatment result.

This is a supplementary common-compound qualification outside the current 289-appraisal curated drug-decision ledger. Its source identities and saved-abstract provenance are recorded in the companion JSON and external source manifest. It adds no original-queue reading credit.

## Human strength evidence

**Park et al. 2020 (PMID 32260466; PMCID PMC7177795).** The complete open-access paper and official UMIN record were reviewed. Fifty-two healthy postmenopausal women aged 55–78 were randomized; 45 were analyzed after 12 weeks (24 extract, 21 starch placebo). Participants with chronic disease and those in regular exercise programs were excluded. The administered product was 1 g/day of Bio-Port Korea extract (lot BPK-SC-001), made with 20% ethanol at 90 °C for four hours and standardized to 4.92 ± 0.06 mg/g schizandrin. Quadriceps strength showed a group-by-time interaction (P=.001); the paper reports a 7.7% increase within the extract arm. Resting lactate also had an interaction (P=.038). There was no group-by-time effect on handgrip, one-leg stand, 4-m walk, five-times chair stand, appendicular muscle mass, CK, LDH or pyruvate. The paper gives no between-group strength estimate with a confidence interval. Seven randomized participants did not complete the study: two active-arm adherence records were unclear, and five placebo participants withdrew consent or reported abdominal pain.

The study used repeated-measures ANOVA and paired tests after significant interactions with nominal P<.05; no multiplicity adjustment is reported. The UMIN record states that trial information was first disclosed in September 2016, after data were complete in March 2015 and analysis concluded in May 2015. It labels results unpublished and gives a target sample of 45. This retrospective registration weakens confidence in outcome-selection control.

A 2017 conference abstract (PMCID PMC6246645) reports 48 women, the same 12-week 250-mg capsule schedule, overlapping Dong-A investigators and a closely matching trial title. The UMIN record links the same study concept and a target of 45; the 2020 paper later reports 52 randomized and 45 analyzed. **The conference abstract is treated as a probable preliminary report of the 2020 cohort, not an independent third trial.** Its sample-count differences remain unresolved.

**Cho et al. 2021 (PMID 33710261; NCT03402308).** The PubMed abstract, official ClinicalTrials.gov record and publisher-indexed article/table text were reviewed; the publisher’s complete article returned HTTP 403, and its full supplement and SAP were not available. The trial randomized 54 adults over 50 with relatively low muscle mass to 1 g/day extract or placebo for 12 weeks; both groups were asked to walk regularly. Publisher-indexed text reports 67 screened/recruited and 46 completers. The indexed primary-outcome table identifies an intention-to-treat set of 54 and reports baseline-, sex- and age-adjusted absolute peak-torque differences favoring extract: right knee extension +10.22 Nm (95% CI 3.70–16.75; P=.003) and left +6.71 Nm (95% CI 0.29–13.14; P=.041). Muscle mass, handgrip, other physical-function tests, inflammatory/oxidative markers and EQ-5D did not differ. The abstract reports no adverse events; the complete safety table and denominator were not available in this review. The registry specifies peak torque/bodyweight at 60°/s as the primary measure, whereas the publisher’s primary-outcome table presents absolute torque in Nm; retain this as an unresolved metric/reporting discrepancy. The registry was first posted in January 2018, after recruitment began in June 2017 but before the reported March 2018 completion. The 2021 extract was from the same supplier, with publisher-indexed text reporting 3.16 mg/g schizandrin; equivalence to the 2020 lot is not established.

These studies are separate randomized trials, but share the Bio-Port supplier/research network and do not establish independent-laboratory replication. Both test product-specific extracts in older adults over 12 weeks. Their force findings do not demonstrate transfer to a child with MVA, sustained daily function, muscle growth or faithful chromosome segregation.

## Product and competitive interpretation

Korea’s MFDS recognizes a Bio-Port Korea *Schisandra* ingredient as a health-functional-food ingredient with a “may help improve muscle strength” claim and a recommended intake of 1,582 mg/day. The page cautions children, pregnant or nursing people, and people before or after surgery. This is a jurisdiction-specific functional-food recognition, not a medicine indication or evidence of pediatric suitability; the trials used 1 g/day and do not show that the higher label dose is more effective.

Compared with niacin, Schisandra has a better-controlled human strength signal, but niacin retains the distinct NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 restoration hypothesis. The endpoints and populations do not permit an efficacy ranking between them. Compared with apitegromab, Schisandra has no pediatric neuromuscular-disease motor-function trial and did not improve measured mobility or quality of life in these studies. Apitegromab’s SMA evidence also does not establish MVA benefit. **Schisandra therefore merits an appendix-level muscle-force reserve, not a Track 2 lead or a claim of BUBR1 engagement.**

If this route is carried into a research plan, first authenticate the exact extract and exposure, then test useful absolute muscle output, renewal and chromosome fidelity in relevant affected and corrected models. A BUBR1 assay is required only if the proposal claims restoration. No human dosing or treatment recommendation follows from this appraisal.

## Bounded independent source check (28 September 2026)

A separate, unblinded check reread the complete 2020 PMC article and official UMIN record, then checked the 2021 PubMed abstract, current ClinicalTrials.gov record and publisher-indexed Table 2. It confirmed the outcome distinctions above and identified the registered normalized-torque versus published absolute-Nm discrepancy. The 2021 full article, supplement and SAP remain unavailable in this check. The review found no BUB1B/BUBR1, chromosome-fidelity or MVA outcome and does not change the reserve-only disposition. This is an independent source check, not independent biological replication or a blinded review.
