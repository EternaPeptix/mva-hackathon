# Functional competitors: what each positive result establishes

**21 September 2026.** These are research-priority decisions. Neither the
current niacin/apitegromab nominees nor these alternatives have demonstrated
benefit in the actual BUB1B genotype. The comparison credits compensation and
absolute muscle output without requiring every useful intervention to repair
the initiating lesion.

## Ursolic acid earns a substantive muscle-compensation reserve

The initial [liver-fibrosis discovery abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26347199/)
led to a stronger, separate muscle literature. That discovery abstract does not
provide the muscle result, and its HSC-T6/human-cell wording remains unqualified.

| Primary source and reading depth | Positive finding | Material limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Ducharme et al. 2026, PMID42117587](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpcell.00159.2026); complete public author-manuscript text, all main captions and official supplement reviewed | Dietary ursolic acid preserved grip strength and absolute EDL force in KPC pancreatic-cancer mice. Treatment began after palpable tumor development. Four additional tumor models support muscle-mass preservation. | Specific force was unchanged; the other models do not independently replicate force benefit. The 5-FU arm supports mass preservation, not demonstrated functional benefit during chemotherapy or long-term tumor safety. |
| [Cione et al. 2021, PMID34330458](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34330458/); complete abstract and publisher excerpts | In 26 postmenopausal women randomized 13/13 during exercise, handgrip improvement favored ursolic acid by 1.8 kgf, 95% CI0.3–3.2. | Handgrip was secondary; the waist-circumference primary outcome and other secondary outcomes were null. Full analysis, multiplicity, attrition and safety were not independently checked. Publisher discussion describes rosemary-leaf-derived supplementation; formulation equivalence is unverified. |
| [Lobo et al. 2021, PMID32593524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32593524/); complete abstract | Both groups improved during high-protein intake and resistance training. | Ursolic acid added no demonstrated strength or muscle-mass benefit versus placebo in 22 active men. This is a different population/regimen, not an MVA efficacy null or grounds to erase the female handgrip result. |

The mouse study used male animals. Its principal force experiment used a
0.27% diet from day5 to day12; force-frequency data came from 7–8 mice per
group. There was no untreated-tumor-free plus ursolic-acid arm in that main
comparison. Food intake was calculated per cage, then divided by mouse count.
Unchanged tumor size does not establish statistical equivalence or freedom from
later recurrence. Mice were euthanized when control tumor-bearing animals reached
the humane endpoint; survival was not the efficacy outcome. These are studied
regimens, not human dose recommendations.

There is a reproducibility issue in the supporting C2C12 experiment: Methods
specify **1 µM**, but Figure6's caption specifies **1 mM**. Do not choose one
silently or derive a clinical exposure margin. Transcript normalization is not
proof of the causal mechanism: the heatmap uses several FDR thresholds, and
the supplemental Mstn treatment comparison has FDR0.1061. The Nmrk2 RNA signal
is not a measured NAD or BUBR1 response. The supplement and figure-level results
do not justify saying every measured muscle improved significantly.

Main figure images were unavailable; sample sizes are reported only where the
text or captions establish them. The [official supplement](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.32129449)
was inspected, including four figures and the 92-row selected-gene table.
Blinding, prospective power and attrition accounting are not reported in the
retrieved material. The manuscript's independent-replication sentence also
contains a citation mismatch; no additional independent replication is counted
here. Disclosed patents, company ownership and company-linked study participation
make independent confirmation particularly valuable without negating the result.

**Decision:** retain ursolic acid as a serious, unelected compensation reserve.
It has actual functional evidence and merits an exposure-qualified comparison
if the program expands. Apitegromab retains stronger human evidence for the
current pediatric neuromuscular objective, while niacin retains the distinct
gene-proximal restoration hypothesis. This is not proof of either nominee's
superiority in MVA. The census links ursolic acid to CHEMBL169, a clinical
development record without an approval entry. That does not prove worldwide
nonapproval; it leaves the eligible medication/product and pediatric exposure
bridge unresolved. Supplement availability cannot resolve that requirement.

The [apitegromab assessment](apitegromab-review.md) already credits absolute
preclinical torque despite unchanged mass-normalized torque. We apply the same
rule to ursolic acid. Its reserve status rests on comparative human evidence,
product/exposure uncertainty and disease transfer, rather than imposing a
muscle-quality requirement that the elected comparator cannot meet. Reopening
priority would require a defined formulation/exposure and useful output with
preserved renewal and fidelity in the relevant muscle models. The reserve does
not automatically add a fifth confirmatory claim or a combination arm.

Ursolic acid challenges niacin more closely than apitegromab on demonstrated
muscle benefit: its placebo-controlled human functional signal is a stronger
causal foothold than niacin's unblinded strength changes. Niacin is retained to
test restoration, not declared the empirical muscle-benefit winner. If the
selection objective were only the two strongest muscle-compensation hypotheses,
and product eligibility were resolved, ursolic acid would deserve direct
reconsideration of niacin's place. Failure of usable BUBR1 rescue weakens the
reason to retain that distinct arm.

## MG132: alignment recovery is not completed division

In [CENP-50-deficient chicken DT40 cells, PMID18094054](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2262965/),
MG132 improved chromosome alignment after nocodazole withdrawal. The inspected
methods specify 10 µM MG132 for the four-hour alignment endpoint, following
1.7 µM nocodazole for 10–12 hours. Crucially, cells remained mitotic after MG132
removal; the authors report this without displayed data. The alignment-specific
denominator and independent replication remain unresolved. This is a narrow
positive observation, not demonstrated faithful division or lasting growth.

Reading depth is partial: primary methods, result passages and Figure4's caption
were recovered through indexed official text, but complete text and figure
images were unavailable. This does not justify a claim that all MG132 muscle
biology is negative, or transferring the result to bortezomib. A separate
[rat-muscle study](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI119513) reports reduced proteolysis;
its full paper, tables and figures were separately reviewed. MG132 at10 µM for
two hours reduced protein breakdown in isolated denervated, T3-treated and
septic rat muscles. These are actual muscle anti-proteolysis results, not
systemic dosing or demonstrated force, endurance or sustained mass recovery.
The direct synthesis/balance curve and washout experiment use the related
compound LLN, not MG132. In sepsis, the measured excess breakdown fell82% and
the remainder was nonsignificant; this is not literal100% recovery of muscle.
The short incubation does not establish a long-term therapeutic window.

**Decision:** retain an ex vivo muscle protein-sparing lead and the separate
alignment observation. Neither a mitotic-arrest result nor a tissue biochemical
result can replace the missing useful-output and exposure bridge. A distinct
bortezomib experiment requires its own appraisal rather than inheriting either
MG132 result.

## Bortezomib plus humanin: contracture prevention with a growth tradeoff

[Nikolaou et al. 2019, PMID31661460](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/128454)
provides a distinct positive result for an actual bortezomib-containing regimen.
The complete 17-page article, captions and three-page supplement were reviewed;
Figure4 and the supplemental treatment/survival figures received independent
visual checks. This updates the existing bortezomib appraisal, leaving the
ledger at 96 route/compound appraisals.

Neonatal CD-1 mice underwent permanent unilateral brachial-plexus denervation
at postnatal day5. Optimized treatment combined bortezomib with
**[Gly14]-Humanin G**, beginning three days after injury and before contracture
onset. Humanin alone did not improve the contracture outcome. All bortezomib
arms included humanin, so the experiment does not establish bortezomib-alone
efficacy or isolate a survival-protective contribution from humanin.

| Finding | What it supports and what remains unresolved |
|---|---|
| Markedly smaller elbow/shoulder contracture deficits versus saline | A treatment-blinded observer measured passive range immediately after sacrifice. Optimized endpoint groups contained 15–16 mice. This earns functional credit for contracture prevention, without implying active force, voluntary use, reinnervation or reversal of established contractures. |
| Approximately 70% reduction in denervation-associated sarcomere elongation | Supports preserved longitudinal muscle growth, while residual differences remain. It is not a direct count of all serial sarcomeres or demonstrated muscle-stem-cell renewal. |
| Reduced proteasome activity, six animals per treatment | Supports drug-target engagement without complete normalization. The text/caption descriptions differ on the exact assay day. |
| No significant rescue of denervated whole-muscle area or volume | Does not erase the length/range benefit. Distinct muscle dimensions need separate endpoints. |
| Smaller normally innervated contralateral muscles | Figure4H/I mark reduced body-weight-normalized area and volume versus saline (P < 0.0001). An enlarged figure resolves the short within-contralateral comparison bracket for area. The opposite limb shares systemic exposure and is not an untreated control. |

Mortality was strongly regimen-dependent and remained in the optimized group.
Supplemental curves, rather than a complete enrollment/death table, supply the
survival evidence; analyzed endpoint sample sizes cannot be treated as enrollment
denominators. Survivor selection can affect the endpoint comparison. Reported
normal weight gain and humerus-length findings do not exclude the normal-muscle
growth cost or unmeasured developmental toxicity.

The optimized regimen used 0.3 mg/kg bortezomib with 1 microgram humanin per
dose, injected every other day from P8 during the P8–P33 study interval; the
supplement labels the last injection P32. These are mouse study details, not a
pediatric regimen. Comparisons reuse the original P5-start saline/humanin
controls; a separately matched P8-start control cohort is not documented.
Randomization, sex and litter-level accounting are not described. The authors
report prospective power calculations and blinded range measurement, but not
complete exclusion counts. There is no treatment-withdrawal follow-up, active
force endpoint, chromosome-fidelity test or long-term tumor/developmental study.

**Decision:** upgrade bortezomib to a **combination-specific contracture-
compensation reserve**. Lack of BUBR1 restoration does not disqualify a useful
compensation route. Combination attribution, prevention-only evidence, mortality,
normal-muscle growth effects and the missing relevant-exposure/durable-function
bridge prevent election as a primary single-medicine MVA candidate. A matched,
independently replicated comparison must establish useful output and a normal-
tissue window; neither MG132 arrest experiments nor oncology experience can
settle this distinct question.

## DHMMF: more phospho-BUBR1 accompanies cell damage

The [2025 primary letter, PMID40836602](https://doi.org/10.1002/ctm2.70460)
tests purified DHMMF in HepG2 and SK-HEP-1 cells. The complete scientific letter,
captions and supplemental Methods1.1–1.17 were read; main Figures1–3 were
visually checked, with an independent check extending to Figure4. Higher
phospho/total BubR1 accompanies multipolar spindles, DNA damage and lower
viability/proliferation. This earns antitumor-cell activity, not restoration.

Biotin competition, protein-stability assays, SPR and RACK1 knockdown support
target involvement beyond docking. Reported binding KD is 3.52 µM. One siRNA
and normalized untreated groups cannot prove an exclusive mechanism or equal
absolute baseline output. CETSA/DARTS use10 µM, above key phenotype exposures
of0.6/1.2 µM; the other binding assays use different concentrations. The paper
does not establish intracellular occupancy at the phenotype doses. Main and
supplemental DARTS/comet timings also disagree.

**Decision:** retain a cytotoxic research observation and a marker-interpretation
counterexample; decline nomination from these outcomes. Muscle benefit,
normal-tissue selectivity, actual-allele rescue and eligible clinical exposure
remain unestablished. Prior in-vivo work is cited by the letter, not newly
measured or independently qualified here.

## What this qualification does not close

The [576-record review](next576-findings.md) contains 62 historical follow-up
flags, including genetic and unnamed-intervention questions. Three new
compound appraisals and the revised bortezomib decision do not complete all of
them. Six selected conference
abstract texts supply additional source recovery without creating six new
whole-paper reviews. Source figures, abstracts, full texts and reviewer working
files remain outside this repository; the public-facing files contain original
assessments and source links.
