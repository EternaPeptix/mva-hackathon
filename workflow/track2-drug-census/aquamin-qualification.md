# Aquamin Soluble versus calcium in human adenoma colonoids

**Retain a real human-tissue differentiation positive, with mixture identity and endpoint limits.** This is not BUBR1 restoration, demonstrated barrier function, adenoma eradication or MVA treatment evidence. Add one **new-aquamin** entry with the calcium comparator retained in its appraisal; no component-by-component efficacy assignment.

## Primary source and reading depth

McClintock et al., *Calcium-Induced Differentiation of Human Colon Adenomas in Colonoid Culture: Calcium Alone versus Calcium with Additional Trace Elements* (2018), [PMID 29636350](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29636350/), [PMC6030430](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6030430/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1158/1940-6207.CAPR-17-0308).

Read the complete main Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Table 1A-B and all five main figure legends using NCBI BioC and PMC HTML. All five main figure images were visually inspected. Read all eight publisher supplements: Figures S1, S2, S3A and S3B visually; Tables S1-S4 and the complete supplemental Methods. Tables S1-S2 were also rendered and visually checked because Table S2 was an image; Tables S3-S4 were read as complete extracted text, not independently checked page by page as images. Supplemental legends came from the main PMC HTML and publisher metadata. The publisher's official AACR Figshare supplied the original supplement files after ordinary PMC links returned challenge HTML; no challenge was bypassed. No raw proteomics, original microscopy fields or sequencing data were reanalysed. This follow-up adds **zero abstract-reading credit**. BioC's 2019 front date is manuscript availability, not a new 2019 experiment/publication.

## Identity and experimental exposure

- **Actual mixture:** the Table S1 source identifies a **2017 test certificate for Aquamin Soluble**, Advanced Laboratories, for Marigot Limited. It reports calcium **117,000 micrograms/g (11.7% by mass)** and magnesium **10,210 micrograms/g (1.021%)**, consistent with the main text's approximate 12:1 ratio, plus a broad trace-element profile. Several listed elements are below detection limits; “72 trace minerals” does not mean each has a quantified active dose. Marigot supplied the material as a gift; the paper declares no financial/personal conflict. The certificate does not authenticate a current lot.
- **Calcium comparator:** medium labeled calcium alone at 0.15 or 1.5 mM. Its salt, supplier, stock preparation and vehicle are **not stated** in the read main or supplement Methods. Do not silently relabel it calcium carbonate or calcium chloride. Aquamin is a mixture, not a molecule and not interchangeable with calcium alone.
- **Adenoma arms:** 30 days in nominal study-labeled conditions of calcium 0.15 mM (control), calcium 1.5 mM, or Aquamin supplying calcium 0.15 or 1.5 mM. The medium already contains 0.15 mM calcium; the paper does not clearly explain any offset of basal calcium, final free-calcium measurement, pH matching or carrier/organic-acid matching. Preserve the authors' nominal labels instead of inventing corrected totals or a mass dose. Figure S1 additionally tests calcium/Aquamin at 3.0 and 4.5 mM in **one donor only**.
- **Background culture:** adenomas in Matrigel 8 mg/mL and serum-free KGM Gold with EGF/pituitary extract; passaged every 4-7 days and given Y27632 10 micromolar on the first day after plating. These are common culture dependencies, not newly attributed Aquamin effects.
- **Normal-colon arm:** three other donors, 14 days; normal cultures require a different L-WRN/KGM medium with Wnt/R-spondin/noggin, 2.5% serum and basal calcium **0.25 mM**, versus nominal calcium 1.5 mM or Aquamin supplying 1.5 mM. This is useful normal-tissue context, not a matched therapeutic-index experiment in identical culture conditions.

## Biological denominators and controls

There were **three independent adenoma donors**: #584 (61-year-old man, 20 mm ascending-colon lesion), #590 (58-year-old woman, 35 mm lesion), and #282 (66-year-old woman, 45 mm lesion). Each had multiple reported gene alterations. Proteomics compared each donor's treated material with its own low-calcium control and then summarized the three donors. There were separately **three histologically normal colon donors**; they were not described as paired normal tissue from those adenoma patients.

Image denominators are subsamples, not hundreds of independent patients: Figure 1C uses 20 fields/condition with 10-20 colonoids/field for morphology, and 5-10 fields with 10-20 colonoids/field for buds. Figure 2B uses 15-25 crypts/condition; Figure 3B uses 25-75 crypts/condition. Figure S1 ranges are 250-424 colonoids for #282, 53-77 for #590, and 15-111 for #584. Donor-level allocation/aggregation of every morphology and staining comparison is not fully specified. No blinded scoring or randomization statement was found.

Main statistics are ANOVA followed by two-sided unpaired t-tests, nominal p<0.05, with SD. A hierarchical model accounting for crypts/fields nested within donors and multiplicity adjustment across the many morphology/protein comparisons is not reported. The supplemental **≤2% FDR is the protein/peptide identification confidence filter**, not demonstrated 2% false discovery control for every intervention effect or pathway. EdgeR is named for differential proteomics; Reactome supplies enrichment p-values. No exact p-values should be manufactured from stars.

## Positives and limits by endpoint

| Endpoint | Qualified finding |
|---|---|
| Differentiation morphology, Figure 1 | At 1.5 mM, calcium and Aquamin both produced fewer small buds, larger lumens and a more columnar phenotype. Low Aquamin produced differentiated morphology in **13 ± 7% versus 2 ± 7%** under low-calcium control (main text; p<0.05 annotation). This is a real subset response, not complete normalization of the culture. |
| Junctional ultrastructure | TEM shows desmosomes and tight junctions in **calcium 1.5 mM versus calcium 0.15 mM**. Those TEM panels are not a direct Aquamin-versus-calcium comparison. No electrical resistance, tracer permeability or tissue mechanical barrier assay is reported here. |
| Ki67, Figure 2 | Reported positive fractions: control **85 ± 9%**, low Aquamin **64 ± 21%**, calcium 1.5 mM **50 ± 15%**, high Aquamin **41 ± 21%**. Supplemental Methods specifies comparison of **3+ intensity nuclei**, rather than every weakly stained nucleus. Figure 2B marks significance for both high arms; low Aquamin has **no significance star**. Do not make its numerical decrease a proven significant proliferation reduction. |
| Differentiation proteins, Figure 3 | CK20 and occludin staining increases are annotated significant for all three intervention arms. E-cadherin membrane localization changes; total pixel positivity is not significantly increased at low Aquamin, while both high arms are marked. Localization, abundance and actual barrier function are separate endpoints. |
| NF2/merlin, Figure 4/Table 1 | Proteomic fold changes are **1.5 ± 0.1** with high calcium, **1.9 ± 0.3** with low Aquamin, **2.4 ± 0.7** with high Aquamin; each differs from control. The larger Aquamin-versus-high-calcium proteomic averages are **not statistically significant**. In contrast, NF2 immunostaining is significantly greater with either Aquamin concentration than high calcium/control. No NF2 perturbation establishes mediation. |
| Heterogeneity | 223 versus 106 proteins met the average 1.8-fold change threshold for high Aquamin versus high calcium; this is not a count of independently validated beneficial mechanisms. Only NF2 met the up-regulation threshold in all three donors under high Aquamin. Many mean changes have large SD or lack statistical significance. |
| Growth, Figure S1 | No measured colonoid-area growth effect through 1.5 mM, despite differentiation and Ki67 changes. In **#584 only**, Aquamin at **3.0 and 4.5 mM** lowers growth index, with stars whose legend specifies p<0.01; the exact contrasted group is not explicitly named. Do not generalize this high-dose single-donor finding or equate area with viable cell number. |
| Withdrawal, Figure S2 | After 30 days at high calcium or high Aquamin, switching to low-calcium medium produced morphological reversion within **6 days** in the three specimens according to the main text. This is a direct reversibility result, not durable correction. |
| Apoptosis and normal tissue | Cleaved caspase-3 staining is low overall but increases significantly in both high-dose adenoma arms (Figure 3B). Figure S3A even shows a rare arrow-marked positive cell in low Aquamin despite Figure 3's “not detected” description, so zero apoptosis is too strong. Normal cultures show little change in morphology/Ki67/CK20, but a few caspase-positive cells after high calcium/Aquamin and none described in control. No quantitative normal-cell safety margin or long-term toxicity study is provided. |

Table S4 also includes decreases in mitochondrial energy-related proteins, metallothioneins, claudin-4 and mucin-2 in relevant arms; these are expression observations, not validated beneficial or harmful organ effects. Reactome apoptosis/senescence labels often derive from shared histone proteins and do not independently establish senescence or wholesale cell killing. The authors' broad suggestion of absent normal-mucosa toxicity is stronger than the measured scope. This study does not test human polyp incidence, cancer progression, survival or systemic supplement safety; prior animal and clinical claims in its Introduction were not newly qualified here.

## Exact BUB1B connection and fair comparison

**BUB1B occurs in the baseline mutation list of donor #590**, alongside CTNNB1 and multiple other altered genes. This paper does not give its specific BUB1B allele/zygosity or establish pathogenicity, deficient BUBR1 protein, aneuploidy, segregation failure or BUB1B-dependent treatment response. BUB1B/BUBR1 is not an intervention-responsive entry in Table 1 or Tables S3-S4 and no dedicated BUB1B RNA/BUBR1 protein assay or genetic rescue is reported. The source is therefore **response in one genetically complex adenoma with a listed BUB1B mutation**, not “Aquamin rescues BUB1B deficiency.” The larger proteome cannot be declared to have measured zero BUBR1 without inspecting unreported raw data.

These controlled differentiation findings in patient-derived tissue deserve retention. They support a colon-epithelial differentiation route and a calcium-versus-mixture comparison. They do not supply muscle function or chromosome fidelity. Apply the same transfer limit to the provisional nominees: niacin's human muscle NAD engagement is not proven MVA restoration; apitegromab's SMA motor-function benefit is not demonstrated MVA compensation. Their tissue/endpoints differ from this paper's colonoid differentiation evidence; no numerical probability or claim that one is clinically superior follows. This source alone does not justify changing the research pair, but MVA's absence is not a reason to discard the Aquamin positives.
