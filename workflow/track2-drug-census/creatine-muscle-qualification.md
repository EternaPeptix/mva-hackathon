# Creatine monohydrate: pediatric muscle-strength comparator, with mixed outcomes and limited source access

Assessment date: 23 September 2026. Integrated source qualification; two distinct administered-intervention assessments, with no new outer-corpus reading or independent-study credit. Repository comparison was checked at `f2fe957bb3ae2167228ed25247deef59f2202ade` (222 appraisals, no existing creatine entry).

Creatine monohydrate merits a distinct **prioritized muscle-strength research comparison**. Selected controlled pediatric Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) trials report handgrip or manual-strength benefits, so its evidence is not restricted to lean mass or biochemical markers. They also report functional nulls, and the larger six-month CINRG trial did not meet its primary strength comparison. This is a credible challenge to a muscle-compensation shortlist, not established MVA benefit or a reason to call every creatine preparation equivalent.

**Actual reading depth:** complete literal abstracts of the three original trials, plus the complete current ClinicalTrials.gov protocol record for CINRG. No complete primary scientific main text, native figure/table or supplement was retrieved/read. The [CINRG registry, NCT00016653/CNMC0599](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00016653) supplies useful identity and design context, not missing outcomes. It describes water-mixed supplements and a powdered hot-cocoa preparation, with parents, children and treating doctors masked. Its structured enrollment is 48, versus 50 in the publication abstract; structured eligibility is 5–9 years, while narrative says 5–10. These discrepancies remain unresolved. It was first posted in 2001 after a 2000 start; the current record was updated in 2011, lists completion in 2006 despite the 2005 publication, and contains no posted results or documents. It cannot establish the original prespecified statistical hierarchy. Planned blood/urine monitoring is not evidence of its results.

All three abstracts describe tolerability. Only the 2004 abstract explicitly lists serum creatinine among its measurements; numerical renal outcomes and assay interpretation were not accessible. No conclusion about measured GFR, pediatric MVA renal safety, rare harms or long-term safety is justified from this material. Supplier, purity, catalog number and detailed formulation remain unresolved, except the CINRG protocol's vehicle description.
