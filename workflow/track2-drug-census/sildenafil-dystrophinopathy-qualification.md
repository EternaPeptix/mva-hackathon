# Sildenafil in human dystrophinopathy: bounded clinical evidence

These two studies did not demonstrate the anticipated benefit from oral sildenafil in their studied populations. Witting studied ambulatory adults with Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD) for four weeks per crossover period; Leung studied predominantly Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) with established cardiomyopathy for six blinded months. Their different populations, outcomes and designs should remain separate. Leung also produced a cardiac-worsening signal that led to early termination, but its uncertainty and mixed exposure periods prevent a simple causal-harm interpretation. Neither study establishes MVA benefit or harm, universal sildenafil ineffectiveness, or a class-wide PDE5 conclusion.

**Access is materially incomplete for Witting.** Its complete abstract and retained primary PDF-reader text through `L371@P5` were read: abstract, introduction, methods, results, Table 2, figure captions 1–2 and part of the discussion, ending at the first row of Table 3. The advertised eight-page PDF now returns a 404 page; native figures/tables, the rest of the discussion/SF36 table, disclosures and supplement were not obtained. Leung's complete scientific main was read through the public NCBI BioC representation and PMC reader, including original table XML, captions and disclosures; all three native figures were inspected. Its main PDF, native table pixels and supplementary PDF remained unavailable. No unread material earns reading credit.

This independently accepted follow-up qualifies the existing **`new-sildenafil`** comparison. Its earlier [AR-12 cancer/chaperone evidence](ar12-sildenafil-qualification.md), rationale, alias and source attribution remain separate and intact. The oral clinical exposures do not establish equivalence to the earlier 2 µM cellular exposure. ## Witting 2014: tested primary results versus untested secondary comparisons

[Witting et al., Annals of Neurology 2014;76:550–557](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ana.24216), PMID **25042931**, DOI **10.1002/ana.24216**, NCT01350154/EudraCT 2010-024659-10, used a randomized, double-blind crossover design. There were two four-week treatment periods, a two-week intervening washout and two-week final follow-up. Sildenafil was given as **20 mg orally three times daily**, with tablets enclosed in capsules indistinguishable from placebo. The current registry describes gelatin capsules and lactose-monohydrate placebo. Its product-name fields are inconsistent—“Revation,” “Revatio,” and cross-assigned other-name entries—so they are not used to establish new aliases, precise salt/batch identity or current retail equivalence.

The Danish trial screened 47 people: 10 were ineligible and 20 declined; 17 entered, one withdrew after visit 2, and **16 supplied skeletal-muscle analyses**. Two participants had claustrophobia and one a transplanted heart, leaving **13 cardiac analyses**. Mean age was 38 years, with variable weakness and genotype. Paper eligibility included confirmed BMD, ambulatory adults, EF below 50% and/or MRC strength below 5, with stable background medication where used. The current registry instead uses MRC below 4+; its eligibility wording is not silently substituted for the paper's.

Authors describe an intention-to-treat analysis but excluded the participant without post-treatment measurements. Eight of 256 skeletal data points were partly or wholly missing; four missing baseline observations used the other baseline, and other unavailable measurements remained missing. Cardiac MRI had only one baseline examination. A washout was used, but the retained methods do not supply formal sequence, period or carryover estimates. There was no conventional prospective power calculation: investigators reasoned that 12 completers would suffice, planned 20 and enrolled 17 when eligible participants were exhausted. This is not an equivalence study.

**ND must not be converted to a formally tested null.** Methods state that secondary means were visually inspected and an ANOVA was performed only when a difference appeared potentially significant. Authors report four ANOVAs and no multiplicity adjustment. Most visible secondary rows have no inferential P value. The unavailable remainder of Table 3 prevents verification of all SF36 subcategories and the fourth ANOVA. The authors' broad “no effect” interpretation is therefore stronger than the accessible formal testing supports.

The retained significance sentence prints “<0.05%” and a type I error risk of “0.95.” These are inconsistent as statistical statements. Without native pixels, a publication error cannot be distinguished from extraction corruption; neither is silently corrected to a conventional threshold. The current registry lists resting LVEDV as cardiac primary and exercise cardiac function as secondary, whereas the paper calls exercise cardiac output primary. Historical registry versions and the original SAP were not read, so exact prespecification of that distinction remains unresolved.

Five previously collected biopsies reportedly showed reduced nNOS and still lower PDE5 relative to normal = 1: **0.50 ± 0.08** and **0.18 ± 0.06**, respectively. The retained passage does not identify the uncertainty measure as SD or SE. This selected baseline subset suggests a possible target-availability limitation; it does not prove that PDE5 reduction caused nonresponse, demonstrate randomized target engagement, or show muscle regeneration. The native blots were unavailable.

Five participants reported dyspepsia/headache during sildenafil, versus one headache report on placebo; separate per-symptom counts are not inferred. The abstract reports no serious adverse effects. Four spontaneously volunteered improved erectile function during sildenafil and none during placebo; this was deliberately not systematically solicited, so it is a reported positive outside the prespecified muscle outcomes, not a controlled muscle-function benefit. Full funding/author-conflict disclosures remain unverified. The academic registry sponsor does not replace those disclosures.

## Leung 2014: small randomized null and imprecise stopping signal

[Leung et al., Annals of Neurology 2014;76:541–549](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4191982/), PMID **25042693**, DOI **10.1002/ana.24214**, NCT01168908/REVERSE-DBMD, randomized **20 adult males, 10 per arm**: 18 DMD and two BMD. Initial criteria were DMD, age at least 15 and EF at most 45%; amendments admitted BMD, increased EF eligibility to 50% and subsequently raised age to 18. No minors had enrolled before that age amendment. Stable ACE-inhibitor/ARB treatment was required; beta-blocker or corticosteroid doses were stable when used.

Participants received **20 mg oral sildenafil three times daily** or identical white placebo tablets for six months, then planned open-label sildenafil for another six months in both arms. Pfizer supplied study drug/placebo; the study and registry identify Revatio. Exact batch, salt/excipients and equivalence to current retail preparations were not independently established.

Only **seven sildenafil and eight placebo participants** completed the blinded cardiac-MRI analysis; it was explicitly per protocol, with no missing-data imputation. The sildenafil arm lost one participant to heart-failure death, one to withdrawal and one to study closure; placebo lost one to withdrawal and one to closure. Native Figure 1 shows six and seven completing 12 months. The planned sample was 30, with 15 per arm to detect a 10% LVESV improvement at 80% power and two-sided .05.

Registry outcome entries reuse 7/8 denominators more broadly; they do not overwrite the paper's endpoint-specific footnotes. †The printed FVC and pinch intervals are inconsistent with their point estimates, ordering or P values. The primary interval also has an apparent centering problem. These strings are reproduced as supplied in original table XML/PMC text, **not repaired or re-estimated**; native final-PDF table verification was unavailable.

The main comparison did not demonstrate improved LVESV. It was small, incomplete and imbalanced: all-randomized baseline LVESV was 99.8 mL in sildenafil versus 86.7 mL in placebo, with greater beta-blocker, digoxin and noninvasive-ventilation use in sildenafil. The article's Table 1 and Table 2 complete-case baseline values also differ and remain separately attributed in the JSON. Nonsignificance does not establish a zero effect or exclude every clinically meaningful effect.

The stopping analysis reported **4/14 sildenafil exposures versus 1/8 placebo exposures** with at least 10% LVESV worsening. Two sildenafil events occurred during the blinded phase and two during open-label treatment. The reported clustered-model OR was **2.8 (95% CI 0.20–40.10)**. Participants receiving placebo could later contribute sildenafil exposure, so these are not independent parallel randomized groups or a simple randomized risk ratio. The main's exposure accounting is not silently reconciled with the native completion diagram. The safety signal prompted DSMB termination, but its very wide interval, baseline severity and possible disease progression prevent a confident causal-harm estimate.

The significant longitudinal FVC decline during sildenafil exposure—reported as 0.06 L over six months, P = .011—is a separate time trend, **not** the randomized between-group contrast, whose P is .863. Grip/pinch time trends and between-group SF36v2/INQoL results were not statistically significant. The authors' futility calculation relies on assumptions about future participants; it is not independently rerun here and is not proof of ineffectiveness across all doses or populations.

One heart-failure death in the blinded sildenafil arm was judged unlikely related by the DSMB. The unavailable supplement limits the detailed published safety review. Current registry results report sildenafil-exposed/placebo-exposed denominators **18/10**, with deaths 1/0, serious-event participants 5/1 and other-event participants 13/7; low blood pressure is listed in 4/18 versus 0/10. These exposure groups overlap through later treatment and cannot be recast as six-month randomized event rates. Individual causality and timing are not inferred.

Patient foundations supported the study and travel; NIH/NCATS institutional clinical/translational support was acknowledged, and Pfizer provided treatment. Sponsors proposed the study idea and received DSMB reports; authors state that design, implementation, analysis and publication decisions were independent. Pfizer and other industry consulting relationships and a patent were disclosed. These facts are retained without treating disclosure alone as proof of invalidity.

## Registration, attribution and transfer limits

Current NCT01350154 shows first posting on 9 May 2011 before its recorded November 2011 start, 17 actual participants and no posted results. Current NCT01168908 shows first posting on 23 July 2010 before its recorded September start, termination on DSMB recommendation, 20 participants, and results posted on 21 March 2018. These are current records, not a reconstruction of original field versions or proof that all final analyses were prespecified. No full trial protocol or SAP was obtained.

The cited earlier BMD blood-flow positive is **Martin et al., “Tadalafil alleviates muscle ischemia in patients with Becker muscular dystrophy,” Science Translational Medicine 2012;4:162ra155**—Witting reference 14 and Leung reference 18. It belongs to tadalafil. Witting's abstract wording about sildenafil should not confer that tadalafil result on sildenafil. The earlier trial was identified by citation/attribution only, not freshly appraised. The optional cerebral companion PMC5233618 was not scientifically read after access failures and receives no independent-replication credit.

These findings can qualify the existing sildenafil comparison under the same function, exposure, durability, renewal and fidelity standards as other compensation candidates. BUBR1 mediation is additionally necessary for a restoration claim, not an extra exclusion requirement for every compensation comparison. Neither clinical trial measures MVA response, BUBR1 restoration, healthy tissue renewal or chromosome segregation fidelity. Source access limits, untested outcomes and imprecise safety evidence must survive any later synthesis. No new nominee, clinical regimen, catalog join or analysis rerun follows. This portable synthesis adds no fresh primary or original-queue reading credit.
