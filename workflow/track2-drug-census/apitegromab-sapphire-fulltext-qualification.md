# Apitegromab: full SAPPHIRE report and prespecified hierarchy

**Disposition: retain apitegromab as the strongest pediatric human motor-function comparator in the current shortlist, but narrow the claim.** The positive confirmatory result is for pooled 10 and 20 mg/kg doses in SMA patients aged 2–12 years receiving SMN-targeted therapy. The 20 mg/kg-only primary comparison did not pass; therefore the 10 mg/kg point estimate, other single-dose comparisons, and secondary outcomes were exploratory under the prespecified hierarchy. No MVA, BUB1B/BUBR1, chromosome-fidelity, or myogenic progenitor outcome was tested.

This is a deeper source review of the existing apitegromab candidate, not a new candidate or ledger entry. It does not recommend use in the MVA case.

## Trial and source update

SAPPHIRE was a 52-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre Phase 3 trial at 48 hospitals in nine countries. It enrolled nonambulatory patients with genetically documented SMN-deficient type 2/3 SMA, HFMSE scores 10–45, and at least 10 months of nusinersen or six months of risdiplam before screening. The 2–12-year main-efficacy population randomized 156 participants equally to apitegromab 10 mg/kg, 20 mg/kg, or placebo every four weeks. The 13–21-year group was exploratory: 22 received 20 mg/kg and 10 placebo. Participants were screened before randomization; 155/156 in the main population and 31/32 in the older group completed 12 months. One participant in each 20 mg/kg group discontinued; neither withdrawal was due to an adverse event.

The complete 13-page journal article was retrieved from the University of Liège institutional repository and visually reviewed page-by-page. The official ClinicalTrials.gov CDN supplied Protocol Amendment V3.0 dated 11 June 2024 and SAP V1.0 dated 30 August 2024. The official EU Clinical Trials Register results page (EudraCT 2021-005314-34) was also reviewed; it posted results version 1 on 3 January 2026. The current NCT API record remains completed with no results or document section, despite the public protocol/SAP files and the EU results record.

## Confirmatory versus exploratory efficacy

The protocol and SAP prespecified two simultaneous primary HFMSE comparisons in the 2–12-year population, controlled by Hochberg at two-sided α=.05: 20 mg/kg versus placebo, and pooled 10+20 mg/kg versus placebo. If just one comparison passed the Hochberg step, the primary claim for that comparison could pass, but the hierarchical testing of secondary outcomes stopped. The SAP was dated before the last visit and describes the intended estimands, analysis sets, MMRM, and sensitivity approaches.

| Week-52 comparison | LS mean change / contrast | Statistical status under the prespecified plan |
|---|---|---|
| Pooled 10+20 mg/kg vs placebo; n=106 vs 50 | +0.6 vs −1.2; difference **1.8** (95% CI **0.30–3.32**), p=.0192 | One primary comparison passed Hochberg (p≤.025). This is the positive confirmatory result. |
| 20 mg/kg vs placebo; n=53 vs 50 | +0.2 vs −1.2; difference **1.4** (−0.34–3.13), p=.1149 | The second primary comparison failed. Gatekeeping stopped after Step 1. |
| 10 mg/kg vs placebo; n=53 vs 50 | +1.0 vs −1.2; difference **2.2** (0.49–3.95), nominal p=.0121 | Listed at Step 5 after the 20 mg/kg secondary sequence. It is exploratory because the hierarchy had already stopped, notwithstanding the nominal p value and current 10 mg/kg label dose. |

The prespecified 20 mg/kg secondary results were RULM difference 0.7 (95% CI −0.31 to 1.71), ≥3-point HFMSE response odds ratio 2.4 (0.82–7.07), and WHO motor-milestone difference 0.2 (−0.10 to 0.40). The corresponding 10 mg/kg estimates were 0.6 (−0.42 to 1.61), odds ratio 3.8 (1.33–10.90), and 0.1 (−0.15 to 0.35). These were not confirmatory tests after the primary gate failed; the registry provides no inferential tests for them. The paper’s supplementary appendix was not recovered, so appendix-only subgroup and sensitivity tables were not independently checked.

The primary analysis used an mITT set: randomized 2–12-year participants who received at least one dose and had at least one postbaseline HFMSE measure. The main article reports MMRM under a missing-at-random assumption. The protocol and SAP specify main/supportive estimands and sensitivity analyses for intercurrent events and missingness. The EU result record agrees with the pooled and 20 mg/kg HFMSE estimates; its status fields label the analysis “Final” (18 December 2024) while also saying it is not an analysis of primary-completion data. That registry-field mismatch is retained unresolved and does not change the article’s analysis interpretation.

## Safety, sponsorship, and transfer limits

The article reports adverse events in 91% of pooled apitegromab recipients versus 87% with placebo, treatment-related events in 19% versus 18%, no treatment-related serious adverse events, no AE-related discontinuations, and no deaths. Serious adverse events were numerically more frequent with apitegromab (21/128, 16%) than placebo (6/60, 10%), driven mainly by infection and respiratory events; investigators did not assess these as treatment-related. The paper says no adverse events of special interest were prespecified or identified. The current US label separately flags fractures: 5/53 at 10 mg/kg, 3/53 at 20 mg/kg, and 1/50 on placebo. These source-specific statements should be shown together, not collapsed into a claim of no bone risk.

The paper names Scholar Rock as funder and states the sponsor was responsible for trial design, the SAP, study-drug procurement, trial management, conduct, and oversight of data collection and analysis. It also reports an independent data-monitoring committee and double blinding. Several authors were sponsor employees or stockholders, and medical-writing support was sponsor-funded. This warrants attention to sponsor influence while preserving the randomized evidence.

The participants had SMA and ongoing nusinersen or risdiplam, not chromosome-instability-driven MVA. Most were White; several countries did not collect race/ethnicity. Severe contractures/scoliosis and previous onasemnogene were among exclusions, narrowing transfer to more affected patients. Myostatin target engagement was measured, but BUB1B/BUBR1, chromosome segregation, lineage renewal, or cancer surveillance were not. The pharmacologic route is compensation of surviving muscle, not repair of the initiating BUB1B defect.

## Competitive interpretation

Apitegromab still has the strongest controlled pediatric motor-function evidence among the reviewed candidates and remains a defensible compensation arm ahead of everolimus for this proposal. The precise claim is narrower than “the approved 10 mg/kg dose independently met the trial’s confirmatory endpoint”: the confirmatory win was the pooled-dose comparison, while the 10 mg/kg-only estimate is nominal and hierarchical-exploratory. The FDA’s approval and recommended dose remain separate regulatory facts; the trial-level statistical qualification does not invalidate the label.

Against niacin, the routes remain distinct. Niacin is closer to BUBR1 biology but lacks a controlled disease-specific functional benefit or direct variant rescue. Apitegromab has the stronger randomized motor-function signal in a different disease, but no BUB1B/MVA transfer. No head-to-head or MVA experiment ranks the two. **Do not change the provisional pair; present apitegromab as an orthogonal compensation hypothesis and state the pooled-dose / single-dose distinction.**
