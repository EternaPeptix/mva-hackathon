# Potentilla extract and ECGU: real protection signals, distinct evidence for each preparation

**Retain two substantive preclinical reserves:** the tested methanolic *Potentilla fulgens* root extract (PRE), and the equal-mass mixture of **epicatechin, catechin, gallic acid and ursolic acid (ECGU)**. Both reduced premature sister-chromatid separation in an exposed-mouse model. PRE also reduced the fraction of mice with stomach carcinoma at a fixed observation time. These are administered-preparation results, stronger than a gene co-mention. Neither demonstrates BUBR1 restoration, MVA rescue or superiority on useful muscle output.

The [2019 primary paper, PMID 31740710](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-53747-5), and its native supplement support the distinctions below. One shared source qualification produces two preparation-specific assessments, not four monotherapy claims. The existing ursolic-acid assessment remains unchanged.

## What the experiment actually compared

Swiss albino mice, initially 25–30 g and 2–3 months old, received raw areca-nut extract plus lime (RAN + lime) in drinking water. The estimated RAN intake increased during the experiment. Both preparations were provided in feed. The Methods explicitly places PRE initiation **30 days before** RAN exposure; the Discussion states a **one-month lead-in for both PRE and ECGU**, with continued co-exposure. This tests prophylactic co-exposure, not treatment of established cancer or reversal of a congenital checkpoint defect.

| Preparation | Reported daily amount per mouse | Direct endpoints in this study |
|---|---|---|
| PRE | 2 mg of methanolic root extract | Bone-marrow metaphase analysis at 60, 120 and 180 days; tissue DNA-damage and molecular assays; stomach histology at 260 days |
| ECGU | 50 µg **each** of epicatechin, catechin, gallic acid and ursolic acid; 200 µg total | Premature anaphase-separation percentages at 60, 120 and 180 days; p53/Securin immunoblots at 180 days |

The study used **160 mice in total across separate assays and timepoints**, not 160 animals in the cancer comparison. Histology and immunohistochemistry shared animals. There were no individual-component arms, extract/mixture-only arms, or a factorial test of synergy. Sex, random allocation and blinded pathology classification were not specified. The supplement describes two independent IHC readers; that does not establish blinded treatment allocation or pathology assessment.

PRE was a taxonomically identified, voucher-documented root preparation. The supplement describes fractionation and identification of isolated constituents. It does not give a numerical purity specification, the stock solvent for the four administered compounds, enantiomer specifications, measured tissue exposure or a current standardized human product. Equal mass is not equal molarity, nor the same proportions as the crude extract. Root pieces, teas, other *Potentilla* species and unrelated commercial products cannot inherit these results.

## Measured benefits and the limits of their attribution

**PRE histology:** at day 260, carcinoma was reported in **2/10 PRE + RAN + lime mice versus 10/10 RAN + lime mice**. Of the other eight PRE-treated mice, five had hyperplasia and three were described as normal. This is a substantial observed difference in a small group, not complete protection. Our exploratory two-sided Fisher calculation from those published counts gives **P = 0.000714**; the authors did not report that test. It does not supply missing randomization, blinded diagnosis, individual pathology records or independent replication. A single terminal assessment does not establish cancer-onset time, lifetime prevention or survival extension.

**Do not assign that cancer result to ECGU.** Table 1 and the Methods contain no ECGU day-260 histology arm. The abstract's combined PRE/EA-mixture wording overstates the direct tumor evidence for the mixture.

**Chromosome-separation phenotype:** about 100 metaphase spreads per mouse were scored after colchicine exposure. The biological unit is the mouse, not each spread. The native tables report these means:

| Day of RAN exposure | RAN + lime, 5 mice | PRE + RAN + lime, 5 mice | ECGU + RAN + lime, 4 mice |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 60 | 15.68% | 8.38% | 12.40% |
| 120 | 23.78% | 12.40% | 14.175% |
| 180 | 27.96% | 16.96% | 19.75% |

These values are recalculated from the published per-mouse percentages. At day 180, PRE reduces the observed percentage by **11.0 points**, and ECGU by **8.21 points**, relative to RAN alone. Neither reaches the untreated table's zero value. This is partial protection in a chemically exposed, non-BUB1B-mutant model. Static spreads after spindle inhibition do not establish a lower rate of new errors in untreated living cells or faithful lineage renewal.

For PRE, independent-group tests on the printed values preserve the direction and nominal significance under both equal-variance and Welch assumptions. At day 180 they give P = 0.000285 and 0.00140, respectively. These are limited reanalyses of rounded published values, without multiplicity correction or a raw-slide audit. For ECGU, the supplement labels the test “paired” despite four treated mice and five RAN comparators; the pairing is not explained. An independent equal-variance calculation gives approximately P = 0.0379, 0.000447 and 0.0000894 across the three timepoints, while the supplement reports 0.028, 0.0004 and 0.0001. The day-60 discrepancy remains unresolved.

**Aneuploidy and selection:** Table 2 reports lower hypodiploid fractions with PRE, but four rows have inconsistent totals versus chromosome-bin counts. The displayed bins cover chromosomes 37–40; a complete distribution, including gains, is not supplied. ECGU's Table S2 gives separation percentages only, despite prose claiming reduced aneuploidy. No ECGU chromosome-count table supports that stronger claim. Neither experiment measures absolute marrow output, clone survival, exposure pharmacokinetics or lineage-specific error generation. Selection, altered carcinogen exposure or upstream damage reduction therefore remain alternatives to restored checkpoint function.

## The proposed mechanism is less established than the phenotype

BUBR1 appears in discussion of a **different arecoline study**. This experiment did not assay BUB1B/BUBR1. Its qPCR targets were Mad2 and Aurora A; its immunoblots targeted p53, Securin and β-actin.

- Figure 2's qPCR P labels compare untreated with RAN, rather than directly testing PRE rescue. Mad2 comparisons are P = 0.09 and 0.12; Aurora A is 0.005 and 0.18 at 180/260 days. Higher bars with PRE do not establish a statistically tested restoration effect.
- PRE's oxidative-DNA-damage comparisons are mixed: significant in stomach at 180 days and esophagus at 260 days, but not esophagus at 180 days or stomach at 260 days. PRE protein comparisons are likewise not uniformly significant.
- **All four displayed ECGU-versus-RAN protein comparisons are nonsignificant**: p53/Securin P = 0.06/0.08 in esophagus and 0.07/0.07 in stomach. The Figure 3F bottom label says PRE although the adjacent blot/caption specifies ECGU. The paper's blanket significant-protein-normalization claim is not supported by those labels.

The reduced PRE tumor burden and separation phenotype remain worth investigating. They do not prove that lowering p53/Securin caused protection or that endogenous BUBR1 was restored. An antioxidant or exposure-interaction mechanism is plausible but not identified conclusively by these experiments.

## Competitive decision

| Criterion | What this adds relative to the provisional leaders | What it still needs |
|---|---|---|
| Actual pharmacological chromosome-separation benefit | PRE and ECGU have a measured partial positive; niacin's proposed BUBR1 bridge spans distinct interventions. | Replication in affected/corrected BUB1B models, with direct segregation and absolute-output measurements |
| Cancer constraint | PRE has a relevant observed cancer-incidence difference in this chemical-challenge model. | Independent replication, established-tumor and genotype-specific contexts; ECGU has no direct tumor endpoint here |
| Useful muscle function | No muscle-force, renewal or exercise endpoint was tested. | A justified myogenic phenotype and exposure; these results do not displace apitegromab's separate functional evidence from SMA |
| Formulation and translation | Constituents are named and the tested preparations can be distinguished. | Batch identity, purity, exposure and tolerability; no transfer to dietary consumption or a human regimen |

**Keep both on the experimental reserve list.** Their chromosome-separation observations justify more attention than a generic antioxidant rationale. They do not currently replace the niacin/apitegromab comparison, whose MVA efficacy also remains unproved. A meaningful follow-up must distinguish fewer newly generated errors from selective loss of abnormal cells or reduced effective carcinogen exposure. No clinical benefit or harm is inferred for this child.

## Source integrity and reading scope

The quantitative observations are preserved alongside four main-table count inconsistencies, a visible Figure 1D/table offset, the ECGU test-label/count mismatch and the nonsignificant molecular comparisons. The main qPCR method describes ΔΔCt, while the supplement describes an OE33-derived standard curve; the IHC text labels its scale 0–250 although its stated formula can reach 300. A supplementary isolation label also differs between prose and diagram for a compound outside ECGU. These are unresolved reporting issues, not grounds to erase all measured benefits.

Root read the complete scientific main text, both native main tables, all four figure captions/images and the complete supplementary text; supplement pages 1/3 were inspected visually. An independent reader checked the native supplement, chemistry, allocation, selected figures and ECGU arithmetic. Fresh source metadata carried no linked correction or retraction; that bounded check is not integrity clearance. Raw source bodies, images, supplement and calculations remain external. This adds no outer-abstract reading credit: PMID 31740710 was already read in Batch13. A later diabetic-wound paper surfaced as an **unqualified follow-up** and contributes no evidence to this assessment.
