# Tieland 2012: the tested MPC80 drink and physical performance

The randomized trial reports a nominal improvement in secondary physical performance with the tested drink, despite a null primary lean-mass outcome. It does not establish a statistically significant additional leg-strength benefit, BUBR1 restoration, healthy cell renewal or MVA efficacy. A functional positive should be retained fairly alongside the nulls and reporting limits.

Tieland et al., *Protein supplementation improves physical performance in frail elderly people: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial*, JAMDA 2012;13:720–726; [PMID 22889730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22889730/), [DOI 10.1016/j.jamda.2012.07.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamda.2012.07.005), [NCT01109628](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01109628).

## Exact preparation and comparator

Subjects consumed a **250 mL drink after breakfast and after lunch for 24 weeks**. Each active serving contained **15 g protein as milk protein concentrate MPC80, 7.1 g lactose, 0.5 g fat and 0.4 g calcium**, supplied by FrieslandCampina Consumer Products Europe. The daily supplemental protein amount was 30 g. The matching 250 mL placebo contained **no protein, 7.1 g lactose and 0.4 g calcium**. Both were vanilla flavored in opaque packages. Adherence, assessed from calendars and unused drinks, was at least 92% without a reported group difference.

The recovered composition does **not establish calorie matching**: the active drink supplies protein and its energy, while the comparator lists the same lactose/calcium but no protein. Complete energy labels and a placebo-fat quantity were not recovered. The intervention therefore cannot isolate protein-specific effects from added energy. No missing calories or ingredients were invented.

MPC80 must not be called isolated whey or interpreted as an 80:20 casein/whey ratio. The report does not establish its casein/whey proportions, amino-acid profile, leucine content, batch assay or equivalence to a current retail product. The evidence belongs to the tested formulation and regimen, not every dairy drink or protein supplement. This trial prescribed no resistance-training program; familiarization and repeated strength testing are separate from exercise training.

## Participants, endpoint hierarchy and analysis

Of 734 people invited and 165 screened, **65 were randomized: 34 protein and 31 placebo**. Participants were at least 65 years old and pre-frail or frail by Fried criteria. Mean ages were 78 ± 1 and 81 ± 1 years respectively (SEM). Cancer, COPD, diabetes and renal insufficiency below eGFR 60 mL/min/1.73 m² were excluded; the registry additionally names milk-protein allergy/sensitivity and most anticoagulants. These exclusions restrict safety and population transfer.

An independent person used computer-generated, sex-stratified permuted blocks of four. The report is described as double blind and explains opaque flavored packaging; biopsy image assessment was blinded. The current registry says quadruple masking. No successful-blinding assessment was reported in the recovered chapter.

**DXA total lean mass was primary.** Muscle fiber area, strength and SPPB were secondary. The sample-size calculation targeted a 1.14 kg lean-mass difference, SD 1.4 kg, 80% power and alpha .05. DXA lean tissue is not a direct assay of contractile protein or productive cell renewal.

Authors report a predefined analysis plan and intention-to-treat mixed linear models: time, treatment and their interaction were fixed effects, participant a random effect, with Toeplitz covariance. No recovered multiplicity correction or hierarchical testing gate was specified. A secondary nominal positive remains secondary evidence; it is neither a primary success nor erased by the primary null. No adjusted difference-in-change estimate or confidence interval was supplied.

Eight participants withdrew, four per group; four withdrawn participants accepted final assessments. Withdrawal reasons and arm-by-visit missingness are not tabulated. Authors say per-protocol conclusions did not differ, without full estimates. **The endpoint analysis samples are not all 65, and must not be presented as 65 completers.**

Body weight (n = 65), fat mass, appendicular lean mass and bone mineral content (n = 62), and type I/II fiber area (n = 46) had no significant treatment/time/interaction effects. The author-copy fiber-area table prints mm² beside values around 3–5; no absolute-area interpretation or silent unit correction is made.

The SPPB combines balance, walking and chair-rise performance. The report does not provide a separate significant balance result. Its discussion extrapolates from SPPB to later disability risk using other literature; **falls, disability prevention, hospitalization and mortality were not measured benefits of this intervention**.

Habitual diet excluding supplements showed no protein/macronutrient interaction. Energy intake declined over time (P = .04), without an interaction (P = .60). With supplements, the authors report total protein intake increasing from about 1.0 to 1.4 g/kg/day in the active arm. Follow-up glucose, insulin, eGFR, SF-12, blood pressure and MMSE were described as unchanged, with numeric follow-up results **data not shown**.

## Registry, safety and disclosure limits

The recovered current [registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT01109628) identifies 65 actual participants, muscle mass as primary and physical performance among secondary outcomes. It lists study start November 2009, first submission February 12, 2010 and first public posting April 23, 2010; chapter recruitment began December 2009. Registration therefore followed study/recruitment start. The current record was last posted April 4, 2025, and has no posted results. Registry history returned 403; no original-version protocol or statistical analysis plan was recovered. Do not claim fully prospective, unchanged prespecification from the current record alone.

The discussion reports no health complaints or side effects and no renal-function change over 24 weeks. This is retained as reported, but no adverse-event table, ascertainment method, serious-event counts or withdrawal reasons were recovered. High adherence is not proof of safety, and renal follow-up is unshown. The restricted older-adult sample does not establish pediatric, renal-disease or MVA safety.

FrieslandCampina is named as beverage provider. The thesis states that its studies were conducted within TI Food and Nutrition; the registry names Wageningen University as sponsor, with Maastricht and TI Food and Nutrition collaborators. Its final printing acknowledgments name additional organizations specifically for **printing the thesis**, not necessarily trial funding. Indexed publisher text reports no author conflicts; the complete journal declaration page and trial-specific funder roles were not recovered.

## One companion comparison, kept separate

The neighboring author-report Chapter 4 and [NCT01110369](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01110369) identify the **62-person** trial, PMID 22770932, DOI 10.1016/j.jamda.2012.05.020. Both of those arms received twice-weekly resistance training plus protein or placebo. Its original abstract reports added lean-mass benefit (interaction P = .006), with **no added protein interaction for strength or SPPB**. Only its title, abstract, selected Methods/product pages and registry metadata were read. Its full tables/plots were not qualified here.

This is a distinct registered comparison and exercise background. Do not pool it with the 65-person trial, transfer its mass gain, or call it an independent replication of the SPPB positive. Individual participant overlap was not audited.
