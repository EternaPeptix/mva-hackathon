# KIF18A compounds: tumor regression is a real result, with a different therapeutic purpose

**Retain four distinct research compounds and their measured antitumor effects.**
AM-0277, AM-1882, AM-5308 and AM-9022 are not interchangeable names. Three were
administered to tumor-bearing mice. The evidence supports selected cancer-cell
vulnerability, not restoration of BUBR1, faithful division or useful muscle
output in constitutional BUB1B deficiency. This updates the target-level
[route comparison](../../reports/appendices/track2-route-decisions.md).

## Primary source and scope

Payton et al., *Small-molecule inhibition of kinesin KIF18A reveals a mitotic
vulnerability enriched in chromosomally unstable cancers*, online 2023, print
2024: [PMID 38151625](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38151625/),
[primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10824666/),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43018-023-00699-5).

The primary XML main narrative, all Results, Discussion and main/extended figure
legends were read. A second reader audited the complete Methods, animal ethics,
conflicts and selected figure legends. Original figure pixels, supplementary
reporting files and raw/source-data workbooks were not inspected. The numerical
effects below are the authors' reported results, not independent image or raw-data
reanalysis. Normal PMC/Nature figure access and the official OA-package route
did not supply usable original images; no access control was bypassed. This
primary follow-up adds zero abstract-reading credit. A linked commentary is not
a replication or correction.

## Compound-specific evidence

| Compound | Direct evidence in this source | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| AM-0277 | Cancer-cell growth inhibition, mitotic arrest and apoptotic markers. After six days at 0.5 micromolar, susceptible cell lines had impaired regrowth during 7–9 days without compound. | The regrowth experiment was one independent experiment. No mouse treatment result for this compound; do not transfer the analogues' regressions. |
| AM-1882 | In OVCAR-3 xenografts, 100 mg/kg intraperitoneally daily for 18 days produced reported 73% tumor regression. OVCAR-8 regressions were 16% and 73% at 50 and 100 mg/kg. | Selected cancer models; near-diploid CAL-51 tumors did not respond under the tested regimen. Regression is not chromosome repair. |
| AM-5308 | OVCAR-3 regression 46% at 25 mg/kg intraperitoneally daily for 18 days. OVCAR-8 regressions 19% and 75% at 25 and 50 mg/kg. | Most tumors eventually regrew after treatment stopped. One treated OVCAR-8 animal lacked regrowth through day 81; this is not a universal durable response. |
| AM-9022 | Oral 30 mg/kg daily for 18 days produced 95% OVCAR-3 regression; 6/10 animals had no measurable tumor on day 45. JIMT-1 regression was 16%/94% at 30/100 mg/kg daily for 21 days, ending on day 40. | No measurable tumor is not histological eradication. Dose and exposure relationships varied across tumor models. |

These are **animal experimental exposures**, not a human dosing proposal.
Efficacy arms generally contained ten mice; pharmacodynamic arms three. OVCAR-3,
CAL-51, OVCAR-8 and the four TNBC patient-derived xenografts used female athymic
nude mice; JIMT-1 used CB.17 SCID mice. These immune-deficient hosts cannot establish
preserved human tumor surveillance. Amgen studies were unblinded and did not use
a statistical sample-size determination. Most authors were current/former Amgen
employees/shareholders; this disclosure does not negate the results, but the study
is not independent replication.

AM-9022 also had heterogeneous effects in four TNBC patient-derived xenografts at
60 mg/kg orally daily on model-specific schedules. The Methods says at least
27 consecutive days, while Results and the Figure 6 legend say at least 28 days;
this day-count wording is not resolved here. CTG-0017 and CTG-0437 stopped dosing
on day 27 and were observed without drug through days 58 and 34, respectively.
CTG-0888 and CTG-1019 dosing ended on days 43 and 52, with last measurements on
days 41 and 51. CTG-0017 had 83% regression and 90%
without measurable tumor by day 58; CTG-0437 had 101% tumor-growth inhibition;
CTG-0888 had 63% growth inhibition; CTG-1019 had no reported antitumor effect.
**Growth inhibition and regression use different reference quantities** and must
not be pooled as equivalent percentage shrinkage. In particular, 101% growth
inhibition does not mean a tumor shrank by 101%. TGI compares the change in
group mean tumor volume against the control group
(100 − 100 × change in treated volume/change in control volume); regression
compares the final and initial group mean treated volume
(100 − 100 × final treated volume/initial treated volume).

## Target attribution and the BUBR1 connection

The biochemical work supports microtubule-dependent, allosteric KIF18A ATPase
inhibition. The tested kinesin selectivity panel had an exception at KIF19A;
the 96-kinase panel also found an AM-5308 TRK-A binding hit at 1 micromolar. The
limited panels do not establish exclusive cellular target action. None of the
four directly altered tubulin polymerization in the tested 10-micromolar assay.

The pooled 631-cancer-line PRISM screen used AM-1882 for five days. Response was
enriched in certain TP53-mutant, whole-genome-duplicated and highly aneuploid
subsets, but many such cancers remained resistant. For example, within the
reported breast/ovarian TP53-mutant subset, 12/22 whole-genome-duplicated lines
met the source's sensitivity threshold versus 4/19 non-duplicated lines. This is
enrichment under a study-defined threshold, not a clinical biomarker or universal
dependency of aneuploid cells.

In OVCAR-3 cells, AM-0277 produced BubR1/KIF18A doublets during a 4–24-hour mitotic
time course, together with cyclin B1 accumulation and apoptotic markers. This
qualitative, single-experiment blot supports an arrest-associated state; it does
not demonstrate increased usable BUBR1, improved protein half-life or rescue of
Leu737Ter/Asn1002Lys. Other treated cancer models showed prolonged mitosis, death,
DNA-damage markers and micronuclei. Surviving daughter-cell production was not
shown to restore normal copy-number inheritance.

The existing [separate SCLC appraisal](pubmed-supplement-review.md) provides an
additional constraint: impaired MAD1/BUBR1 kinetochore recruitment accompanied
AM-9022 resistance, and concurrent checkpoint inhibition could weaken arrest.
Its schedule-dependent killing result is neither a new experiment here nor
evidence that every BUB1B-deficient tumor will resist KIF18A inhibition. Taken
together, the studies make checkpoint competence a question to measure rather
than an assumption supplied by the word aneuploidy.

## What the normal-cell comparisons actually show

- Bone-marrow mononuclear cells had limited effects at 1 micromolar over
  48-hour cell-cycle or 96-hour growth assays, relative to the tested antimitotic
  comparators. AM-5308 and AM-9022 were each tested in two healthy donors;
  AM-0277 and AM-1882 were each tested in four.
- Foreskin fibroblast experiments were single experiments. Mammary epithelial
  cells and activated T cells were inhibited at concentrations more than twenty
  times those active in sensitive cancer lines; they were not universally spared.
- Sensory-neurosphere neurite area was assessed over 24 hours in two independent
  experiments. AM-1882/AM-5308 had a partial adverse effect at 10 micromolar.
- Mouse body weight and endpoint blood counts support limited tolerability
  observations. The OVCAR-3 blood-count subgroup was six animals per group,
  after 18 days of AM-1882/AM-5308 treatment; platelet counts were not reported
  because of technical processing problems. These measurements do not establish
  developmental, fertility, organ or long-term
  safety. Germ-cell findings from genetic Kif18a models are background evidence,
  not drug safety experiments in this study.

These useful controls are stronger than an unsupported assertion of cancer
selectivity. They are still narrower than “normal cells do not depend on KIF18A.”
Neither nonmalignant BUB1B-deficient tissue nor patient-derived myogenic cells
was tested in this source.

## Research availability and identity

Current manufacturer-indexed listings identify [AM-5308, HY-144894,
CAS 2410796-89-1](https://www.medchemexpress.com/am-5308.html) and
[AM-9022, HY-162110, CAS 2446872-46-2](https://www.medchemexpress.com/us/en/am-9022.html)
as research products. This verifies catalog identity, not current stock, an
authenticated study-equivalent lot, regulatory approval or clinical availability.
Direct product-page opening returned 403, so current indexed manufacturer text
supports only those limited assertions. No dependable manufacturer listing for
AM-0277 or AM-1882 was established in this check. Their study identities remain
valid despite unresolved procurement.

Do not transfer these results to a separately sold stereoisomer, BTB-1, AMG-650
or another KIF18A program merely because they share a target or similar name.
Supplier “microtubule/tubulin” categories are not evidence of direct tubulin
binding. The source's synthesis/structure assignments control its compound
identity; no new commercial lot was analytically authenticated.

## Competitive consequence

**Keep KIF18A inhibition as a specific tumor-research route, not the lead for
restoring nonmalignant MVA tissue.** The actual tumor regressions deserve more
weight than a target-only hypothesis when the intended endpoint is tumor control.
They answer a different question from restoration or muscle compensation and
do not establish safety against a background of constitutional chromosome
instability.

This does not make niacin or apitegromab proven alternatives: their proposed
BUB1B-MVA benefits remain unmeasured. A fair future comparison needs the relevant
tumor context, preserved checkpoint competence and useful nonmalignant tissue
function. This appraisal changes the specificity of the comparison, not the
provisional research nomination or the clinical management of the child.
