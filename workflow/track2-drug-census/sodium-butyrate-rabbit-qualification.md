# Sodium butyrate: rabbit muscle morphology with an indirect BUB1B signal

**29 September 2026. Retain as a common-compound research reserve, not a
restoration lead.** A dietary sodium-butyrate study in rabbits reports muscle
fiber and growth changes, and includes BUB1B within a cecal cell-cycle gene
cluster correlated with muscle-fiber density. It does **not** establish that
butyrate raises BUB1B, restores BUBR1 protein or improves faithful division.
This is useful route-discovery evidence, but it does not displace the current
candidate evidence or establish an MVA advantage.

## Rabbit feeding study

[Ni et al., *Animal Research and One Health*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aro2.71)
was first published 11 June 2024. The source randomized **14 weaned male New
Zealand White rabbits** (35 ± 2 days; 1.70 ± 0.09 kg), seven per group, to basal
feed or basal feed supplemented with **0.75 g/kg sodium butyrate** (Aladdin,
China) for ten weeks after acclimatization.

The treated group had higher body weight during weeks 6–10 and higher average
daily gain during weeks 2–7; feed intake and feed-conversion findings varied by
week. Diarrhea scores were lower. Quadriceps histology showed a higher reported
fiber count, total area and density, with smaller fiber diameter. The study did
not measure absolute muscle force, fatigue or endurance, so these are growth
and morphology outcomes rather than demonstrated functional muscle rescue.

The cecal RNA-seq analysis reported **466 differentially expressed unigenes**
(250 up and 216 down under its stated global FDR criterion). Upregulated gene
sets were enriched for cell-cycle, microtubule and chromosomal-region terms.
The paper lists **BUB1B** in a cell-cycle gene cluster whose expression was
positively correlated with rabbit muscle-fiber density. That correlation does
not identify a BUB1B-specific treatment effect or mediation: no individual
BUB1B fold change, gene-level P/q value, BUBR1 protein, checkpoint activity or
chromosome-fidelity endpoint is reported in the accessible main text. BUB1B
was not identified among the selected genes validated by qPCR/protein in the
text. Cecal expression is also a different tissue from skeletal muscle.

The source describes increased serum GGT, total bile acids, cholesterol,
triglycerides, creatinine, urea and glucose, decreased total CO2, and reduced
heart, liver and lung weights. These are measured changes requiring context;
they are not by themselves evidence of toxicity or safety. The small animal
study does not establish a human exposure or pediatric safety window.

In cultured rabbit cecal epithelial cells, 0.75 mmol/L sodium butyrate promoted
CCK-8 signal, wound closure and selected proliferation markers. This supports
the epithelial-renewal rationale in that system, not BUB1B-specific rescue.
Because these experiments emphasize cell proliferation, an MVA-oriented test
would need chromosome-fidelity and viable productive-output endpoints alongside
growth. That is a design implication, not evidence that sodium butyrate harms
aneuploid cells.

## Keep the preparations distinct

The repository separately qualifies a 12-week **BODYBIO sodium butyrate
300-mg capsule** study in older men with COPD in
[gut-muscle-qualification.md](gut-muscle-qualification.md). Its reported handgrip,
gait and selected SPPB changes are within-arm comparisons; a randomized
between-arm functional contrast was not recovered. No plasma butyrate was
measured. The rabbit feed additive, the BODYBIO capsules, endogenous microbial
butyrate, sodium butyrate used in cell culture, sodium phenylbutyrate,
glycerol phenylbutyrate and hydroxybutyrates are not interchangeable
interventions.

The publisher's primary article states that no RNA-seq data were deposited in
an official repository. The supplemental DOCX was linked from Wiley but its
download returned HTTP 403 in this review; no access control was bypassed.
Consequently, the reported BUB1B association was not independently quantified
or reanalyzed. Reading covered the article's treatment and transcriptome
methods, relevant growth/muscle and gene-correlation results, figure captions,
limitations and data-availability statement. This was a targeted source read,
not a claim of exhaustive article review; the supplementary file and raw data
were not read.

## Competitiveness for MVA

Sodium butyrate earns a **common-compound muscle/gut reserve**: it has actual
administered-compound animal morphology and growth results, plus a separate
human preparation-specific functional report with an unresolved between-arm
contrast. Its BUB1B link remains a tissue-level correlation, not a direct
restoration experiment. It is therefore less competitive than NMN for direct
BubR1-abundance evidence, less competitive than niacin for human muscle NAD
engagement, and less competitive than apitegromab for randomized human muscle
function. None of those comparisons proves efficacy in MVA.

A useful next experiment would measure BUB1B RNA and BUBR1 protein in the same
treated samples, then quantify checkpoint function, spontaneous segregation
errors, progenitor renewal and absolute contractile output in affected and
corrected cells. A proliferation increase or larger muscle-fiber count alone
would not pass the MVA rescue criteria. This source adds no appraisal count and
does not change the current nominations.
