# Creatine in pediatric FSHD: a primary motor-function null with uncertain secondary signals

The newly identified [Woodcock 2025 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12149790/) adds a directly relevant pediatric neuromuscular comparison to the existing creatine assessment. It **did not demonstrate an improvement in its primary motor-function endpoint**. Walking and several other estimates favored creatine, but their confidence intervals included no effect. This supports retaining creatine as a research comparison with mixed evidence; it does not supply a new positive result that displaces either provisional nominee.

The result belongs to powdered **creatine monohydrate**, compared with maltodextrin, in children with FSHD. It is neither a BUB1B-restoration experiment nor an MVA efficacy result. The [earlier DMD strength positives and nulls](creatine-muscle-qualification.md) remain distinct evidence at their previously recorded, predominantly abstract-only reading depth.

## What was administered and measured

Thirteen children were randomized, seven to creatine first and six to placebo first; eleven completed both periods. Mean age was 12.2 years, with an observed range of 7–15. Two 12-week periods were separated by a planned six-week washout. Two participants withdrew before period two because of pandemic-related concerns. A fall during washout left another participant unable to perform walking assessments in the second period; this was not established as a creatine-caused injury.

The source used weight-banded daily doses of 2.5, 5, 7.5 or 10 g, described as approximately 100 mg/kg/day. Both preparations contained 5 g of Splenda sweetener and were taken in 250 mL skimmed milk. The main does not establish supplier, purity, lot or a current retail equivalent. No change in usual diet or activity was prescribed. These are research exposures, not a proposed patient regimen.

The primary analysis used change in MFM-32 over each period, with treatment and period fixed effects and a participant random effect. The flowchart reports all thirteen analyzed, including those with only one period. Supplementary individual MFM rows contain twelve creatine-period changes, twelve placebo-period changes and eleven paired observations; this does not make thirteen complete pairs. Denominators for each secondary model are not supplied in Table 2 and must not be assumed equal.

## What constrains confidence

The planned sample was twenty; thirteen were recruited. Disease severity and sequence-group baselines differed, and the authors identify a ceiling effect in MFM-32. Those limitations leave room for an undetected benefit, but do not convert the secondary trends into efficacy. The paper initially treats washout as ensuring clearance, then acknowledges that pharmacokinetic and excretion measurements were not performed and that carryover was not empirically settled. No subgroup benefit was established.

The available statistical analysis plan is **version 1, dated 5 October 2022**, after the reported final trial visit in May 2021 and the current registry's July 2022 completion date. It says it was developed blinded, but the retrieved material does not independently establish when unblinding occurred. It specifies no multiplicity adjustment and no subgroup analyses. It also contains unrelated Friedreich-ataxia headings, a different registration identifier in its administrative section and pill-count wording despite sachet administration. These internal inconsistencies limit treating it as clean proof of prospective specification; they do not by themselves invalidate the trial.

The [current registry, NCT02948244](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02948244), records first posting in October 2016, before recruitment. Its current primary outcome agrees with MFM at three months, but historical field versions were not inspected. Current enrollment is eleven versus thirteen randomized in the publication, and some listed secondary measurements, including quantitative strength, ultrasound, GPX3 and step counts, have no result identified in the retrieved article and supplements. Missing results remain unknown, not null. The registry has no posted results.

Additional reporting discrepancies are retained without guessed corrections: main text/Table 1 describe twelve initially ambulant children and one wheelchair-dependent child, whereas the supplementary demographics list eleven and two; the main gives placebo MFM mean change 0.06 while the supplementary table prints 0; some overall Table 1 averages do not reconcile with the displayed sequence-group averages and denominators. The adjusted treatment contrast cannot be reconstructed simply by subtracting unadjusted means. No original participant-level model was rerun.

## Safety and competitiveness

No discontinuation due to adverse events or creatine-related serious adverse event was reported. The creatine–placebo serum-creatinine difference was +12.63 μmol/L (95% CI 1.14–24.12; supplementary P=.031); calculated eGFR fell by 25.79 mL/min/1.73 m² (95% CI −44.3 to −7.25; P=.006). The authors attribute these changes to increased creatinine production and report reversal after cessation. Because eGFR used creatinine, and measured GFR or cystatin-C confirmation was not reported, this does not establish either kidney injury or unaffected filtration. Two participants lacked blood testing during creatine exposure. Renal exclusions, a small sample and short exposure limit safety transfer.

Creatine remains an accessible-preparation research challenger on the strength of the broader, mixed pediatric muscle evidence; this FSHD study adds **a controlled primary null and uncertain secondary estimates**, not a generic ineffectiveness conclusion. There is no head-to-head comparison with niacin or apitegromab and no BUBR1, chromosome-fidelity or MVA endpoint. Compensation should be judged by useful function, renewal and fidelity at justified exposure; BUBR1 mediation is additionally needed only for a restoration claim. The new study neither establishes superiority nor justifies removing the earlier favorable DMD evidence.

The separately accepted source review records FSHD Global Research Foundation funding and investigators’ disclosed other pharmaceutical honoraria, grants or trial roles. It does not identify manufacturer sponsorship of the tested preparation or establish that disclosures caused the findings.
