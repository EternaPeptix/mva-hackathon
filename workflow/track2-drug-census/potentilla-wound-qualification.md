# Potentilla topical extracts: wound repair without a demonstrated BUBR1 mechanism

**Retain two preparation-specific tissue-repair reserves at low priority for the present MVA muscle proposal.** The [2025 primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaim.2024.101075) reports smaller wounds with 10% topical ethyl-acetate extract (EAPF) and a later positive with 10% topical methanol extract (MEPF). It does not assay BUBR1, chromosome fidelity or muscle function. These gels are distinct from the previously reviewed oral 2019 extract and ECGU mixture.

Roots were extracted successively with petroleum ether, chloroform, ethyl acetate, methanol and water. EAPF and MEPF were incorporated into Carbopol gel at 5% or 10%. In male Wistar rats with chemically induced diabetes, treatment began the day after surgical foot wounding and continued to day 21. Eight groups of six account for 48 of 60 acquired animals; disposition of the other 12 is unspecified. Randomization, blinding, gel mass per wound and application frequency are not described.

## Measured repair and its comparators

Table 2 reports wound areas as mean plus or minus SEM, with six rats per group:

| Arm | Day 14 area, mm² | Day 21 area, mm² | Reported comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Diabetic wounded negative control | 15.2 ± 2.1 | 10.9 ± 1.26 | Reference |
| Plain gel placebo | 15.3 ± 1.5 | 8.2 ± 1.34 | No significant difference from negative control reported |
| Silver sulfadiazine 1% | 5.4 ± 0.9 | 1.2 ± 0.22 | Significant versus negative control at both times; versus gel placebo marked at day 14 |
| EAPF 10% | 5.8 ± 0.6 | 1.6 ± 0.26 | Significant versus negative control at both times |
| MEPF 10% | 10.0 ± 1.1 | 2.5 ± 0.46 | Significant versus negative control at day 21 |

Neither 10% extract is marked significant versus the plain-gel comparator in the wound-area table. **This is an unreported comparison result, not proof of a statistical null.** The paper reports no significant wound-area benefit from either 5% preparation. Representative histology supports repair; it is not a blinded quantitative score, mechanical-strength measurement or complete-closure-time result.

Biochemical changes support a local tissue response. Total tissue protein is not BUBR1 protein. Concurrent oxidative-stress markers cannot establish mediation, constituent causality or correction of diabetic physiology.

## Limits that affect interpretation

- Printed wound-contraction percentages do not reproduce straightforward ratios of the printed group means. Individual paired data are unavailable, and a mean of individual ratios need not equal a ratio of means; no replacement percentage is asserted. Figure 2 labels a decreasing area-like curve as percentage contraction.
- Methods identifies an epigallocatechin-gallate reference, while Results and Figure 1 identify epigallocatechin. The second chemical marker is unresolved; isolated constituents were not tested as treatment arms.
- Some claims of significance versus both controls exceed the displayed figure markings. For example, MEPF 10% GSH is marked only versus the negative control. Missing markings do not establish the unreported tests as null.
- Safety statements lack detailed current adverse-event and systemic-exposure data. The cited acute dermal study was not read here; it cannot establish human or pediatric safety.
- The later methanol fraction was extracted after other solvents and delivered topically. It cannot inherit the crude 2019 extract's cancer/chromosome outcomes, and those preparations cannot inherit this wound result.

The study supplies actual noncancer repair evidence, which merits retention. Its model, route, formulation and endpoints do not presently challenge niacin's protein-supply hypothesis or apitegromab's muscle-compensation rationale. No MVA efficacy or comparative clinical superiority is established for any of them.
