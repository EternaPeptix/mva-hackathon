# Muscle senescence: two additional intervention appraisals

Checked 22 September 2026. This is a bounded literature appraisal, not a treatment plan. All quantities below describe published laboratory experiments. No biological experiment was performed.

**Recommendation:** add fisetin as a conditional muscle-function research reserve and add dasatinib plus quercetin as a **combination-only** senotherapeutic reserve. These primary experiments strengthen the administered-drug implementation of a muscle senescence hypothesis. They do not establish BUBR1 restoration, chromosome-error correction or MVA benefit, and do not justify replacing the adopted niacin-restoration/apitegromab-compensation comparison. Neither adopted candidate has demonstrated MVA rescue either.

## Scope and novelty

The preceding 104-entry decision ledger was checked before selection. Fisetin was not appraised. `prior-22` is explicitly **dasatinib alone**, and explicitly cannot inherit combination effects. D+Q therefore requires its own combination record; it does not supply efficacy for either ingredient alone. Navitoclax and genetic senescent-cell depletion were already covered and are not new appraisals here.

The two seed reviews were retrieved as full text from the public NCBI BioC service. Their senolytic, muscle-regeneration/function and mitochondrial sections and relevant references were read for discovery. They were not treated as primary efficacy evidence. The reviews' BUBR1 precedent is a transgene-dependent cell-clearance system, not demonstrated fisetin or D+Q treatment of a BUBR1 model. No same-gene drug rescue was identified in the selected primary experiments.

## Fisetin: measured muscle outcomes, with important reporting limits

### Progeria-associated muscle study

[Liu et al., 2022, PMID 36218080, PMC9745459](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9745459/) used Zmpste24-deficient mice, a lamin-processing/progeria model. This is not a BUB1B model. Five-month-old mutant and age-matched wild-type muscles established the cellular phenotype. Both sexes were used, without a sex-stratified efficacy result. In the drug arm, four-month-old mutant mice received oral fisetin 100 mg/kg once weekly for four weeks versus mutant controls receiving the same volume of 10% ethanol/90% PEG-400 vehicle by gavage. Figure 5 specifies six biological replicates. Randomization and blinding are **not established**: the retrieved text contains no instance of "blind" and none of "random"/"randomiz" applied to allocation — the legends' "random pictures" refers to microscope field selection only. The functional endpoint is also operator-scored and effort-dependent, taken from the single longest hang trial. The two fisetin studies are therefore asymmetric on design quality, and it is the weaker-design study that supplies the more MVA-adjacent lesion.

Fisetin improved **four-limb holding impulse**, calculated from longest inverted-grid hang time multiplied by body weight, with conversion to N·s. Figure 5B shows the treated/control significance bracket. This is whole-animal hanging performance weighted by body mass, not a force-transducer grip measurement, isolated-muscle contraction or body-weight-independent endurance. Raw hang times and treatment-group body weights were not separately reported in the inspected result, so the two contributions cannot be disentangled. No precise percent functional improvement is inferred from bar heights.

Figure 5 also shows fewer senescence-marker-positive cells and FAPs, more Pax7-positive progenitors, less fibrosis and larger myofibres. These support a niche hypothesis but do not themselves establish functional regeneration. The cell experiment is a separate, useful result: 20 µM fisetin in a transwell system containing mutant FAPs and **wild-type** muscle progenitors improved progenitor proliferation and myotube fusion, with untreated co-cultures and wild-type FAP/progenitor controls. Figure 3's nominal N=6 is **three biological replicates with two technical replicates each**, not six independent donors. No rescue of mutant muscle-progenitor intrinsic function was demonstrated by that co-culture. The text describes 48 hours for the rescue experiment, whereas assay methods describe 24+12 hours for proliferation and 96 hours for myogenesis; these should remain assay-specific/reporting uncertainties.

That same experiment ran the selectivity control, and it was positive: 20 µM fisetin raised TUNEL-positive apoptosis in mutant FAPs **but not in wild-type FAPs**, and left wild-type progenitor and wild-type-FAP co-culture behaviour unchanged. Selective clearance is therefore demonstrated **in vitro**. What is not established is (i) selectivity *in vivo*, where only marker counts on sections were measured — those show what is present, not what was killed — and (ii) selectivity in **aneuploid** tissue. That second gap is the MVA-specific hazard and the reason this cannot be carried across on mouse data: in this patient the senescent or p16-positive compartment may itself be aneuploid cells the child is currently relying on for tissue mass. A research test must measure that specific question, not a generic unknown.

### Natural-aging physical-function study

[Murray et al., 2025, PMID 40437670, PMC12341784](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12341784/) supplies a distinct administered-fisetin experiment. Young mice were 6–8 months; old males 27 months and old females 29 months. Fisetin and vehicle groups combined wild-type and p16-3MR mice after the authors found no model interaction. The fisetin arm **did not activate a genetic suicide system**. Assignment was randomized, baseline body weight matched, and investigators were blinded for collection and biochemical analysis.

The oral schedule was one week on, two weeks off, one week on; functional testing was about two weeks after the final course, followed by tissue assessment.

**Dose, resolved: 100 mg/kg/day.** Methods 2.2 prints 50 mg/kg/day while Figure 1A prints 100 mg/kg/day on both dosing blocks; both were inspected. The conflict resolves against the Methods once that paragraph's own cited source is checked. It is a near-verbatim reuse of the same group's [Mahoney et al., 2024, PMID 38062873, PMC10928570](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10928570/) cohort description — which states 100 mg/kg/day, with the identical vehicle, the identical one-week-on/two-off/one-on schedule and the identical group sizes. The 50 mg/kg/day figure is this paper's *navitoclax* dose from section 2.4. The same reused paragraph also miscounts the intervention deaths relative to Mahoney (ten versus twelve), which indicates imperfect transcription rather than a second dosing decision. Treat 100 mg/kg/day as administered and the Methods value as a transcription error. This also establishes something the earlier reading missed: **the 2025 muscle outcomes are a new readout on the 2024 cohort, not an independent replication of it** — which is why the paper outsources its morphological characteristics to the earlier reports. Mahoney was fetched during audit and is not part of the fixed saved-abstract corpus.

The reported old-group forelimb grip result was **3.30 ± 0.1 versus 2.90 ± 0.1 g force/g body weight**, fisetin versus vehicle, **n=30/group**, p=0.0038: the authors' approximately 14% benefit is for **body-weight-normalized grip**. It is not a measured 14% absolute-force gain. The paper states no treatment-related body-mass or muscle-mass differences, and Table 2 shows no muscle-mass benefit. Group body-weight values and unnormalized grip effects were not independently reconstructed. Frailty scores were 0.23 ± 0.01 (n=39) versus 0.27 ± 0.01 (n=31), p=0.0264. Grip is itself one component of this frailty index, so these are partly overlapping outcomes. The same treated arm is n=39 for frailty but n=30 for grip, and the nine-animal gap is not explained in the source. Young mice did not show a functional improvement.

Two inferences follow that a flat normalization caveat would suppress. Because Table 2 shows no fisetin effect on soleus, gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior or quadriceps mass, the per-gram gain is **force per unit mass and not a denominator artifact from mass loss** — and equally, it is not a mass rescue. And the two animal endpoints depend on body weight in **opposite directions**: progeria holding impulse multiplies by body weight, while aging grip divides by it. No single body-weight confound can inflate both positives, so the normalization limit must not be read as a reason to discount both results together.

The study's separate ganciclovir/p16-3MR and navitoclax experiments are comparators, not additional fisetin trials. Cross-study normalization found no statistically significant difference in improvement; this is **not an equivalence/noninferiority test**. Cohort composition, baseline levels and mortality differ. No niacin or apitegromab arm was included. Transcript changes (notably p21-related signals) do not prove selective senescent-cell killing; the authors acknowledge that additional cellular/histological confirmation is needed. Ten old mice died during the fisetin intervention period, described as age-related attrition; the paragraph reports mouse model but not each treatment arm. The authors found no overt toxicity at dissection. This does not establish pediatric safety, long-term tumor safety or compatibility with cancer treatment.

### Interpretation and fair comparison

**Why this reserve is held at all.** The saved seed reviews carry the link that motivates the senolytic route for *this* gene: p16-positive fibro-adipogenic progenitors accumulate in BubR1-insufficient mice, and systemic genetic inactivation of p16 in those mice attenuated the degenerative loss of muscle mass and strength. That is a review's secondhand account of transgenic clearance. It cannot be transferred to administered fisetin and it supplies no efficacy. But the FAP lesion it describes in BubR1 mice is the same lesion fisetin acted on in Zmpste24-deficient muscle, and **that convergence — not the mouse function numbers — is the reason to hold a fisetin reserve rather than close it.** Stating only the refusals would leave an entry a reader cannot act on.

Fisetin has more direct administered-compound **mouse muscle functional evidence** than an inference from senescence markers alone. Its progeria co-culture also offers a testable preservation-of-progenitors mechanism. It is not a demonstrated BUBR1-restoration intervention, and neither study measures chromosome fidelity. Supplement availability or dietary occurrence does not establish an approved muscle indication, pediatric exposure or a senolytic therapeutic window. These eligibility and exposure questions remain unresolved here; no food/supplement dose is substituted for the experimental compound.

Relative to niacin, fisetin adds direct mouse muscle-performance data but lacks the proposed proximal protein-supply route and niacin's separate human myopathy evidence. Relative to apitegromab, it offers a niche/regeneration hypothesis but lacks the adopted comparator's actual-drug randomized human motor-function evidence. Cross-disease efficacy cannot be ranked numerically. A relevant research test would need to show preserved useful muscle output and progenitor capacity while measuring which cells are lost, chromosome errors and tumor/cancer-treatment effects.

## Dasatinib plus quercetin: combination-specific function, incomplete structural rescue

### Direct isolated-muscle experiment

[Borowik et al., 2024, PMID 38233728, PMC11009189](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11009189/) randomly assigned female Sod1-knockout and C57BL/6J wild-type mice to four arms: WT vehicle, WT D+Q, knockout vehicle, knockout D+Q. Starting at six months, the **mixture** was gavaged at dasatinib 5 mg/kg plus quercetin 50 mg/kg for three consecutive days every 15 days for seven months. The final dose preceded assessment by seven days. This is oxidative-stress-associated frailty, not BUBR1 deficiency. There were no single-agent arms, so synergy and component attribution are untested.

Both administered-combination muscle results in this appraisal are **female-only, and males were never tested** — not merely unreported. This study used females by design, with males kept for breeding; the aging study below dosed 20-month-old females only and titles its figure accordingly. That absence deserves stating affirmatively, because the aging paper's own characterization arm is sex-divergent in the axis the mechanism targets (p16 more pronounced in old males, p21 notably greater in old females), so the intervention was run only in the sex with the stronger p21-high-myofibre signal. Male transfer is untested, not established.

Isolated extensor digitorum longus (EDL) muscles underwent electrically stimulated contraction in an oxygenated bath. Figure 5B shows a treated-versus-vehicle knockout significance bracket for **specific force in N/cm²**, calculated from force with muscle mass/length-based normalization. The genotype-by-treatment interaction is p=0.0016. Figure 5A reports maximal force in mN and a genotype-by-treatment interaction p=0.0334, but does **not** show the same within-knockout pairwise significance bracket. Accordingly, this appraisal credits specific-force improvement and does not claim established absolute-force rescue. Group means/effect-size confidence intervals and exact force sample counts were not stated in the inspected main text or legend; plotted points are not substituted for an authoritative sample count. The n=5–6 stated for transcript assays cannot be assigned to force assays.

The drug mixture did not improve body composition, muscle mass, motor-neuron count, neuromuscular-junction innervation/fragmentation or measured protein-synthesis rates. The marker experiment used tibialis anterior, whereas force used EDL; selective cell clearance in the contracting muscle was not directly shown. Gadd45 expression increased rather than normalized; that is a molecular observation, not an established clinical harm. Respiration reporting is inconsistent: the Results describe genotype-dependent treatment effects for maximally stimulated complex I and I+II respiration, whereas Discussion says D+Q did not alter respiration. No headline respiratory-rescue claim is adopted. The study does not supply a dedicated pediatric, cancer-context or long-term toxicity assessment. Only one muscle was tested for force.

### Natural-aging combination experiment

[Zhang et al., 2022, PMID 36147777, PMC9491365](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9491365/) treated **20-month-old female mice** with combined dasatinib 5 mg/kg and quercetin 50 mg/kg, or matched vehicle, three consecutive days every two weeks for four months (24 doses). Young six-month-old mice supplied an aging reference. Figure 6H and its legend show improved **body-weight-normalized grip**, n=9–10/group. The figure verifies the old-DQ versus old-vehicle significance bracket; no exact effect percentage is digitized. Absolute grip and treated-group body-weight contributions were not separately established in this appraisal. Baseline comparisons of young and old treadmill/rotarod performance must not be reassigned as D+Q treatment benefits.

Myofibre cross-sectional area and muscle-mass loss were not rescued. Centrally nucleated fibres fell (n=4/group), which could indicate less damage or a different regeneration response; this is not direct proof of enhanced regeneration. The authors did not directly assess cell death and could not distinguish a local senolytic action from senomorphic or systemic effects. The accompanying human biopsies were observational, not a human D+Q trial. Allocation/blinding details were not clearly specified for the treatment experiment in the inspected methods.

### Interpretation, harms and fair comparison

D+Q supplies an administered-combination precedent for improving a genuine mechanical endpoint even without restoring muscle mass. This is relevant to the adopted distinction between useful output and size. Neither component alone inherits that benefit, and it is not an implementation of the transgene-dependent BUBR1 clearance result.

Dasatinib is an approved oncology drug, but this does not establish an approved senolytic combination or MVA indication. The [retrieved DailyMed label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f60261dd-b8ea-4511-bed6-ad6d7f4dd828) establishes considerably more pediatric characterization than a bare "no pediatric data" would suggest, and one hazard that is specifically wrong for this patient.

On feasibility: the label carries **pediatric dosing from one year of age**, weight-banded once daily (10–<20 kg: 40 mg; 20–<30 kg: 60 mg; 30–<45 kg: 70 mg; ≥45 kg: 100 mg), with pharmacokinetics in 43 children and 97 chronic-phase CML children treated a median of 51.1 months. The 100 mg/day used in the human senolytic regimen is the same per-day dose as the top pediatric band, so pediatric exposure at senolytic-relevant daily doses is characterized. What is absent is any **intermittent, non-oncology schedule** in children.

On risk: the label's pediatric-specific hazard is skeletal — bone growth and development reactions in five of 97 (5.2%) after at least two years of continuous dosing, one Grade 3 growth retardation, plus delayed epiphyseal fusion and osteopenia, and no data below one year. For a growing child with a muscle deficit, **growth-plate toxicity is the specific risk to weigh**, and it is the wrong toxicity to accept for this indication. These oncology observations are not toxicity estimates for an intermittent senolytic schedule. An MVA-compatible combination exposure and preservation window is unestablished. The muscle studies do not determine tumor survival or interaction with a patient's cancer therapy.

Compared with niacin, D+Q has a clearer mouse mechanical-outcome precedent but no demonstrated BUBR1 restoration and a substantially different mechanism involving cell elimination or behavior. Compared with apitegromab, the two mouse studies do not supply randomized human motor-function evidence, but they preserve a potentially useful alternative when absolute muscle quality and renewal are measured together. There is no justified cross-study numerical therapeutic rank. Keep the adopted primary comparison and retain D+Q as a separately tested reserve.

## Existing navitoclax record: limited update from the 2025 comparison

Murray et al.'s separate ABT-263 experiment adds actual-drug normalized-grip evidence to existing `prior-23`, rather than a new compound. Young and old male C57BL/6N mice received vehicle or oral navitoclax 50 mg/kg/day on the same one-week-on/two-week-off/one-week-on schedule. Printed old-group normalized-grip means are 3.34 ± 0.21 (n=8) versus 2.55 ± 0.19 (n=14) g force/g body weight, p=0.0172; old treated frailty n=10 differs from grip n=8. These are neither absolute-force measurements nor MVA data. There were sixteen old-mouse deaths reported around this experiment: eight before baseline testing, two during vehicle dosing and six during ABT-263 dosing. Authors attribute them to age-related attrition and report no overt dissection evidence of toxicity; these observations do not establish absence of drug harm.

**Arithmetic limitation:** the stated ABT-263 23% gain does not equal the ratio of printed means (3.34/2.55 is about 1.31). Similarly, the GCV 22% statement differs from 3.44/2.73 (about 1.26); the cross-study normalized ratios are 1.23 and 1.22. No attempt is made to reconcile missing raw-data/normalization details. Use the printed means, units and sample counts rather than promoting either disputed percentage, and do not infer equivalence across cohorts. This additional mouse benefit does not remove the existing human platelet-toxicity or tumor-context qualifications.

## Human evidence tempers the animal findings

This targeted human-source check is separate from the two mouse-route appraisals.
It changes their competitive assessment without erasing their positive experiments.

**Fisetin, knee osteoarthritis:** the original [ORS 2025 conference report,
paper 429](https://www.ors.org/wp-content/uploads/AM25/429.pdf) describes a
randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled FDA-IND trial with 74 adults:
34 assigned fisetin and 40 placebo. It reports no between-group benefit in
walking tests, timed up-and-go, knee flexion/extension strength, gait, pain or
cartilage measures at the tested schedule. Its power calculation concerned
cartilage, not muscle strength; the brief report does not provide functional
effect estimates and confidence intervals.

Two features bound how far that null travels. The exposure was low and
intermittent — 20 mg/kg/day for two consecutive days per cycle, three cycles,
28 days off between — and **neither arm changed over time** on any function
measure, so this cohort had no decline to prevent. A null in a non-declining
adult population does not transfer to a child with an actual muscle deficit.
Equally, the trial's **primary endpoint was 12-month treatment-emergent safety,
and it found no adverse-event difference**: 185 adverse events, four serious
split two per group, all apparently unrelated operations, all comparisons
p>0.5. Citing this trial only as negative efficacy evidence discards a
12-month randomized adult human safety dataset. That dataset is real and it
still cannot establish pediatric safety. Poor absorption is suggested by the
authors as an explanation for nonbenefit; the report does not demonstrate an
exposure-response explanation.

The poster's historical claim about fisetin use in an IPF trial is not accepted:
its reference 5 is Justice et al.'s **dasatinib-plus-quercetin** study. This
citation cannot establish fisetin safety or efficacy. A current [NCI fisetin
breast-cancer-survivor trial listing](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials-search/v?id=NCI-2022-08061)
identifies an active physical-function trial and its planned outcomes, not
completed efficacy results. Trial registration is not promotion evidence.

**Dasatinib plus quercetin, IPF:** [Nambiar et al., 2023,
PMID 36857968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36857968/) randomized six participants
per arm to the combination or placebo. All completed treatment and assessment.
The quercetin intervention was a specified phytosome formulation; this is not
proof that arbitrary quercetin products reproduce it. The three-week pilot was
designed for feasibility and tolerability, with physical outcomes exploratory.
The authors report no meaningful between-group functional difference. Table 3
shows mean grip change of +1.8 ± 4.7 kg with D+Q versus +7.7 ± 6.6 kg with
placebo, and mean six-minute walking change of -6.4 ± 15.9 m versus
+16.6 ± 20.7 m, respectively, with between-group 95% confidence intervals of
[-1.6, 13.4] and [-1.0, 46.9].

The direction matters and "no difference" hides it. Both exploratory function
estimates moved **against** D+Q, with intervals that only just include zero.
This is the only human muscle-function readout that exists for the exact
intervention being nominated here on mouse grip and mouse isolated force, so
**null-to-unfavourable is materially different from silent**. It is also
confounded rather than clean: the D+Q arm had worse baseline forced vital
capacity (61.0% versus 75.5% predicted), worse short physical performance
battery, and less antifibrotic use (3/6 versus 5/6), across nine doses over
three weeks in a phase I single-blind single-centre pilot. These estimates
neither confirm benefit nor establish a robust harmful effect in such a small
study — but they are not evidence of benefit either, and they should be read
as pointing the wrong way.

The same investigators describe an earlier uncontrolled 14-participant signal;
it remains an uncontrolled precedent, not randomized confirmation. The 2023
trial had baseline disease/functional differences, unequal background antifibrotic
use and potential learning effects. Non-serious adverse-event reports numbered
65 with D+Q versus 22 with placebo; these are event counts, not patient counts,
and both arms had the same 50% severe-adverse-event patient rate. The severity
split is the decision-relevant part and should be carried with the totals:
**25 of the 65 D+Q reports were graded severe** — precluding normal activity —
against four of 22 on placebo. The Discussion calls this a roughly threefold
increase in non-serious adverse events. No myelosuppression was observed.
A later postoperative fatal event was adjudicated unrelated to study treatment.
The source gives inconsistent intervals for that event, so no exact timeline is
reconstructed here. No drug-related serious event was identified in this small
trial, which does not establish a pediatric senolytic safety window.

**Decision:** fisetin and D+Q remain credible preclinical muscle-function
reserves. The human evidence reviewed here does not provide the randomized
motor-benefit support available for apitegromab in its studied SMA setting,
nor does either route establish niacin's proposed BUBR1 protein-supply mechanism.
All three hypotheses still need relevant MVA models, useful output, renewal and
chromosome-fidelity measurements. Selection must be demonstrated rather than
assumed to preserve a growing child's muscle compartment.
