# Glucosamine plus chondroitin: randomized CRP benefit, unresolved plasma BUB1B signal

**Retain the tested combination as a human anti-inflammatory research reserve. It does not currently displace the provisional BUBR1-restoration or muscle-compensation candidates.** The trial provides an actual administered-compound result in humans: lower circulating CRP. Its BUB1B evidence is an exploratory, unvalidated plasma antibody signal, not demonstrated restoration of working BUBR1 in muscle. The two components were not tested separately, so neither receives the combination's efficacy credit.

## What was administered and measured

[Navarro et al., 2015, PMID 25719429](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0117534) randomized 20 healthy, overweight adults to a blinded crossover comparison. Eighteen, nine men and nine women, contributed to the final analysis; one participant began a diet/exercise program and another attended while developing a cold. Figure 1 and the supplied biomarker workbook corroborate these exclusions. These are post-randomization exclusions, not an intention-to-treat analysis of all 20.

The intervention was **1,500 mg/day FCHG49 glucosamine hydrochloride plus 1,200 mg/day TRH122 sodium chondroitin sulfate**, three capsules daily for 28 days. The matched placebo used crystalline cellulose; a 28-day washout separated periods. The manufacturer supplied the materials, and an independent laboratory confirmed the tested capsule contents. These are reported adult study exposures, not an MVA regimen. The study does not establish equivalence to glucosamine sulfate, each component alone, an arbitrary supplement brand, or food intake.

The adjusted geometric mean CRP was **0.90 versus 1.17 mg/L**, a 23% reduction, with reported **P = 0.048**. IL-6, soluble TNF receptors I/II, urinary PGE-M and F2-isoprostane did not differ significantly. No adverse events were reported during this small, short trial; pediatric and long-term safety were not established. Muscle force, renewal, chromosome segregation, cancer incidence and survival were not trial outcomes. Cancer/mortality associations cited in the introduction are observational background, not randomized benefits demonstrated here.

CRP deserves credit as the intended primary result: both [NCT01682694](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01682694) and Specific Aim 1 in the supplied protocol specify hsCRP. It should not be dismissed as an arbitrary positive selected from six biomarkers. Its modest significance, small sample, exclusions and need for replication remain relevant. The protocol describes a pre/post interaction analysis, while the article reports baseline-adjusted Day-28 GEE models with age, sex and body fat. The supplied workbook contains one baseline row per participant, not a clearly separated baseline for each period. We did not reproduce the original adjusted model or reinterpret its reported P value as independently verified.

## What the BUB1B mention actually means

The exploratory assay tested plasma using an antibody array, not muscle tissue or intracellular checkpoint activity. Table 3 includes BUB1B among genes assigned to the enriched “nucleus” category. That category's result cannot establish an individual BUB1B treatment effect, its direction, or a mechanism of clinical benefit.

The native S2 workbook contains one row labelled **`Plate6_C23_BUB1B_SDI`**, at `SA10+_18pairs_AfterNorm_M!A2134:AK2134`. It has 35 numeric values and one blank, providing 17 complete subject pairs. These are processed antibody signals, not concentrations or fold increases in functional protein. The row has no gene-specific P value. The sheet codes the groups as `case` 0/1 without a named placebo/combination key; we preserve those codes rather than assume a treatment direction. The case-0 mean and SD are approximately 0 and 1, consistent with the paper's stated placebo standardization, but this numerical consistency alone is not a treatment-key validation.

Descriptively, the coded case-1 minus case-0 paired difference is **−0.045 standardized signal units**, with SD 0.524 across the 17 complete pairs. This is not the paper's covariate-adjusted analysis, a significance test, or evidence of a useful increase. The supplied workbook has 3,586 data rows, whereas the final paper describes 2,938 analyzed antibodies; the precise filtering and final analysis are not reconstructed here. BUB1B is absent from the paper's top-100 individual-protein table. None of these facts proves that the compound is inactive on BUBR1; they prevent promotion of a pathway-list membership into a validated rescue claim.

The authors themselves state that antibody specificity was not established for every target and recommend orthogonal validation. A plasma antibody labelled BUB1B could reflect cross-reactivity or extracellular material from cells; it cannot establish the amount, location, integrity or function of intracellular BUBR1. No BUB1B-specific immunoblot, mass-spectrometric confirmation, allele measurement or functional checkpoint assay resolves this in the reviewed study. The original article also mixes sign-based effect-size language with a Table 4 footnote describing values above/below one; we do not convert its standardized effects into protein fold changes.

## Competitive decision

This is useful human evidence for a short-term inflammation biomarker response to the tested supplement combination. It is stronger than a computational drug prediction for that specific endpoint. It has not established relief of an MVA symptom, useful muscle output or BUBR1-dependent rescue. Niacin and apitegromab also remain unproved in MVA; their provisional ranking is not a claim of demonstrated superiority over this combination.

Retain the combination for a relevant inflammatory phenotype or a reproducible mechanistic follow-up. Advancement toward the restoration question requires confirmation of target identity and intracellular BUBR1 function; advancement toward compensation requires a functional outcome. A generic CRP decrease alone does not meet either objective. No clinical harm, treatment recommendation or benefit from either ingredient alone is inferred.

## Read scope and reproducibility

Read the complete main scientific text and tables, the complete supplied protocol, the current registry entry, all nonblank biomarker rows and the selected plasma metadata/BUB1B row. Figure 1 was inspected visually. The CONSORT checklist was retrieved but not read; other extracted images and the remainder of the plasma matrix do not receive manual-reading credit. No biological replication, complete proteomic reanalysis, or independent reproduction of the reported GEE P value was performed. No linked correction/retraction was present in the retrieved Europe PMC record; this is a bounded metadata check, not integrity clearance.

Raw XML, figures, protocol and workbooks remain outside Git. The external `source-check.json` records source hashes and the narrow derived checks. This appraisal adds no outer-corpus reading credit; the source already has a reading record. The combined product receives one assessment rather than two monotherapy assessments.
