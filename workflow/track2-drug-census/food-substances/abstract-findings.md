# FDA literal-name abstract review

**All 67 selected source/ID records were read in full at the saved-abstract level.** Their 73 food-name matches are annotated individually in [abstract-review.json](abstract-review.json). Seventeen batches contained at most four records each; every actual tool output was untruncated and its end marker was observed. The logs contain 111,634 abstract characters. This layer covers only the 73 literal-name/context-unassessed rows, **not the 161 common-name rows**.

No reviewed abstract establishes BUB1B-variant/MVA rescue. Useful evidence includes a direct adverse response in BubR1-low mice, compound-associated BUB1B downregulation, checkpoint activation during anticancer cytotoxicity, an already-appraised fly Sirt2 mechanism, and formulation-specific positive results. Many other hits name derivatives, pathways, reagents, endogenous metabolites or computational associations rather than a treatment with the FDA-listed substance.

This is a separate reading overlay; its raw logs and sources remain external. **Automatic outer-corpus credit = 0; new-curated-appraisal credit = 0; full-text reads in this layer = 0.** These records may overlap earlier project reads. Sixty-seven source records are not necessarily 67 independent studies, because one preprint/journal pair is explicitly linked.

Niacinamide formula and BITC+caffeic-acid results require study-specific controls if pursued for their own indications; neither currently supplies an MVA bridge.

## Retraction, reviews and version identities

The saved metadata marks [PMID 23406204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23406204/), the titanium-dioxide nanoparticle nephrotoxicity study, as **Retracted Publication**, with a retraction link to [PMID 26169674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26169674/). Its claimed findings are recorded only to identify what the retracted abstract says. It receives no supportive causal-evidence credit, including no reliable positive or negative treatment/toxicity inference from this layer. The retraction notice itself was not separately read; no reason for retraction is inferred.

There are 11 reviews by actual abstract reading and 3 records marked PPR/preprint. The astaxanthin carotenoid scoping review (40161411) is treated as a review even though its saved publication-type list says Journal Article. Review/class-level statements do not become compound-specific primary experiments.

The source metadata explicitly links **PPR1114548 ↔ PMID 42211827**. Both were read, and both describe ethyl acetate as a bark-extraction solvent. They are one publication lineage, not independent replications. Their abstracts differ in compound counts and labels: the preprint says 65 compounds and C57 among leads; the journal abstract says 64 and C55. These identifiers were retained rather than reconciled by assumption. The preprint also calls its hub list six genes while listing five; the journal version includes CDK1. Docking/MD does not validate biological binding or efficacy.

PPR154041 and PPR273225 remain explicit preprint records. Their caffeine hits are pathway-enrichment terms, not caffeine interventions. Similarity between the HBV-HCC preprint title and PMID 31392101 does not establish identity: their datasets/counts differ and the saved metadata supplies no explicit link. Saved erratum links for 21305043, 23406204, 36104717 and 40595080 are preserved; notices were not independently reviewed.

Important nulls were retained: no additional total gpt mutant-frequency increase with potassium bromate in Msh2-knockout mice despite altered mutation spectra (34225823); mostly null caffeine/theobromine methylation associations (34669932); no robust dose-dependent hydrazine hprt mutagenicity despite other genotoxic responses (36176055); no impairment of sensitization from nano-ZnO despite challenge-phase immunosuppression (39401296); minimal caffeic-acid-alone cytotoxicity in the BITC combination study (40799801). Sodium lactate partially restores **MESP1**, a cancer-associated expression endpoint, in 40610403; that word “restore” does not imply BUB1B or disease rescue. Formic acid promotes a microbial CRC metabolic mechanism in 41803973 rather than treating MVA.
