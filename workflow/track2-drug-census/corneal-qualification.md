# Glycolysis and STAT3 probes in corneal injury

[Guan et al., Journal of Translational Medicine 2026;24:452](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-07771-5), PMID 41749258, PMC13041143. Assessed 22 September 2026. This primary follow-up adds no abstract-reading credit.

**Retain the positive tissue findings.** Local 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) and Stattic treatment reduced corneal swelling, inflammatory scores and haze after mouse alkali injury. This is more than a gene-signature change. Neither the corneal experiment nor its human keratocyte experiments measured BUB1B/BUBR1. The study therefore supports a context-specific injury-research route, without establishing a restoration mechanism or displacing either provisional MVA nominee. Rotenone is a separate adverse-direction cell probe, not a repair candidate.

## What improved, and what did not get measured

Male C57BL/6 mice, 6–8 weeks old, were randomly assigned. Figure 4 evaluates day 7: OCT thickness has n=3 per group; inflammatory and haze scores have n=6. Three masked ophthalmologists scored eyes, and a masked investigator measured OCT. The three graders do not multiply the number of independent animals.

Both compounds reduced thickness, inflammation and haze compared with injured eyes. The visible means favor 2-DG, but Figure 4 does not display a direct 2-DG-versus-Stattic significance comparison. Different nominal concentrations are not matched exposures or a demonstrated efficacy ranking. Treated eyes do not fully normalize. Histology compares normal, injured and 2-DG-treated eyes, without a matched Stattic histology panel. No vision, barrier function, corneal mechanical strength, long-term durability or quantitatively demonstrated drug-induced epithelial-closure benefit is supplied. Methods describe fluorescein measurements, but this does not create a drug-versus-control closure result in Figure 4.

Figure 4 includes phospho-STAT3/total-STAT3 denominators and densitometry. Both agents lower inflammatory signaling; Stattic's METTL3 and alpha-SMA comparisons are nonsignificant, while 2-DG lowers these markers and H3K18 lactylation. These are pathway-associated findings in injured cornea, not a BUBR1 assay. The generic statistical description uses normality checks, t-tests or ANOVA/Bonferroni; ordinal-score assumptions and independent biological units for every blot remain incompletely described. Methods list different score times from the day-7 result, so the result's displayed time is retained.

## Human cell findings and mechanism limits

Commercial primary human keratocytes, passages 3–5, were stimulated with IL-1beta (5 ng/mL, 48 h in the main downstream experiments). 2-DG reduced lactylation, METTL3 and inflammatory signaling. Rotenone generally shifted the measured protein markers upward relative to its DMSO control; IL6 RNA in Figure 5E is nonsignificant for that comparison. The phospho-STAT3 comparison between IL-1beta alone and normal cells in Figure 5F is also nonsignificant. Do not label every panel an induced-and-rescued signal or substitute a different vehicle comparison.

Under TGF-beta1 (10 ng/mL, 48 h), 2-DG attenuated H3K18 lactylation, METTL3 and alpha-SMA markers. LPS at 200 ng/mL, but not the two lower tested concentrations, increased lactate and H3K18 lactylation after 24 h. These are cell-state markers, not demonstrated tissue repair or muscle output.

LDHA knockdown partly reproduced the drug's cell-marker direction. This strengthens a metabolic interpretation but does not exclude all 2-DG off-target actions or constitute a drug-resistant-target rescue. Promoter-specific H3K18 occupancy, site-specific m6A, transcript-decay/translation mechanisms and pharmacokinetics were not established. The Discussion's genetic corneal-thickness claim is not supported by the displayed cell-only LDHA knockdown experiment. The proposed multistep mechanism should remain a hypothesis rather than a fully demonstrated causal chain.

Supplementary Figure S1 compares IL-1beta alone with IL-1beta plus 5/10/15/20 mM 2-DG after 24 h. The pixels show raw CCK8 OD450, with numerically lower means in all drug groups and nonsignificant comparisons. The caption instead describes percentages and normal controls. A metabolic assay, small n=3 and nonsignificance do not establish equivalent viable-cell counts or absence of toxicity. No separate count/death assay resolves that ambiguity. Other reporting mismatches include IL-1beta in the Figure 5F caption versus IL6 in its image, and STAT1 in the primer table where text/plots describe STAT3.

## The actual BUB1B connection

BUB1B occurs only in pancreatic-cancer background discussion. Its cited references 2 and 41 are, respectively, a dry-eye study and a 2023 review; they do not supply a direct corneal BUBR1 measurement.

A separately located [2024 pancreatic-cancer study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11071201/) reports lower BUB1B RNA and protein after 2-DG in MIA PaCa-2 cells (5 mM, 24 h; Figure 7). Here only its relevant Methods, Results and legend were checked, not the complete paper or images. This is context-specific counter-evidence to assuming protein restoration, not proof that 2-DG worsens MVA. Its other interventions require their own qualification.

## Decision

- **2-DG:** retain measured corneal injury benefits and a research mechanism route. No demonstrated BUBR1 restoration, useful skeletal-muscle output, chromosome fidelity or actual-genotype efficacy. Resolve exposure, safety and direction in the intended cells before it could compete as a restoration lead.
- **Stattic:** retain the mouse tissue benefit independently of 2-DG. The smaller biomarker response and absent direct head-to-head test do not justify an absolute inferiority claim. No MVA mechanism or clinical exposure bridge is supplied.
- **Rotenone:** retain as an adverse-direction experimental control. Its administration is not therapeutic rescue, and no result here qualifies it as a lead.

These findings do not prove niacin or apitegromab effective in MVA. They add evidence in other models while keeping the same unmet standards for the current nominees and challengers.
