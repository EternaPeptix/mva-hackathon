# All-trans retinoic acid: functional barrier induction in a culture model

**PMID37237379 supports a real all-trans retinoic acid (RA) effect on electrical barrier resistance during model development. It does not show repair of an injured barrier or restoration of BUBR1.** The separately assessed **all-trans retinoic acid** object is retained for this narrow functional culture result; retinol and other RA isomers must remain separate. [Primary paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12987-023-00437-0).

The preparation is explicitly Sigma-Aldrich **all-trans retinoic acid**; catalog, purity, solvent and any salt are not specified. Mouse ESC-D3 cells undergo mesoderm induction, then endothelial-like differentiation with bFGF and RA, followed by transwell culture with RA, rat astrocyte-conditioned medium (ACM), hydrocortisone and Y-27632. Results put the RA/bFGF transition on day 6, replating after 2 days and barrier assessment over 11 days postplating. This is a multicomponent differentiation protocol, not treatment of a disease model.

**The dose inconsistency is present in the original PDF.** On publisher PDF page 3, Methods says **10 mM** all-trans RA at the day 6 transition, while Fig1 on that same page says **10 µM**. Results, transwell Methods, Fig3A and supplementary Fig. S4 also specify 10 µM. The native page was visually checked; this is not a Greek-µ extraction error. The initial-stage concentration remains unresolved, while the transwell/+RA comparisons are described as 10 µM. The schematic day 5/day 7 labels also differ from prose day 6/day 8. No clinical dose or exposure bridge is inferred.

Day 8 supplementary Fig. S4 provides six marker comparisons with/without RA (two independent differentiations), supporting increased displayed median fluorescence for GLUT1, CD31, ZO1, occludin, claudin 5 and VE-cadherin, without panel-level statistical tests. This is marker support alongside TEER, not a substitute for function. Native Fig. 2 reports GLUT1-positive 100%, CD31-positive 77%, VE-cadherin 79.4% and claudin 5-positive 82.3%. The authors nevertheless acknowledge **mixed endothelial–epithelial identity**; native S7 shows epithelial/choroid-plexus transcripts. Marker percentages and matrix selection do not establish pure mature brain endothelium. Matrigel tube formation is also not lineage-specific, as the authors note. Independent differentiations from one cell line are not independent donor/genotype validation.

**BUB1B is not an RA-response measurement.** Native Table2, checked in XML and publisher PDF page 16, lists BUB1B separately from BUB1 under the upregulated cell-cycle pathway for **mBECs versus primary mouse BECs**. The footnote describes GSEA C2 Canonical pathways with 1000 permutations; significance belongs to the pathway analysis. The table provides no individual BUB1B abundance, fold change, P/FDR or protein assay. RNAseq uses DESeq2 normalization and averages three replicates; plotted transcript abundances are log2-transformed. The authors associate the cell-cycle program with the differentiated cultures' proliferative state. No +/-RA BUB1B comparison, BUBR1 antibody/primer, stabilization measurement or chromosome-fidelity assay is supplied. The data-availability statement offers data upon request; no raw RNA analysis was performed here.

Additional constraints: Fig3B's native bars favor 750,000 cells/cm² (about 310 Ωcm²) over 1,000,000 (about 210), opposite to the prose claim that the latter is highest. Vehicle control, drug concentration validation, randomization/blinding and RA-specific survival/apoptosis readouts are not reported. Protocol outcomes are conditional on culture cofactors; formal interaction/synergy was not tested. TNFα/IFNγ stimulation was a separate VCAM1-response demonstration, not injury followed by RA repair. Healthy-derived, immortalized and primary comparators evaluate model properties; they do not establish systemic benefit, safety or MVA efficacy.
