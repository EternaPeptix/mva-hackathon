# Apigenin and beta-lapachone: NAD mechanisms do not yet establish BubR1 restoration

23 September 2026. Six targeted discovery queries, followed by one mechanistic primary qualification, found **no qualified direct compound-induced BubR1 restoration or chromosome-fidelity benefit in the returned and inspected sources**. This is a bounded result, not evidence that no such study exists. That source-review task proposed no new entry. Subsequent independent qualification supports an apigenin NAD-preservation reserve and retains beta-lapachone redox counterevidence; a [separate aged-mouse qualification](beta-lapachone-muscle-qualification.md) broadens its functional assessment. This integration records both compounds without changing nominees or adding original-queue reading credit.

The initial clean repository was `97b54464f6ebf8540cbce8d5df382ad652f963d4`, with 233 decisions. Neither compound occurs in those decision objects. Beta-lapachone already has a catalog identity, CHEMBL15192, and NIH DSLD group 6990. ## Beta-lapachone: measured redox perturbation, not protein supply

The decisive source is [Siegel et al., *Redox modulation of NQO1*, PMID29298345](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0190717), published 3 January 2018, PMC5752044.1. It used beta-lapachone purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, **10 µM in cell culture**, with DMSO comparison. Product catalog/purity was not supplied. This does not describe alpha-lapachone, an oxime derivative, lapacho bark or tea.

Native Figure4 and the full Results establish the main distinction: in 16HBE human bronchial epithelial cells, **two hours of beta-lapachone depleted both NADH and NAD+**. Adding 1 µM olaparib, with a 30-minute pretreatment, prevented NAD+ depletion while NADH remained low. Figure4C reports pmol/mg protein, mean ± SD, n=3; no exact P value, confidence interval or definition of independent biological replication is supplied. No numerical effect was reconstructed from the bars. A shift toward an oxidized ratio is not automatically increased NAD+ supply, and the combination cannot be credited to beta-lapachone alone.

Treatment increased NQO1 immunoprecipitation and non-denaturing antibody staining. **The accompanying denaturing NQO1 blot did not establish a protein increase.** Native Supplementary Figure2 includes beta-actin controls in 16HBE, ARPE19 and transformed human bone-marrow endothelial cells. The authors describe no increase over two hours; there is no quantified abundance effect or equivalence test. Later TrHBMEC bands appear weaker, but this is not a statistically established decrease. The readout concerns redox-dependent antibody accessibility, not BubR1 stabilization. The stronger fluorescence in Figure5 at 30/120 minutes has no supplied cell denominator, quantified contrast or inferential test despite the prose using “significant.”

Native Figures8/9 show NQO1, acetylated tubulin and Sirt2 colocalization **in untreated cells**. They do not measure beta-lapachone-induced Sirt2 activity, BubR1 abundance/deacetylation, successful mitotic completion, aneuploidy or faithful daughter-cell output. The proposed local provision of NAD+ by NQO1 remains a mechanistic hypothesis. Recombinant NQO1*2, NQO1 siRNA, reduced nucleotides and dicumarol are separate assay interventions; their effects cannot become beta-lapachone rescue. Protection of recombinant NQO1 against trypsin is not a BubR1 half-life assay.

This source supplies an adverse NAD-pool direction for the proposed supply mechanism at the tested exposure, **not a measured cytotoxicity rate**. It has no animal, muscle, patient, clinical-safety or MVA outcome. ARPE19 appears in figures/results but lacks detailed culture provenance in the inspected Methods. Representative imaging and blots have limited denominators; allocation, masking, inferential tests and multiplicity control were not established. The authors and University of Colorado disclose licensing interests in the A180 antibody. That matters for interpretation but does not invalidate the experiments.

## Apigenin: retain actual positives without substituting SIRT2 for SIRT1

The selected passages of [PMID23172919, *Flavonoid apigenin is an inhibitor of the NAD+ase CD38*](https://doi.org/10.2337/db12-1139) support an actual NAD-sparing intervention. In the mouse experiment, 12 C57BL/6 mice after four weeks of high-fat diet were randomized to **100 mg/kg/day intraperitoneal apigenin or DMSO for seven days**, six per group. Reported benefits include hepatic NAD+, glucose handling and hepatic lipid measures. These are real metabolic endpoints; the experiment did not establish muscle performance or chromosome fidelity in the inspected sections.

The cellular deacetylation evidence uses SIRT1-related substrates and the SIRT1 inhibitor EX527. Apigenin did **not directly activate recombinant SIRT1**, and there was no further NAD+ increase in CD38-knockout fibroblasts. Liver CD38, SIRT1 and Nampt abundance did not significantly change, nor did food intake/body weight. These results do not establish SIRT2 activation or BubR1 supply. The administered named apigenin reagent must remain separate from glycosides, extracts and mixtures. This was a targeted textual check; no native apigenin figures or supplements were reviewed.

Two returned abstracts retain useful positive pointers without becoming qualifications. [PMID33098770](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2020.10.001) reports improved oxidative/developmental and some sex-dependent behavioral outcomes after apigenin in Down-syndrome models, including prenatal/lifelong chow exposure; that is not demonstrated correction of trisomy or segregation fidelity. Its abstract reports no significant birth-defect/pup-death increase, which is not proof of safety. A [2024 porcine-oocyte study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.theriogenology.2024.01.007) reports improved embryo development during postovulatory in-vitro aging with SIRT1 upregulation; dose, denominators, figures and measured chromosome outcomes were not qualified here.

## Competitive consequence and reading boundary

Apigenin is a plausible direct-assay comparator for a NAD-sparing hypothesis, but these sources do not supply the compound-to-SIRT2/BubR1 or functional-fidelity bridge needed to strengthen that nomination. Beta-lapachone provides a caution against treating all NQO1 substrates as NAD-supply interventions. Neither conclusion demands proven MVA efficacy selectively: the current nominees also retain model-transfer uncertainty. No ordering change follows from this bounded evidence set.

The existing repository `nqo1-sirt2-followup.md` was read in full. Its NMN mitotic-completion and dicoumarol impairment findings are different interventions; the underlying Kang PMID30114477 primary was **not newly independently read** in this pass.

For PMID29298345, the complete scientific main text and all nine captions were actually read in untruncated endmarked parts, along with all supplement captions and the extracted antibody table. Native main Figures4/5/8/9 and Supplementary Figure2 were viewed. Other figure pixels, raw data and bibliography were not examined. Apigenin PMID23172919 was limited to its complete abstract and enumerated Methods/Results sections; the other two apigenin sources were abstract-level pointers. Official Europe PMC XML and PLOS assets were available despite ordinary PMC pages returning a browser check. Exact metadata did not list a correction for the two mechanistic sources; this is not an exhaustive notice-history audit. The external reading manifest and [structured qualification](nad-modulator-qualification.json) preserve exact scopes and limitations. Raw sources remain external.


## Independent precision addendum and integration decision

The independent reviewer inspected native apigenin Figures 4–8, with the actual apigenin effects in Figures 5–8, and retained controlled hepatic/cellular NAD responses. Native Figure 6 shows higher hepatic NAD+ and lower CD38 activity (six mice per group, reported P<.05). Figure 7 supports specified fed/fasting glucose and glucose-challenge timepoint contrasts; its AUC panel has no significance annotation or stated inferential test. Figure 8 supports lower triglycerides and higher LCAD/MCAD transcripts, without directly measuring fatty-acid oxidation flux. Cellular EX527 experiments support SIRT1 involvement, not in-vivo mediation or SIRT2 interchangeability. No direct recombinant SIRT1 activation was established.

These actual-compound responses justify a distinct NAD-preservation reserve even though the BUBR1 bridge remains untested. Niacin retains human muscle NAD engagement; apigenin supplies controlled cellular/hepatic evidence. Neither proves comparative MVA benefit. The source-author's narrower no-direct-BUBR1 conclusion remains valid within its reading scope. Additional abstract pointers above are not independently qualified or assigned new efficacy credit.

The 2018 beta-lapachone result is retained as exposure-specific redox counterevidence alongside its separately qualified [2012 motor benefits](beta-lapachone-muscle-qualification.md). Neither the cellular depletion finding nor the dietary motor benefit establishes universal NAD-pool expansion or a clinical regimen. Its exact ASCII beta alias matches CHEMBL15192 and NIH group 6990. Apigenin has no exact main-catalog match; its shared NIH name spans groups 222/6916, with glycoside/extract aliases in the latter. The apigenin entry therefore preserves source aliases while withholding automatic group matching. Identity relations establish neither approval nor product equivalence.

Source-owner, independent and root reading scopes remain separate. The structured qualification retains the original source fields and the independent precision addendum, with external source/check hashes. No raw article or native figure is added to the repository.
