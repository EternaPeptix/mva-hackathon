# Ergothioneine: mouse endurance evidence with different source-access depths

**Retain Fovet 2022 as actual-compound mouse endurance evidence, and retain the later MPST study as an explicitly abstract-level published report pending full-text access.** Fovet's performance finding is more direct than a metabolic marker. Its two-hour molecular measurements do not establish improved functional recovery, satellite-cell renewal, long-term adaptation or general safety. The MPST lineage has a 2025 peer-reviewed publication; the 2024 preprint is not a second independent study.

This bounded source review was completed on 26 September 2026, America/Phoenix (retrievals 27 September UTC). The source reviewer made no repository edits or nominations. Root subsequently admitted a separate ergothioneine appraisal, without original-queue reading credit. Two study lineages are in scope; no other experimental paper was qualified. Source access and unknowns are recorded below rather than treated as efficacy failures.

## Fovet 2022: preparation and controlled functional outcome

[Fovet et al., Frontiers in Physiology 13:834597](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.834597), published 9 February 2022; [PMID 35222093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35222093/), [PMC8864143](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8864143/). The publisher labels this original research; the official PubMed record also carries a Review indexing label, so study design was determined from the primary main rather than that label alone.

The study used five-month-old female mice, nine per group, with the strain printed as “C56B6J.” I have not silently substituted a standard strain name. Tetrahedron supplied what the authors describe as pure ergothioneine. It was delivered in drinking water at a target **70 mg/kg/day for one week**, adjusted for each individually housed mouse's water intake and body weight. This was actual ergothioneine, not a mushroom extract or a genetic intervention. A lot identifier, quantitative purity certificate, independently assayed consumed dose and muscle/plasma pharmacokinetics were not established in the read report. No retail formulation or another manufacturer's product is equated to it.

After four treadmill habituations, mice were allocated to groups matched on baseline maximal aerobic speed (MAS). The main calls the subsequent exhaustion test double-blind, but does not describe a random allocation algorithm, allocation concealment or exactly who was blinded. A baseline endurance test was not reported. MAS was measured before supplementation and was identical at the group-summary level, 26.66±3.32 m/min in each arm; it was not an improved post-treatment endpoint.

After supplementation, animals ran at **70% of their individual pre-supplementation MAS**, following the stated warm-up/ramp. Exhaustion meant inability to resume running after ten seconds. Native Figure 2 confirms time in **minutes**, with nine animals per arm: **71.55±14 with ergothioneine versus 50.4±8.41 with control, reported P<.01**. The difference in reported means is 21.15 minutes. The paper repeatedly prints +41.22%; arithmetic on its displayed means gives `(71.55/50.4−1)×100 = 41.96%`. Retain the printed percentage and this discrepancy explicitly; do not fabricate raw values or replace the author's result silently. No confidence interval or individual-animal data was reconstructed.

This is a controlled, reported functional positive. Its modest sample, one sex, healthy-mouse context, one exposure duration and incompletely described allocation limit generalization, but do not turn it into merely a biomarker observation.

## What the post-exercise samples can and cannot show

Muscles were collected **120 minutes after each animal's exhaustion**, with puromycin injected at 100 minutes. The ergothioneine group had run substantially longer. Equal recovery time after exhaustion does not equal matched exercise duration, work or elapsed time from exercise start. There was no rested/non-exercised arm or matched-work exercise arm. Therefore, between-group molecular differences can reflect the supplement, different exercise histories, or their interaction; they do not isolate an exposure-independent recovery mechanism. This limitation applies to the post-exercise mechanistic interpretation, not by itself to the observed endurance contrast.

Puromycin incorporation rose in gastrocnemius and soleus, as did RPS6 phosphorylation. These support selected early protein-synthesis signals. PAX7, MyoD, myogenin and Spry-1 protein were higher in soleus, with MyoD also higher in gastrocnemius. They are whole-muscle marker measurements: there was no direct counting or lineage tracing of satellite cells, repeated injury/regeneration test, demonstration of asymmetric division, or functional measurement of restored muscle force. A larger quiescent stem-cell pool and maintained renewal are hypotheses, not measured outcomes.

The main specifies **one gastrocnemius and two soleus muscles per mouse** for protein analysis; both solei were used for protein, while RNA was assessed only in gastrocnemius. Those tissue counts are not independent animal counts. The report does not clearly state whether bilateral solei were pooled or averaged, how dependence was handled if analyzed separately, or the analyzed animal denominator for every protein panel. I have not asserted that 18 independent mice were assayed or that pseudoreplication is proven. Figures show bar summaries, not auditable individual-animal allocations.

The following nulls and limits are material:

| Domain | Read result and boundary |
|---|---|
| Body weight and synthesis | Weight was comparable over the week. 4EBP1 phosphorylation and gastrocnemius IGF1/Akt/Mtor/4ebp1/Rps6 transcripts were nonpositive. Puromycin/RPS6 signals do not establish hypertrophy or durable strength. |
| Breakdown and autophagy | Ubiquitinated proteins, MAFbx/MurF1 protein and transcripts, ULK1 ratio, LC3 ratio, p62, Atg7 and Pink1 were comparable. Parkin transcript was lower. Static marker levels do not measure degradation or autophagic flux. |
| Metabolic stress | Gastrocnemius AMPK phosphorylation was lower; soleus was **P=.054**, not a significant positive. Soleus GSK3 phosphorylation was lower, gastrocnemius was not. Redd1 protein/transcript and the reported AMPK/Gsk3 transcripts were comparable. |
| Inflammation | Selected inflammatory transcripts and soleus IL-1β protein were lower. TNF-α protein in both muscles and gastrocnemius IL-1β protein were comparable. |
| Oxidative damage/defenses | Native Figure 9 and Results show **no significant difference** in 4-HNE adducts, oxidized proteins, or p53/p38 phosphorylation ratios. The abstract's statement that oxidative-damage markers decreased is too broad. SOD1/SOD2 protein/transcripts and Gpx1 transcript were comparable; Nrf2 transcript increased. This is not direct NRF2 binding or a target-dependence experiment. |
| Mitochondrial and satellite markers | PGC1α, cytochrome C, COX IV and Pgc1α/Nrf1/Tfam transcripts were comparable; Myf5 and gastrocnemius Pax7/myogenin/Spry-1 protein were nonpositive, and Pax7/MyoD/myogenin transcripts were comparable. Marker abundance does not establish respiration, mitochondrial capacity or post-exercise recovery. |

Statistics used t tests or Mann–Whitney tests, with a paired two-way ANOVA for weight, an alpha of .05 and mean±SD summaries. No multiplicity correction, prospective power calculation or explicit per-panel exclusion accounting was described. Numerous molecular comparisons are nominal. No absence of adverse events, comprehensive toxicity assessment or general “no harm” conclusion follows from unchanged selected markers at one timepoint. The one-week body-weight null is not long-term safety evidence.

Funding was from CNES; Tetrahedron supplied ergothioneine and expertise. Authors declared no commercial/financial conflict. The acknowledgments are retained alongside that declaration. No supplement file was linked in the retrieved JATS or identifiable publisher attachment links; the generic data-availability reference to article/supplementary material does not certify that additional files were read.

## MPST lineage: published status, limited reading depth

The requested [2024 preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.10.588849), PMID38645260/PMC11030429, was followed by [Sprenger et al., Cell Metabolism (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2025.01.024), PMID39965563. Official PubMed links them through UpdateIn/UpdateOf. The journal article is the primary version for this lineage, not an independent replication of the preprint.

The accessible final abstract reports exercise-associated muscle-mitochondrial ergothioneine accumulation, direct MPST binding/activation, increased respiration and mouse exercise-training performance. These reported drug/target claims go beyond an association alone. **The journal and preprint scientific mains, native outcomes and supplements were not obtained or read.** Consequently, dose/formulation, effect size, units, randomization/blinding, replicate denominators, knockout/rescue contrasts, meaningful nulls and safety controls remain **unassessed**, not absent or negative. No detailed result from a third-party excerpt is imported. The final PubMed conflict statement names Aevum Therapeutics interests for B.M.S. and E.T.C., plus Matchpoint interests for E.T.C.

Public ScienceDirect/Cell main/PDF requests were forbidden; the public Elsevier API returned metadata only. The ordinary bioRxiv PDF request was also forbidden. The institutional publication page did not yield a readable full-text link, and an attempted in-app-browser rendering was unavailable. Bounded retrieval stopped without a bypass or treating the abstract as a full-paper review. Accessible evidence is retained under the same standards applied to nominees with access limitations.

The checked Crossref/PubMed notice fields disclose no correction/retraction beyond the MPST publication update. This is a bounded as-of check, not an exhaustive notice guarantee.

The official [FDA GRN734 inventory entry](https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?id=734&set=GRASNotices) names Blue California, specified food uses at 5 mg/serving, closure on 7 May 2018 and a “no questions” letter link. Only the inventory entry was read, not the letter/dossier. It does not identify Tetrahedron's study preparation or establish approved-drug/MVA status. No clinical dose conversion or exhaustive product-availability claim is supplied.

EGT deserves consideration as a compound with direct mouse endurance evidence and a later, published target/function report whose detailed appraisal is access-limited. There is no head-to-head comparison with niacin or apitegromab here, no comparative ranking, and no MVA-genotype, BUBR1 or chromosome-fidelity result established by this review. These are limits shared with the appropriate comparator evidence, not an extra MVA-only threshold imposed on EGT. Source qualification does not itself create a nomination or clinical recommendation.
