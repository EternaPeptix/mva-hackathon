# BUB1B/BUBR1 intervention literature census

Research review dated **21 September 2026**. This is an evidence-search record, not a treatment recommendation. It complements the [existing route decisions](../../reports/appendices/track2-route-decisions.md); it does not itself change the current proposed medication pair. No drug experiment was performed.

## Finding and decision boundary

The broader search found **additional lines of investigation**, including a direct AZD1152 experiment in BubR1-hypomorphic mice, genetic sFRP3/FRZB and PAK2 rescue, decitabine-mediated restoration of BUBR1 abundance in non-MVA cells, and pharmacological BUBR1 rescue in the different causal context of progerin accumulation. It also identified a myostatin route for an independent downstream medication comparison. No reviewed medication, including niacin or everolimus, has demonstrated **actual-BUB1B-allele, chromosome-fidelity and muscle-function rescue**. That common gap does not establish the absence of competitive hypotheses and cannot be used to reject challengers while exempting the elected pair. Niacin remains an indirect NAD/SIRT2 hypothesis; everolimus has an unestablished disease-specific signaling premise. The comparison must weigh the same evidence dimensions for every candidate: disease-mechanism relevance, actual-drug functional evidence, tissue/population transfer, feasible exposure and material harms.

AZD1152 has more direct same-gene **drug-treated organismal survival evidence** than niacin. Its primary report is a conference abstract, however, and explicitly reports no improvement in the other age-related phenotypes that it monitored, including sarcopenia. Therefore it deserves a named mechanistic reserve, rather than being erased from the comparison or promoted on lifespan alone. sFRP3/FRZB is a substantial same-gene **genetic** developmental/neural rescue opportunity. PAK2 is a same-gene **genetic** survival/senescence opportunity with explicitly unchanged physical function. Neither is an established pharmacological implementation.

The defensible closing statement is: **the search identified new competitive research routes but no validated medication rescue of BUB1B-MVA muscle**. A universal statement that no better hypothesis exists, or that every possible mechanism has been exhausted, would exceed the coverage. The [apitegromab comparison](apitegromab-review.md) specifically examines whether a candidate with stronger human muscle-function evidence but a weaker gene-specific bridge should outrank either elected medication. Catalog matches and genetic target opportunities remain hypotheses until qualified experimentally.

## New or materially sharpened opportunities

### AZD1152 / barasertib: direct same-gene pharmacology, limited muscle relevance

Hurley, Yamada, Terzic and van Deursen reported **“Effect of AZD1152 on BubR1 hypomorphism”**, ASCPT 2013 late-breaking abstract LBII-2. Seventy BubR1-H/H mice per group received AZD1152 or vehicle; the stated regimen was 6 micrograms/g intraperitoneally twice weekly starting at 5–7 days of age. Median survival was 136 versus 118 days, reported as +15%, p=0.04. Growth, cataracts and sarcopenia were monitored; the abstract reports no improvement in the other age-related phenotypes. Preliminary echocardiography involved 10 vehicle and 11 treated mice and suggested improved cardiac parameters. These are **author-reported conference results**, without a complete treatment paper, raw data, precise cardiac effect estimates or independent replication located here. [Official ASCPT program, abstract LBII-2](https://www.ascpt.org/Portals/28/docs/Annual%20Meetings/Annual%20Meeting%20Archive/2013%20Final%20Program.pdf?ver=2018-01-19-101927-487).

Decision: retain as a **named Aurora-B compensation research route**, including non-muscle cardiac biology. It does not displace the muscle-focused lead on this evidence. Lack of current approved-medication eligibility is a separate implementation question, not the reason to dismiss the biology. No dose or exposure bridge to MVA patients is inferred.

### Aurora-B balance, proteasome rescue and acetylation: function is more specific than abundance

The 2013 PP2A study used **ZM447439, 2 micromolar for one hour**, to rescue alignment defects in engineered BUBR1-B56-binding-defective HeLa cells. Its MVA-patient-cell rescue was engineered BUBR1 or kinetochore-targeted PP2A recruitment, not a demonstrated drug rescue in those patient lines. **BI2536** did not rescue the relevant B56-depletion K-fiber defect in that experiment. This supports compensation for a phosphorylation imbalance, not restoration of every checkpoint function. [Xu et al., 2013, PMID 23789096](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3654266/).

A 2026 study sharpens the competing functions. **Hesperadin** and **ZM447439** reduced BUBR1-K250 acetylation and maintenance of the mitotic checkpoint complex (MCC) in nocodazole-arrested HeLa cells. **MG132** restored MCC levels with engineered BUBR1-S16A/S39A variants, but did not restore the defective kinetochore intensity/volume of K250R. **Trichostatin A (TSA)** increased the expanded kinetochore-corona markers in a nocodazole condition. These are useful biochemical/localization endpoints with artificial variants, not rescued division fidelity or MVA muscle. [Choi et al., 2026, PMID 41533580, Figures 2–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12802887/).

Decision: broaden the functional control panel for Aurora-B/proteostasis experiments. Do not describe all proteasome evidence as abundance-only, but do not promote MCC abundance to successful chromosome segregation. An attachment-improving Aurora-B regimen could simultaneously weaken surveillance; measure both independently. Neither TSA nor a clinically used HDAC inhibitor can inherit this narrowly timed experimental effect as a disease rescue.

### sFRP3 / FRZB / Wnt: substantial same-gene developmental genetic rescue

Genetic deletion of one or both copies of **sfrp3/FRZB** in BubR1-H/H mice improved body/brain size and corpus-callosum myelination. Local shRNA suppression increased dentate-gyrus neural-progenitor proliferation. A subsequent genetic study reduced glial reactivity. These are actual same-gene-model rescue findings, but neither publication administers a candidate small-molecule sFRP3 inhibitor, measures muscle force/renewal, or establishes corrected chromosome fidelity. [Cho et al., 2019, PMID 30609266](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6413750/), [Corujo-Ramirez et al., 2020, PMID 33271003](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7731880/).

Decision: retain a **new causal-qualification route**, especially for developmental/neural outcomes. A target-specific pharmacological implementation remains missing. **Lithium/GSK3 inhibition, generic Wnt activation and SFRP1 inhibition are not FRZB inhibition** and cannot inherit these results. Tissue and developmental timing matter: a separate 2025 cardiac BubR1-knockout study found sustained Wnt-pathway activation, illustrating why global Wnt activation cannot be inferred from the neural knockout result. [BubR1 heart-development study, PMID 40055864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12132738/).

### PAK2: same-gene genetic survival benefit with a physical-function null

Crossing a **Pak2 hypomorphic allele** into BubR1-H/H mice extended median lifespan to 159 days, approximately 30%, with 27 versus 28 animals in the survival cohorts. Cataract onset and adipose measures improved. The authors explicitly report that dwarfism, infertility and **physical function remained unchanged**. No PAK2 inhibitor produced these mouse outcomes; this was genetic reduction. ABT-263/ABT-737 appear in its discussion as earlier senolytic work, not as drugs administered to these BubR1 cohorts. [Lee et al., 2019, PMID 31209047, Figure 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613284/).

Decision: retain a PAK2/senescence mechanism reserve, with the physical-function result reducing priority for the current muscle objective. PAK-family drugs need PAK2-specific target/exposure qualification; PAK4 activity is not interchangeable. The genetic result does not show that an adult drug regimen can reproduce developmental gene reduction.

### Decitabine: a distinct transcription-restoration hypothesis, not MVA rescue

**5-Aza-2'-deoxycytidine (decitabine)** increased BUBR1 in four of five tested cell lines in a promoter-methylation study; the reported experiment used 4 micromolar for 48 hours. A subsequent study found BUBR1 abundance restoration and promoter demethylation in late-passage adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells, with little change in early-passage cells. Neither tested a disease-causing BUB1B allele or demonstrated drug-mediated chromosome fidelity or muscle rescue. [Park et al., 2007, PMID 17464181](https://doi.org/10.1038/emm.2007.22), [Lee et al., 2009, PMID 19745606](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2802683/).

A useful counterexample is AML: the Schnerch study found only a mild BUBR1-expression increase with decitabine and no appreciable TSA-mediated protein restoration in its cell context. Its functional rescue relied on BUBR1 re-expression, not on assigning that effect to either drug. [Schnerch et al., 2013, PMID 23812934](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3856964/), [primary supplementary Figure S5](https://www.haematologica.org/article/download/6864/39785).

Decision: a conditional **endogenous-transcription reserve** only if relevant BUB1B promoter repression is actually demonstrated. It is not a solution to an unstable/inactive variant merely because it can raise a wild-type protein elsewhere. Productive renewal and genetic-fidelity costs of global epigenetic intervention require direct measurement; no absent-data efficacy is imputed.

### FTI-277 / lonafarnib context and UPCP: BUBR1 rescue in a different disease mechanism

In **LMNA/progerin-driven HGPS**, the experimental unique-progerin-C-terminal peptide **UPCP** countered progerin-related BUBR1 sequestration/downregulation and improved cellular and mouse phenotypes. **FTI-277** was a cell comparator that increased BUBR1, CyclinA2 and Ki67 and reduced senescence staining, but did not block the progerin–BUBR1 interaction. The same paper discusses approved lonafarnib as HGPS background; the comparator experiment was **FTI-277**, not lonafarnib. There is no demonstrated progerin dependency or UPCP/FTI rescue of BUB1B-mutant MVA. [Zhang et al., 2023, PMID 37118121, Extended Data Figure 10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10154249/).

Decision: valuable proof that a **specific BUBR1-interfering interaction** can be pharmacologically addressed, but a low-priority MVA repurposing route until the same lesion is established. Broader farnesyltransferase inhibition also perturbs mitosis: FTI-277 impaired Spindly kinetochore recruitment and chromosome alignment in another primary study. That is contextual counterevidence, not an MVA trial. [Moudgil et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4436830/).

### Myostatin / apitegromab: a downstream comparator with no identified same-gene rescue

The myostatin route was identified in the parallel medication-eligibility review and warrants its own downstream comparison. This gene-specific literature check found no pharmacological or genetic myostatin/activin blockade experiment demonstrating BUBR1-deficient muscle rescue, and no evidence establishing excess myostatin or SMAD2/3 signaling as the causal lesion in MVA muscle. This is a search finding, not proof that the route cannot work.

The BubR1-depleted C2C12 model has impaired myotube formation, reduced MyoD/myogenin and increased Atrogin-1/MuRF-1. Its citation of an older myostatin paper does not demonstrate a myostatin intervention or establish that shared downstream markers have the same upstream cause. Similarly, the BubR1-+/GTTA mouse paper cites a myostatin-inhibition study in the context of treadmill methods; it does not administer a myostatin inhibitor. The frequently retrieved TGF-beta/SMAD2/3 checkpoint-kinase paper studies **BUB1**, a different gene. [Nozaki et al., PMID 26464273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5023544/), [Wijshake et al., PMID 23300461](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3531486/), [Nyati et al., PMID 25564677](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4440544/).

The rhabdomyosarcoma literature does **not** support a blanket claim that myostatin inhibition necessarily promotes this tumor type. Ricaud and colleagues found that blocking anti-myostatin antibodies promoted terminal differentiation of RD and Rh30 cells; antisense-myostatin or follistatin constructs also enhanced RD differentiation. Conversely, Langley and colleagues found that added recombinant myostatin suppressed RD proliferation. In a 2024 study, myostatin addback increased proliferation and reversed enhanced differentiation after BRD4-L depletion, while modestly decreasing proliferation in the control and BRD4-S-depleted conditions. These are context-dependent tumor-cell experiments, not apitegromab studies or MVA cancer-safety outcomes. [Ricaud et al., 2003, PMID 14614446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614446/), [Langley et al., 2004, PMID 14724580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14724580/), [2024 BRD4 study, Figure 4H–J](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897194/).

Decision: retain as a **real downstream muscle comparator**, subject to its separate clinical, exposure and eligibility appraisal. Its case need not depend on increasing BUBR1 abundance. Nevertheless, neither a muscle-growth effect elsewhere nor a shared MyoD marker establishes preserved muscle renewal, chromosome fidelity or benefit in BUB1B-MVA. Generic follistatin, activin-receptor blockade, antibodies against mature myostatin and precursor-selective apitegromab are distinct interventions; they cannot inherit one another's efficacy or tumor findings. The oncology evidence defines uncertainty to test, not a demonstrated contraindication or demonstrated safety in MVA.

## What could change prioritization

1. **A relevant functional drug result**, rather than catalog membership: actual endogenous MVA alleles or a defensible BUB1B-deficient model, greater absolute muscle output, preserved renewable progenitors and independently measured chromosome fidelity.
2. **A selective implementation of the new genetic opportunities**: FRZB/sFRP3 and PAK2 perturbations with dose, timing and tissue-specific effects established. Neural/developmental or cardiac benefits should be reported in their own right rather than relabeled muscle rescue.
3. **A better-defined Aurora-B window**: replicate the AZD1152 organismal observation, establish exposure and mechanism, and separate attachment correction from weakened checkpoint maintenance. The reported sarcopenia null remains material.
4. **A demonstrated endogenous transcription or interaction lesion**: BUB1B promoter repression for a decitabine-like hypothesis, or a causal BUBR1-sequestering interaction for an interaction-disrupting approach. Neither lesion is inferred merely from an abnormal BUBR1 quantity.

This search strengthens the route map and reveals missed direct pharmacology, but does not turn absence of a published rescue into a negative experiment. Non-indexed studies, unavailable supplements, secondary full-text mentions and newly published work remain coverage limits. No candidate discovered here has demonstrated rescue of **Leu737Ter/Asn1002Lys**, and none resolves the existing phase/genetic-causality stop.
