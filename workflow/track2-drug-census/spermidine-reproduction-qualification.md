# Spermidine: aged-mouse reproductive function and meiotic chromosome fidelity

**Bounded result:** PMID 37845508 supplies real treatment-associated improvements in nonmalignant aged-mouse reproductive function, including larger litters and lower metaphase-II (MII) oocyte aneuploidy. It is a useful preclinical reproductive-function and chromosome-fidelity comparator. It does **not** establish BubR1 protein restoration, BUB1B-mediated rescue, MVA treatment, human reproductive benefit or superiority to another candidate. The transcriptome supplement directly measures **Bub1b RNA**, but its treatment contrast is downward and fails the supplied Q-value threshold.

**Access boundary:** this is a partial primary qualification, not a full-paper reading. The publisher supplies a subscription preview; its main-PDF request returned HTML. All eight main-figure captions, all nine Extended Data captions, the complete scientific Supplementary Information, all six reporting-summary pages, selected native figures and targeted official source-data cells were actually read. Full main narrative and Methods remain unavailable. [Published source, Zhang et al., Nature Aging 3, 1372–1386 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s43587-023-00498-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00498-8).

## Intervention and model

The reporting summary identifies Sigma-Aldrich spermidine but does not supply a catalog number, salt or purity. Do not silently identify the administered material as free base or trihydrochloride, a food extract or a retail supplement. Young controls were 6–8-week and aged animals 52–56-week **ICR female mice**. These were reproductive-aging models, without an identified BUB1B mutation or tumor model.

## Bub1b RNA is measured, but it does not show restored protein supply

The official transcriptome workbook `43587_2023_498_MOESM4_ESM.xlsx`, sheet **Transcriptome data**, row **2332**, identifies mouse GeneID **12236**, symbol **Bub1b**, type **mRNA**. Young/aged/spermidine groups have **3/3/4 labeled samples**. The treatment contrast is source **log₂(spermidine/aged)=−0.5400807590**, equivalent to **0.6877-fold**; nominal **P=.0064987051**, source **Q=.1012831891**. The aged/young contrast is log₂=.6876212234, P=.0001519861, Q=.0030597117. Thus the supplied normalized RNA is higher in aged than young oocytes and decreases with treatment; the drug contrast does not meet Q≤.05 (or .10).

The workbook supplies TPM, FPKM and read counts, not BubR1 protein. No targeted Bub1b RT-PCR primer, BubR1 antibody, BubR1 abundance/localization/activity endpoint, BUB1B genetic intervention or causal BubR1 rescue was identified in the accessed figures, reporting summary or scientific supplement. This is an explicitly bounded assay-absence statement because the full main Methods were unavailable. Do not infer per-oocyte absolute transcript abundance or protein change from normalized RNA.

GEO **GSE239551** and **GSM7667174–GSM7667183** corroborate mouse oocyte sample labels (young 1–3, aged 1–3, spermidine 1–4). ## Mechanism, nulls and experimental limits

- **Pharmacology versus genetics:** LC3/lysosome/mitochondrial colocalization, membrane-potential probes and selected autophagy/mitophagy transcripts support the proposed mitochondrial-quality mechanism. **Mdivi-1 is a separate administered inhibitor**, not a genetic rescue. In Extended Data 8, adding it reduces the spermidine-associated PB1-extrusion benefit (49.77% → 29.44%, source P=.0041) and LC3 signal. The combination's exact Mdivi-1 concentration is not supplied in its accessible caption. This pharmacological attenuation does not establish an exclusive mitophagy mechanism or BUB1B mediation. [Mechanistic main figures](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00498-8/figures/8), [inhibitor experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-023-00498-8/figures/16).
- **Ovarian reserve is not restored:** primordial, primary and secondary follicle counts are null (P=.6912, .9060, .7925); only pre-antral/antral counts and TUNEL-positive follicle proportion improve in these panels. Treated values remain far from young reference values across several functional outcomes.
- **Dose dependence matters:** IP 25 mg/kg has no PB1 benefit (P=.9842), whereas 50/100 mg/kg do. In-vitro mouse 100 µM lacks a significant PB1 benefit (P=.4078), unlike 25/50 µM. In the porcine stress arm, 50 µM is null (P=.6526), while 100 µM improves PB1 extrusion (52.48% → 69.28%, P=.0017). Porcine chromosome counts, fertilization and births were not shown in the accessed panels. The high-dose Mdivi-1 maturation impairment belongs to that inhibitor, not to spermidine.
- **Animal/donor clustering and bias:** the reporting summary states no randomization, no blinded data collection/statistical analysis, no statistical sample-size calculation, no excluded data and at least three independent repeats. Captions use two-sided unpaired t tests across many endpoints; multiplicity adjustment for these phenotypes is not reported in the accessed assets. Source percentages commonly have 3–7 replicate entries while captions count many oocytes; animal/donor and experimental-batch mapping is absent. Do not treat the oocyte totals as independent animals. Fig. 2b gives 60/36/36 mice but only three source concentration values/group; pooling is unresolved.
- **Harms boundary:** fragmentation, apoptosis, oxidative stress and DNA-damage markers were examined, generally in the favorable direction. The accessed material does not establish systematic maternal/offspring toxicity, long-term offspring chromosome status, reproductive lifespan or safety at human supplement doses. A non-significant high-dose benefit is not a demonstrated toxic effect.

The public publisher back matter reports Chinese national/university grant support and no declared competing interests. The bounded publisher/Crossref notice check found no correction/retraction notice or update relation; it is not a universal integrity guarantee.

## Place among provisional candidates

Spermidine adds **actual preclinical normal reproductive function and meiotic aneuploidy improvement**. That is a different evidence type from the project's provisional **niacin/BubR1-supply hypothesis** and **apitegromab/SMA functional compensation** context; those sources were not newly appraised here. The result merits retention as a specific reproductive-aging research comparator, with mitochondrial/autophagy compensation a mechanistic interpretation. It does not rank above those candidates, establish a polyamine-class benefit, or justify substituting food or retail formulations for the studied exposures. This integration adds one spermidine assessment, with no nomination change, full-primary-reading or original-queue credit.
