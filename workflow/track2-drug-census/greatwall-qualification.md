# Okadaic acid restores entry timing after Greatwall depletion

Burgess et al., PNAS 2010;107:12564–12569. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0914191107), PMID 20538976, PMC2906566. Assessed 22 September 2026.

At 500 nM, okadaic acid shortened the delay before mitotic entry in Greatwall/MASTL-depleted HeLa cells. This is a genuine chemical rescue of the measured timing defect. It does not establish accurate chromosome segregation, healthy daughter cells or restoration of BUBR1. The existing assessment separately retains the 20 nM partial completed-mitosis benefit under CDK1 inhibition; this new experiment neither replaces nor downgrades that distinct positive.

## What was actually compared

| Feature | Source finding |
|---|---|
| Model | HeLa cells with human Greatwall/MASTL siRNA; EB3-GFP/H2B-CherryFP imaging. This is not a BUB1B-mutant or patient-derived MVA model. |
| Chemical intervention | Calbiochem okadaic acid, **500 nM**, added six hours after release from a thymidine G1/S block. Cells received siRNA 24 h before synchronization. The study does not identify the drug catalog, chemical form, lot or stock vehicle. |
| Figure 3H controls | Scrambled siRNA, scrambled plus OA, Greatwall siRNA, Greatwall plus OA; imaging at ten-minute intervals. The plotted endpoint is time from block release to mitotic entry. |
| Entry timing, mean | Scrambled **10 h 20 min**; scrambled + OA **9 h 35 min**; Greatwall **12 h 12 min**; Greatwall + OA **9 h 45 min**. |
| Numbers and tests | Respectively **93, 96, 83, 90 cells**. Figure brackets report P=.0139 for scrambled versus scrambled+OA, P=.0001 for scrambled versus Greatwall, and P=.0001 for Greatwall versus Greatwall+OA. Independent culture/experiment counts and handling of cells that never entered are not fully specified. |
| Immediate interpretation | OA advances entry in control cells too. The Greatwall-delay result supports rescue of entry timing; it is not a demonstrated equivalence between the two OA-treated groups or a percentage of rescued healthy cells. |

The dose is specific to this experiment. It must not be conflated with the earlier **20 nM OA under partial RO3306/CDK1 inhibition**, reviewed in [clearance and fidelity](clearance-and-fidelity.md), or with other localization experiments.

## Separate genetic and BUBR1 observations

Figure 3G co-depletes PP2A catalytic subunits and Greatwall. The reported normalized CDK-substrate phosphorylation is about **20% for Greatwall depletion relative to scrambled control**, and **64% for double depletion relative to PP2AC depletion alone**. The denominator differs. These are separate biochemical comparisons, not a common-baseline 20-to-64% drug response or a functional recovery probability. PP2AC-only depletion also increased cell death by approximately 15% in the authors' description; that is a genetic-perturbation observation, not an OA toxicity measurement.

Figure 2B examines BUBR1 and Aurora B kinetochore localization after Greatwall depletion. It does not measure OA-mediated BUBR1 abundance, half-life or rescue. The mitotic abnormalities, slippage and death in Figure 3A–C characterize knockdown conditions without an OA rescue arm. They cannot be assigned to the drug-treated groups in Figure 3H. Figure 4 is the authors' proposed signaling model, not a measurement of every pathway edge.

## Competitive assessment

**Retain OA as a defect-specific research comparator.** Its measured positive is chemically reversible entry timing in this MASTL-depletion setting; the earlier completed-mitosis result remains separate evidence. Neither paper establishes sustained faithful descendants, Leu737Ter/Asn1002Lys rescue, muscle function or a clinically usable exposure window. The relevant next question is whether faster entry produces better daughter-cell fidelity and productive output, not whether cells merely leave G2.

The current [Calbiochem 495604 manufacturer listing](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/product/mm/495604) identifies free-acid OA, CAS 78111-17-8, as a research phosphatase inhibitor. The paper does not establish that this was its product. The listing reports activity against both PP1 and PP2A in biochemical assays; **selectivity for a particular intracellular PP2A holoenzyme at 500 nM is not demonstrated here**. Supplier availability is not a therapeutic formulation or clinical safety result. No clinical efficacy rank against niacin/apitegromab follows, and neither provisional nominee has established MVA efficacy.

This is an additive qualification of the existing OA decision. No new compound identity, separate medicine count or source-reading credit is added.
