# L-carnitine / levocarnitine: a bounded exercise-endurance reserve

23 September 2026, Arizona (retrievals occurred on 24 September UTC). Integrated qualification of two selected original studies. The 227-entry baseline had no carnitine appraisal; this update adds one while preserving all previous objects.

**Recommend a distinct, qualified L-carnitine functional reserve.** A small controlled mitochondrial-myopathy study reports an actual exercise-endurance benefit, with muscle-strength and peak-exercise nulls. The selected DMD study reports no functional benefit, but only its complete original abstract was accessible here. Neither result establishes BUB1B restoration or MVA efficacy, and neither justifies declaring the molecule universally effective or ineffective.

## Controlled endurance positive in mitochondrial myopathy

[PMID25714882, PMC4418367, DOI10.1590/1414-431X20143467](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4418367/) was read in full scientific text, including all six native table images and its flowchart. Twelve biopsy-confirmed patients with chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia (CPEO) underwent a randomized, double-blind crossover: six began with **L-carnitine 3 g/day** and six with **3 g/day placebo**, for eight weeks, followed by a four-week washout and the opposite eight-week treatment. Identical-looking capsules came from DrogaDerma, Brazil, taken orally once daily with breakfast or lunch. Placebo composition, salt, purity, catalog number and batch certificate are not specified. The authors report full adherence and no withdrawals, assessed through contacts and returned bottles, without a biochemical adherence measure.

Patients had exercise intolerance and mild weakness; severe weakness, renal/cardiac/pulmonary dysfunction, other ergogenic supplements and recent regular physical activity were excluded. Eligibility was age >14; the mean was in the mid-thirties, but the abstract and Table1 give different mean/SD values. An actual pediatric age range is not established. Ten healthy reference participants received neither treatment nor the constant-work endurance test.

| Actual comparison | L-carnitine versus placebo | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Constant-work cycling time to exhaustion | **14±1.9 versus 11±1.4 minutes**, marked P<.05 | Genuine task endurance, at a workload specified as 70% of peak. No everyday-mobility or long-term benefit measured. |
| Oxygen uptake at six-minute isotime / exhaustion | 1151±115 versus1049±104 / 1223±114 versus1060±108 mL/min, marked P<.05 | Physiological responses accompanying endurance, not direct proof of repaired mitochondria. |
| Inspiratory capacity | **2.45±.23 versus2.26±.22 L**, marked P<.05 | Preserve this pulmonary-volume positive; it is not respiratory muscle force or fewer clinical respiratory events. |
| Peak incremental exercise, peripheral torque/work and body composition | No significant treatment differences reported | Do not call peak aerobic capacity, strength or hypertrophy restored. |

The gas-exchange ratio was lower at isotime (1.03 versus1.11), not marked significant at exhaustion (where the printed mean is higher). Oxygen pulse increased at exhaustion, but it is not direct cardiac-rescue evidence. Values are printed as mean±SD; exact P values and confidence intervals are not supplied.

There is a consequential reporting inconsistency: **Methods describes a repeated-measures crossover model accounting for treatment/carryover, while native Tables4–6 label their tests unpaired**. Sequence, period and carryover estimates are not reported. The protocol specifies 70% of peak workload but does not establish identical absolute constant-work loads across visits. No adequate washout or definitive adjusted contrast can be independently verified from these summaries. Prospective endpoint hierarchy, multiplicity correction and a sample-size justification were not established; the authors' power calculation for endurance was post hoc. A Discussion sentence claims body-composition improvement, contrary to Results and Table4; this assessment retains the latter null.

Crucially, **neither plasma nor muscle carnitine was measured, and genotypes were not analyzed**. The experiment therefore does not prove correction of carnitine deficiency, improved muscle carnitine retention, or a particular metabolic mediator. The measured context is biopsy-confirmed myopathy and exercise impairment, the measured treatment effect is selected endurance/physiology, and the BUB1B/MVA defect remains untested. Side effects were monitored, but no detailed adverse-event summary or renal-safety analysis is supplied.

## DMD counterevidence at abstract depth

Escobar-Cedillo et al., *L-carnitine supplementation in Duchenne muscular dystrophy steroid-naïve patients: a pilot study* (2013), is documented in the [UNAM institutional record](https://produccion.siia.unam.mx/Publicaciones/ProdCientif/PublicacionFrw.aspx?id=260670&scopus=0) and an [author-uploaded ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257927285_L-carnitine_supplementation_in_duchenne_muscular_dystrophy_steroid-naive_patients_A_pilot_study). The complete institutional abstract was read with an endmarker. It describes **20 patients assigned** to L-carnitine or placebo for one year, **50 mg/kg twice daily**, with four-monthly evaluations and ANOVA. Upper/lower-extremity clinical function showed no between-group benefit (P>.05); tolerability and no significant side effects were reported.

At this depth, randomization, group sizes, actual ages, route, scale definitions, effect sizes, attrition, placebo formulation and confidence intervals remain unverified. Do not import review-derived 10/10 allocations or characterize the null as an equivalence result. Background statements about carnitine deficiency are not proof that these participants' deficiency was measured or corrected.

The public author-PDF was indexed as six pages, but direct retrieval returned403; initial screenshots timed out and later screenshot responses yielded no inspectable page content. **No DMD full-body, figure/table or supplement reading is claimed, and no native PDF hash exists.** Institutional pagination is97–101 versus97–102 in the author-upload record. No PMID/DOI was verified in this bounded search.

## Identity, gene relevance and decision

The official [PubChem levocarnitine synonym record](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/rest/pug/compound/name/levocarnitine/synonyms/JSON) places L-carnitine and levocarnitine under CID10917; the [ChEMBL1149 record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/api/data/molecule/CHEMBL1149.json) specifies levocarnitine. Thus those two names can support one molecule appraisal, while the trial capsule's exact salt/product remains unresolved. Generic **carnitine** collides with two current catalog entities and is not proposed as an alias. Acetyl-/propionyl-carnitine, acylcarnitine associations, tartrate preparations and combinations do not inherit these outcomes. Catalog and NIH names do not establish current availability or equivalent product efficacy.

A focused carnitine/BUB1B/BUBR1 search returned86 co-occurrence records, with no literal carnitine in their returned titles/abstracts; the saved7,362-record canonical corpus also had none. This was a lexical check, not86 source readings or a worldwide absence proof. No administered-compound BUB1B result was identified in the examined material. Reproductive spindle and combination leads are retained separately as unqualified follow-ups, not evidence of L-carnitine-mediated aneuploidy rescue.

Against the provisional nominees, this actual controlled endurance finding deserves more weight than an endogenous association or marker-only claim. It does not presently displace niacin's distinct NAD/restoration hypothesis or apitegromab's larger disease-specific motor-function comparison. Creatine has separate selected DMD-strength positives, also with functional nulls; there is no head-to-head ranking. The lack of MVA proof applies equally to all. **Retain L-carnitine as a limited human endurance comparator, not a BUBR1-restoration lead or established general muscle-strength intervention.** The source reviewer made no repository changes. Integration adds one molecule appraisal, without original-queue or independent-study credit; no administration recommendation follows. Fresh exact-source metadata/searches identified no correction or retraction notice, with limited coverage for the DMD record; this is not an exhaustive guarantee.

Neither a healthy reference group nor a generic carnitine alias supplies the missing treatment, genotype or preparation evidence.
