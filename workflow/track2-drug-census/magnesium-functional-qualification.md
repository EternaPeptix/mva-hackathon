# Oral magnesium preparations: bounded functional qualification

Source review completed 23 September 2026. This integration adds three preparation-specific comparisons without changing nominee order or original-queue reading counts. Four discovery queries selected three actual-intervention primaries. Aquamin Soluble and ascorbate-2-phosphate magnesium salt remain separate existing entries.

**Conclusion:** retain the older-women magnesium-oxide mobility signal and the Magnosolv CAD exercise-tolerance signal as preparation-specific functional comparators. Neither establishes muscle-force restoration, and the CAD product also supplies potassium. Retain the ReMag magnesium-chloride trial's adverse performance direction and important nulls. These results do not establish a general magnesium benefit or harm, a BUBR1 mechanism, or comparative MVA superiority. Their functional evidence deserves the same consideration as that of provisional nominees without selectively demanding MVA proof.

## Magnesium oxide: older women, mobility positive and strength null; partial-source qualification

PMID25008857, DOI10.3945/ajcn.113.080168, NCT01971424. The complete official abstract and complete current registry were read. The publisher's indexed conclusion was also encountered. Publisher HTML/PDF and institutional-repository access failed; the indexed CiteSeer PDF returned404. No complete-main, native-figure/table, full adverse-event or supplement reading is claimed. Four linked comment/author-reply records are identified in the metadata, not classified as corrections; their text was not read. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25008857/), [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01971424).

The trial randomized **139 healthy older women**, mean age 71.5±5.2 years, already attending a mild weekly fitness program:62 assigned supplementation and77 control. Analysis included 53 versus 71, **124 total**. The article specifies **300 mg magnesium/day in magnesium-oxide form for 12 weeks**; the current registry instead abbreviates this as “300 mg of daily oral magnesium oxide.” Do not silently treat the salt mass and magnesium-equivalent mass as interchangeable. Brand, excipients and full received-dose data remain unverified.

This was **not placebo-controlled**. The abstract states no placebo/intervention for controls and researcher masking; participants could know assignment. The registry labels masking NONE and describes healthy-diet education for controls, with healthy diet also listed alongside the active intervention. Preserve the shared fitness/diet context; this is neither exercise-free monotherapy nor proof of an exercise-by-magnesium interaction.

The author-declared primary outcome was change in Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB). The abstract reports better treatment-versus-control SPPB (Δ=.41±.24 points, P=.03), chair-stand time (Δ=−1.31±.33 seconds, P<.0001) and4-m walking speed (Δ=.14±.03m/s, P=.006). The abstract does not define the dispersion attached to these deltas; it is not converted into a confidence interval or standardized effect. **Peak isometric/isokinetic lower-limb strength and handgrip secondary outcomes were null.** The authors say mobility findings were more evident below recommended dietary magnesium intake, but the subgroup denominator, formal interaction, multiplicity and biochemical-deficiency mapping are not verified at this access depth. Low dietary intake is not automatically clinically diagnosed magnesium deficiency.

The current registry supports SPPB as primary but lists appendicular muscle mass as its only secondary outcome, actual enrollment100 versus the publication's 139, and first submission October 2013 after completion March 2013. These discrepancies and retrospective registration constrain prospective-prespecification claims. No posted results or registry history was read. The abstract reports no serious adverse effects; it does not establish absence of milder symptoms or safety equivalence. Full-paper funding, attrition reasons, adherence and the strength/diet subgroup tables are unresolved. No BUBR1 assay is reported in the portions read; absence from the inaccessible main is not asserted.

## Magnosolv-Granulat: controlled CAD endurance signal, with potassium and missingness limits

PMID12615252, DOI10.1016/S0002-9149(02)03297-6. The complete five-page original article was read from a lawful public copy, with all native pages, both figures and all three tables inspected. Title, authors, journal/pages and DOI match official metadata. The paper is not a meta-analysis. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12615252/), [original PDF copy](https://www.mgwater.com/ft/common_mg/oralmg.pdf).

The double-blind, placebo-controlled trial randomized **187 CAD patients**,151 men/36 women, age 63±10(range 42–83), in Austria, Israel and the US, to 94 active/93 placebo for six months. The Methods specifies **15 mmol magnesium ions/365 mg magnesium as citrate plus5.4 mmol potassium as hydrogen carbonate, twice daily**, as Magnosolv-Granulat. Thus the assigned daily amounts are twice those per-administration amounts; “365 mg” is not the daily total. The paper does not disclose enough placebo composition to establish potassium matching or an isolated magnesium-specific causal effect. Preserve this actual formulation; no automatic transfer to plain magnesium citrate, MgO, MgCl2 or Aquamin.

Usual medications and diet continued; selected cardiac medicines were held 24 hours before exercise testing. The paper uses maximum symptom-limited treadmill or bicycle testing and calls the protocol Bruce. It reports no assigned exercise-training intervention. Significant renal impairment, recent infarction, unstable angina, severe heart failure and other conditions were excluded; these are population limits, not newly established universal contraindications.

There were 75/94 active and 86/93 placebo completers; two in each group lacked the exercise test, leaving **73 active/84 placebo in the efficacy analysis**. Active withdrawals included 12 by patient decision, 3 poor adherence, 2 diarrhea, 1 abdominal-pain/constipation and 1 elective surgery; placebo withdrawals were 3 patient decisions/4 poor adherence. The Methods says all randomized patients were included by ITT and describes both documented-data and last-value-carried-forward exercise-time analyses. Native Figure 2 labels94/93, whereas Table 2 uses73/84. Separate estimates for the analysis variants are not supplied; do not claim187 fully observed outcomes or rebuild the analysis.

At six months, **exercise duration was8.7±2.1 versus7.8±2.9 minutes, P=.0075**, a direct post-treatment comparison (mean±SD). No contrast CI is given. This is actual exercise tolerance in CAD, not isolated muscle force or a demonstrated change in everyday walking. Exercise-induced chest pain was6/73(8%) versus18/84(21%),P=.0237. Important nulls are test termination for angina(P=.0522), time to chest pain(.7196), ST depression(.5244), time to1-mm ST depression(.1787), arrhythmias(1.0), and NYHA functional class.

**The broad quality-of-life benefit should not be inherited from within-arm significance.** Table 3's direct change comparisons are null for physical functioning(P=.1340), role functioning(.1843), emotional(.3718), cognitive(.6452), social(.3417), fatigue(.0515), pain(.2318), global health(.0847), and all five Seattle Angina Questionnaire outcomes(.5760,.2093,.0972,.1072,.2609). Constipation improved(P=.0420), while the diarrhea symptom score worsened(P=.0040):active8→25,placebo10→11,with higher scores worse. The separate question asking whether medication improved the condition favored active66% versus47%,P=.0165. No study-wide multiplicity adjustment or prospectively registered endpoint hierarchy is documented in the article; no registry/protocol was identified or read for this 2003 trial.

The paper reports overall adverse events at six months in 32% active versus 61% placebo(P=.0054), and serious events in 2 versus 3 people; these do not erase diarrhea-related withdrawals and questionnaire worsening or establish general safety superiority. Source-defined low intracellular magnesium was common in the 106-person sublingual-cell substudy (36/50 US and44/56 Israeli), but no randomized deficiency-treatment interaction is demonstrated. The post-treatment intracellular Mg/exercise association is not causal mediation; native Figure 1 prints r=.67 while Results prints .63(both P=.0166), an unresolved source discrepancy. Muscle Mg, BUB1B/BUBR1, chromosome fidelity, muscle mass and contractile force were not measured in this article.

The earlier US 50-person endothelial-function report is explicitly an ancillary subset of this cohort, **not independent replication**. The separately encountered 2006 CAD study was not qualified here, and its independence is not assumed. Asta Medica supported the 2003 study. No supplementary material was supplied with the five-page copy, and no supplement completeness claim is made.

## ReMag magnesium chloride: controlled adverse performance and null endurance findings

PMID40077784/PMC11901567, DOI10.3390/nu17050915, NCT05282693. Complete scientific main/declarations, all seven native main figures, complete three-page supplement text and native supplement Tables S1/S2, and the current registry were read. [Primary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11901567/), [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05282693).

The randomized blinded crossover study used lemon-flavored ReMag magnesium-chloride drinks versus otherwise matching drinks without magnesium chloride, twice daily for nine days, with ≥21-day washout. Both main and registry write **“300 mg magnesium chloride” per dose**; the registry also says300mg ReMag. Hydration state and independently inspectable lot certificates are not supplied. Do not silently relabel this as600mg elemental magnesium/day or use a guessed formula conversion. The paper says independent certificates existed, not that this reviewer examined them.

Native Figure 2 shows41 assessed,22 randomized(12 placebo-first/10 active-first),19 receiving the assigned first intervention(three placebo-first did not collect supplements), and15 completing both periods after four active-first scheduling withdrawals. Analyzed participants were8 men/7 women, age 26±5, regular exercisers. Sprint paired data were available for13 owing to technical problems; only six consented to muscle biopsy. This is a selected-completer analysis, not22-person ITT. No additional exercise-training program was assigned. A VO2max fitness inclusion threshold was dropped during recruitment and remains in the current registry. Usual dietary magnesium intake was not measured. The authors describe participants as free of hypomagnesemia; the Introduction says participants had not previously been diagnosed, and the Discussion describes their baseline ionized values as within the reference range. Reported baseline ionized Mg was.47±.20mmol/L; no independent deficiency classification is reconstructed.

There is no basis for calling all endurance impaired when the actual time-trial endpoint is null. Ionized Mg increased versus baseline on days 8/9; the reported baseline comparison does not supply a formal active-versus-placebo treatment test simply because placebo's own change was nonsignificant.

In six biopsied participants, complex-I ADP kinetics/maximal respiration were null. Native Figure 7A–C carbohydrate-plus-fatty-acid State 3 comparisons are **nonsignificant**(P=.0577,.0871,.0887). Figure 7D complex-II-supported respiration and7E uncoupled respiration were lower(P=.0384,.0371); these are selected respiration endpoints, not measured ATP synthesis, BUBR1 restoration or a cause proven to mediate the performance change. The Methods deliberately reports mitochondrial trends at P<.10. No study-wide correction across the performance/respirometry family is described; microbiome analyses have their own stated FDR control. The main's general “no adverse events” statement coexists with questionnaire-reported symptoms:magnesium periods had four mild,three moderate and one severe diarrhea report, versus one mild/one moderate on placebo. These are reports, not an adjudicated unique-person incidence reconstructed from sums. Headache/abdominal symptoms were also reported. Plastic-bottle preparations were recalled when mold was visible and replaced in glass, pausing recruitment; authors state no known ingestion of mold-containing drinks. No causal attribution of performance effects to contamination is made.

The current registry lists actual enrollment41(the paper's assessed count, versus22 randomized), first posting March 2022 after December 2021 start, and completion February 2024 versus the main's collection/analysis window through November 2024. It lists many primary outcomes, including cytokines not reported in this main; the paper calls performance primary and other assays additional. No history was read, so original prospective specification is not inferred. No results are posted. New Capstone funded/provided the drinks; the authors declare no conflicts and no sponsor role in data collection/analysis/publication decisions, while Think Healthy Group participated in design discussions and administered sponsor interactions. These disclosures are retained alongside the unfavorable results.

## Competitive and identity judgment

The older-women mobility result and CAD exercise-duration result are closer to real function than a magnesium biomarker rise alone. They merit explicit functional-reserve consideration without pretending niacin's proposed restoration bridge is proven superior. Apitegromab's controlled SMA motor-function evidence has a different pediatric disease context; these sources do not supply a head-to-head basis to replace it. No new primary reading of those nominees is claimed. None of these sources establishes a BUBR1 restoration mechanism or MVA benefit, and the healthy-exerciser negative cannot erase a different preparation/population's positive or prove all magnesium harmful.
