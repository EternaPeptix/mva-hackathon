# Calcium-HMB during bed rest: mass sensitivity, measured functional nulls

**Disposition:** this 2013 trial can refine the existing `new-calcium-hmb` assessment; it does not support a new HMB-family identity or nominee replacement. The full main shows that the overall evaluable lean-mass comparison was nonsignificant, with a favorable result after excluding one active participant. Strength and functional tests were performed, but no between-treatment benefit was demonstrated in the reported main comparisons. Preserve both the exclusion-sensitive mass signal and the measured functional findings; neither an unavailable estimate nor an imprecise null establishes universal ineffectiveness. This source-stage packet preceded the integrated HMB/krill update; independent review has since accepted the bounded refinement.

## Trial, preparation and analysis populations

[Deutz et al., PMID 23514626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23514626/), DOI [10.1016/j.clnu.2013.02.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2013.02.011), used a randomized, masked, parallel design in healthy adults eligible at age 60–79 and SPPB ≥9. The evaluable group was aged 60–76. Abbott generated permuted-block randomization stratified by sex and age; site staff used sequential sealed envelopes within strata. The main describes masking through data analysis, including participants, investigators and involved Abbott personnel.

The [native supplementary flow](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0261561413000757-mmc1.docx) shows 217 screened, 193 excluded, and **24 randomized: 11 control, 13 HMB**. The abstract says 20 women/four men. Two control and one HMB participants had positive D-dimer tests before bed rest; another HMB participant had knee pain/swelling; one control participant exited for vomiting. Thus **8 control/11 HMB were evaluable for bed rest**. A control pulmonary embolism during recovery left **7 control/11 HMB** evaluable for the whole study. This is not a demonstrated intention-to-treat analysis. The separately excluded active male DXA outlier reduces relevant mass analyses to 8/10 at bed-rest end and generally 7/10 after rehabilitation; selected later regional measurements have HMB n=9. Figure 2's general control n=8 caption does not override the supplement's recovery n=7. The 15-woman analysis (7/8) is a subgroup of the same cohort, not replication or an established sex interaction. Mixed-muscle synthesis had 8/10 participants because one active participant could not undergo the procedure; that exclusion is not established as the same person as the DXA exclusion.

Each active sachet contained **1.5 g Ca-HMB (TSI, Salt Lake City), 4 g maltodextrin and 200 mg calcium**, plus flavor/sweetener. Two sachets were taken daily, morning/evening around meals, from five days before bed rest through eight weeks of rehabilitation. The control is described as identical except for Ca-HMB, so the listed maltodextrin/calcium background is nominally shared. The report does not analytically verify total elemental-calcium matching or clarify whether the listed 200 mg is salt-associated or separately supplied calcium. Do not rewrite the nominal 3 g/day Ca-HMB as a verified free-acid-HMB equivalent. Third-party packaging was described without naming the packager; batch assay, purity and retail interchangeability remain unresolved.

Participants received a controlled diet during run-in/bed rest, including 0.8 g protein/kg/day. Ten days of bed rest were followed by shared resistance rehabilitation: approximately one hour, three days/week for eight weeks, three sets of 8–10 repetitions around 80% 1RM. Supplementation continued during rehabilitation, so later findings are not durability after withdrawal. The authors excluded outcomes for wrong product, excluded concomitant treatments or <67% total product consumption; no imputation-based rescue of excluded participants is established. Urinary HMB supports ingestion during bed rest (active mean 2258.88 and 2383.86 nmol/mL versus control 16.93 and 27.44 at the two assessments), not verified plasma exposure, BUBR1 engagement or a pediatric dose.

## Outcomes: do not substitute mass for function

All displayed variation below is **SEM**, not SD; no treatment-effect confidence intervals were supplied.

| Mass analysis over ten-day bed rest | Control | Ca-HMB | Reported treatment comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| All evaluable total lean mass (8/11) | −2.05 ±0.66 kg | −0.60 ±0.47 kg | P=.16, ANOVA |
| Total lean mass after active DXA exclusion (8/10) | −2.05 ±0.66 kg | −0.17 ±0.19 kg | P=.02, ANOVA |
| Leg lean mass, excluded set (8/10) | −1.01 ±0.35 kg | −0.08 ±0.17 kg | P=.02, ANOVA |
| Female-only total lean mass (7/8) | −1.89 ±0.75 kg | −0.20 ±0.24 kg | P=.04, ANOVA |

The excluded man lost substantially more DXA lean mass and did not recover it during rehabilitation. The authors inferred baseline DXA error after noting no unusual CRP/body-weight/strength pattern and confirming urinary HMB. They did not demonstrate that measurement error. No prespecified outlier rule or independent rescan was recovered. The abstract associates the excluded active estimate with within-arm P=.23; the main gives P=.42 and native Table 3 .4177, while .23 belongs to the 11-person active estimate. Preserve the discrepancy rather than correcting the abstract silently. Native Table 3 also prints an arm-lean change of +.02 kg despite displayed means 4.07→4.04; it is not repaired here.

At rehabilitation end, total lean-mass changes from original baseline were −0.43 ±0.67 kg in control and +0.07 ±0.26 kg in active; both within-arm tests were nonsignificant. Leg-lean change was −0.06 ±0.22 versus +0.71 ±0.33 kg, with active within-arm P=.06/.061. These are not demonstrated treatment differences. The supplement's mass-table title/columns specify baseline-to-rehabilitation changes, but its P-value footnote says post-bed-rest-to-rehabilitation; the conflict remains unresolved.

Strength testing included isokinetic knee extension/flexion labelled 60° and 180°, isotonic 1RM, leg press, plantar-flexor testing and stair power. **Tests were feasible and performed**; the abstract's wording about unobserved differences does not mean testing failed. The main gives these controlled contrasts:

| Strength comparison | Control | Ca-HMB | Inference retained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knee-extension 60°, bed-rest change | −12.54 ±7.84 | +0.67 ±6.91 | Main reports between-group P=.84; do not reconstruct or repair it |
| Knee-extension 180°, bed-rest change | −11.00 ±8.31 | −0.18 ±7.07 | Between-group P=.10; nonsignificant under stated .05 main-effect rule |
| Knee-extension 60°, post-bed-rest to rehabilitation change | +22.24 ±8.16 | +23.64 ±9.61 | Within-arm P=.03 each; no group difference at recovery end reported |
| Knee-extension 180°, post-bed-rest to rehabilitation change | +15.90 ±8.06 | +11.15 ±8.49 | Within-arm P=.10/.22; not a supplement advantage |

The prose prints Nm/s, whereas Figure 3 axes print Nm; the table above retains the reported numbers without resolving that unit discrepancy. Figure 3's active-only baseline-to-rehabilitation significance (60° P=.004/.0041, 180° P=.03) is not a treatment effect. Individual strength-test denominators and full numerical results for every named strength/power endpoint were not supplied in the recovered main/supplement; absence of those estimates is not a null.

The original supplementary function table provides the following trajectories (control n=8 at baseline/post-bed rest, 7 after rehabilitation; HMB n=11):

| Function | Control: baseline → bed-rest end → rehabilitation end | Ca-HMB: same visits |
|---|---|---|
| SPPB | 11.6 ±.3 → 11.5 ±.3 → 11.6 ±.4 | 11.5 ±.2 → 11.5 ±.3 → 11.9 ±.1 |
| Five-item performance score | 14.4 ±.3 → 14.1 ±.2 → 14.7 ±.2 | 14.5 ±.2 → 13.9 ±.4 → 14.5 ±.2 |

| Standing plantar-flexor repetitions/minute | 30.9 ±2.5 → 32.8 ±2.5 → 43.1 ±4.7 | 34.3 ±5.1 → 34.7 ±5.1 → 41.9 ±3.9 |

The main reports no between-treatment difference at bed-rest end for SPPB, Get-Up-and-Go or the five-item score. The supplement's P values are **within-arm** changes: Get-Up-and-Go improves during rehabilitation in both groups (.0323/.0025); plantar-flexor repetitions improve nominally in active (.0456), with control .089. Differing within-arm significance does not establish a treatment effect. SPPB/performance within-arm comparisons are labelled ns (defined as P>.1). Ceiling effects and high variability were the authors' explanations, not demonstrated reasons to convert nulls into benefit. No precise rehabilitation treatment contrast for these tasks is supplied. VO₂peak has fewer observations (control 7→5, active 11→9), within-arm ns and no rehabilitation measurement; it is not a verified controlled endurance benefit. Physical-activity tables likewise show within-arm comparisons, with an unexplained £ symbol on active baseline cells. Six-hour mixed-muscle protein synthesis had a nonsignificant treatment change (P=.30); it does not establish anabolic mediation or renewal.

## Hierarchy, harms, correspondence and remaining limits

The [current official NCT00945581 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00945581) names **lower-extremity strength changes during bed rest and recovery as primary**, with lean mass, function and activity secondary. Its first posting predates the trial, but original version history/SAP was not recovered; prospective stability of every analysis detail is unverified. The registry lists 36 actual enrolled versus 24 randomized in the paper, and dates differ modestly. These are unresolved accounting/source differences, not additional participants to add to the paper denominator. No registry results or full protocol documents were supplied in the current API record.

The main uses two-sided .05 main-effect and .10 interaction thresholds, without adjustment across multiple variables. Holm correction is described only for follow-up comparisons after a significant interaction. There is no verified family-wide correction, and no demonstrated prespecification of the female-only or DXA-excluded analyses. Authors' retrospective power estimates do not demonstrate equivalence or benefit.

Two serious adverse events occurred, one participant per arm: control pulmonary embolism/hypertension and active hyponatremia, both judged unrelated to product by the authors. Both left hospital stable; the control event caused recovery-phase withdrawal. Vomiting and active knee pain/swelling also appear in the flow. Nonsignificant differences in reported adverse-event categories are not zero events or safety equivalence. No detailed graded harm table was present in the recovered supplementary package despite the main's general reference to more detailed supplementary results. Abbott funded the trial and participated in design, planning and statistical analysis; three authors were employees, and Wolfe received advisory-board compensation. These facts constrain appraisal without erasing the data.

[PMID 23706872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23706872/) is indexed as a **Letter/Comment on this trial**, titled *Establishing exercise habit is important when assessing lean body mass in elderly*. The linked [reply, PMID 23725606](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23725606/), is a separate one-page Letter/Comment by Pereira, Deutz and Wolfe. The title indicates an exercise-habit issue, but neither full correspondence text was recovered and no specific criticism or author concession is inferred. The inspected records classify these as comments, not corrections or retractions. That bounded notice check is not an exhaustive guarantee that no other notice exists; correspondence and supplement are not independent trials.

Under equal function/exposure/durability/renewal/fidelity criteria, this source adds a fragile mass-preservation result and directly measured, nonsignificant functional comparisons. It does not overturn the different older-women gait signal, demonstrate chronic generalized harm, or establish BUBR1 restoration, chromosome fidelity, productive tissue renewal, pediatric benefit or MVA rescue. Niacin's provisional restoration hypothesis and apitegromab's separate motor-function evidence gain no automatic superiority from these nulls. Independent review accepted this bounded refinement of existing calcium-HMB evidence; it adds no new HMB assessment identity.
