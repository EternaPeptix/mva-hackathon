# Taurine: retain a muscle-physiology reserve, separating depletion from treatment

**23 September 2026.** The primary TauT study supports the importance of taurine homeostasis to muscle aging; it does **not** test taurine replacement. One directly cited aged-rat treatment paper adds an actual-compound electrophysiology/contraction-threshold positive, at a limited accessible reading depth. Neither establishes BUBR1 restoration or MVA rescue.

## What the TauT primary actually tested

[Ito et al., 2014; PMID 25229346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4167997/) compare whole-body taurine-transporter knockout mice with littermate WT/heterozygotes on standard chow. **There is no taurine-treatment or supplementation-rescue arm.** The shorter pooled survival is a genetic-depletion outcome: median 591 days in knockout, 795 in WT and 723 in heterozygotes. The sex-stratified controls pool WT and heterozygotes. The male comparison was significant; the female comparison was not (reported P=.089), despite female muscle-marker/histological changes.

Older knockout muscle has more p16 RNA and central-nucleated fibers, lower mitochondrial complex-I activity and increased UPR signals. Other respiratory-complex activities were nonsignificant, young complex activities were reported unchanged, and ragged-red fibers were not detected. These outcomes retain biological and enzymatic-function evidence. Earlier exercise intolerance is cited background, not a new taurine-treatment endurance experiment.

BubR1 appears in discussion of separate senescent-cell-clearance work. It is not the studied genotype or a measured treatment-response protein. Whole-body transporter loss cannot establish supplement sufficiency, relevant tissue causation of death or a BUB1B rescue mechanism.

Two reporting discrepancies remain unresolved: the 520-day analysis prints a discordant chi-square/P pair, and Figure 2A's liver p16 star differs from the narrative null. Neither is used to infer a corrected effect or to erase the central muscle findings.

## One actual-treatment source, with access limits

Direct reference 54 is [Pierno et al., 1998; PMID 9732377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9732377/). The complete primary abstract reports oral taurine, **1 g/kg daily for 2–3 months**, in aged male Wistar rats, with isolated EDL-fiber assays. Muscle taurine increased. The paper reports increased chloride conductance and improved excitability; contraction voltage threshold moved toward adult values. Conductance equivalence to adult muscle is not established by the read abstract. Those are actual functional-physiology results, distinct from a marker-only claim. They do not establish greater absolute force or endurance.

The [publisher's accessible section snippets](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022356524377109) retain no food-intake/body-weight change and no deaths in either aged treatment condition. This is not survival-extension evidence. The full paper/figures were unavailable through the checked publisher and public institutional routes; the author repository explicitly has no attached file. Per-group animal counts, analysis units, complete controls and figure effect sizes remain unqualified. **No complete-paper reading credit is claimed for this follow-up.** The animal regimen is a source description, not a clinical dose.

## Competitive implication and provenance

Retain taurine as a **muscle-physiology research reserve**. The actual-treatment physiology is useful; the knockout survival result cannot be relabeled supplementation benefit. Neither lack of MVA testing nor a different model uniquely excludes a challenger: niacin's restoration bridge spans different interventions, and apitegromab's clinical function evidence is in SMA. Here, BUBR1 function, faithful renewal and useful absolute muscle output remain unestablished, and the treatment source has an access limit. No superiority or replacement of the provisional nominees is supported.
