# Claramine: retain metabolic function, separate it from BUBR1 restoration

**Claramine has measured receptor-processing/signaling activity and a separate diabetic-mouse functional positive. Neither establishes correction of a BUB1B/MVA defect.** Retain an experimental metabolic-function reserve, without currently replacing the provisional niacin/apitegromab comparison. The exact ledger/qualification search at baseline `46c556f` found no existing Claramine appraisal; suggested new ID: `new-claramine`.

## Identity and source-assigned targets

The [2021 primary, PMID 34029592](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8254121/) identifies **Claramine as synthetic aminosterol 4f**, distinct from trodusquemine, Claramine A1 and other screened aminosterols. Supplementary Figure S5 gives its structure; supporting chemistry supplies NMR/MS and formula C37H72N4O. Hydrochloride-salt preparation is described, but the exact bioassay salt/lot, quantitative purity and vehicle are unclear. No CAS or present medicine availability is inferred.

The [2015 antecedent](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25623533/) measured PTP1B inhibition in F11 neuronal-cell extracts and intact-cell treatments, with no inhibition of the related TC-PTP assay. This is useful comparative selectivity, not a broad phosphatase panel. Its authors say the binding mechanism remains unresolved; trodusquemine's allosteric sites or selectivity ratio cannot be inherited. The 2021 paper establishes **reduced BACE1-dependent IR cleavage**, not direct binding to or purified catalytic inhibition of BACE1.

## What the 2021 experiments establish

At **10 µM for 17 h**, Claramine increased surface insulin receptor (IR) in BACE1-expressing HEK293 cells, reduced IR cleavage fragments, and modestly lowered soluble IR release. Endogenous HepG2 experiments also showed less IR fragment and greater surface IR. The inactive-BACE1 control had no surface IR increase. These are actual treatment effects, not gene-name associations.

A shift from core- to complex-glycosylated proBACE1 supports altered early-secretory-to-Golgi transport; it is not a directly timed trafficking-rate measurement. Decreased LC3-HiBiT reporter accumulation, increased LC3II ratios and autophagosome-like structures support an autophagy effect. The reporter tests 5–15 µM: 10/15 µM are marked significant, 5 µM is not. Resveratrol does not reproduce the proBACE1 shift. No genetic experiment establishes that autophagy mediates all of Claramine's receptor effects.

Claramine also increased IR phosphorylation and basal/insulin-stimulated EGR1 transcription. **This signaling response persists with inactive BACE1**, but is lost with kinase-dead IR; IGF1R phosphorylation did not increase. Thus receptor-cleavage protection and insulin-mimetic signaling must remain separate effects. Greater receptor availability alone does not explain all signaling.

Retain the qualifications: BACE1-independent soluble IR release increased; NRG1 full-length and fragment signals both decreased, limiting a cleavage-specific interpretation. Figure S5's screening bars place Claramine highest, but do not establish that it was the only compound with an increased reporter. The paper reports experiments repeated at least three times; exact biological units are not supplied for every panel. Formal viability, metabolic flux, muscle output and chromosome fidelity were not tested.

**The 2021 mice were not a Claramine treatment experiment.** Its male db/db and high-fat-diet liver comparisons establish increased proBACE1 and disturbed protein regulation in diabetic context. No Claramine dosing, glucose-tolerance or insulin-tolerance study is reported there.

## Separate 2015 functional positive

The [public author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271516057_Functional_properties_of_Claramine_A_novel_PTP1B_inhibitor_and_insulin-mimetic_compound) reports a single **5 mg/kg intraperitoneal** treatment in 3.5-month male CaMK2αCre/LMO4flox diabetic mice, with improved glucose tolerance 24 h and insulin tolerance 48 h later. Figure 3's caption gives **6–8 mice/group, P<.05**, alongside littermate controls and trodusquemine comparison. This is organismal function, beyond markers.

A separate 2-month WT CD1 cohort, **eight/group**, showed transient feeding suppression and weight loss, without increased energy expenditure or locomotion. No pair-feeding design is described; feeding/weight mediation of the diabetic cohort's glycemic benefit remains unresolved. These observations do not establish pediatric harm. Direct skeletal-muscle/hepatocyte effects were not tested in that paper. Full scientific text and captions were readable, but native figures/PDF were unavailable, so exact curves and effect sizes were not visually verified.

## Three links that must not be collapsed

| Link | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| **Measured Claramine activity** | Cell receptor-processing/signaling effects plus the separate mouse glycemic benefit are positive evidence. |
| **Defect in the tested models** | Diabetic-liver proBACE1 accumulation in 2021; neuronal LMO4-loss/PTP1B-associated insulin resistance in 2015. |
| **Unmeasured BUB1B/MVA defect** | Neither source measures a corresponding lesion in the relevant BUB1B genotype or tests its correction. |

The [existing receptor-trafficking qualification](intervention-qualification.md) preserves prior evidence that BUBR1 depletion **delays receptor endocytosis**, with increased insulin sensitivity in hypomorphic mice described in the existing pipeline. The original Choi papers were not requalified here. Preventing early-secretory receptor cleavage is distinct from correcting surface-receptor internalization; “more surface IR” is not automatically the missing rescue direction.

Different disease alone does not disqualify Claramine: niacin and apitegromab also lack established MVA rescue. Its metabolic outcome merits reserve status. Current competition still lacks a demonstrated relevant metabolic defect, useful muscle output/renewal or fidelity result, and a qualified exposure bridge. Preserve feeding/weight changes as context, not a prediction of harm or a reason to dismiss the glycemic positive.
