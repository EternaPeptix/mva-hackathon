# Copenhagen resveratrol–exercise studies: one overlapping cohort, different endpoints

**Retain a real, regimen-specific attenuation of aerobic-fitness improvement, alongside substantial training benefits and several null drug comparisons.** These studies strengthen the counterevidence to resveratrol as a universal exercise enhancer. They do not establish general supplement harm, invalidate the separately qualified muscle-function positives, or test BUB1B-MVA rescue. Several broader “blunting” claims are less secure than the directly reported oxygen-uptake contrast because the displayed tests are within-arm comparisons.

This review qualifies [Gliemann et al., 2013, PMID23878368 / PMC3810808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3810808/), [DOI10.1113/jphysiol.2013.258061](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2013.258061), and [Olesen et al., 2014, PMID24514907 / PMC4001758](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4001758/), [DOI10.1113/jphysiol.2013.270256](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2013.270256). Both complete scientific main texts, their main captions and all fourteen native main figures were read. Supplementary bodies remain inaccessible through the lawful routes checked. No new appraisal, unique-source, outer-queue or experimental replication credit is created.

## Shared participants and exposure

The 2014 paper explicitly identifies the 2013 cardiovascular results as previously published data from its larger project. Its trained groups have the same **13 placebo and 14 resveratrol participants**. It adds a separate nontraining part with **seven placebo and nine resveratrol participants**, for 43 men overall. The Methods describe **two parts, each randomized and double blinded for supplement assignment**. They do not establish a single 43-person randomization to training versus nontraining. These are overlapping publications, not two independent exercise replications. No participant-ID/raw-data audit was performed.

Participants were healthy, physically inactive men aged 60–72, reporting less than two hours of moderate activity weekly. They were nonsmokers without diagnosed cardiovascular disease, hypertension, renal dysfunction, insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes. Two trained participants, one per arm, continued physician-managed hypercholesterolemia medication. The papers describe allocation based on initial oxygen uptake, BMI, glucose and cholesterol, but not the random sequence or allocation-concealment procedure in reproducible detail. No participant-flow/withdrawal or intention-to-treat framework is supplied in the inspected main texts.

Both papers identify **Fluxome trans-resveratrol, 250 mg orally each morning for eight weeks** (the 2014 acknowledgement explicitly says trans-resveratrol). Purity, tablet excipients, lot and catalog are unspecified. Exercise comprised two supervised interval-cycling sessions and one full-body circuit session weekly, plus a weekly five-kilometre walk. Supervised compliance and intensity were similar between trained arms. Tablet adherence and tolerability were self-reported. There was no significant side-effect report, but no comprehensive adverse-event assessment or long-term safety conclusion follows.

The 2014 biopsies/blood samples were collected approximately one hour after the postintervention tablet; its Discussion describes measurements around 48 hours after the last training session. **Plasma resveratrol was not measured in these participants.** The paper's approximately 0.75 µM estimate extrapolates from a different study's 500-mg exposure and is not observed pharmacokinetics here.

The source's relative-percentage wording needs care: the 2013 abstract says the placebo gain was about 45% greater, whereas its Discussion calls the resveratrol gain 45% lower. Those statements use different denominators and are not equivalent. The absolute reported gains are preferable. A second internal issue is that the 2013 Results calls the GPX-1 time effect an increase, while the native Figure 6 and Discussion indicate a decrease. Neither discrepancy removes the measured oxygen-uptake difference.

## Statistics, assays and limits

Both papers use two-way repeated-measures ANOVA and Student–Newman–Keuls comparisons; the 2014 paper also explicitly uses paired t tests within groups and log transformation when assumptions are unsuitable. The nominal threshold is P<.05. There is no reported correction across the full collection of physiological, transcript and protein outcomes. Specific interaction effect estimates/P values and confidence intervals are generally not supplied. I did not reconstruct inferential tests from plotted bars or summary SEMs.

Neither inspected main labels a prespecified primary/secondary endpoint hierarchy or supplies a trial-registration/protocol identifier. The ethics approval number is not trial registration. Accordingly, these are reported functional and molecular endpoints; I do not relabel them as verified preregistered primary outcomes. The supplementary materials and any external protocol remain unresolved.

The 2013 paper documents endpoint-specific sample shortages/technical exclusions, with muscle assays sometimes using 11–14 participants per arm and vascular assays 12–13; 27 is not every assay's denominator. The 2014 main figure captions generally state 7/9/13/14. PCR technical triplicates are not extra participants. SIRT1 protein was unchanged; this does not establish unchanged enzyme activity. Neither main reports a BUB1B/BubR1 assay, relevant genotype, chromosome-fidelity measurement or causal rescue. The inaccessible supplements prevent a broader whole-publication absence claim.

## Source status and existing-entry refinement

Public PMC HTML authenticates the exact DOI/PMID pairs even though current Europe PMC metadata omits their PMC identifiers. All seven main figures per paper were retrieved from the declared public NCBI image links and viewed. Supplementary API attempts returned XML error bodies; PMC PDF/TIFF requests returned HTML challenges, and publisher PDF requests returned 403. These are retained as error/invalid-response assets, not counted as read supplements. No access control was bypassed and no author was contacted.

The 2013 metadata links seven commentaries; they are comment links, not correction/retraction notices, and their scientific bodies were not read in this two-paper task. No correction/retraction is identified by the inspected metadata. This is a bounded notice check.

**The existing resveratrol entry is refined; no new compound is added.** Preserve the separately qualified 2017 muscle power/torque interactions, the later pilot's limited strength signal and walking-speed uncertainty, the access-limited rat BUBR1-staining lead, and the distinct oocyte result. Add this cohort's smaller aerobic-fitness gain, null muscle-endurance augmentation and the weaker evidentiary status of several marker “blunting” claims. Differences in population, formulation/dose, training and endpoints leave the broader comparison unresolved. Niacin and apitegromab remain provisional hypotheses; these studies establish neither resveratrol superiority nor general ineffectiveness or harm, and they provide no MVA treatment recommendation.
