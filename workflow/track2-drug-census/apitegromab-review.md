# Apitegromab versus the niacin and everolimus research hypotheses

**Independent research appraisal, 21 September 2026.** Primary experimental publications and official regulatory records only. This recommends research prioritization, not treatment or a patient dose. No experiment, submission or publication was performed.

## Recommendation

**Advance apitegromab as a competitive downstream medication candidate, ahead of everolimus for a muscle-function objective. Keep niacin as a complementary BUBR1-restoration hypothesis, not as a proven overall winner.** If the two nominated drugs are intended to maximize the credibility of future muscle benefit, my evidence-based preference is **niacin plus apitegromab, tested separately**; everolimus can remain a targeted mTOR mechanism probe. This is an independent recommendation for the coordinating review, not an automatic change to the current proposed pair.

Apitegromab has randomized evidence of motor benefit in children with a different genetic neuromuscular disease, defined pharmacology and current US medication eligibility. Those advantages materially exceed everolimus's present case-specific functional evidence. Apitegromab has no demonstrated MVA benefit, no established BUBR1-restoration mechanism and a meaningful fracture signal. These limitations prevent a clinical recommendation, but they do not uniquely disqualify it as a research candidate: niacin and everolimus also lack actual-MVA functional rescue. Conversely, the stronger clinical trial design does not prove that its effect transfers to chromosome-instability-driven developmental muscle disease.

## Verified approval and clinical evidence

The FDA approved **ISEMBYLD (apitegromab-mstn)** on **11 September 2026**, for SMA in adults and children aged at least two years already receiving an SMN2-targeted treatment. This establishes US drug eligibility; it does not make BUB1B-MVA an approved indication or establish that this case meets the SMA indication. [FDA approval announcement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-therapy-target-muscle-loss-spinal-muscular-atrophy), [FDA dated approval listing](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/notable-approvals-drugs).

SAPPHIRE enrolled 188 nonambulatory participants with genetically documented SMN-deficient type 2/3 SMA, HFMSE 10–45, receiving nusinersen or risdiplam. Ages 2–12 formed the 156-person primary population, randomized to 10 mg/kg, 20 mg/kg or placebo every four weeks. The 32 participants aged 13–21 received 20 mg/kg or placebo, 2:1. These populations and analyses must remain distinct. [Crawford et al., 2025, PMID 40818473](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40818473/).

| Population and comparison at 12 months | Reported HFMSE result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Ages 2–12; combined 10/20 mg/kg versus placebo | LS difference **1.8 points**, 95% CI **0.30–3.32**, p=**0.019**; change +0.6 versus −1.2 | Positive primary comparison; combines modest mean improvement with prevention of decline. |
| Ages 2–12; 20 mg/kg versus placebo | **1.4**, 95% CI **−0.34–3.13**, p=**0.11**; change +0.2 versus −1.2 | Not statistically significant; do not describe every dose comparison as positive. |

The first two rows come from the [primary SAPPHIRE paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1474-4422(25)00225-X); the third from [label section 14](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1cd8ab8-8a10-48ed-9806-75a44f22b963); the fourth from the investigators' [2025 subgroup conference abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2025.105589). The latter is supplementary conference evidence, not an independent trial. The FDA also reports that **34.2% versus 13.5%** achieved a clinically meaningful response in the 10 mg/kg comparison. [FDA assessment](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-therapy-target-muscle-loss-spinal-muscular-atrophy). HFMSE is a functional clinical outcome, not a muscle-mass surrogate; the measured effect remains specific to the enrolled disease and background treatment.

## Pharmacology and transfer to BUB1B disease

Apitegromab targets promyostatin/latent myostatin, preventing activation. It does not bind mature myostatin or broadly block other related growth factors. Thus follistatin, activin-receptor blockade and mature-myostatin antibodies are different interventions. The phase 1 study established target engagement in healthy adults; increased total latent myostatin after treatment reflects target binding/accumulation, not proof of worsening active signaling. [Barrett et al., 2021, PMID 33963971](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8189951/).

The relevant preclinical functional precedent used **muSRK-015P**, a related mouse antibody, in SMN-restored SMA mice. It increased muscle mass and absolute plantarflexor torque; torque normalized to muscle mass did not improve. This was a real functional increase, with no evidence of improved intrinsic muscle quality. The more affected model had reduced baseline circulating latent myostatin but still responded. Consequently, **elevated baseline myostatin cannot be required as the sole justification for a compensatory trial**. Target availability and a useful response are more relevant than assuming that overactivation must cause the original disease. The mouse antibody and SMN treatment must not be relabeled apitegromab monotherapy. [Long et al., 2019, Figures 2, 3 and 6, PMID 30481286](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6423420/).

The [gene-specific census](literature-review.md) retrieved all 549 records from its BUBR1/BUB1B–myostatin/activin/SMAD query and found no myostatin-blockade rescue in MVA or BUBR1-deficient muscle. This leaves transfer uncertain; it is not a negative treatment experiment. Inference: increasing output from surviving fibers might help despite persistent chromosome instability, whereas a severe progenitor/differentiation bottleneck could limit that benefit. Neither alternative has been tested here. The BubR1-depleted C2C12 model documents a differentiation defect, not its rescue by myostatin inhibition. [Nozaki et al., PMID 26464273](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5023544/).

## Lean mass is not sufficient evidence of function

EMBRAZE randomized 102 adults with overweight/obesity to apitegromab or placebo during tirzepatide treatment. At 24 weeks, the primary completer analysis (43 versus 44 participants) found **1.9 kg** more lean mass retained, **80% CI 1.2–2.7**, nominal p=0.0014. Physical-function assessments used handheld dynamometry and chair sit-to-stand repetitions; no notable between-group differences were reported. The trial used a two-sided 20% significance level without multiplicity adjustment, and DEXA cannot fully distinguish lean-tissue compartments. This supports a body-composition effect in another context, not general functional efficacy. Its exploratory function result does not negate the SMA trial or establish a definitive MVA null. Active malignancy and recent cancer treatment were excluded, limiting oncology transfer. [Primary EMBRAZE report, DOI 10.1038/s41591-026-04440-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04440-4).

## Material safety and regulatory limits

The label recommends 10 mg/kg every four weeks; 20 mg/kg offered no added efficacy. Fractures occurred in **5/53 (9%)** at 10 mg/kg, **3/53 (6%)** at 20 mg/kg, versus **1/50 (2%)** on placebo. The open-label extension, using 20 mg/kg (twice the recommended dose), reported 22 patients with fractures (9%), including five serious femur-fracture events. That extension incidence is not a recommended-dose estimate. Some lacked clear trauma. Rat bone toxicity occurred below recommended human exposure, without an identified no-effect dose. Carcinogenicity/genotoxicity studies were not conducted. Half-life was approximately 31 days, relevant to reversibility. [Official prescribing information, sections 2, 5, 12 and 13](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1cd8ab8-8a10-48ed-9806-75a44f22b963).

These findings carry direct weight for a muscle/function research program; a favorable movement score cannot by itself settle net benefit when bone outcomes matter. The FDA also identifies potential fetal and reproductive harm. [FDA announcement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-first-therapy-target-muscle-loss-spinal-muscular-atrophy). Earlier favorable preclinical tolerability claims do not override the current label. No cross-trial adverse-event ranking against niacin or everolimus is justified from unmatched populations.

The European application was withdrawn on **13 August 2026** because manufacturing-site compliance with EU GMP requirements had not been demonstrated within the required time. The published EMA explanation does not identify an efficacy rejection; it also does not constitute EU approval. [EMA withdrawal explanation](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/medicine-qa/questions-answers-withdrawal-application-marketing-authorisation-isembyld-apitegromab_en.pdf).

Rhabdomyosarcoma findings are mixed and intervention-specific. Blocking anti-myostatin antibodies promoted terminal differentiation in RD/Rh30 cells; recombinant myostatin inhibited RD proliferation in another experiment. A later study found myostatin addback increased proliferation after BRD4-L depletion, while modestly reducing proliferation in controls. None tested apitegromab in MVA or established cancer recurrence risk. This evidence supports neither blanket exclusion for assumed tumor promotion nor a claim of oncology safety. [Ricaud 2003, PMID 14614446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614446/), [Langley 2004, PMID 14724580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14724580/), [BRD4 primary study, Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10897194/).

## Symmetric comparison and the result that would matter

| Evidence dimension | Niacin | Apitegromab | Everolimus |
|---|---|---|---|
| BUBR1-specific rationale | NAD/SIRT2 stabilization bridge; actual niacin and actual-variant functional rescue unproved | Compensatory muscle route; no established BUBR1 repair | Proposed mTOR excess in the relevant disease remains unestablished |
| Actual-drug human muscle-function evidence | Small open-label adult mitochondrial-myopathy study; treated healthy controls also gained strength | Randomized pediatric SMA functional benefit, with dose/age limits above | No relevant functional drug rescue identified for the nominated MVA-muscle mechanism |
| Central transfer uncertainty | Whether precursor exposure improves useful endogenous variant function | Whether surviving BUBR1-deficient muscle can gain useful output without unacceptable tissue costs | Whether reducing this pathway preserves rather than sacrifices productive growth/renewal |
| Present research role | Distinct restoration hypothesis worth retaining | Higher-priority downstream medication competitor | Conditional mechanism interrogation |

Niacin's primary human study demonstrated muscle NAD repletion and strength changes but lacked a placebo-controlled disease-specific effect; this is weaker causal functional evidence than SAPPHIRE, despite its useful tissue-engagement result. [Pirinen et al., PMID 32386566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32386566/). North's BubR1-H/H survival intervention was **genetic SIRT2 overexpression**; **NMN**, not niacin, raised wild-type BUBR1 abundance in the pharmacological experiments. This gives niacin a gene-proximal rationale, not demonstrated mutant rescue. [North et al., PMID 24825348](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088/).

For everolimus, the motivating [Sieben Figure 5C](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/126863/figure/5) presents limited monoallelic-carrier phosphoprotein observations rather than a treatment result. Actual everolimus reduced BUBR1 abundance in late-passage Wharton's-jelly MSCs, a different setting and an abundance endpoint. Neither finding establishes its benefit in MVA muscle. [Goutas et al., Figure 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10149375/). Retaining everolimus solely because it was nominated earlier would therefore be difficult to justify when apitegromab is evaluated on the same dimensions.

All three require exposure-qualified evaluation in the relevant genetic and muscle context. A fair comparison measures **absolute contractile output**, durable progenitor renewal and chromosome fidelity, with both differentiating and mature-muscle conditions. A downstream benefit need not correct BUBR1, but must not be mislabeled chromosome repair or conceal worse renewal. For apitegromab, demonstrate that the assay contains the relevant myostatin precursor/activation system and retains drug-specific target engagement; a cell model lacking that system cannot deliver an interpretable drug null. Bone and longer-term tumor outcomes remain separate limitations that a favorable culture experiment cannot resolve.
