# CCDC41-deficient oocytes: three drug-specific leads and an MG132 refinement

**CP5V and CA-074 produce actual drug-treated meiotic phenotype improvements. Concanamycin A has a narrower timing/marker positive, with important null comparisons and inhibition of normal controls.** Retain these as experimental, defect-specific cell-function evidence. None establishes drug-treated euploidy, BUBR1 restoration, fertility, muscle function or MVA benefit.

This qualification concerns [Tian et al., PMID41331237 / PMC12884753](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12884753/), [DOI10.1002/advs.202504665](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202504665). It supports separate CP5V, CA-074 and Concanamycin A appraisals and adds a distinct source to existing MG132 evidence.

## Model, identity and measured outcomes

Oocytes came from 3-week-old CB6F1 mice. Ccdc41 morpholino depletion was followed by in vitro maturation; this was not a Bub1b-mutant model or systemic drug treatment. Equivalent DMSO controls were specified at 1:1000 or less. Reported concentrations identify the experiments; they are not clinical regimens.

## What the normal controls and source errors mean

Reducing premature MII can be useful in a defective model, but it can also reflect delayed or arrested progression. MG132 and Concanamycin A suppress the same early maturation endpoint in control oocytes. This constrains interpretation without proving general clinical harm. A single nonsignificant comparison to control is not evidence of equivalence or complete rescue.

The S9 caption repeats panel letters. Native D/E/F are drug-spindle images, abnormal-spindle proportion and MII; G/H are MAD1 imaging/ratio; I/J are immunoblots/protein quantification. Native A–C are **genetic Ctsb-MO** fluorescence experiments, not CA-074. The caption also misnames the genetic third arm as CtrlMO+CtsbMO, while its image identifies Ccdc41MO+CtsbMO.

Timing remains unresolved: S9 main text says MAD1 at 8h; its caption says 6h post-GVBD and blots at 4h. MII axes in S4/S7/S9 say **10h of in vitro maturation**, not necessarily 10h after GVBD. MainFigure8 explicitly distinguishes 8h post-GVBD from 10hIVM. These clocks must not be silently merged. Concanamycin prose describes a 5h incubation after GVBD and protein assessment at 6h post-GVBD.

At least three experiments are reported, with many oocytes pooled or sampled per experiment. Those counts are not independent mouse counts. Relevant blots pool 90 oocytes; Figure2 gives 60–150 per sample without clearly separating GVBD denominators. S9 reports 75/63/68 oocytes for MII and 86/91/92 for abnormal spindles. Its MAD1 caption lists 315/272/299 as oocytes, but the centromere measurement hierarchy is insufficiently defined. Donor-mouse counts and clustering are not established here.

## Mechanistic and competitive boundary

Figure5's chromosome-count rescue uses **Ccdc41 cRNA**, with 24/22/21 eggs in control/depleted/genetically rescued groups. No comparable drug-treated aneuploidy result is supplied. MAD1, BUB3, CDC20, CyclinB1 and Sirt1 measurements cannot substitute for BUBR1. The Sirt1–BubR1–CDH1 chain remains a hypothesis/reference connection; no BubR1 antibody or treatment-response measurement was identified in the checked methods and figures.

These results justify experimental follow-up beyond an anticancer-only search. They do not establish usable cell output, durable progenitor renewal, muscle compensation or MVA rescue, and do not displace niacin or apitegromab as though either were already MVA-proven. Other reagents, including Bafilomycin A1 and Brefeldin A, remain experimental-use mentions; the source's printed **200µM Bafilomycin A1** is preserved without correction or additional efficacy credit.
