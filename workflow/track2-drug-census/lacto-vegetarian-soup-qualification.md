# Protein-enriched lacto-vegetarian soup: source-specific functional comparison

Kao and colleagues report favorable intervention-versus-control GEE coefficients for SPPB and chair-rise performance after 12 weeks of a commercially described protein-enriched soup preparation in older adults at nutritional and sarcopenia risk. These reported comparative functional positives should be retained. Their exact model coding, baseline adjustment and interpretation as change-from-baseline effects are not specified sufficiently to reconstruct. Handgrip strength, six-meter walking time and body-composition coefficients were not statistically significant. This is a preparation-specific compensation comparison, not proof that isolated protein, any retail soup, or the preparation benefits MVA.

The complete scientific main and all eight native journal pages were read, including the flowchart, all three tables, footnotes, disclosures and references. The current official registry was read separately. No original protocol, SAP, participant data, analysis code or separate supplement was recovered. [Primary paper, PMID 39823681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39823681/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnha.2024.100477); [current registry NCT05243472](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05243472).

## Preparation and comparator

Participants were assigned one pack daily for 12 weeks, supplying **24–30 g protein**, of a lacto-vegetarian soup designed by licensed nutritionists and named **LAUREL NUTRILAB, Taiwan**. The paper describes it as commercially available and delivered by frozen logistics or self-collection. The current registry says the protein may come from soy, beans or milk products. These descriptions do not identify the exact recipe, variants, ingredient proportions, pack weight/volume, amino-acid composition, caloric content, micronutrient amounts, sodium, lot or an equivalent current retail SKU. Blood vitamin measurements are not the soup's micronutrient composition. One sentence in Methods ambiguously describes 24–30 grams of soup; the allocation paragraph, abstract and surrounding discussion explicitly identify that range as protein per serving. The soup's total serving mass remains unknown.

The control received healthy-aging education and **no nutritional supplement**. Participants were asked to keep their usual diet and lifestyle and avoid additional nutritional supplements or exercise programs. This is not a calorie-, protein-, attention- or ingredient-matched placebo. Maintaining habitual diet does not isolate the effect of protein from additional food energy, other ingredients, expectations or behavior.

Its “Protein” coefficient is 8.61 (−3.21 to 20.43), P=.15; “Water” is 292.62 (1.32–583.9), printed P=.05, without a significance star. These nutrient rows do not print units, baseline/final intake values, or full assessment methods. They must not be turned into the soup's nutrient label or a verified increase of 24–30 g in total daily intake. The registered food-frequency assessment is a relevant pointer, not a recovered detailed dietary dataset.

## Design, population and denominators

This was an **open-label**, 12-week randomized trial at Hualien Tzu Chi Hospital, Taiwan, conducted from 1 March 2022 to 31 August 2023. Computer-generated blocks of four were stratified by sex and age; an independent researcher performed allocation, and sealed envelopes were opened after initial assessment. There was no assigned exercise intervention. Outcome-assessor masking is not established; the registry records no masking.

Eligibility required age at least 65, MNA-SF ≤11 and either SARC-F ≥4 or SARC-CalF ≥11, plus consent and ability to complete the study. Severe/uncontrolled illness, cirrhosis, eGFR below 30, relevant malignancy, severe sensory limitations and inability to consume the preparation were among exclusions. The registry adds some exclusion details, including nuts/gluten intolerance and recent hormone therapy. These are selected community participants at risk, not necessarily a universally confirmed sarcopenia diagnosis or a population consisting entirely of vegetarians.

Of 102 screened, two were excluded for serious comorbidities and **100 were randomized, 50 per group**. The final analysis included **48 intervention and 49 control participants**. Two intervention participants withdrew; the text attributes this to difficulty with the regimen, while the flowchart says personal reasons. One control participant was lost after a fall with fracture and associated lifestyle change. These descriptions are retained without assigning an unreported causal adverse reaction.

The 97 analyzed participants averaged 72.4 ±5.1 years; 73 were women and 45 (46.4%) were vegetarians. Mean age was 72.6 in intervention and 72.3 in control. Table 2 uses n=48/49 for most outcomes, but the chair-rise row uses **46 intervention and 48 control**, without explaining those additional missing assessments. Table 3 does not give endpoint-specific GEE denominators. The main does not report an ITT definition, imputation method or sensitivity analysis for missing data; the three withdrawn participants were excluded from the final analysis.

## Reported function and the statistical estimand

The manuscript identifies several primary domains: body composition, physical performance/strength and functional assessment. Sample-size planning was based on a lean-body-mass difference of 1.14 kg, SD 1.25 kg, power .90 and alpha .05, with a stated minimum of 72 after allowing for dropout; 100 were enrolled. This does not establish power or a single prespecified priority for the favorable functional endpoints. No multiplicity correction is described.

Table 3 is titled as the impact on outcomes **at the 12th week using generalized estimating equations**. The Results and Discussion describe its coefficients as intervention-versus-control findings. Credit those author-reported comparative endpoint results. Methods describe paired t tests for within-group changes and a GEE model for intervention effects, but give no formula, link/distribution, working-correlation structure, group/time/reference coding, covariates, baseline-outcome adjustment, interaction specification or supplied code. The values should not be called independently verified *adjusted changes* or treatment-by-time effects.

SPPB combines balance, walking and chair-stand tasks; its chair component and total score are not independent replications. Methods describe a four-meter gait component within SPPB, while the separate table and registered walking outcome use six meters. Those labels should not be silently harmonized. Handgrip was the maximum of three dominant-hand efforts with a Smedley dynamometer.

Table 2 provides a separate descriptive view. Intervention SPPB was 10.8 ±1.4 before and 11.4 ±1.1 after, versus control 10.1 ±2.1 and 10.4 ±2.3. Intervention chair time was 10.8 ±3.1 to 9.8 ±2.3 s, versus control 13.4 ±6.3 to 12.1 ±5.5 s. Thus the groups began at different functional levels, and both improved in chair time. These raw trajectories are not the same quantities as the reported GEE betas. Their differences neither reconstruct the GEE nor prove it erroneous; the precise final-score versus change-effect construction remains unresolved. The intervention SPPB values were also near the scale's upper limit. No new statistical test or participant-data reanalysis was performed.

## Body composition, laboratory results and reporting limits

The abstract's broad wording says there were no significant body-composition changes in the intervention group. The main's **within-group** Results and Table 2 instead report intervention increases in waist circumference, hip circumference, total body water and BIA skeletal muscle mass (18.8 ±3.0 to 19.0 ±2.9 kg, P=.003). The **GEE comparisons** in Table 3 are nonsignificant for every body-composition row, including skeletal muscle mass −0.32 kg (−1.49 to 0.86), P=.60, and ASM index 0.06 kg/m² (−0.21 to 0.34), P=.65. Preserve this abstract/main wording distinction and the test types; neither a within-group mass gain nor a comparative null should erase the other.

The paper reports within-group lipid, renal, blood-count and electrolyte changes in both arms. None of the Table 3 cardiometabolic, inflammatory, renal, hepatic, electrolyte or vitamin coefficients is statistically significant under its printed P values. Within-arm changes are not verified treatment effects, renal safety equivalence or mechanistic mediation. BIA estimates and task improvements do not establish new tissue formation, BUBR1 restoration or chromosome fidelity. Complete table values and printed units are retained in JSON, including source labels that are not independently corrected.

## Adherence, harms, planning and disclosures

Follow-up contacts occurred every two weeks. Among the 48 intervention completers, three reportedly used 80–90% and 45 used 90–100% of the regimen. These are completer adherence figures, not 100% adherence among all randomized participants. The authors report no severe adverse reactions, without a graded event table, systematic event definitions, comparative event counts or long-term observation. The control fall/fracture and two intervention discontinuations remain part of the disposition. Normal or nonsignificant laboratory comparisons do not prove absence of harm.

The current registry identifies the same paper, institution, investigator and IRB110-246-A. It was first submitted 6 January 2022 and first posted **17 February 2022**, before the recorded **1 March 2022** start. This supports registration before the reported start. However, the current record was updated in August 2024; original historical fields could not be recovered (history access failed). It cannot certify that every current endpoint or analysis detail was prospectively fixed.

The current registry lists 11 primary and 28 secondary outcomes. Handgrip, six-meter walking and SPPB are among its primaries, while body/muscle mass is secondary there but belongs to a primary domain in the paper. Clinical Frailty Scale, FRAIL, WHOQOL-BREF and IPAQ results were not recovered in the main. Some registered laboratory measures are also absent; the companion mapping records them. The paper reports SARC-F/SARC-CalF as outcome assessments, which are not separately named in the current primary list. This is a current-record/publication comparison, not proof of prospective outcome switching. No posted results, original protocol/SAP, attached analysis code or separate supplement was obtained. The paper offers data on reasonable request; none were requested or reanalyzed.
