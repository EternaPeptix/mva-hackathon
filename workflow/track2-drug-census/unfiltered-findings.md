# What an unfiltered literature pass adds

21 September 2026. **Nicotinic acid has an additional human-cell protection
precedent, but it does not establish the proposed SIRT2–BUBR1 restoration
mechanism.** Other records identify compensation mechanisms and counterevidence
that merit follow-up. Niacin and apitegromab remain research nominations; these
readings neither establish their superiority nor close the search.

## Nicotinic acid: a positive result with a different mechanism and tissue

[The 2025 primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40062928/)
used **nicotinic acid itself**, concurrently with benzo(a)pyrene during mouse
and human oocyte maturation in culture. This is a protective co-treatment
experiment, not reversal of an established inherited defect or a clinical trial.

At 15 micromolar nicotinic acid, the reported human maturation means were
**56.7% with toxin plus drug, 31.7% with toxin alone and 73.5% in controls**.
Figure 4C gives 16, 22 and 21 oocytes, respectively, across three experiments;
these are not numbers of independent patients. The paper reports 78 human
oocytes overall, without a donor count or donor-clustered analysis. The figure
labels control-versus-toxin significance and a nonsignificant
control-versus-combination comparison; it does **not** label the direct
drug-plus-toxin versus toxin comparison. A nonsignificant difference from
controls is not equivalence or normalization. No uninjured drug-only arm is
shown in these treatment comparisons.

Mouse experiments also report higher maturation, lower oxidative-stress and
gamma-H2AX signals, and fewer abnormal spindle/alignment appearances with
co-treatment. Supplementary
Figure 7 compares drug-plus-toxin groups with controls, without toxin-only
groups in that panel. Alignment morphology is not a chromosome-count assay.
Neither fertilization, embryo/live-birth outcome nor long-term offspring health
was measured. Human mitochondrial potential improved incompletely; Supplementary
Figure 8 does not label a direct toxin-versus-combination significance test.

**The mechanistic distinction is decisive.** The paper measured SIRT1 protein,
not SIRT2, and used it as an indicator rather than directly measuring NAD.
SIRT1 overexpression separately reduced mouse oxidative stress; it did not
establish that nicotinic-acid protection requires SIRT1. The BUBR1 staining in
Supplementary Figure 1 compares toxin-exposed and control oocytes: increased
kinetochore retention accompanies checkpoint activation. It is **not** a
measurement of drug-induced BUBR1 restoration. No MVA genotype or muscle was
tested, and the culture concentration supplies no pediatric exposure bridge.

This strengthens the actual-compound cellular-protection precedent for niacin.
It cannot substitute for our proposed measurements of NAD utilization,
SIRT2/BUBR1 dependence, useful muscle output, renewal and chromosome fidelity.
The same tissue, intervention and endpoint distinctions apply to challengers.

**Reading scope:** complete primary Methods, Results and Discussion; original
Figure 4; complete supplement captions and selected original supplemental
Figures 1, 7 and 8. An independent reviewer checked the primary interpretation.
Official full text: [PMC11949519](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11949519/),
DOI [10.1530/REP-24-0364](https://doi.org/10.1530/REP-24-0364).
Raw source text and images remain outside this repository.

## Coverage without a favorable-result filter

The [fixed selection](outer-unfiltered-selection.json) took the first 384 records
under an explicitly seeded SHA-256 ordering of the **5,326 remaining saved
abstracts**. It excluded the exact source/ID union of the gene query and the prior
60 + 120 + 253 readings. It did not filter on biology, drug name, cancer, benefit,
date or publication type. All 384 complete saved abstracts received
[original assessments](outer-unfiltered-review.json). This is coverage work,
not a random-sample estimate of drug efficacy or literature prevalence.

Together the four selections contain **817 distinct outer source/ID records**;
**4,942 originally saved outer abstracts remain unread**. Study-level duplicates
and secondary reviews remain distinct and labeled. These counts do not imply
817 independent experiments or complete full-text coverage.

Separately, the [missing-abstract recovery](outer-missing-review.json) records all
**143** outer entries lacking a saved abstract. Official retrieval yielded 18
recovered abstracts and 53 short available bodies, all read. **Fourteen of those
bodies are availability notices rather than scientific articles**, and another
is only a keyword index. Eleven long bodies remain unread, five retrieved
records have no body, and 56 requests
returned HTTP 500. Failed retrieval is not evidence of absent or negative results.
Three recovered abstract entries contain only a teaser, subject label or meeting
description. In total, 53 recovered records contain some assessed scientific
content; that does not make them 53 efficacy studies. Recovery follow-up flags
also include unresolved access, not just therapeutic leads.
Correction notices and collection/title discrepancies retain explicit identities.

Recovered material includes genetic sFRP3 rescue in a BubR1 model, an unrelated
jasplakinolide osteogenic signal, and sodium-crotonate effects that reduce
Bub3/BubR1 kinetochore localization. The last paper's nicotinamide probe does
not test nicotinic acid or establish harm from the proposed niacin regimen.
Medicines benefiting a different patient, tumor or injury model receive no
transferred MVA efficacy credit. Detailed per-record evidence and reading limits
remain in the recovery ledger.

**Decision:** additional relevant lines remain open, so the condition to publish
and submit after finding no further leads is still unmet. The fixed proposal
remains a testable comparison; missing efficacy remains UNKNOWN.
