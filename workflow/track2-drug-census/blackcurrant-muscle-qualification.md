# New Zealand blackcurrant extract: short-term performance reserve

**Disposition: retain as an appendix-level common-compound challenger; no nominee change.** A small 2026 crossover reports selected strength, lifting-volume and barbell-power differences after seven days of a New Zealand blackcurrant extract in resistance-trained adults. The result is short-term exercise performance, not durable muscle function, BUB1B/BUBR1 rescue or MVA efficacy.

This qualification is outside the 289-appraisal curated drug-decision ledger. It does not close the still-pending independent lane review of the 160-record batch-14 abstract screen or add coverage credit to that screen.

## What the trial reports

Koozehchian et al. (PMID 42356316; DOI 10.3390/nu18121929) report 20 resistance-trained adults completing four seven-day conditions: no-capsule control, placebo, 250 mg/day low-dose extract, and 600 mg/day high-dose extract. Capsule conditions followed randomized order, but the no-capsule control was completed first by all participants. The article reports selected bench-press and leg-press 1RM, lifting-volume, barbell-power and Stroop differences. Specifically, the abstract reports bench-press 1RM increases versus the no-capsule control of 3.33 kg for low dose (P=.005) and 2.34 kg for high dose (P=.041); the article reports both extract conditions above placebo on bench and leg-press 1RM. Low dose produced the largest leg-press volume contrast versus control (+2627 kg, P<.001), while high dose exceeded placebo on Tendo-derived peak and mean bench power (+79.5 W, P=.006; +46.2 W, P=.026). Wingate outcomes did not differ.

The no-capsule period is a weak comparison for ingredient-specific benefit: it always came first, and the placebo condition itself improved leg-press 1RM and volume relative to that period. The authors therefore point to learning, expectancy and period effects and emphasize placebo-controlled contrasts. Dose-response was not monotonic across outcomes. This was a broad outcome battery in 20 trained adults over one week; no durable function, daily mobility, disease population or BUB1B/BUBR1 measure was reported.

## BUB1B and competition

The source provides no BUB1B mechanism, checkpoint measurement, chromosome-fidelity result or MVA model. It is a plant-extract mixture; no result transfers automatically to blackcurrant fruit, juice, seed oil, isolated anthocyanins or other retail formulations.

Compared with niacin, blackcurrant has a controlled acute exercise-performance signal but no NAD/SIRT2/BUBR1 restoration rationale. Compared with apitegromab, it has no pediatric neuromuscular-disease motor-function evidence. Those outcomes cannot be ranked as equivalent efficacy. It may be considered as a low-priority performance benchmark, but it does not approach the project’s two distinct primary hypotheses and should not enter the pitch’s lead pair.
