# Sodium pyruvate: exploratory metabolic function comparator

**27 September 2026 UTC.** Retain sodium pyruvate as an exploratory comparator, with actual functional observations and a small controlled sports result. Its clinical evidence is uncontrolled, and the reviewed work does not establish BUBR1 restoration or MVA benefit. It does not currently displace the provisional niacin/restoration and apitegromab/compensation nominations.

The proposed metabolic rationale is support for redox balance and energy production in oxidative-phosphorylation disorders. That is a different disease mechanism from BUB1B insufficiency. A NAD-related rationale does not transfer the NMN/SIRT2/BUBR1 findings to pyruvate, nor establish that this child's muscle has the same metabolic bottleneck.

**Clinical changes are not interchangeable.** GMFM values are percentages of that scale's total score, not percentage strength gains. Fujii's patient 3 went from 13.6% to 16.0% to 12.8% on GMFM while NPMDS improved; a favorable broad score did not establish sustained motor recovery. The selected long-term cohort and age-specific NPMDS versions limit generalization. Patient 4 is the previously published Saito 2012 case, not independent replication. Kuroha's adult profile is clinically distinct; overlap with the later adult series remains unresolved, so these reports are not summed into a unique-patient total.

**The sports result has a defined boundary.** Significant individual-sprint contrasts were limited to peak power in sprints 1 and 5 and mean power in sprint 5. Estimated phosphagen resynthesis was not a direct muscle ATP/PCr measurement. The sample-size calculation concerned acid-base measures, and the flow diagram repeats condition labels despite the Methods describing a crossover. Raw paired data would be needed to resolve the effect estimate's precision and analysis concerns; the direction of any statistical distortion is unknown. The separate [fixed-work physiology study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4042581/) is not credited as improved maximal performance.

**Safety and identity:** diarrhea prompted a reduction in the Kuroha case and occurred in Fujii patient 3 at the higher exposure. The sports trial's short-term absence of reported gastrointestinal discomfort does not establish clinical safety. Kuroha's alcohol/vomiting episode and recovery after fluids, rest and treatment restart do not isolate a withdrawal effect. Different preparations and schedules remain separate; sodium pyruvate is not treated as an interchangeable alias for pyruvic acid, calcium pyruvate or an unspecified retail product. No clinical regimen is proposed.

**Competitive decision:** the disease observations and controlled sports signal justify keeping a research comparator. They establish neither superiority to niacin nor apitegromab. A relevant-model comparison would first need justified exposure and improved useful absolute function, renewal and chromosome fidelity under the same standards as the nominees. A protein-restoration claim would additionally require functional BUBR1 engagement. The [BubR1-context paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5191862/) includes sodium pyruvate in shared culture medium, not as an intervention contrast; it supplies no restoration credit.

**Scope:** bounded primary appraisal with independently checked source findings. Root/native-source reading and access gaps are separately recorded in the companion JSON; raw articles remain external. Koga's full main was not recovered through normal publisher/author/institutional routes, and an unverified registry candidate was not borrowed. No new original-queue reading, biological replication, current-market equivalence or global-search completion follows.
