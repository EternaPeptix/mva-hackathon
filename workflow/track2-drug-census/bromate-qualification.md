# Potassium bromate: adverse evidence in a BubR1-low model

**23 September 2026.** Decline a therapeutic nomination. Potassium bromate is
the experimental oxidative stressor in this study. Its direct adverse results
in a low-BUBR1 model are useful to assay design; they do not establish that any
antioxidant reverses the phenotype.

[Matsuda et al., In Vivo 2016](https://doi.org/10.21873/invivo.10993)
(PMID 27815460) exposed BubR1-low and wild-type mice to bromate or tap water
for sixteen weeks. The model expresses about 20% of normal BUBR1 and is
reported to lack overt progeria under baseline conditions. It is not the
child's genotype, nor the more severe BubR1 hypomorph used in other studies.

The reported findings separate three outcomes:

- **Survival:** five low-BubR1 mice and one wild-type mouse died in the bromate
  experiment; no tap-water controls died. The reported log-rank P is 0.0082.
  The Results and survival-caption group counts are not sufficiently clear
  to calculate a new relative-risk estimate here. The author's institutional
  thesis summary shows the same adverse survival direction; it is the same
  work, not an independent replication.
- **Pathology among survivors:** two surviving low-BubR1 mice had splenomegaly
  with proliferative, non-B/non-T-marker cells, suggestive of neoplasia.
  That is distinct from a demonstrated cancer cause of the earlier deaths.
  The body discusses dehydration or gastrointestinal hemorrhage as possible
  causes of death; most early deaths lacked an explanatory visible lesion.
- **Progenitor culture:** bromate reduced reported hematopoietic progenitor
  proliferation more strongly in the low-BubR1 condition, which already had
  lower baseline proliferation. The accessible article text prints 10 mM in
  Methods but 10 µM in Results/Figure 4. This discrepancy remains unresolved;
  it is not a settled concentration or a clinically transferable exposure.

Important nulls remain visible. Both treated genotypes lost weight without a
significant between-genotype difference. Urinary 8-OHdG increased with bromate
but did not significantly distinguish the two treated genotypes. A
nonsignificant difference does not prove equal internal exposure or equal
tissue damage. In survivors, BUN increased in the low-BubR1 group, while
creatinine, AST and ALT did not show the stated treatment-related increases.
Survivor measurements cannot establish the physiology or cause of death of
the animals that had already died.

**Competitive implication:** this is evidence of vulnerability under an
oxidative challenge, not evidence that bromate helps, or that niacin, NAC,
ascorbate or another antioxidant improves this model. It supports retaining
absolute survival, proliferation and lesion burden alongside apparent cell
selection in the experimental plan. It does not validate a drug ranking or
establish a BUB1B-dependent redox mechanism for the child's variants.
