**Separate icariin from fecal microbiota transfer (FMT).** The abstract describes FMT from icariin-treated old donors to old recipients, with reported rotarod/biomarker benefits; young-donor FMT reportedly gave equal or greater improvement. Recipients of donor feces are not an additional directly dosed icariin arm. FMT is neither genetic rescue nor an isolated bacterial/metabolite treatment. These findings support a transferable fecal-component hypothesis, but do not by themselves establish exclusive microbiome mediation, BUB1b dependence or per-cell BubR1 stabilization. Donor pooling, cage/recipient clustering, conditioning, matched old-donor/vehicle controls, residual compound/metabolite transfer controls and mediation experiments remain unread.

**Nulls and harms are unknown, not absent.** The abstract does not enumerate nonpositive comparisons or systematic toxicity. Sample sizes, randomization, blinding, sex allocation, cage effects and statistical models cannot be reconstructed. Do not treat the absence of these details as either a clean design or proof that they were not performed.
