# NR chloride in NEM6: preventive mitochondrial evidence, not treated muscle-function rescue

**Recommendation:** retain this as a qualified preclinical mitochondrial addendum to the existing NR appraisal. It is scientifically useful actual-compound evidence in a congenital-myopathy model, with a marked ultrastructural observation and selected protein changes. It does **not** add a demonstrated NR-treated grip, treadmill or clinical motor benefit. The respiratory analysis supports a preventive hypothesis, but lacks a direct treatment-effect test and placebo-controlled allocation. It should not independently upgrade NR's functional priority beyond the separately qualified NICE trial.

The exact primary is [Baelde et al., PMID42323895, DOI10.1093/hmg/ddag023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13283445/). The initial observed repository was committed `3db5c8961499131d8feafbe87f47eb8e6bfbb0e0` with **230 committed decisions**, while the concurrent working tree had **233 decisions**, including the pending NICE-based NR upgrade. NR already has `prior-05`; the NR/resveratrol combination is separate. At that source-review checkpoint this NEM6 source was an unadmitted pointer. The later integration refines existing NR rather than adding a compound; the [NICE human-function qualification](nr-pad-qualification.md) remains its functional priority evidence. No original-queue reading credit follows.

## What was administered, and when

The experiment uses the homozygous **Kbtbd13 R408C knock-in** mouse model of NEM6. The human founder variant is associated with autosomal-dominant NEM6; the homozygous mouse is a model, not a BUB1B/MVA genotype. Natural-history observations first identify impaired soleus mitochondrial respiration at about three months, with no detected genotype difference at one month.

Treatment begins at **four weeks of age** and continues for **eight weeks**, ending at approximately three months. This tests intervention before the observed mitochondrial deficit develops. It does not test reversal after established weakness or mitochondrial dysfunction, nor persistence after treatment withdrawal. The paper states **500 mg/kg/day NR**, supplied as **NR chloride, MedChemExpress HY-123033A**, dissolved in drinking water at **3 mg/ml**, refreshed twice weekly. The Methods once call it “nicotinamide ribose,” but the title, defined abbreviation and catalogued reagent in the same paragraph identify riboside chloride.

The 500-mg/kg/day value is the stated regimen, not an independently measured received dose: individual water intake, adherence, body-weight dose adjustment and measured systemic exposure are not reported. The mice were group-housed with free access to water/food. No conversion between chloride mass and free-riboside equivalent is supplied. Do not infer identity with arbitrary retail NR products, niacin, NMN or administered NAD+.

WT and NEM6 mice both received NR. Untreated WT/NEM6 comparisons are also plotted, but the authors explicitly acknowledge **lack of placebo-controlled mice for the treatment study**. Random allocation, blinded endpoint assessment, contemporaneous matching and a full animal-flow/exclusion account are not reported. These are important controls on a causal treatment claim; the presence of an NR-treated WT group alone does not supply a randomized NR-versus-placebo contrast.

The morphological quantification uses six images per mouse. Methods describe 80–260 healthy and 55–110 damaged mitochondria per mouse for the morphology procedure and approximately 40 mitochondria in three to four high-magnification images for cristae analysis. These generic methodological counts should not be imposed on the treated <1%-damaged group, nor summed to manufacture an independent sample size. The figure legend provides n=3 mice per morphology condition; it does not provide a comprehensive treated-cohort animal total or endpoint-by-sex denominator for every assay.

NR-treated electron micrographs still show disrupted Z-line continuity. Improved mitochondrial appearance therefore is not complete repair of the sarcomeric lesion or whole-muscle structure.

## The respiratory comparison is narrower than “NR significantly rescued respiration”

Figure 5H–J displays untreated WT/NEM6 and NR-treated WT/NEM6, **normalizing untreated animals to untreated WT and treated animals to treated WT**. The legend specifies unpaired genotype comparisons **within each treatment condition**. The native figure gives:

| Respiratory measure | Untreated WT versus NEM6 P | NR-treated WT versus NEM6 P |
|---|---:|---:|
| OXPHOS | .033 | .439 |
| NADH-linked | .135 | .483 |
| Succinate-linked | .002 | .895 |

Thus, the OXPHOS/succinate genotype difference present without NR is not detected after NR. The untreated NADH-linked comparison was already nonsignificant. Neither loss of significance nor similarity of separately normalized bars establishes a statistically significant treatment effect. A direct treated-versus-untreated NEM6 test, a genotype-by-treatment interaction, a common absolute-scale effect estimate and its confidence interval are not supplied. Endpoint-specific n/sex counts for these respiratory panels are not explicitly stated in the main legend or essential supplement; plotted symbols were not converted into invented animal counts.

High-resolution respirometry is a functional organelle assay on permeabilized soleus bundles, normalized to wet sample mass before presentation as fold changes. It deserves that functional credit. It is not measured whole-animal strength or endurance and does not by itself establish equivalence to WT or full respiratory restoration. The data are compatible with the authors' prevention hypothesis, with the control and contrast limitations above.

## Natural history, NAD and mechanism must stay separate

The untreated natural-history series finds soleus OXPHOS impairment from three months onward, but no corresponding EDL respiratory difference at the reported older ages. Untreated treadmill measures show no significant deficit at three to four months, followed by poorer running/metabolic performance at eight to nine months and poorer running at eighteen to twenty months. These comparisons characterize genotype progression. They are not NR treatment outcomes, and the study did not treat through those later ages to test prevention of the running deficit. The paper also notes that weakness itself may affect the treadmill findings; it does not isolate mitochondrial dysfunction as their sole cause.

Proteomics/metabolomics in untreated muscle motivate the NAD hypothesis. Authors report lower NAD+ in **three- and nine-month-old** NEM6 soleus; that wording should not expand to measured NAD deficiency at every age, in this study's human patients, or in the four-week-old animals at treatment initiation. No precise NAD treatment effect is supplied, and **post-NR muscle NAD levels were not reported**. NADH histoenzymatic staining is not a direct assay of the endogenous NAD+ pool. Lower tryptophan abundance is not a measured flux defect proving a causal de novo synthesis failure.

Supplement Figure 5A–B tests a different intervention: acute **10 μM NAD+ added ex vivo** to NEM6 soleus preparations. It does not improve NADH-linked respiration (**paired-test P=.9405**; three paired observations visible, biological sampling denominator not explicitly stated there). This is not chronic NR, not oral NAD therapy, and not proof that every NAD-directed intervention is ineffective.

No BUB1B/BUBR1 protein, checkpoint, chromosome-fidelity or allele-rescue assay is reported. Neither the protein changes nor presumed NAD boosting establish a causal chain from NR to BUBR1 stabilization. Even within NEM6, the study does not establish NAD restoration as mediator of the morphology/respiratory findings. KBTBD13 is not BUBR1.

## Harms and essential counterevidence

The report does not present systematic treatment-arm adverse events, mortality, organ toxicity, cancer outcomes, received exposure or long-term safety. Statements that NR is generally well tolerated cite other literature; they are not safety results from this experiment. No specific treatment harm is established here, but absence of a harms table is not proof of safety or pediatric suitability.

At **complete-abstract depth only**, [PMID33092650, DOI10.1186/s13395-020-00249-y](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33092650/) supplies useful counterevidence in dystrophin-deficient mice under repeated eccentric challenge. It reports that NR did not significantly change the muscle molecular phenotype, sustainably expand bulk NAD, reduce injury markers or improve maximal hindlimb strength; a separate CD38 inhibitor changed metabolites more extensively without functional protection. This is a context-specific actual NR functional null, not a null for all congenital myopathies. Dose, preparation, sample sizes and detailed analyses were not established from that abstract and are not borrowed from the NEM6 paper. The two drugs' findings and the two disease models remain separate.

## Research disposition and reading scope

This source strengthens a **preclinical mitochondrial/morphology hypothesis** for an already appraised NR route. Its actual administered-compound observations should not be dismissed simply because they are not BUBR1-specific. Conversely, it supplies no new demonstrated treated strength/endurance result to compete with the existing NICE human walking signal, niacin's separate open-label strength evidence, or apitegromab's disease-specific motor evidence. All retain disease-transfer limits; no selectively stricter MVA-proof requirement or quantitative cross-trial ranking is used. Reversal, later-life treated function, NAD target engagement and relevant exposure remain unresolved research questions.
