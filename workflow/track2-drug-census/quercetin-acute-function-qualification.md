## What the primary source establishes

[Nishikawa et al., DOI 10.1139/apnm-2025-0169, PMID 41056568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41056568/) describes 38 older adults divided among exercise at 40%, 60%, or 80% of maximal voluntary contraction. Each participant completed two exercise sessions, with placebo or 500 mg quercetin glycoside before a session. This establishes repeated-session controlled exposure. The displayed correlation caption supports paired, within-person QUE-minus-PLA change contrasts for contributing participants; allocation/order, washout and complete paired outcomes for all 38 remain UNKNOWN. Randomization of intensity allocation is not established by the accessible abstract. PubMed classifies the article as a randomized controlled trial and indexes double blinding; that indexing is not a substitute for inspected allocation methods.

Motor-unit behavior and electrically elicited contraction torque were measured before ingestion, after ingestion and after exercise. The accessible results are:

| Outcome/comparison | Reported result | Permitted interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise-related torque decline, glycoside versus placebo | Low intensity P=.014; moderate P=.025 | Greater acute contractile decline with active ingestion in each of these intensity groups |
| Same contrast, high intensity | P=.792 | No statistically significant difference reported; not equivalence or proof of no effect |
| Motor-unit recruitment threshold, before to after ingestion | P<.001 for the reported decrease | Physiological signal; abstract does not specify its full treatment contrast or model |
| Association of treatment differences in torque decline and higher-threshold motor-unit recruitment changes | Low: rs=.566, P=.047; moderate: rs=.692, P=.011; high P=.228 | Association consistent with the authors' recruitment hypothesis; not mediation or proof of a beneficial training adaptation |

**No treatment-by-intensity interaction result was accessible.** Different within-intensity P values do not establish effect modification. The lower threshold is not itself a clinical benefit. No torque means, treatment-effect magnitudes, confidence intervals, standardized effects, participant-level data, or full statistical model were accessible. This remains eligible measured-function evidence despite those missing quantities; missing reporting is not a null result. There is no reanalysis or reconstruction from P values.

The [publisher-provided first-page preview on ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396292695_Effects_of_quercetin_ingestion_on_neuromuscular_system_following_single_session_of_resistance_exercise_at_differing_intensities_in_older_adults) frames acute contractile decline as a possible indication of peripheral exercise stress. Its displayed correlation-figure caption identifies treatment-difference change scores, nine panels across intensity and motor-unit threshold groups, Spearman correlations, and a nominal P<.05 display rule. That context does not demonstrate later strength gain or clinical harm. A future chronic adaptation remains a hypothesis in this acute experiment.

## Preparation, design and access gaps

Only oral ingestion of **500 mg quercetin glycoside** is primary-source verified here. Manufacturer, glycoside distribution, batch purity, excipients, aglycone-equivalent dose, plasma exposure and exact pre-exercise interval remain unresolved. Secondary summaries naming enzymatically modified isoquercitrin, a 60-minute interval or 13/13/12 participants were encountered but are not used as verified trial facts. The primary abstract gives total n=38; randomized, completed and analyzed n by intensity/condition/outcome are unavailable. The caption-supported paired contrasts do not establish complete paired data for all 38 participants or an intention-to-treat analysis.

The existing six-week assessment concerns 200 mg/day of San-Ei Gen F.F.I. enzymatically produced glycosides plus dextrin, with matched training. The acute source belongs under the existing glycoside evidence discussion with its own identity caveat. The broad glycoside label alone does not establish the same manufacturer or molecular composition. No aglycone, D+Q, arbitrary retail-product or catalog inheritance is made.

Primary-versus-secondary outcome hierarchy, prospective registration, sample-size plan, allocation concealment, blinding implementation, period/carryover handling, repeated-measure model, motor-unit clustering, exclusions and multiplicity correction are UNKNOWN. The accessible correlation caption shows at least nine correlations, so nominal subgroup correlations should not be promoted to multiplicity-controlled evidence. Bounded registry searches located other quercetin studies, including the existing six-week trial and a seven-day young-adult trial; neither is assigned to this acute source. Its own registry/protocol was not identified. This does not establish that none exists.

Adverse-event ascertainment, events and withdrawals are inaccessible; no safety or zero-event claim follows. PubMed's conflict statement reports no competing interests. Publisher-deposited Crossref metadata names Japan Society for the Promotion of Science award JPJSBP-82626; the complete funding statement, supplier role and author funding interpretation were not read. Publication metadata disagree on the date (print-year indexing versus a Crossref publication-history assertion of 19 November 2025); identity is bound by DOI/PMID, with no unsupported exact chronology asserted.
